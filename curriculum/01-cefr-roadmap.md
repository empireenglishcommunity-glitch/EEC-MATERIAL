# EEC CEFR Roadmap (v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 1.1 · **Requirements:** R2 (Curriculum & Roadmap), R3 (Assessment)
> **Reads with:** `methodology/01-eec-methodology-handbook.md` (how we teach)
> **Purpose:** The backbone learner journey — every stage from absolute beginner to the flagship B2
> graduation (and beyond to C1/exam). Each stage defines entry/exit criteria, vocabulary and grammar
> targets, Accent Lab focus, mindset focus, study-hour budget, and its summative assessment. The Stage-0
> curriculum (Task 1.3) and all later content are built to fit this spine.

---

## 1. The spine at a glance

EEC uses **CEFR (A1–C1)** as its internal proficiency spine. Each stage has a friendly EEC label.

| Stage | EEC label | CEFR | Core outcome (headline) | Arabic support |
|--|--|--|--|--|
| 0 | **Foundations** | Pre-A1 → A1 | Speak your first real sentences; survive basic interactions | Heavy (~70%) |
| 1 | **Elementary** | A2 | Handle everyday conversations and routines | Moderate (~40%) |
| 2 | **Intermediate** | B1 | Express opinions, tell stories, cope independently | Light (~15%) |
| 3 | **Confident** ⭐ | B2 | **Flagship graduation:** fluent 20–30 min conversation on everyday & professional topics, clear American accent | None (immersion) |
| 4 | **Advanced / Exam** | C1 (+TOEFL/IELTS) | Near-native range and nuance; optional certification | None |

⭐ **Stage 3 (B2) is the flagship graduation benchmark** the whole program engineers backward from.

### Study-hour budgets & cohort length

Guided-learning-hour estimates below follow widely used CEFR planning ranges (e.g., Cambridge-style
guided hours). They are **planning estimates**, not guarantees — actual pace varies by learner effort
and starting point. "Cohort length" assumes ~1 study hour/day (≈7 hrs/week) with the hybrid model.

| Stage | CEFR | Guided hours to *reach* level (cumulative) | Increment for this stage | Approx. cohort length @ ~7 hrs/wk |
|--|--|--|--|--|
| 0 | A1 | ~90–100 | ~80–100 | ~10–14 weeks |
| 1 | A2 | ~180–200 | ~100 | ~10–14 weeks |
| 2 | B1 | ~350–400 | ~150–200 | ~16–24 weeks |
| 3 | B2 | ~500–600 | ~150–200 | ~16–24 weeks |
| 4 | C1 | ~700–800+ | ~200+ | ~24+ weeks |

> **Founding-cohort note (build-just-ahead, design §3.4):** build **Stage 0 fully before launch**;
> build Stage 1 while the founding cohort completes Stage 0, and so on. The founding cohort's sellable
> scope is Stage 0 (optionally Stage 0→A2) — see Task 3.1.
> **Practical framing for learners:** "zero to confident B2" is a multi-stage journey of roughly a year+
> of consistent effort — we sell *stages with visible wins*, not a vague "fluent fast" promise (honesty, GC-5).

## 2. Cross-stage design rules

- **Every stage** is taught via the Learning Loop (Handbook §4) and standard lesson template (§12).
- **Speaking-first** weighting per stage (Handbook §5).
- **Arabic fades** on the schedule in Handbook §6.
- **Accent Lab** runs in every stage, sequenced from foundational sounds → prosody → refinement.
- **Mindset track** runs in every stage, sequenced from "starting & identity" → "consistency" →
  "plateau & immersion" → "refinement & real-world use."
- **Entry/exit criteria** are expressed as measurable CEFR-style "can-do" statements and verified by the
  assessment suite (Task 1.6), with a speaking-weighted summative test at each stage's end.

## 3. Stage 0 — Foundations (Pre-A1 → A1)

**Who enters:** absolute beginners, including those who can barely read English or know only scattered
words. *Entry criterion:* none — this is the true zero start (placement confirms Pre-A1/A1).

### Exit criteria (A1 "can-do")
By the end, the learner can:
- Introduce themselves and others; give basic personal information (name, job, country, family).
- Ask and answer simple questions on familiar topics (numbers, prices, time, daily routine).
- Understand and use everyday expressions and very basic phrases for concrete needs.
- Speak in simple, connected sentences using the present, with frequent support.
- Read and write short, simple phrases and sentences.
- **Produce their first genuine spoken exchanges in English** (the emotional "I can speak!" win).

### Vocabulary target
~**500–800** high-frequency words (survival + daily life: family, food, work, time, places, common verbs).

### Grammar & functions
- Verb **to be**; subject pronouns; **present simple** (habits/facts); **there is/are**.
- Articles (a/an/the), plurals, basic prepositions (in/on/at), possessives.
- Question formation (what/where/when/who/how much); short answers.
- Functions: greetings, introductions, ordering, asking prices/directions, describing routine.

### Accent Lab focus (foundational sounds)
- The English **vowel system** vs Arabic (key short/long contrasts, e.g., ship/sheep).
- **/p/ vs /b/** (Arabic lacks /p/); **/v/ vs /f/**; hard **/g/**.
- Introduction to **word stress** and the "music" of English.
- Technique: minimal-pair perception → production; simple shadowing.

### Mindset focus ("Starting & Identity")
- "I am becoming an English speaker" identity; why consistency beats intensity.
- Setting the kickoff goal; building the daily practice habit; overcoming fear of speaking/embarrassment.

### Summative assessment
- Short quiz (vocab/grammar recognition + production) **plus** a **speaking task** (self-introduction +
  simple Q&A) scored on the 5-dimension rubric. Graduation "after" sample recorded and compared to the
  Day-1 baseline.

## 4. Stage 1 — Elementary (A2)

**Entry criterion:** A1 achieved (Stage 0 exit or placement).

### Exit criteria (A2 "can-do")
- Communicate in simple, routine tasks requiring direct exchange of information.
- Describe in simple terms their background, environment, and immediate needs.
- Handle short social exchanges (shopping, travel basics, appointments, small talk).
- Tell a simple story or describe past events and future plans.

### Vocabulary target
~**1,000–1,500** words (cumulative) — expanded daily life, work basics, travel, feelings, descriptions.

### Grammar & functions
- **Past simple** (regular/irregular); **present continuous**; **going to / will** (future).
- Comparatives/superlatives; countable/uncountable + quantifiers; adverbs of frequency.
- Modals (can/could/should/have to) for ability, requests, advice, obligation.
- Functions: narrating past events, making plans, giving advice, describing people/places.

### Accent Lab focus
- **/θ/ and /ð/** ("think," "this"); **consonant clusters / vowel-insertion** fix (street, school, asked).
- Word stress in longer words; beginning **sentence rhythm** (stressed vs unstressed).

### Mindset focus ("Consistency & Momentum")
- Habit-stacking and environment design; tracking practice; handling missed days without quitting.
- Building a personal English input diet (music/shows/podcasts at level).

### Summative assessment
- Quiz + a **speaking task** (short narration + role-play exchange) on the rubric; conversation length
  and range expectations increase vs Stage 0.

## 5. Stage 2 — Intermediate (B1)

**Entry criterion:** A2 achieved.

### Exit criteria (B1 "can-do")
- Deal with most situations likely to arise while travelling or in daily/work life.
- Produce connected text/speech on familiar topics; describe experiences, events, hopes, plans.
- Give reasons and brief explanations for opinions and plans.
- **Cope independently** — begin functioning largely in English (Arabic support now light).

### Vocabulary target
~**2,000–2,500** words (cumulative) — abstract-ish topics, work/study, opinions, connectors.

### Grammar & functions
- Present perfect (experience/recent past) vs past simple; used to; past continuous.
- First & second conditionals; relative clauses; reported speech (intro); passive (intro).
- Discourse connectors (however, although, because, so that) for connected speech.
- Functions: expressing/justifying opinions, storytelling with detail, hypotheticals, agreeing/disagreeing.

### Accent Lab focus
- **Connected speech**: linking, weak forms, **reductions** (gonna/wanna/gotta).
- Sentence-level **intonation** (questions, lists, emphasis); refining stress-timing (the American "beat").

### Mindset focus ("Breaking the Plateau & Going Immersive")
- Pre-teach the intermediate plateau (normal, not failure); pushing into fuller immersion.
- Speaking without translating in the head; building thinking-in-English habits.

### Summative assessment
- Quiz + an extended **speaking task** (opinion + discussion, 8–12 min) on the rubric; immersion expected
  (English-only instructions).

## 6. Stage 3 — Confident (B2) ⭐ Flagship graduation

**Entry criterion:** B1 achieved.

### Exit criteria (B2 "can-do") — the graduation benchmark
- **Hold a natural 20–30 minute conversation on everyday and professional topics, clearly and
  confidently, with a neutral American accent** (the EEC flagship promise).
- Understand the main ideas of complex speech, including some technical/professional discussion.
- Interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes interaction with native speakers
  comfortable for both sides, with limited strain.
- Produce clear, detailed speech/text on a wide range of subjects; explain a viewpoint with pros/cons.

### Vocabulary target
~**3,500–4,000+** words (cumulative), including professional/workplace and topic-specific range.

### Grammar & functions
- Full range of tenses used accurately; third & mixed conditionals; passive across tenses; reported
  speech; relative clauses (defining/non-defining); modals of deduction/speculation.
- Nuance: hedging, register (formal/informal), collocations and natural phrasing.
- Functions: professional conversation, presenting/defending opinions, negotiating, handling nuance and
  abstract topics, small talk to deep talk.

### Accent Lab focus (refinement)
- Fine-tuning residual interference; naturalness of prosody, thought groups, and emphasis.
- Sounding **clear and confident** in professional contexts; self-monitoring and self-correction.

### Mindset focus ("Real-World Fluency & Identity Lock-in")
- Using English in real life (work, calls, content); owning an English-speaking identity.
- From "learner" to "user"; strategies to keep improving independently after the program.

### Summative assessment (flagship)
- The **B2 benchmark task**: a 20–30 min conversation covering everyday + professional topics, scored on
  the rubric with a defined minimum across all five dimensions. Final "after" sample is the headline
  transformation proof and, with consent, a flagship testimonial asset.

## 7. Stage 4 — Advanced / Exam (C1 + TOEFL/IELTS)

**Entry criterion:** B2 achieved (flagship graduate).

### Exit criteria (C1 "can-do")
- Understand a wide range of demanding, longer texts/speech and recognize implicit meaning.
- Express ideas fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions.
- Use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic, and professional purposes.

### Vocabulary target
~**5,000+** words (cumulative), including idiomatic, academic, and domain-specific language.

### Grammar & functions
- Full mastery with stylistic control; advanced cohesion; nuance, idiom, and tone.

### Accent Lab focus
- Near-native prosody and naturalness; individual polish (aspirational ceiling, never guaranteed — GC-5).

### Exam Track (optional add-on)
- **TOEFL iBT** (academic/US) and **IELTS** (migration/UK) preparation: format familiarization, strategies,
  timed practice, and full mock tests with scoring. Offered as a specialized track, not the core promise.

### Mindset focus ("Mastery & Contribution")
- Lifelong maintenance; mentoring others; using English as a career/opportunity multiplier.

### Summative assessment
- C1-level rubric assessment; for exam students, official-style mock TOEFL/IELTS with band/score reporting.
