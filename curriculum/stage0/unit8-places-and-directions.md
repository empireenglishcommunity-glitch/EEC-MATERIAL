# Stage 0 · Unit 8 — Places & Directions (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 1.3 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage0/00-stage0-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~30% (fading), instructions/explanations in Egyptian Arabic; learner outputs in English.
> **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can ask for and give simple directions and talk about places in
> a town — and can give directions to a place from memory.
> **Grammar spine:** places in town · Is there a…? (recycled there is/are) · imperatives (go/turn/take) ·
> prepositions of movement (to/into/across/along/past) · **Accent Lab:** intonation of instructions +
> clear vowels in direction words · **Mindset:** confidence in real interactions (ask strangers; be ok with mistakes).

**Unit lessons:** L01 Places in town · L02 "Is there a…near here?" · L03 Giving directions (imperatives) ·
L04 Following & giving a route (prepositions of movement) · L05 Unit speaking task: give directions + review.

**Assets to produce:** places-in-town flashcards, a simple town map, a "directions" clip (American model),
direction-word audio, imperative + preposition frames, a Google Form per lesson, community prompts.

## S0-U8-L01 — Places in Town

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 8 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Unit 7.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can name common places in a town (bank, supermarket, hospital, school, restaurant, station, pharmacy, mosque/church).
- The learner can say what places are near them (*"There is a bank near my house."*).
- The learner can say where they go (*"I go to the supermarket."*).

**Target language:** places in town; *"There is a ___ near ___." / "I go to the ___."*
**Grammar:** there is + place (recycled U3); go to + place. **Accent Lab focus:** clear vowels in
*bank, park, station*; stress the place word.
**Arabic support:** ~30%. **Mindset focus:** confidence grows by doing — plan one real English interaction this week.

**Materials:** places flashcards; town map; clip; frames slide; Google Form (match place → picture).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: describe a person (U7) + there is/are (U3). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "There is a bank near my house. I go to the supermarket on Friday."; text + Arabic gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Places vocab; *there is a + place*; *go to the + place*; explain (Arabic) any new words. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clear vowels: bank /æ/, park /ɑːr/, station /eɪ/; stress the place. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Point on the map: *"There is a ___."*; say 3 places you go to. | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: name 3 places near you; record one. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check vocab + there is/go to; queue weak items. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a voice note: 3 places near your home. Places quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 4 places + "There is a…". Stretch — add frequency (from U4): "I go to the gym twice a week".
**Success check:** learner names ≥4 places intelligibly. **Queue:** places feed L02–L05.
**Coach notes:** recycle there is/are and go to explicitly — this unit reinforces earlier grammar in a new context (spaced review in action).

## S0-U8-L02 — "Is There a … Near Here?"

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 8 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can ask if a place exists nearby (*"Is there a pharmacy near here?"*).
- The learner can answer (*"Yes, there is. / No, there isn't."*) and say roughly where (*"It's near the bank."*).
- The learner can use *Excuse me* to start (recycled U0).

**Target language:** Is there a…? + near here; *Excuse me*; location answers (near/next to — recycled U3).
**Grammar:** Is there…? short answers; prepositions of place recycled. **Accent Lab focus:** polite
*"Excuse me"* + rising question intonation.
**Arabic support:** ~30%. **Mindset focus:** it's okay to ask strangers — everyone does; a question is not a failure.

**Materials:** town map; a "asking on the street" clip; frames slide; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: places from L01. | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "Excuse me, is there a bank near here?" "Yes, it's next to the pharmacy."; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Is there a…near here?; short answers; *Excuse me*; location answers; explain (Arabic). | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Polite "Excuse me"; rising intonation on the question. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Map drill: ask *"Is there a ___ near here?"*; partner answers with a location. | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: ask about 2 real places near you; answer; record. | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check question form + short answers; queue. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: ask *"Is there a ___ near here?"* to a classmate and answer theirs. | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — the question + yes/no answer. Stretch — add the location ("It's next to…").
**Success check:** learner asks a correct Is-there question + answers. **Queue:** feeds L03 (directions).
**Coach notes:** connects U0 survival phrases + U3 prepositions + U8 places — a nice integrative moment. Reassure the "asking is normal" mindset.

## S0-U8-L03 — Giving Directions (Imperatives)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 8 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can give simple directions using imperatives (go straight, turn left/right, take the first/second…).
- The learner can understand directions given to them.
- The learner can say *"It's on the left/right."*

**Target language:** imperatives (go, turn, take, stop); left/right/straight; first/second; on the left/right.
**Grammar:** imperative form (base verb, no subject). **Accent Lab focus:** falling **instruction
intonation**; clear /l/ vs /r/ in left/right.
**Arabic support:** ~25%. **Mindset focus:** mistakes when asking directions cost nothing — practice boldly.

**Materials:** town map with a route; direction gestures visual; clip; frames slide; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: "Is there a ___ near here?" (L02). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip giving directions: "Go straight, turn left, it's on the right."; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Imperatives (no subject); left/right/straight; take the first/second; explain (Arabic). | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | /l/ (left) vs /r/ (right) — a key clarity pair; firm falling instruction tone. | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Follow a route on the map as the teacher gives directions; then give a short route. | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: give directions from A to B on the map; record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check imperative use + left/right clarity; queue. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: give directions from your home to a nearby place (2–3 steps). | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — 2 steps (go straight, turn left). Stretch — a 4-step route + "it's on the…".
**Success check:** learner gives a 2–3 step route intelligibly. **Queue:** directions feed L04–L05.
**Coach notes:** left/right clarity matters practically; drill /l/–/r/. Imperatives are simple (no subject) — a confidence-builder.

## S0-U8-L04 — Following & Giving a Route (Prepositions of Movement)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 8 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use prepositions of movement (go to, into, across, along, past, up/down).
- The learner can give a fuller route combining imperatives + movement prepositions.
- The learner can check understanding (*"Sorry, turn left or right?"* — recycled survival phrases).

**Target language:** to/into/across/along/past/up/down; combine with imperatives.
**Grammar:** preposition of movement + place. **Accent Lab focus:** linking *"go-past-the"*; clear vowels.
**Arabic support:** ~25%. **Mindset focus:** you can now handle a real navigation conversation — a big life skill.

**Materials:** detailed town map; movement-preposition visual; frames slide; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: give a 2-step direction (L03). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "Go along this street, go past the bank, and turn into King Street."; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Movement prepositions (along/past/into/across); combine with imperatives; explain (Arabic). | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Link *"go-past-the"*, *"go-along"*; keep it smooth. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Give a fuller route on the map (3–4 steps with movement prepositions). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: A asks for a place, B gives a full route; A confirms; record. | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check preposition + imperative combo; queue. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: give a 3–4 step route to a place near you. | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — imperatives + "to". Stretch — full route with 3 movement prepositions + a clarification exchange.
**Success check:** learner gives a 3–4 step route intelligibly. **Queue:** routes recycle into U10 review.
**Coach notes:** encourage learners to use survival phrases (U0) to clarify — this integrates conversation-management with directions.

## S0-U8-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: Give Directions (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 8 · ~30 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can conduct a full directions exchange (ask → give a multi-step route → confirm/thank).
- The learner can play both the asker and the direction-giver.
- The learner records a **directions exchange** (interactive proof).

**Target language:** integrated Unit-8 language. **Grammar:** Is there…? + imperatives + movement prepositions consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** /l/–/r/ + instruction intonation + linking; each learner's flagged weak sound.
**Arabic support:** ~25%. **Mindset focus:** this is confident, real-world English — celebrate the boldness.

**Materials:** a town map; a model directions dialogue; a checklist card; recording prompt; Unit-8 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Chain recall: places, Is there…?, imperatives, movement prepositions. | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a model directions exchange (ask → route → confirm → thanks). | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break into stages; build the "good directions exchange" checklist. | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills their weakest sound flagged in U8 (often /l/–/r/). | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse both roles with the map + card; peer practice (pairs). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the directions exchange (in pairs live, or solo playing both parts). | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level delayed feedback. | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the exchange; complete Unit-8 quiz; celebrate progress. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — asker role + a 2-step route. Stretch — both roles + a clarification + a 4-step route.
**Success check (Unit-8 summary):** completes a directions exchange with a multi-step route, intelligible (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation + Interaction at A1). **Queue:** directions recycle into Unit 10 review.
**Coach notes:** ~9 weeks in — a real navigation conversation is a very practical, confidence-boosting proof clip. Log with consent.
