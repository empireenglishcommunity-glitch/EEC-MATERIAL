# Stage 0 · Unit 4 — Daily Routine (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 1.3 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage0/00-stage0-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~50% (fading), instructions/explanations in Egyptian Arabic; learner outputs in English.
> **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can describe their daily routine, tell the time, and say how
> often they do things — and can record a "my typical day" description.
> **Grammar spine:** present simple (I/you/we/they + he/she/it -s) · telling the time · frequency
> adverbs (always/usually/sometimes/never) · **Accent Lab:** 3rd-person -s endings (/s/,/z/,/ɪz/) +
> sentence rhythm/stress · **Mindset:** tracking practice; recovering after a missed day (no guilt spiral).

**Unit lessons:** L01 My day (present simple I/you) · L02 He/she + verb-s · L03 What time is it? ·
L04 How often? (frequency) · L05 Unit speaking task: describe your typical day + review.

**Assets to produce:** daily-activity flashcards, a clock visual, a "my day" clip (American model),
3rd-person -s audio, a frequency-adverbs scale visual, a Google Form per lesson, community voice-note prompts.

## S0-U4-L01 — My Day: Daily Activities (Present Simple I/you)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 4 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Unit 3.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can name daily activities (get up, have breakfast, go to work, come home, sleep…).
- The learner can describe their own routine using present simple (I/you): *"I get up at… I go to work."*
- The learner can ask *"What time do you…?"* (basic).

**Target language:** daily-routine verbs/phrases; *"I get up / have breakfast / go to work / study / sleep."*
**Grammar:** present simple for I/you/we/they (base form). **Accent Lab focus:** linking verbs in phrases
(*get-up, go-to*); clear content-word stress.
**Arabic support:** ~50%. **Mindset focus:** track your practice — a simple daily tick keeps momentum.

**Materials:** daily-activity flashcards (with times); "my day" clip; frames slide; Google Form (order the day).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: describe your room in 2 sentences (U3). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I get up at 7. I have breakfast. I go to work."; text + Arabic gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Present the routine verbs in order; explain (Arabic) present simple = habits/every day. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Link phrases naturally: *get-up, go-to-work*; stress the key word. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Sequence your morning: *"First I…, then I…"* (base verbs). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: tell a partner 3 things you do every day; record one. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check base-verb use + linking; queue weak items. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a voice note: 3 daily activities (*"I … every day."*). Order-the-day quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 3 activities. Stretch — add times (preview L03) and "then/after that".
**Success check:** learner states ≥3 routine activities intelligibly. **Queue:** routine verbs feed L02 (he/she) and L04.
**Coach notes:** keep to I/you here so learners get fluent before the he/she -s rule (L02). Don't pre-load the -s yet.

## S0-U4-L02 — He/She + Verb-s ("She Works…")

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 4 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can describe someone else's routine using the 3rd-person **-s** (*"He works. She gets up early."*).
- The learner can pronounce -s endings correctly (/s/, /z/, /ɪz/: works /s/, goes /z/, watches /ɪz/).
- The learner can ask *"What does he/she do every day?"* (basic).

**Target language:** routine verbs in 3rd person; *"He/She + verb-s."* **Grammar:** present simple he/she/it (+s/es).
**Accent Lab focus:** the 3rd-person -s sounds — the same three sounds as plurals (transfer from U3-L02).
**Arabic support:** ~50%. **Mindset focus:** missed a day? Just restart today — don't quit the whole thing.

**Materials:** 3rd-person -s audio (3 groups); "his/her day" clip; frames slide; Google Form (choose the -s sound).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: your own routine (I) from L01. | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "My brother gets up at 6. He works in a bank. She watches TV."; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | The -s rule for he/she/it; connect to plural -s sounds from U3; explain (Arabic). | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Sort verbs by -s sound: works /s/, goes /z/, watches /ɪz/; drill. | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Transform: I get up → *"He gets up."*; describe a family member's routine (3 sentences). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: describe a family member's day (he/she + -s); record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Target the missing -s (very common error) + the -s sound; queue. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: 3 sentences about someone's daily routine (he/she + verb-s). | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 2 verbs with clear -s. Stretch — mix I and he/she in one description.
**Success check:** correct 3rd-person -s in ≥3 sentences with intelligible endings. **Queue:** 3rd-person -s recycles across all later units.
**Coach notes:** "He work" (dropped -s) is one of the most persistent Arabic-transfer errors — target it relentlessly but kindly; it's worth the investment.

## S0-U4-L03 — What Time Is It? Telling the Time

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 4 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can tell the time (o'clock, half past, quarter past/to, and the digital form 7:15).
- The learner can ask *"What time is it?" / "What time do you…?"* and answer.
- The learner can add times to their routine (*"I get up at 7 o'clock."*).

**Target language:** time expressions; *"What time is it?" / "It's ___." / "at ___."*
**Grammar:** at + time; present simple + time. **Accent Lab focus:** clear /kw/ in *quarter*; linking "It's".
**Arabic support:** ~50%. **Mindset focus:** anchor practice to a fixed time (habit-stacking).

**Materials:** clock visual (analog + digital); time flashcards; frames slide; Google Form (match clock → time).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: numbers 0–20 (U0) + he/she routine (L02). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Listen/watch times said in context: "It's seven o'clock. It's half past eight."; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | o'clock / half past / quarter past / quarter to + digital (7:15 = seven fifteen); *at + time*. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | /kw/ in *quarter*; link *"it's-seven"*; clear numbers. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Read clocks aloud; then add times to your routine (*"I go to work at 8."*). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: ask *"What time do you get up?"*; answer with a real time; record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check time forms + at + time; queue weak items. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: your morning routine with 3 times. Clock quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — o'clock + digital only. Stretch — full half/quarter forms + a partner Q&A.
**Success check:** learner tells ≥3 times and links them to routine. **Queue:** times recycle in U9 and later.
**Coach notes:** the digital form (seven fifteen) is easiest to start; add half/quarter gradually to avoid overload.

## S0-U4-L04 — How Often? Frequency Adverbs

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 4 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use frequency adverbs (always, usually, sometimes, never) with the correct word order.
- The learner can say how often they do things (*"I always have breakfast. I never drink coffee."*).
- The learner can ask *"How often do you…?"* (basic) and say days of the week.

**Target language:** always/usually/often/sometimes/never; days of the week; *"How often…?"*
**Grammar:** adverb position (before the main verb; after *to be*). **Accent Lab focus:** stress on the
frequency adverb; days ending clearly (*-day*).
**Arabic support:** ~45%. **Mindset focus:** consistency = your own "always" — be the person who always practices.

**Materials:** frequency scale visual (always→never); days flashcards; frames slide; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: times from L03 + a he/she routine sentence. | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I always get up early. I sometimes work on Friday."; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | The frequency scale; **word order** (I *usually* get up…; I am *always* late); days of the week. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Stress the adverb (*"I ALways…"*); clear *-day* endings. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Add frequency to routine: *"I always…, I never…"*; say what you do on which day. | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: *"How often do you…?"* → answer with an adverb; record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check adverb position (common error) + stress; queue. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: 3 sentences with always/usually/sometimes/never. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 2 adverbs (always/never). Stretch — add "on Mondays / at the weekend".
**Success check:** correct adverb placement in ≥3 sentences. **Queue:** frequency + days recycle in U5 (free time) and U9.
**Coach notes:** word order is the challenge (not the words) — drill the "before the main verb" position; expect "I get up always".

## S0-U4-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: Describe Your Typical Day (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 4 · ~30 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can describe their typical day in 6–8 connected sentences (activities + times + frequency).
- The learner can ask another person about their routine.
- The learner records a **"my typical day"** description (portfolio proof).

**Target language:** integrated Unit-4 language. **Grammar:** present simple (I + he/she) + time + frequency consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** 3rd-person -s + sentence rhythm; each learner's flagged weak sound.
**Arabic support:** ~45%. **Mindset focus:** you can now narrate a whole day — a big jump from week 1.

**Materials:** a model "my day" description; a checklist card; recording prompt; Unit-4 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Chain recall: routine verbs, he/she -s, times, frequency. | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a model "my typical day" (7 sentences with times + frequency). | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break the model into learned frames; build the "good routine description" checklist. | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills their weakest sound flagged in U4 (often the 3rd-person -s). | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse the full day with the card; peer practice (pairs). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the typical-day description (morning → evening, with times + frequency). | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level delayed feedback. | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the routine description; complete Unit-4 quiz; celebrate progress. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 6 sentences with the card. Stretch — describe your day AND a family member's day (I vs he/she).
**Success check (Unit-4 summary):** connected 6–8 sentence routine, intelligible, correct present simple (incl. he/she -s) + times (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at A1). **Queue:** routine description recycles into Unit 10 review.
**Coach notes:** ~5 weeks in — this is a strong proof clip (learners now produce a paragraph of connected speech). Log with consent in the Cohort Tracker.
