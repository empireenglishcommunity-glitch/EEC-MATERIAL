# Stage 0 · Unit 6 — Food & Shopping (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 1.3 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage0/00-stage0-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~40% (fading), instructions/explanations in Egyptian Arabic; learner outputs in English.
> **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can talk about food, ask prices, and order/request politely —
> and can perform a café/shop role-play from memory.
> **Grammar spine:** some/any · would like ("I'd like…") · countable vs uncountable (intro) · how much /
> how many · **Accent Lab:** clear numbers/prices + polite rising intonation for requests · **Mindset:**
> getting real-world speaking reps (order in English; use it outside class).

**Unit lessons:** L01 Food & drink vocab (I like/eat…) · L02 some/any + countable/uncountable (intro) ·
L03 "I'd like…" (ordering & requests) · L04 Prices & shopping ("How much is it?") · L05 Unit speaking task:
role-play a café/shop + review.

**Assets to produce:** food/drink flashcards, a menu + shop-price list, a café/shop role-play video
(American model), price/number audio, polite-intonation audio, a Google Form per lesson, community prompts.

## S0-U6-L01 — Food & Drink Vocabulary (I like / I eat…)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Unit 5.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can name common foods and drinks (bread, rice, chicken, fruit, water, tea, coffee, juice…).
- The learner can say what they eat/drink and like (*"I eat rice. I drink tea. I like fruit."*).
- The learner can say meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner).

**Target language:** food/drink vocab; meals; *"I eat/drink/like ___."* **Grammar:** present simple recycled
(eat/drink + like from U5). **Accent Lab focus:** clear vowels in food words (rice, juice, bread); linking.
**Arabic support:** ~40%. **Mindset focus:** name your food in English while you eat today (real-life reps).

**Materials:** food/drink flashcards; meals visual; clip; frames slide; Google Form (food vs drink).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: 2 likes with -ing from U5 ("I like cooking"). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "For breakfast I eat bread and drink tea. I like fruit."; text + Arabic gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Food/drink vocab + meals; recycle eat/drink/like; explain (Arabic) any new words. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clear vowels: rice /aɪ/, juice /uː/, bread /e/; link *"I-eat"*. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Say what you eat/drink at each meal (*"For lunch I eat ___."*). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: tell a partner your favourite food + drink; record one. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check vocab + clear vowels; queue weak items. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a voice note: what you eat/drink for breakfast. Food/drink quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 4 food words + "I like…". Stretch — a full meal description with 3 meals.
**Success check:** learner names ≥5 foods/drinks and forms eat/drink/like sentences. **Queue:** food words feed L02–L05.
**Coach notes:** keep it personal and appetising — food is a high-motivation topic; learners talk easily here.

## S0-U6-L02 — some / any + Countable vs Uncountable (Intro)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use **some** (positive) and **any** (questions/negatives) with food.
- The learner can distinguish countable (an apple, two eggs) vs uncountable (rice, water) at a basic level.
- The learner can say what they have/haven't got in the kitchen (*"I have some bread. I don't have any milk."*).

**Target language:** some/any; countable/uncountable food; have got recycled (U2). **Grammar:** some (+),
any (−/?); a/an with countables. **Accent Lab focus:** weak *some* /səm/; linking *"some-apples"*.
**Arabic support:** ~40%. **Mindset focus:** small consistent effort — like stocking a kitchen a bit each day.

**Materials:** countable/uncountable sort visual; fridge/kitchen image; frames slide; Google Form (some/any; count/uncount).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: food words from L01. | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I have some rice but I don't have any eggs. Is there any milk?"; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | some (+) / any (−,?); countable (eggs, apples) vs uncountable (rice, water, milk); explain (Arabic). | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Weak *some* /səm/; link *"some-bread"*. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Look at the fridge image: *"There is some ___. There isn't any ___."* | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: say 2 things you have and 1 you don't (some/any); record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check some/any use; queue weak items. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: 2 foods you have (some) + 1 you don't (any). | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — some in positives only. Stretch — full some/any across +/−/? with count vs uncount.
**Success check:** correct some/any in ≥3 statements. **Queue:** some/any feed L03–L04 (ordering, quantities).
**Coach notes:** keep countable/uncountable light at A1 — a few clear examples, not exhaustive rules. Communication first.

## S0-U6-L03 — "I'd Like…" — Ordering & Requests

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can order food/drink politely using **I'd like** (*"I'd like a coffee, please."*).
- The learner can make polite requests (*"Can I have…?"*) and respond (*"Here you are." "Thank you."*).
- The learner can use *would you like…?* to offer (basic).

**Target language:** I'd like / Can I have…? / Would you like…?; please/thank you (recycled U0).
**Grammar:** would like (polite want); Can I have…? **Accent Lab focus:** polite **rising intonation** on
requests; clear /aɪd/ in "I'd".
**Arabic support:** ~35%. **Mindset focus:** politeness + a smile makes real-world English easier — try it out.

**Materials:** café menu; role-play model clip; frames slide; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: some/any with food (L02). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Café clip: "Can I help you?" "I'd like a tea, please." "Here you are."; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | I'd like (polite) vs I want; Can I have…?; Would you like…?; explain (Arabic) the politeness. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Rising polite intonation; clear "I'd like"; soft "please". | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Order from the menu: *"I'd like ___, please."*; partner responds. | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: order 2 items politely from the menu; swap roles; record. | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check I'd like form + polite intonation. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: order your favourite café items (*"I'd like…"*). | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — one item with "I'd like…, please." Stretch — full mini-order with a follow-up (size, "anything else?").
**Success check:** learner places a polite order with correct form + intonation. **Queue:** requests recycle in L04–L05 and U8 (asking for help).
**Coach notes:** learners often default to "I want" (can sound rude) — reframe "I'd like" as the polite, natural choice. Model warmth.

## S0-U6-L04 — Prices & Shopping: "How Much Is It?"

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can ask and understand prices (*"How much is it?" / "It's ___."*).
- The learner can use **how much** (uncountable/price) and **how many** (countable) at a basic level.
- The learner can complete a simple shopping exchange.

**Target language:** how much / how many; prices/money; shop language (*"That's ___, please."*).
**Grammar:** how much (price/uncount) vs how many (count). **Accent Lab focus:** clear numbers/prices
(recycle 20–100 from U2); the -teen/-ty distinction in prices.
**Arabic support:** ~35%. **Mindset focus:** you're now handling real transactions — huge practical win.

**Materials:** shop price list; number/price audio; frames slide; Google Form (listen → write the price).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: order something politely (L03) + numbers 20–100 (U2). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Shop clip: "How much is this?" "It's fifteen." "How many do you want?"; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | how much (price/uncount) vs how many (count); saying prices; explain (Arabic). | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clear prices; thir**teen** vs **thir**ty in a price (13 vs 30 matters!). | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Ask prices from the list: *"How much is the ___?" "It's ___."* | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: a shopping exchange — ask 2 prices + buy; record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check how much/how many + clear prices; queue. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: ask the price of 2 things and answer. Price quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — how much + say a price. Stretch — full exchange with how much + how many + total.
**Success check:** learner asks/answers ≥2 prices intelligibly. **Queue:** numbers/prices consolidate; feed U10 review.
**Coach notes:** the -teen/-ty price confusion has real consequences — reinforce the stress cue from U2. Practice realistic prices.

## S0-U6-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: Role-Play a Café/Shop (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can perform a café or shop role-play (greet → order/ask price → pay → thank).
- The learner can play both customer and server.
- The learner records a **café/shop role-play** (interactive proof).

**Target language:** integrated Unit-6 language. **Grammar:** I'd like + some/any + how much consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** polite intonation + clear prices; each learner's flagged weak sound.
**Arabic support:** ~35%. **Mindset focus:** this is English you'll actually use — notice how usable it feels.

**Materials:** café + shop menus/price lists; a model role-play; a role-play checklist card; recording prompt; Unit-6 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Chain recall: food words, some/any, I'd like, how much. | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a model café role-play (greet → order → price → thanks). | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break the role-play into stages; build the "good service exchange" checklist. | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills their weakest sound flagged in U6 (often prices or intonation). | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse the role-play both roles with the card; peer practice (pairs). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the role-play (in pairs live, or solo playing both parts). | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level delayed feedback. | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the role-play; complete Unit-6 quiz; celebrate progress. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — customer role only, short order. Stretch — both roles + handle "anything else?" and total.
**Success check (Unit-6 summary):** completes a café/shop exchange with polite requests + prices, intelligible (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation + Interaction at A1). **Queue:** role-play recycles into Unit 10 review.
**Coach notes:** ~7 weeks in — a functional real-world role-play is a compelling proof clip and very motivating. Log with consent.
