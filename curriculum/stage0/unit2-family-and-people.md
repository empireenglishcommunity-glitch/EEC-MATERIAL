# Stage 0 · Unit 2 — Family & People (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 1.3 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage0/00-stage0-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~60% (fading), instructions/explanations in Egyptian Arabic; learner outputs in English.
> **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can talk about their family — say who people are, use my/your/his/her,
> say what family they have, and give ages — and can describe their family in a short recorded piece.
> **Grammar spine:** possessive adjectives (my/your/his/her) + possessive 's · this/that/these/those ·
> have/has · **Accent Lab:** /θ/–/ð/ (this / these / mother / father) + plural & possessive -s sounds ·
> **Mindset:** overcoming fear and embarrassment of speaking.

**Unit lessons:** L01 My family · L02 "This is my…" (his/her) · L03 have/has · L04 Ages & numbers 20–100 ·
L05 Unit speaking task: describe your family + review.

**Assets to produce:** a family-tree visual, family photos/flashcards, a short "meet my family" clip
(American model), /θ/–/ð/ minimal-pair audio, numbers 20–100 audio, a Google Form per lesson, community
voice-note prompts.

## S0-U2-L01 — My Family

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 2 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Unit 1.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can name core family members (mother, father, brother, sister, son, daughter, husband, wife).
- The learner can use **my** and **your** with family words (*"my mother", "your brother"*).
- The learner can say who is in their family (*"I have a big/small family."*).

**Target language:** family vocab; my/your; big/small family. **Grammar:** possessive adjectives my/your.
**Accent Lab focus:** /ð/ in *mother/father/brother*; /θ/ preview.
**Arabic support:** ~60%. **Mindset focus:** it's normal to feel shy — speaking anyway is the skill.

**Materials:** family-tree visual; family flashcards; short clip; frames slide; Google Form (label the family tree).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: quick self-intro from U1 (name, from, job). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: someone shows a family photo and names people; text + Arabic gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Present family words on the tree; *my / your*; explain (Arabic) possessive meaning. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Drill /ð/ in *mother, father, brother* (tongue tip lightly between teeth + voice). | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Point to the tree: *"This is my mother. This is my brother."* (my); ask *"Is this your sister?"* (your) | 5 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: name 3 people in your real family; record. | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check /ð/ and *my/your*; queue weak items. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a voice note naming 3 family members (*"my ___"*). Family-tree quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 3 core words (mother/father/brother). Stretch — add cousin/uncle/aunt.
**Success check:** learner names ≥3 family members with *my* intelligibly. **Queue:** family words + my/your recycle in L02–L05 and Unit 10.
**Coach notes:** /θ/ and /ð/ don't exist in the same way in Arabic — expect /s/ or /d/ substitutions (*"broder", "moder"*). Introduce gently now; it's the unit's Accent Lab thread.

## S0-U2-L02 — "This Is My…" — Introducing People (his/her)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 2 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can introduce another person (*"This is my friend, Ali."*).
- The learner can use **his** and **her** correctly (*"his sister", "her job"*).
- The learner can use **this/that** for near/far.

**Target language:** this/that; his/her; *"This is ___."* **Grammar:** demonstratives this/that; possessives his/her.
**Accent Lab focus:** /ð/ in *this/that*; /h/ in *his/her*.
**Arabic support:** ~60%. **Mindset focus:** speaking about others lowers the spotlight — a comfort trick.

**Materials:** photos of people; this/that visual (near/far); clip; frames slide; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: name 2 family members with *my* (L01). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "This is my friend… his name is… her job is…"; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | this (near) vs that (far); his (male) vs her (female); explain (Arabic) the his/her gender logic (based on the owner). | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | /ð/ in *this/that*; /h/ not dropped in *his/her*. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Introduce photo people: *"This is my brother. His name is ___."* / *"This is my sister. Her name is ___."* | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: introduce a real person (family/friend) using this + his/her; record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check his/her accuracy (common error) + /ð/. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: introduce one person — *"This is my ___. His/Her name is ___."* | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — one person, just *"This is my ___."* Stretch — add job: *"He's a ___."*
**Success check:** correct this + his/her in an introduction. **Queue:** his/her recycle in L03 and later units.
**Coach notes:** Arabic maps possessives differently — expect his/her mix-ups. Drill: gender follows the **owner** (her brother if the owner is female). Be patient; it recurs.

## S0-U2-L03 — have / has: "I Have Two Brothers"

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 2 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can say what family they have using **have/has** (*"I have two sisters.", "She has a son."*).
- The learner can use plural family nouns with the correct **-s sound**.
- The learner can ask *"Do you have…?"* (basic).

**Target language:** have/has; plurals of family nouns; numbers 1–10 with people.
**Grammar:** have (I/you/we/they) vs has (he/she/it); plural -s.
**Accent Lab focus:** plural -s sounds (/s/,/z/,/ɪz/): brother**s** /z/, sister**s** /z/, aunt**s** /s/.
**Arabic support:** ~60%. **Mindset focus:** mistakes are data — keep going, don't freeze.

**Materials:** number+family combos; clip; plural-sound audio; frames slide; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: introduce a person with this + his/her (L02). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I have two brothers and one sister. She has a big family."; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | have vs has (he/she → has); plural nouns; explain (Arabic) the he/she = has rule. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Plural -s endings: brothers/sisters (/z/) vs aunts (/s/); no extra vowel. | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Substitution: *"I have ___ brother(s)/sister(s)."*; then *"He has ___."* | 5 min |
| 6 | Free output | Live/pairs: say your real family numbers; ask *"Do you have brothers?"*; record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check has (3rd person) + plural -s; queue weak items. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: *"I have ___ brother(s) and ___ sister(s)."* | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — only *"I have…"*. Stretch — *"My brother has a son."* (has + family)
**Success check:** correct have/has + intelligible plural -s. **Queue:** has + plurals recycle into U3 (there is/are + plurals).
**Coach notes:** two classic errors here — "he have" and adding a vowel before -s ("brotheres"). Target both; they generalize across the whole course.

## S0-U2-L04 — Ages & Numbers 20–100

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 2 · ~30 min · recorded · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can say numbers 20–100 (twenty, thirty… hundred).
- The learner can ask and give ages (*"How old are you?" / "I'm ___." / "My father is ___."*).
- The learner can distinguish tricky pairs: **-teen vs -ty** (thirteen vs thirty).

**Target language:** 20–100; *"How old are you?" / "I'm ___ (years old)." / "He's/She's ___."*
**Grammar:** *to be* for age (I'm ___); numbers. **Accent Lab focus:** **-teen (stressed) vs -ty (unstressed)**
— thir**teen** vs **thir**ty; /θ/ in thirty/thirteen.
**Arabic support:** ~55%. **Mindset focus:** you can already say a lot — build on it.

**Materials:** numbers 20–100 audio; -teen/-ty minimal-pair audio; age-frames slide; Google Form (listen → write the age).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: numbers 0–20 (Unit 0) + your family count (L03). | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Listen to 20–100 in tens; then ages in context; text + gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | The tens (twenty…ninety, hundred); *"How old are you?"* → *"I'm ___."*; the -teen/-ty trap. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Drill thir**teen** vs **thir**ty (stress + /θ/); fif**teen** vs **fif**ty. | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Say your age; ages of 2 family members (*"My mother is ___."*). | 5 min |
| 6 | Free output | Record: *"I'm ___ years old. My ___ is ___."* | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check -teen/-ty distinction + /θ/. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a voice note with your age + one family member's age. Numbers quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — just your own age. Stretch — ages of 3 people + "How old is your…?" question.
**Success check:** intelligible 20–100 + age statement; audible -teen/-ty difference. **Queue:** numbers 20–100 return in U6 (prices).
**Coach notes:** the -teen/-ty confusion causes real-world misunderstandings (13 vs 30) — worth over-practicing. Stress is the key cue.

## S0-U2-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: Describe Your Family (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / A1 · Unit 2 · ~30 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can describe their family in 4–6 connected sentences (who, how many, names, ages).
- The learner can ask another person about their family.
- The learner records a **"my family"** piece (a growing portfolio of proof).

**Target language:** integrated Unit-2 language. **Grammar:** possessives + have/has + ages consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** /θ/–/ð/ + plural -s + -teen/-ty; each learner's flagged weak sound.
**Arabic support:** ~50%. **Mindset focus:** notice reduced fear — you're speaking more freely than week 1.

**Materials:** a model "my family" description; a checklist card; recording prompt; Unit-2 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Chain recall: family words, my/his/her, have/has, ages. | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a model "my family" description (5–6 sentences). | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break the model into learned frames; build the "good description" checklist. | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills their one weakest sound flagged in U2. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse the full description with the card; peer practice (pairs). | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the family description (who + how many + names + ages). | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level delayed feedback. | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the family description; complete Unit-2 quiz; celebrate progress. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 4 sentences with the card. Stretch — add each person's job/where they live.
**Success check (Unit-2 summary):** connected 4–6 sentence family description, intelligible, correct possessives + have/has (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at A1). **Queue:** family description recycles into Unit 10 review.
**Coach notes:** With Units 0–2 done, the founding cohort can start. This "my family" clip is another proof asset — log it in the Cohort Tracker with consent.
