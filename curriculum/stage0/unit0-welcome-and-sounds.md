# Stage 0 · Unit 0 — Welcome & Sounds (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 1.3 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage0/00-stage0-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~70% (heavy). Explanations and instructions are in Egyptian Arabic; the learner
> **outputs in English**. Arabic here is written in-line so the lesson is usable as-is.
> **Unit goal:** understand classroom instructions, start producing the English sounds Arabic speakers
> find hardest, and say basic survival phrases — building confidence from minute one.

**Unit lessons:** L01 Welcome & how this works · L02 The alphabet & letter sounds · L03 Hard sounds (1):
/p/–/b/, /v/–/f/ · L04 Survival phrases · L05 Numbers 0–20.

**Assets to produce for this unit:** short welcome video (founder on camera), alphabet audio (American
model), minimal-pair audio, survival-phrase audio, numbers audio, one Google Form quiz per lesson, a
community "voice-note" prompt per lesson.

## S0-U0-L01 — Welcome & How This Journey Works

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / Pre-A1 · Unit 0 · ~25 min · recorded + live kickoff · prerequisites: none.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can explain (to themselves) how EEC works and what to do each week.
- The learner can state their personal goal and their daily practice time.
- The learner can say and record one sentence: *"My name is ___ and I want to speak English."*

**Target language:** minimal — the sentence frame *"My name is ___. I want to speak English."*
**Accent Lab focus:** none yet (awareness only — "English has sounds we don't have; we'll train them").
**Arabic support:** ~90% (this is onboarding). **Mindset focus:** identity + consistency.

**Materials:** founder welcome video; a one-page "how it works" graphic; goal-setting prompt; community channel link.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up | Founder greets warmly (Arabic): *"أهلاً بيك. إنت هتتكلم إنجليزي، خطوة بخطوة."* Set the tone: this is a system, not tricks. | 3 min |
| 2 | Input | Welcome video: the promise (clear, confident English + American accent), the honesty (it takes consistency), and the founder's own story in brief. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice (how it works) | Explain the weekly rhythm (Arabic): recorded lessons + live speaking session + community + daily practice. Show the "how it works" graphic. | 5 min |
| 4 | Mindset core | Teach identity (Arabic): *"من النهاردة، إنت شخص بيتعلم الإنجليزي وبيتكلمه."* Explain: **consistency beats intensity** — 20 focused minutes daily > 3 hours once. | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Learner fills the frame aloud: *"My name is ___. I want to speak English."* (model first, slow then natural). | 3 min |
| 6 | Free output / commitment | Learner sets a goal + daily time and says it. Records the sentence + goal as a voice note. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self-check against model; keep this as a keepsake "Day-1" clip alongside the placement sample. | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post your intro voice note + your goal in the community channel; say hello to 2 classmates. | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — provide the full written sentence + audio. Stretch — add *"I want to speak English for ___ (work / travel / study)."*
**Success check:** learner posts the voice note and a stated goal. **Homework → queue:** the frame *"My name is…"* returns in U1-L02.
**Coach notes:** this lesson's job is emotional safety and commitment, not language. Celebrate every post. Reply personally to as many intros as possible — first impressions drive retention.

## S0-U0-L02 — The English Alphabet & Letter Sounds

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / Pre-A1 · Unit 0 · ~30 min · recorded · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can recognize and say the 26 letters of the English alphabet.
- The learner can spell their own name aloud in English.
- The learner can distinguish letter **name** vs letter **sound** (e.g., letter "C" = /siː/, sound often /k/).

**Target language:** the alphabet A–Z; "How do you spell…?" / "It's spelled ___."
**Accent Lab focus:** letters Arabic speakers confuse — **B/P**, **F/V**, **G/J**, and the vowels **A E I O U**.
**Arabic support:** ~70%. **Mindset focus:** small daily reps (5 letters a day).

**Materials:** alphabet audio (American model, slow + natural); alphabet chart; "spell your name" activity; Google Form (match letter → sound).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Recall L01: say *"My name is ___."* aloud. | 3 min |
| 2 | Input | Listen to the full alphabet sung/said clearly; follow the chart. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Explain (Arabic) letter **name** vs **sound**; flag the tricky ones: B≠P, F≠V, G≠J, and the 5 vowels. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab micro-drill | Repeat the confusable letters in pairs: B/P, F/V, G/J; record-and-compare. | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Spell simple words aloud (cat, book, name); then spell your **own name**. | 5 min |
| 6 | Free output | "How do you spell your name?" — learner answers aloud and records it. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Compare to model; note any letter to repeat tomorrow. | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Practice 5 letters/day; post a voice note spelling your name + your city. Quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — group letters in small sets (A–G, H–N…). Stretch — spell a longer word (your full name / country).
**Success check:** learner spells their name intelligibly. **Queue:** confusable letters feed L03 (B/P, F/V).
**Coach notes:** Arabic has no /p/ or /v/ — expect "B" for "P" and "F" for "V". Don't over-correct yet; L03 targets these directly.

## S0-U0-L03 — Hard Sounds (1): /p/ vs /b/ and /v/ vs /f/

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / Pre-A1 · Unit 0 · ~30 min · recorded + live reinforcement · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can **hear** the difference between /p/–/b/ and /v/–/f/ in minimal pairs.
- The learner can **produce** /p/ (with a puff of air) and /v/ (top teeth on lower lip).
- The learner can say target words clearly: *pen, book, van, four*.

**Target language:** words for the drills (pen/Ben, pig/big, pack/back; van/fan, vote/…, very, five, four).
**Accent Lab focus:** /p/ vs /b/ (voiceless vs voiced + aspiration) and /v/ vs /f/ (voiced vs voiceless).
**Arabic support:** ~70%. **Mindset focus:** "your mouth is learning a new skill — reps build it."

**Materials:** minimal-pair audio; a mirror tip (watch your lips); a paper-strip trick for /p/ (the puff moves the paper); record-and-compare tool.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Recall confusable letters from L02 (B/P, F/V). | 3 min |
| 2 | Input | Listen to minimal pairs: **pen/Ben, pig/big, pack/back** and **van/fan, very/ferry, vote/…**. Just listen first. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice (articulation) | Explain (Arabic) **how the mouth makes each sound**: /p/ = lips together + **puff of air** (بينفخ هوا)، /b/ = same lips **without** the puff + voice. /v/ = **سنانك الفوق على شفتك التحت** + voice; /f/ = same place, no voice. | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab drill | Paper-strip test for /p/ (paper moves) vs /b/ (no move). Mirror for /v/–/f/. Repeat pairs; then record-and-compare. | 8 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Say target words in slots: *"a **pen**", "a **book**", "a **van**", "number **four/five**".* | 4 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say 4 words of your choice containing these sounds; record. | 2 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Compare to model; pick your weakest sound → it returns in the review queue. | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | 3-min daily mirror drill; post a voice note saying: *pen, book, van, four, five*. | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — start with listening-only discrimination (point to the word you hear). Stretch — sentence: *"I have a pen and a book."*
**Success check:** intelligible /p/ and /v/ in the recorded words (rubric: Pronunciation). **Queue:** each learner's weakest of the two pairs is re-drilled in U1 warm-ups and Unit 10.
**Coach notes:** This is the first taste of the Accent Lab — make it feel like a "superpower unlock," not a correction of a flaw. Praise audible puffs and teeth-on-lip attempts.

## S0-U0-L04 — Survival Phrases: Staying in the Conversation

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / Pre-A1 · Unit 0 · ~25 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use phrases to keep a conversation alive when they don't understand.
- The learner can ask for repetition and for how to say something.
- The learner can respond politely (please / thank you / sorry).

**Target language (survival set):**
- *Hello / Hi* — أهلاً · *Please* — من فضلك · *Thank you* — شكراً · *Sorry / Excuse me* — آسف / لو سمحت
- *Yes / No* — أيوه / لأ · *I don't understand* — أنا مش فاهم
- *Can you repeat, please?* — ممكن تعيد تاني؟ · *Sorry?* (= repeat) — ممكن تاني؟
- *How do you say ___ in English?* — إزاي تقول ___ بالإنجليزي؟ · *What does ___ mean?* — يعني إيه ___؟

**Accent Lab focus:** clear /p/ in *please*; polite **rising intonation** on questions.
**Arabic support:** ~70%. **Mindset focus:** "you don't need to be perfect to communicate — stay in the game."

**Materials:** survival-phrase audio + printable card; role-play prompts; record-and-compare.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Quick /p/–/v/ reps from L03 (say: pen, van). | 3 min |
| 2 | Input | Listen to a short dialogue where someone uses survival phrases to cope; text + Arabic gloss. | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Present the survival set with Arabic meanings; explain **when** to use each. | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Drill *"please"* (clear /p/) and rising intonation on *"Can you repeat, please?"* | 3 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Substitution: react to prompts — teacher says a hard word → learner replies *"Sorry? / Can you repeat, please?"* | 5 min |
| 6 | Free output | Mini role-play (live: in pairs): one speaks fast, the other survives using the phrases; record. | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self-check; note the one phrase you'll use most this week. | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Use one survival phrase in a real situation this week; post which one you used + how it felt. | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give the printable card to read from. Stretch — add *"What does ___ mean?"* in a real chat.
**Success check:** learner produces ≥3 survival phrases intelligibly and appropriately. **Queue:** survival phrases recycle in every future warm-up ("say two survival phrases").
**Coach notes:** This lesson massively lowers speaking anxiety — it gives learners permission to not understand everything. Reinforce heavily in the live session.

## S0-U0-L05 — Numbers 0–20 & Saying Them Clearly

**Metadata:** Stage 0 / Pre-A1 · Unit 0 · ~30 min · recorded · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can say and recognize numbers 0–20.
- The learner can give a phone number and their age.
- The learner can distinguish tricky pairs (e.g., *thir**teen*** vs *thirty* comes in U2 — here: clear -teen).

**Target language:** numbers 0–20; *"My number is ___." "I'm ___ years old."*
**Accent Lab focus:** /θ/ in *three/thirteen* (preview of U2), clear final sounds; stress on numbers.
**Arabic support:** ~70%. **Mindset focus:** celebrate the first-week win; preview Unit 1.

**Materials:** numbers audio; number chart; "say your phone number" activity; Google Form (listen → write the number).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Say two survival phrases from L04; quick /p/–/v/ word. | 3 min |
| 2 | Input | Listen to 0–20 said clearly; follow the chart; repeat in chunks (0–10, 11–20). | 7 min |
| 3 | Notice | Highlight *three/thirteen* (/θ/ preview) and clear endings; explain (Arabic) the /θ/ tongue position lightly. | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Drill *three, thirteen* (tongue tip between teeth) + count 0–20 with a steady beat (rhythm). | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Say your **age** and a **phone number** (digit by digit). | 5 min |
| 6 | Free output | Record: *"I'm ___ years old. My number is ___."* | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Compare to model; note weak numbers. | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Count 0–20 daily; post a voice note with your age + a (fake if you prefer) phone number. Quiz form. | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — practice 0–10 first, then 11–20. Stretch — count 0–20 backwards.
**Success check:** learner says 0–20 and their age intelligibly. **Queue:** numbers return in U2 (ages 20–100) and U6 (prices).
**Coach notes:** End-of-unit moment — acknowledge that in one week they've produced English on camera, met classmates, and started fixing sounds. Preview Unit 1: "Next week you'll fully introduce yourself in English."
