# Stage 2 · Unit 5 — Hypotheticals: Second Conditional (+ *wish*) (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 5.2 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage2/00-stage2-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~15% (light). **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can talk about unreal/imaginary
> situations and their results (2nd conditional), give hypothetical advice (*If I were you…*), and express
> wishes (*I wish…*) — recorded.
> **Grammar spine:** 2nd conditional (*if* + past, *would*) + 1st↔2nd contrast + *I wish* (intro) · **Accent Lab:**
> *would/'d* reduction, sentence stress on content words · **Mindset:** imagine your English future.

**Unit lessons:** L01 2nd conditional form · L02 real vs unreal (1st vs 2nd) · L03 *If I were you…* ·
L04 *I wish…* (intro) · L05 speaking task ("if I could… / dreams") + review.

**Assets to produce:** an "imaginary situations" clip (American model), a 1st-vs-2nd decision card,
an *If I were you* advice set, minimal audio for *'d*, a Google Form per lesson.

## S2-U5-L01 — Second Conditional: *if* + past, *would*

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 5 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: U4 (1st conditional).

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can form the 2nd conditional (*If* + past simple, + *would* + base verb).
- The learner can talk about unreal/imaginary present or future situations.
- The learner can produce 3–4 hypothetical sentences.

**Target language:** 2nd conditional; imaginary contexts (money, travel, superpowers, "if I had time…").
**Grammar:** *If* + past simple, *would* + base verb (unreal/imaginary). **Accent Lab focus:** *would* → *'d*
reduction ("I'd go"); stress content words. **Arabic support:** ~15%. **Mindset focus:** imagine your English future — picture the fluent you.

**Materials:** the 2nd-conditional structure slide; an "imaginary" clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: 1st conditional (real, U4) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "If I had a million pounds, I'd travel the world." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Form *If* + past, *would* + base; meaning = imaginary/unreal (not likely); brief gloss | 8 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | *would → 'd* ("I'd, she'd"); stress the content words | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Build 2nd conditionals from imaginary prompts; flip clause order | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say 3 "If I…, I'd…" imaginary sentences; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check past-in-if + would-in-result | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 sentences: "If I ___, I'd ___." | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — structure slide + prompt bank. Stretch — add a reason ("…because I've always wanted to").
**Success check:** 3 correct 2nd conditionals. **Queue:** 2nd conditional recycles every warm-up this unit.
**Coach notes:** the "past tense = unreal, not real past" idea is the conceptual hurdle — gloss once in Arabic, then keep it in imaginary contexts so the meaning stays clear.

## S2-U5-L02 — Real vs Unreal: 1st vs 2nd Conditional

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 5 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01; U4.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can choose the 1st conditional (real/likely) vs 2nd conditional (unreal/imaginary).
- The learner can talk about the same topic in both real and imaginary ways.
- The learner can self-correct the choice.

**Target language:** both conditionals, contrasted. **Grammar:** 1st (real: present + will) vs 2nd (unreal: past + would).
**Accent Lab focus:** switch cleanly between *'ll* and *'d*. **Arabic support:** ~15% (rule glossed once).
**Mindset focus:** you can now separate "what will happen" from "what would happen" — a real reasoning upgrade.

**Materials:** a 1st-vs-2nd decision card; a mixed-sentence sort; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: one 1st + one 2nd conditional | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "If it rains tomorrow, I'll stay in." vs "If I lived on the beach, I'd swim every day." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Real/likely → 1st; unreal/imaginary → 2nd; the decision rule; brief gloss | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clean *'ll* vs *'d* switching | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Sort sentences (real/unreal); say a topic both ways | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Talk about a goal realistically (1st) then imaginatively (2nd); record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check the choice; accept reasonable interpretations | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post one 1st + one 2nd conditional on the same topic | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — decision card + sorted examples. Stretch — justify each choice in English.
**Success check:** correct real/unreal choice in most items. **Queue:** the contrast recycles into U12.
**Coach notes:** the mixing error ("If I will…", "If I would…") is common — anchor to meaning (likely vs imaginary), not just form.

## S2-U5-L03 — *If I Were You…* (Hypothetical Advice)

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 5 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can give hypothetical advice with *If I were you, I'd…*.
- The learner can use *were* for all persons in 2nd conditionals (I/he/she were).
- The learner can advise a friend hypothetically.

**Target language:** *If I were you, I'd…*; *were* (subjunctive). **Grammar:** *were* for all subjects in unreal
conditionals; advice function. **Accent Lab focus:** *'d* in advice; empathetic intonation. **Arabic support:** ~15%.
**Mindset focus:** "if I were you, I'd practice speaking daily" — advise your past self.

**Materials:** problem-advice cards; an advice clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: 2nd conditional (L01–L02) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I don't know what to do." "If I were you, I'd talk to your boss." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *If I were you, I'd…*; *were* for all persons; advice register; brief gloss | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | *'d* reduction; warm advice intonation | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Problem cards → hypothetical advice; swap | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | A partner shares a problem; give hypothetical advice; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check *were* + would + advice tone | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "If I were you, I'd ___." for a common problem | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — problem cards + frame. Stretch — give 2 alternatives + a reason.
**Success check:** correct *If I were you, I'd…* advice. **Queue:** hypothetical advice recycles into U10 (discussion) & U12.
**Coach notes:** "was" for "were" ("If I was you") is extremely common and accepted informally — teach *were* as the standard; don't over-penalize *was* in speech.

## S2-U5-L04 — *I Wish…* (Intro)

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 5 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can express wishes about the present with *I wish + past simple*.
- The learner can talk about things they'd like to be different.
- The learner can connect wishes to reasons.

**Target language:** *I wish + past* (present wishes: "I wish I had…, I wish I could…"). **Grammar:** *wish* + past
(unreal present). **Accent Lab focus:** connected speech in "I wish I…"; stress on the wished-for thing. **Arabic support:** ~15%.
**Mindset focus:** turn a wish into a plan ("I wish I spoke fluently" → the 1st-conditional path to get there).

**Materials:** wish prompt cards; a short clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: 2nd conditional + If I were you (L01–L03) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I wish I had more time. I wish I could speak three languages." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *I wish + past* = want the present to be different; link to 2nd conditional logic; brief gloss | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | "I wish I…" linking; stress the content | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Turn present complaints into wishes; pair share | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say 3 real wishes (+ why); record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check *wish + past* | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 wishes + one plan to make one come true | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — wish prompts. Stretch — pair each wish with a 1st-conditional plan.
**Success check:** correct *I wish + past* for present wishes. **Queue:** wishes recycle into U12.
**Coach notes:** keep it to present wishes (wish + past) at B1; past wishes (wish + past perfect) are B2 — flag as later.

## S2-U5-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "If I Could… / My Dreams" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 5 · ~40 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can talk about hopes/dreams (8–10 sentences): 2nd conditionals + *If I were you* + *I wish*.
- The learner can respond to a partner's dreams and give hypothetical advice.
- The learner records their **Unit-5 dreams talk**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-5 language. **Grammar:** 2nd conditional + wish consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** overall clarity; *'d* reduction; content-word stress; each learner's flagged sound.
**Arabic support:** ~10% (mostly English). **Mindset focus:** picture the fluent future you — then reverse-engineer the plan.

**Materials:** a model dreams talk; a checklist card (2+ 2nd conditionals / an If-I-were-you / a wish / a reason);
recording prompt; Unit-5 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: 2nd conditional, 1st-vs-2nd, If I were you, I wish | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean dreams talk (8–10 sentences) | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a flagged sound (*'d* / linking) | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse with the checklist; peer practice + hypothetical advice each | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the full dreams talk + answer one live question | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the talk; complete Unit-5 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 8 sentences with the card. Stretch — sustain a 2-way "dreams + advice" exchange.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 5):** connected 8–10 sentence hypothetical talk (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at B1). **Queue:** hypothetical language recycles into U10/U12.
**Coach notes:** closes the conditionals block — learners can now reason about real AND unreal situations, a major B1 range gain. Note it to sustain momentum on the plateau.
