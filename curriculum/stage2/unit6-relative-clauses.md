# Stage 2 · Unit 6 — Relative Clauses (Defining) (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 5.2 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage2/00-stage2-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~12% (light). **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can add information about people,
> things, and places in one sentence using defining relative clauses (*who/which/that/where/whose*), and
> knows when the relative pronoun can be dropped — recorded.

## S2-U6-L01 — *who / which / that* (People & Things)

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 6 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: A2/B1 descriptions.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use *who* (people), *which* (things), *that* (both) to add information.
- The learner can combine two simple sentences into one with a relative clause.
- The learner can define/identify people and things.

**Target language:** *who/which/that*; people & object nouns. **Grammar:** defining relative clauses (subject relatives).
**Accent Lab focus:** say the clause as one intonation group; light pause before/after. **Arabic support:** ~12%.
**Mindset focus:** upgrade your input — pick sources one notch above your comfort level.

**Materials:** relative-pronoun card; a "combine sentences" set; a short clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: adjectives/descriptions (A2) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "A doctor is a person who helps sick people. A phone is a thing that we use to call." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *who* (people), *which* (things), *that* (both); combining two sentences; brief gloss | 8 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Intonation grouping; smooth the clause (don't chop it) | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Combine sentence pairs; define jobs/objects with who/which/that | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Define 3 people/things using relative clauses; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check pronoun choice + combining | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 definitions ("A ___ is a person/thing who/that ___.") | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — pronoun card + sentence pairs. Stretch — define abstract things (a habit, a skill).
**Success check:** correct who/which/that + a combined sentence. **Queue:** relative clauses recycle every warm-up this unit.
**Coach notes:** the common error is doubling the subject ("A doctor is a person who **he** helps…"). Point out the relative pronoun replaces the subject — no extra pronoun.

## S2-U6-L02 — *where / whose*

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use *where* (places) and *whose* (possession) in relative clauses.
- The learner can define places and describe relationships/possession.
- The learner can combine sentences using *where/whose*.

**Target language:** *where* (place), *whose* (possession); place nouns + possession contexts. **Grammar:** relative
clauses with *where/whose*. **Accent Lab focus:** intonation grouping; *whose* /huːz/ clarity. **Arabic support:** ~12%.
**Mindset focus:** describe your world in English (your city, your people) — build ownership.

**Materials:** where/whose examples; place & people cards; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: who/which/that (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "A café is a place where you drink coffee. That's the man whose car broke down." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *where* = in/at a place; *whose* = possession; brief gloss | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clause grouping; clear *whose* | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Define places with *where*; describe possession with *whose* | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Define 2 places (where) + 1 possession (whose); record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check where/whose choice | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "___ is a place where ___." + one *whose* sentence | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — examples card. Stretch — combine *where* + another relative in one description.
**Success check:** correct *where/whose* usage. **Queue:** where/whose recycle into L04.
**Coach notes:** *where* vs *which* for places trips learners ("the place which I live" → "where I live"). Contrast directly with clear examples.

## S2-U6-L03 — Leaving Out the Relative Pronoun

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can omit the relative pronoun when it's the object of the clause.
- The learner can recognize when omission is possible (object) vs not (subject).
- The learner can speak more naturally with reduced relative clauses.

**Target language:** object relative clauses (the film (that) I saw, the person (who) I met). **Grammar:** omitting
object relative pronouns. **Accent Lab focus:** smooth delivery of the reduced clause; natural rhythm. **Arabic support:** ~12%.
**Mindset focus:** natural > perfect — dropping the pronoun makes you sound more fluent.

**Materials:** subject-vs-object examples; a natural-speech clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: who/which/that/where/whose | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "The film I saw was great." (that omitted) vs "The man who called is here." (kept) | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Omit when it's the OBJECT of the clause; keep when it's the SUBJECT; brief gloss | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Smooth the reduced clause ("the film-I-saw") | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Decide omit/keep; say sentences both ways | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Describe 3 things using object clauses (drop the pronoun); record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check omission is object-only | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 2 sentences with the relative pronoun dropped | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — subject-vs-object examples. Stretch — a short paragraph using natural reduced clauses.
**Success check:** correct omission (object only) + natural delivery. **Queue:** reduced clauses recycle into U11.
**Coach notes:** don't over-drill the rule — the goal is natural-sounding speech. Model many object-clause examples and let learners imitate.

## S2-U6-L04 — Defining People, Places & Things in Detail

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 6 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can give detailed definitions/descriptions using relative clauses.
- The learner can play a "describe without naming" guessing game.
- The learner can integrate relatives into longer descriptions.

**Target language:** integrated relative clauses in description. **Grammar:** all defining relatives combined.
**Accent Lab focus:** clause grouping across a longer description; connected speech. **Arabic support:** ~10%.
**Mindset focus:** describing fluently = you're thinking in English, not translating.

**Materials:** a guessing-game card set; a model description clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: all relative pronouns + omission | 5 min |
| 2 | Input | Model describes a person/place/thing in detail (multiple relatives) | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Layering relatives into a rich description; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Group the clauses; keep the description flowing | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | "Describe without naming" — partner guesses; use relatives | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Describe someone/somewhere important to you (4–5 sentences w/ relatives); record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check relative accuracy + flow | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a "guess who/what" description using ≥2 relative clauses | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — game cards + frames. Stretch — describe an abstract concept using relatives.
**Success check:** a detailed description using ≥2 relative clauses accurately. **Queue:** descriptive relatives recycle into U11/U12.
**Coach notes:** the guessing game makes relatives communicative and fun — great fluency + form practice together.

## S2-U6-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "Describe a Person/Place Who/That…" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 6 · ~40 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can describe an important person and place (8–10 sentences) using several relative clauses.
- The learner can answer follow-up questions and ask one.
- The learner records their **Unit-6 description**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-6 language. **Grammar:** defining relative clauses consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** overall clarity; clause grouping; each learner's flagged sound.
**Arabic support:** ~10% (mostly English). **Mindset focus:** richer descriptions = visibly more advanced English.

**Materials:** a model description; a checklist card (3+ relative clauses / who-which-that / a where or whose / a reduced clause / detail);
recording prompt; Unit-6 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: who/which/that/where/whose + omission | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean detailed description (8–10 sentences) | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a flagged sound (clause grouping / whose) | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse with the checklist; peer practice + one follow-up each | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the full description + answer one live question | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the description; complete Unit-6 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 8 sentences with the card. Stretch — include a reduced clause + *whose* + a reflection.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 6):** connected 8–10 sentence description using ≥3 relative clauses (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at B1). **Queue:** descriptive relatives recycle into U11/U12.
**Coach notes:** relative clauses noticeably raise sentence complexity — a clear marker of B1 range; celebrate the "longer, richer sentences" win.
