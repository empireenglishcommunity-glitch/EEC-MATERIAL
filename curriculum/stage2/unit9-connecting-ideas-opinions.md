## S2-U9-L01 — *because / so / so that* (Reason & Result)

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 9 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: A2 *because*.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can link a reason (*because*) and a result (*so*) in one sentence.
- The learner can express purpose with *so that*.
- The learner can explain why they do/think things.

**Target language:** *because* (reason), *so* (result), *so that* (purpose). **Grammar:** clause linking with
reason/result/purpose connectors. **Accent Lab focus:** connected speech across clauses; slight pause before the connector.
**Arabic support:** ~10%. **Mindset focus:** from facts to opinions — start saying *why* you think things.

**Materials:** connector card (reason/result); a clip; "link the ideas" pairs; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: A2 *because* + a reason | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I study at night because I work all day, so I'm often tired." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *because* + reason; *so* + result; *so that* + purpose; brief note | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clause rhythm; don't over-pause; link smoothly | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Link idea pairs with because/so/so that; explain choices | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Explain 3 real reasons/results in your life; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check connector choice + clause order | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: a because + a so + a so-that sentence | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — connector card + pairs. Stretch — chain reason→result→purpose in one turn.
**Success check:** correct reason/result/purpose linking. **Queue:** connectors recycle every warm-up this unit.
**Coach notes:** *so* (result) vs *so that* (purpose) is subtle — keep examples clear; don't over-explain. Reward smoother, longer sentences.

## S2-U9-L02 — *although / but / however* (Contrast)

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 9 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can express contrast with *but*, *although*, and *however*.
- The learner can concede a point then contrast it.
- The learner can use *however* to start a contrasting sentence.

**Target language:** *but, although, however (,)*. **Grammar:** contrast connectors + position/punctuation.
**Accent Lab focus:** contrast stress + a pause after *however*; intonation of concession. **Arabic support:** ~10%.
**Mindset focus:** you can hold two sides now — nuance is a fluency marker.

**Materials:** contrast-connector card; a clip; contrast pairs; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: because/so (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "Although it's expensive, it's worth it. It's hard; however, I enjoy it." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *but* (mid), *although* (start of clause), *however* (new sentence + comma); brief note | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Stress/pause on *however*; concession intonation | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Combine contrasting ideas three ways; position practice | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Express 2 contrasts about your life/opinions; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check connector position + punctuation | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: an *although* + a *however* sentence | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — contrast card. Stretch — concede + contrast + give a reason.
**Success check:** correct contrast connectors in the right positions. **Queue:** contrast connectors recycle into U10 (disagreeing).
**Coach notes:** *however* punctuation/position (new sentence, comma) is the tricky bit — model it clearly; it's a big "sounds educated" upgrade.

## S2-U9-L03 — Adding & Sequencing Ideas

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 9 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can add ideas (*also, in addition, as well*) and sequence points (*first(ly), then, finally*).
- The learner can organize a short explanation with several points.
- The learner can structure a mini-argument.

**Target language:** *also, in addition, as well (as), first(ly)/secondly/finally*. **Grammar:** addition +
sequencing connectors. **Accent Lab focus:** list intonation (rise on items, fall on the last). **Arabic support:** ~10%.
**Mindset focus:** organized speech = confident speech; structure beats speed.

**Materials:** addition/sequence card; a structured-explanation clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: because/so + although/however | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "First, it's cheap. Also, it's fast. Finally, it's easy." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Adding (*also/in addition*) + sequencing (*first/then/finally*); brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | List intonation (rise-rise-fall) | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Give 3 reasons for a choice, organized with connectors | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Explain a choice with 3 organized points; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check organization + connectors | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a 3-point explanation using add/sequence connectors | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — connector card + a frame. Stretch — combine add/sequence + contrast in one explanation.
**Success check:** an organized 3-point explanation. **Queue:** organizing connectors recycle into U10/U11.
**Coach notes:** structure lowers cognitive load and boosts confidence — praise clear organization; it directly supports the summative discussion.

## S2-U9-L04 — Expressing + Justifying an Opinion

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 9 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can state an opinion with a clear frame and justify it with reasons.
- The learner can use opinion + connector + example.
- The learner can give a short structured opinion on a familiar topic.

**Target language:** opinion frames (*In my opinion…, I think…, I believe…, personally…*) + *because* + *for example*.
**Grammar:** opinion + justification (integrates U9 connectors). **Accent Lab focus:** stress the opinion + reason;
smooth connectors. **Arabic support:** ~10%. **Mindset focus:** your opinion matters — say it in English, imperfectly but clearly.

**Materials:** opinion-frame card; familiar-topic prompts; a model opinion clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: connectors (reason/result/contrast/add) | 5 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "In my opinion, remote work is better because you save time. For example, no commute." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Opinion → reason (*because*) → example (*for example*); the structure; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Stress the opinion + the reason; smooth linking | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Give structured opinions on familiar prompts; peer adds a reason | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Give a structured opinion on one topic (opinion + 2 reasons + example); record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check structure (opinion + justification) | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a structured opinion on a familiar topic | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — opinion-frame card + prompt. Stretch — opinion + reason + example + acknowledge a counter-view (*although*).
**Success check:** a clear opinion with ≥1 justification + example. **Queue:** opinion structure feeds U10 discussion + the summative.
**Coach notes:** this is a keystone B1 skill (the summative is opinion+discussion). Drill opinion→reason→example until it's automatic.

## S2-U9-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "Give Your Opinion On…" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 2 / B1 · Unit 9 · ~40 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can give a connected opinion (8–10 sentences): opinion + reasons (connectors) + example + a contrast.
- The learner can respond to a partner's opinion.
- The learner records their **Unit-9 opinion talk**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-9 language. **Grammar:** connectors + opinion structure consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** overall clarity; list + contrast intonation; each learner's flagged sound.
**Arabic support:** ~5% (near-immersion). **Mindset focus:** you can argue a point in English now — a defining B1 milestone.

**Materials:** a model opinion talk; a checklist card (opinion frame / 2+ reasons w/ connectors / an example / a contrast / a conclusion);
recording prompt; Unit-9 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: because/so/so that, although/however, add/sequence, opinion frames | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean opinion talk (8–10 sentences) | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a flagged sound (however/contrast stress) | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse with the checklist; peer responds with their view | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the full opinion talk + answer one live question | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the opinion talk; complete Unit-9 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 8 sentences with the card. Stretch — acknowledge a counter-view then rebut it.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 9):** connected 8–10 sentence opinion with connectors + justification (rubric: Fluency + Coherence + Accuracy at B1). **Queue:** opinion talk feeds U10/U12.
**Coach notes:** this directly rehearses the summative — a strong proof clip and a confidence marker (from routine at A2 to arguing a point at B1).
