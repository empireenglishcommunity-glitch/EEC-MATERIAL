# Stage 3 · Unit 11 — Abstract Topics & Debate (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 6.1 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage3/00-stage3-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** none (immersion). **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can discuss abstract and
> professional topics, build and defend a structured argument, handle counter-arguments and nuance, and
> move naturally from small talk to deep talk — recorded.
> **Grammar spine:** structured argument + defending/conceding + handling nuance (integrating Stage-3 grammar) ·
> **Accent Lab:** contrastive stress, persuasive intonation · **Mindset:** thinking + arguing in English.

**Unit lessons:** L01 handling abstract topics (frameworks + vocab) · L02 building a structured argument ·
L03 defending & conceding under challenge · L04 small talk → deep talk · L05 speaking task ("debate a professional/abstract topic") + review.

**Assets to produce:** an abstract-topic vocab/framework set, an argument-structure card (claim→evidence→example→conclusion),
a rebuttal/concession phrase card, small-talk-to-deep-talk transition cards, a Google Form per lesson.

## S3-U11-L01 — Handling Abstract Topics (Frameworks + Vocab)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 11 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: B1/B2 opinions.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can approach an abstract/professional topic with a thinking framework (causes/effects, pros/cons, short/long term).
- The learner can deploy topic-relevant vocabulary and collocations.
- The learner can speak coherently about a topic they haven't scripted.

**Target language:** abstract-topic vocab (society, technology, environment, education, work culture) + framework language
(on one hand/on the other, in the short/long term, the root cause). **Grammar:** integrated (opinions/connectors/deduction).
**Accent Lab focus:** thought-group phrasing on longer turns. **Mindset:** frameworks reduce panic — think in structures.

**Materials:** an abstract-topic vocab/framework set; a model topic-talk clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: B1 opinion structure + connectors | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: someone discusses an abstract topic using a pros/cons + short/long-term frame | 7 min |
| 3 | Notice | Thinking frameworks + topic vocab; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Phrase longer turns into thought groups | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Apply a framework to 2 abstract prompts | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Speak ~1.5 min on an abstract topic using a framework; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check coherence + topic vocab | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a framework-based take on a topic | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — framework + vocab set. Stretch — an unprepared abstract prompt.
**Success check:** a coherent framework-based topic talk. **Queue:** frameworks recycle this unit.
**Coach notes:** frameworks are the antidote to freezing on abstract topics — teach 2–3 reusable ones (pros/cons, causes/effects, short/long term) they can apply to anything.

## S3-U11-L02 — Building a Structured Argument

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 11 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can build an argument: claim → evidence/reason → example → conclusion.
- The learner can make the structure audible with signposting.
- The learner can argue a position persuasively.

**Target language:** argument signposting (My main point is…, The evidence for this is…, For instance…, That's why…).
**Grammar:** connectors + opinion + deduction integrated. **Accent Lab focus:** contrastive + emphatic stress for persuasion.
**Mindset:** a clear argument = a confident, credible speaker.

**Materials:** an argument-structure card; debatable prompts; a model clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: framework talk (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: a tight argument (claim→evidence→example→conclusion) | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | The argument structure + signposting; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Emphasis/contrastive stress for persuasion | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Build structured arguments on prompts; peer checks the structure | 9 min |
| 6 | Free output | Argue a position (~1.5 min) with full structure; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check argument structure + persuasiveness | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a structured argument on a debatable topic | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — structure card. Stretch — argue a position you disagree with.
**Success check:** a complete claim→evidence→example→conclusion argument. **Queue:** argument structure feeds L05/U12.
**Coach notes:** the audible structure (signposting) is what makes B2 arguments persuasive — reward clarity of structure + emphatic delivery.

## S3-U11-L03 — Defending & Conceding Under Challenge

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 11 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can defend their position against counter-arguments.
- The learner can concede a fair point without losing their overall stance (*That's a fair point, but…*).
- The learner can handle challenge calmly and diplomatically.

**Target language:** rebuttal/concession language (I take your point, but…; That may be true, however…; I'd still argue…).
**Grammar:** contrast connectors (B1) + diplomacy (U9) + conditionals. **Accent Lab focus:** calm, firm intonation under challenge.
**Mindset:** disagreement is normal — hold your ground gracefully.

**Materials:** a rebuttal/concession card; challenge prompts; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: structured argument (L02) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: a speaker concedes a point then defends the main stance | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Concede-then-defend; rebuttal language; staying calm; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Calm, firm intonation; not defensive | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Partner challenges; learner concedes + defends | 9 min |
| 6 | Free output | Defend a position against 2 challenges; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check concede+defend + tone | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a concede-then-defend response to a counter-argument | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — rebuttal card. Stretch — handle rapid-fire challenges.
**Success check:** graceful concede-then-defend under challenge. **Queue:** feeds L05/U12.
**Coach notes:** the emotional regulation (staying calm/diplomatic under pressure) is as important as the language — model composure; it's central to the flagship conversation.

## S3-U11-L04 — Small Talk → Deep Talk (Transitions)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 11 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can open with natural small talk and transition smoothly into deeper discussion.
- The learner can steer a conversation toward substance without awkwardness.
- The learner can sustain both light and deep registers in one conversation.

**Target language:** small-talk openers + transition moves (Speaking of which…, That reminds me…, On a more serious note…).
**Grammar:** integrated. **Accent Lab focus:** register/prosody shift from light to deep. **Mindset:** real conversations flow between levels — you can too.

**Materials:** small-talk-to-deep-talk transition cards; a model clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: small-talk phrases (B1) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: a chat that moves from weather/weekend → a real topic | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Transition moves; steering smoothly; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Prosody shift light → deep | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Practice opening small + transitioning to depth | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Hold a conversation that moves small→deep; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check transition smoothness | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a small→deep transition example | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — transition cards. Stretch — reverse (deep → lighten to close gracefully).
**Success check:** a smooth small-talk → deep-talk transition. **Queue:** conversation-flow feeds U12 (the 20–30 min ranges across both).
**Coach notes:** the flagship conversation covers "everyday & professional" — this light↔deep flexibility is exactly what it tests. Practice natural transitions.

## S3-U11-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "Debate a Professional/Abstract Topic" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 11 · ~45 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can debate a topic (~4–5 min): structured argument + defend/concede + nuance, in English.
- The learner can respond to counter-arguments and maintain a position diplomatically.
- The learner records their **Unit-11 debate**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-11 language. **Grammar:** argument/debate functions consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** persuasive + contrastive stress; each learner's residual sound.
**Mindset:** thinking + arguing in English — the intellectual core of B2.

**Materials:** a model debate; debatable-topic cards; a checklist card (framework / structured argument / defend + concede / a nuance/hedge / stay in English);
recording prompt; Unit-11 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: frameworks, argument structure, rebuttal/concession | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean short debate | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills persuasive stress + a residual sound | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Debate in pairs (assigned positions); swap sides | 10 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record a ~4–5 min debate | 8 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the debate; complete Unit-11 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — checklist + prep + assigned position. Stretch — argue the side you disagree with, less prep.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 11):** a ~4–5 min debate with structured argument + defend/concede + nuance (rubric at B2, esp. Coherence + Interaction). **Queue:** directly feeds the U12 flagship benchmark.
**Coach notes:** this is the intellectual peak before the flagship — reward reasoning, structure, and diplomatic defense over perfect grammar. Strong proof-clip material.
