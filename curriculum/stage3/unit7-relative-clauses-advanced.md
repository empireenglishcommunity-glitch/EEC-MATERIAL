## S3-U7-L01 — Non-Defining Relative Clauses

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 7 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: B1 defining relatives.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can add extra, non-essential information with non-defining clauses (set off by commas/pauses).
- The learner can use *who/which* (not *that*) in non-defining clauses.
- The learner can enrich a description without changing its core meaning.

**Target language:** non-defining relative clauses (*My boss, who is very kind, …*). **Grammar:** non-defining
clauses; commas; no *that*; no omission. **Accent Lab focus:** the pause + level intonation around the clause.
**Mindset:** add colour to your speech — extra detail signals fluency.

**Materials:** a non-defining examples card; an intonation model clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: B1 defining relatives | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "My brother, who lives in Cairo, is a doctor." | 7 min |
| 3 | Notice | Non-defining = extra info, commas/pauses, *who/which* (not *that*), can't omit; brief explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Pause + intonation dip around the clause | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Add non-defining info to sentences about people/things | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | 3 sentences enriched with non-defining clauses; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check commas/pauses + who/which | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 non-defining sentences about people you know | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — examples card. Stretch — stack two pieces of extra info.
**Success check:** correct non-defining clauses with pausing. **Queue:** recycles this unit.
**Coach notes:** the pause/intonation is what makes non-defining clauses *sound* right in speech — drill the prosody, not just the commas.

## S3-U7-L02 — Defining vs Non-Defining (Meaning + Punctuation)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 7 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can distinguish defining (essential) vs non-defining (extra) clauses by meaning.
- The learner can punctuate/pause each correctly.
- The learner can see how the comma changes meaning.

**Target language:** both clause types, contrasted. **Grammar:** defining (no commas, identifies) vs non-defining
(commas, adds). **Accent Lab focus:** contrastive pausing. **Mindset:** precision of meaning = precision of thought.

**Materials:** a contrast card (same sentence with/without commas); a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: non-defining (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "My sister who lives in Dubai…" (I have several) vs "My sister, who lives in Dubai, …" (I have one) | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Comma changes meaning (identify vs add); brief explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Pause vs no-pause = the spoken comma | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Decide defining/non-defining; punctuate + say correctly | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Produce one of each + explain the meaning difference; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check meaning + pausing | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a defining + a non-defining sentence + the meaning difference | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — contrast card. Stretch — construct meaning-ambiguity examples.
**Success check:** correct distinction + prosody. **Queue:** recycles into L05.
**Coach notes:** the meaning distinction (does the clause identify or just add?) is the anchor — teach it via meaning first, punctuation second.

## S3-U7-L03 — Reduced Relative Clauses

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 7 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can reduce relative clauses (*the man standing there*, *the report written by X*).
- The learner can use participle phrases for more natural, concise speech.
- The learner can recognize active (-ing) vs passive (-ed) reductions.

**Target language:** reduced relatives (active *-ing* / passive *-ed*). **Grammar:** omitting *who/which + be* → participle.
**Accent Lab focus:** smooth participle phrases; phrasing. **Mindset:** concise = confident; trim the scaffolding.

**Materials:** a full↔reduced set; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: relative clauses (L01–L02) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "The woman talking to Ali… The book written in 1990…" | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Reduce *who/which is/are* → -ing (active) / -ed (passive); brief explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Smooth participle phrases | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Reduce full clauses; choose -ing vs -ed | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Describe a scene using 3 reduced relatives; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check active/passive reduction | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 reduced-relative descriptions | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — full↔reduced set. Stretch — describe a busy photo with several reductions.
**Success check:** correct reduced relatives (active/passive). **Queue:** recycles into L05/U12.
**Coach notes:** reductions make speech noticeably more native/concise — a high-value naturalness upgrade. Reward smooth participle phrasing.

## S3-U7-L04 — Prepositions + which/whom; *which* for a Whole Idea

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 7 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use *preposition + which/whom* (formal) and the natural spoken alternative.
- The learner can use *which* to refer to a whole clause/idea.
- The learner can add sophisticated detail.

**Target language:** *the person to whom…, the tool with which…* (formal) / *the person I spoke to* (natural);
*…, which meant…* (whole-clause *which*). **Grammar:** preposition + relative; sentential *which*. **Accent Lab focus:**
register-appropriate phrasing; pause before whole-clause *which*. **Mindset:** match formality to the moment.

**Materials:** a preposition + which/whom card; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: reduced relatives (L03) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "The colleague with whom I work…" (formal) / "…, which meant we had to restart." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Formal prep+whom/which vs natural end-preposition; *which* for a whole idea; brief note | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Register phrasing; pause before sentential *which* | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Convert formal↔natural; add whole-clause *which* comments | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Use whole-clause *which* to comment on 2 situations; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check preposition placement + *which* reference | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a whole-clause *which* comment + a prep+relative | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — the card. Stretch — use the formal register appropriately in a professional sentence.
**Success check:** correct preposition + relative + sentential *which*. **Queue:** feeds U9 register & U12.
**Coach notes:** the natural spoken form (end preposition) is fine and common — teach the formal form for recognition/register, not as the everyday default.

## S3-U7-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "Describe Someone/Something With Rich Detail" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 7 · ~40 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can give a rich description (~2 min): non-defining clauses + reduced relatives + a whole-clause *which*.
- The learner can enrich detail while keeping the listener oriented.
- The learner records their **Unit-7 rich description**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-7 language. **Grammar:** advanced relative clauses consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** pausing/intonation of clauses; phrasing; each learner's residual sound.
**Mindset:** add detail without losing the listener — clarity + richness together.

**Materials:** a model rich description; a checklist card (a non-defining clause / a reduced relative / a whole-clause which / clear pausing / rich detail);
recording prompt; Unit-7 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: non-defining, reduced, prep+which, sentential which | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean rich description | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills clause pausing + a residual sound | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse with the checklist; peer clarifying questions | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the ~2 min rich description + answer one live question | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the description; complete Unit-7 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — checklist + prep. Stretch — layer multiple clause types smoothly.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 7):** ~2 min rich description with ≥2 advanced relative structures, well-paced (rubric at B2). **Queue:** recycles into U12.
**Coach notes:** relative-clause mastery visibly raises sentence sophistication — but reward clarity/pacing, not clause-stuffing.
