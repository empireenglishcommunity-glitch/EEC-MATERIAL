# Stage 3 · Unit 5 — Modals of Deduction & Speculation (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 6.1 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage3/00-stage3-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** none (immersion). **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can make logical deductions
> and speculate about the present and the past with appropriate certainty (*must/might/could/can't* [+ have]) — recorded.
> **Grammar spine:** present + past modals of deduction · **Accent Lab:** intonation for certainty vs doubt,
> stress on the modal · **Mindset:** confident uncertainty — hedge naturally.

**Unit lessons:** L01 present deduction (must/might/can't be) · L02 past deduction (must/might/can't have been) ·
L03 speculating about situations & people · L04 explaining evidence + conclusions · L05 speaking task ("solve a mystery / speculate") + review.

**Assets to produce:** a certainty-scale card (must → might/could → can't), a "picture clues" speculation set,
a short mystery/inference scenario, a Google Form per lesson.

## S3-U5-L01 — Present Deduction: *must / might / could / can't be*

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 5 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: B1 modals.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can deduce about the present with *must* (sure), *might/could/may* (possible), *can't* (impossible).
- The learner can express how certain a conclusion is.
- The learner can justify a deduction with evidence.

**Target language:** *must/might/could/may/can't + be/base verb* (present deduction). **Grammar:** present modals
of deduction. **Accent Lab focus:** stress the modal; intonation signals certainty vs doubt. **Mindset:** confident uncertainty — say what's likely without over-claiming.

**Materials:** a certainty-scale card; a "clues" speculation set; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: B1 must/might for possibility | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "He's not answering — he must be busy. It might be broken. It can't be true." | 7 min |
| 3 | Notice | *must* (certain) / *might-could-may* (possible) / *can't* (impossible); brief explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Stress the modal; certainty vs doubt intonation | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Deduce from clues; justify with evidence | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Make 3 present deductions about situations; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check modal choice = certainty level | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 deductions (must/might/can't) + evidence | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — certainty-scale card. Stretch — chain evidence → deduction → alternative.
**Success check:** correct present deduction matched to certainty. **Queue:** present deduction recycles this unit.
**Coach notes:** the key contrast is *must* (positive certainty) vs *can't* (negative certainty) — NOT *mustn't* for deduction. Drill "can't be" for "I'm sure it's not."

## S3-U5-L02 — Past Deduction: *must / might / can't have been*

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 5 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can deduce about the past with *must/might/could/can't + have + participle*.
- The learner can conclude about what probably happened.
- The learner can use reduced forms ("must've, might've, can't've") naturally.

**Target language:** *must/might/could/can't have + participle* (past deduction). **Grammar:** past modals of deduction.
**Accent Lab focus:** reduced "must've/might've"; certainty intonation. **Mindset:** reason about the past confidently.

**Materials:** past-clue scenarios; a clip; the reduced-forms card; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: present deduction (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "The ground's wet — it must have rained. She might have forgotten." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *must/might/could/can't + have + participle* = past deduction; brief explanation | 8 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Reduced "must've/might've/can't've"; intonation | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Deduce what happened from past clues | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | 3 past deductions from evidence; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check past-deduction form + reduction | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 "it must/might/can't have + participle" | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — scenario cards + form reminder. Stretch — offer 2 competing past explanations.
**Success check:** correct past deduction + natural reduced forms. **Queue:** recycles into L03/L04.
**Coach notes:** the reduced pronunciation ("must've rained") is vital for listening + naturalness. Reuse U1/U4 participle work.

## S3-U5-L03 — Speculating About Situations & People

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 5 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can speculate about people's feelings/intentions and about situations.
- The learner can combine present + past deduction fluidly.
- The learner can hedge speculation naturally (*I'd say…, it looks like…, presumably…*).

**Target language:** speculation language (looks like, seems, presumably, I'd guess) + deduction modals.
**Grammar:** integrated present/past deduction + hedging. **Accent Lab focus:** thoughtful, tentative intonation.
**Mindset:** speculating aloud = thinking in English in real time.

**Materials:** people/situation image prompts; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: present + past deduction | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: speculating about a photo ("They look happy — they must have won something.") | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Combining deduction + hedging to speculate; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Tentative/thoughtful intonation | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Speculate about image prompts (present + past) | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Speculate about a situation for ~1 min; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check deduction range + hedging | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a speculation about an image/situation | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — image prompts + phrase bank. Stretch — build a full speculative theory.
**Success check:** fluent speculation mixing deduction + hedging. **Queue:** feeds U11 (debate) & U12.
**Coach notes:** connects to U3 certainty + U9 hedging — speculation is a hallmark of natural, educated conversation. Reward tentativeness.

## S3-U5-L04 — Explaining Evidence + Conclusions

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 5 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can present evidence and draw a reasoned conclusion.
- The learner can connect clues to deductions explicitly (*given that…, this suggests…, so it must…*).
- The learner can reason aloud in a structured way.

**Target language:** reasoning connectors (given that, this suggests, therefore, so) + deduction. **Grammar:**
deduction + connectors. **Accent Lab focus:** phrasing a reasoning chain; emphasis on the conclusion. **Mindset:** structured reasoning = credibility.

**Materials:** evidence→conclusion scenario cards; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: speculation (L03) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "Given that the lights are off and the car's gone, they must have left." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Evidence → reasoning connector → conclusion; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Phrase the chain; stress the conclusion | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Build evidence→conclusion chains from scenarios | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Reason aloud about a scenario (evidence + conclusion); record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check reasoning structure + deduction | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post an evidence→conclusion reasoning | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — scenario cards + connectors. Stretch — consider + reject an alternative conclusion.
**Success check:** a clear evidence→conclusion chain with correct deduction. **Queue:** reasoning feeds U11/U12.
**Coach notes:** this reasoning structure directly supports the abstract/debate unit and the flagship conversation — reward logical, well-phrased chains.

## S3-U5-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "Solve a Mystery / Speculate" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 5 · ~40 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can work through a mystery/scenario (~2 min): present + past deduction + evidence + reasoned conclusion.
- The learner can discuss competing theories with a partner.
- The learner records their **Unit-5 deduction talk**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-5 language. **Grammar:** deduction modals consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** certainty/doubt intonation + reduced modal-perfects; each learner's residual sound.
**Mindset:** confident uncertainty — reason aloud without needing to be 100% sure.

**Materials:** a short mystery/inference scenario; a model deduction talk; a checklist card (present deduction / past deduction / evidence / conclusion / a hedge);
recording prompt; Unit-5 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: present + past deduction, certainty scale | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean "solve the mystery" deduction talk | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills reduced modal-perfects + a residual sound | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Work a mystery in pairs (competing theories) | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the ~2 min deduction talk + discuss with a partner | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the deduction talk; complete Unit-5 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — scenario + checklist + prep. Stretch — defend one theory against a partner's.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 5):** ~2 min deduction talk with present+past modals + evidence→conclusion (rubric at B2). **Queue:** recycles into U11/U12.
**Coach notes:** fun + rigorous — the mystery format makes deduction communicative. Reward reasoning + natural reduced forms.
