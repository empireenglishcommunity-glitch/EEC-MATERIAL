## S3-U2-L01 — Past Perfect: "Had Done"

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 2 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: B1 narrative past.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use the past perfect (*had + participle*) for an earlier past event.
- The learner can show which of two past events happened first.
- The learner can use it with *by the time / before / after / already*.

**Target language:** past perfect; sequence markers (by the time, before, after, already, when). **Grammar:**
*had + past participle* for "the past before the past." **Accent Lab focus:** *'d* contraction; thought groups.
**Mindset:** owning your story — you can now narrate cause-and-sequence, not just events.

**Materials:** a two-event timeline; a layered-story clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: B1 past simple + continuous narration | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "When I arrived, the meeting had already started." | 7 min |
| 3 | Notice | *had + participle* = earlier past; sequence markers; brief English explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | *'d* contraction ("I'd finished"); thought-group phrasing | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Combine two past events showing order; use by the time/before/after | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Tell 3 sentences with an earlier-past event; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check past perfect for the earlier event | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "By the time ___, I had already ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — timeline + frames. Stretch — a 3-event sequence with correct tenses.
**Success check:** correct past perfect for the earlier of two events. **Queue:** past perfect recycles every warm-up this unit.
**Coach notes:** learners often over-use the past perfect (marking every past verb). Teach it as the *earlier* event marker — use it only when sequence needs clarifying.

## S3-U2-L02 — Past Perfect Continuous: "Had Been Doing"

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 2 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use the past perfect continuous (*had been + -ing*) for activity leading up to a past point.
- The learner can explain the cause of a past situation (*was tired because had been working*).
- The learner can combine it with the past perfect/simple.

**Target language:** past perfect continuous; cause/duration-in-the-past contexts. **Grammar:** *had been + -ing*.
**Accent Lab focus:** phrasing the longer verb string; weak *had been*. **Mindset:** explain your "why" — depth of narration.

**Materials:** a cause-and-effect timeline; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: past perfect (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "She was exhausted because she'd been working all night." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *had been + -ing* = activity up to a past point (often explains a result); brief explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Weak "had been"; smooth the verb string | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Explain past situations with a "had been -ing" cause | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | 3 "result + because had been -ing" sentences; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check the form + cause logic | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "I was ___ because I'd been ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — cause-effect frames. Stretch — combine with past perfect simple in one story.
**Success check:** correct past perfect continuous for pre-past activity/cause. **Queue:** recycles into L03/L04.
**Coach notes:** contrast with past perfect simple (result/completed vs activity/duration) — mirrors the U1 continuous/simple distinction; point out the parallel.

## S3-U2-L03 — Sequencing Complex Narratives (All Past Tenses)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 2 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can narrate a complex story using past simple, continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous together.
- The learner can flash back and explain backstory mid-narrative.
- The learner can keep a long narrative clear and well-sequenced.

**Target language:** full past-tense system + sequence markers. **Grammar:** integrated past tenses. **Accent Lab focus:**
narrative prosody; phrasing a long turn into thought groups. **Mindset:** narrate your journey — you have the whole toolkit now.

**Materials:** a complex-story model clip; a story-arc card (with a flashback); Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: past perfect (simple + continuous) | 5 min |
| 2 | Input | Model complex story (events + background + flashback with past perfect) | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | How the four past tenses combine; where past perfect clarifies order; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Narrative prosody; group clauses; pace the story | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Build a complex story (with a flashback) from an arc card in pairs | 9 min |
| 6 | Free output | Tell a complex story (6–8 sentences, ≥3 past tenses); record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check tense integration + clarity of sequence | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a complex story using ≥3 past tenses | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — arc card + tense reminder. Stretch — non-linear narration (start mid-story, flash back).
**Success check:** a clear complex narrative integrating ≥3 past tenses. **Queue:** complex narration feeds U12.
**Coach notes:** clarity of *sequence* is the goal — reward stories where the listener always knows what happened first.

## S3-U2-L04 — Explaining Causes & Backstory

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 2 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can explain the backstory/causes behind a situation or decision.
- The learner can link cause and effect across time frames.
- The learner can answer "why did that happen?" with depth.

**Target language:** cause/backstory language (the reason was, this was because, it turned out that, by then).
**Grammar:** past perfect (+ continuous) for backstory + connectors. **Accent Lab focus:** phrasing explanations; emphasis on causes.
**Mindset:** depth over speed — explaining *why* is a B2 marker.

**Materials:** situation cards ("explain how this happened"); a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: complex narration (L03) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: someone explains why a plan failed (backstory with past perfect) | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Building a cause/backstory explanation across time; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Phrase the explanation; stress the causes | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Explain the backstory of a situation card; peer asks "why?" | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Explain the backstory of a real decision/event; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check cause logic + tense use | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a backstory explanation of a real event | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — situation cards + frames. Stretch — handle probing follow-up "why" questions.
**Success check:** a coherent cause/backstory explanation across time frames. **Queue:** explanation skills feed U11/U12.
**Coach notes:** this bridges narration → reasoning (needed for the abstract/debate unit). Reward clear causal links.

## S3-U2-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "A Turning Point in Your Life" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 2 · ~40 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can narrate a significant life event (~2–3 min): complex sequence + backstory + reflection.
- The learner can answer probing follow-up questions.
- The learner records their **Unit-2 turning-point narrative**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-2 language. **Grammar:** full past-tense system consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** narrative prosody + naturalness; each learner's residual sound.
**Mindset:** owning your story — a turning point told with depth is powerful proof of growth.

**Materials:** a model turning-point narrative; a checklist card (set-up / earlier-past backstory / the event / reflection / clear sequence);
recording prompt; Unit-2 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: past perfect (simple + continuous), sequencing | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean turning-point narrative | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a residual sound + narrative phrasing | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse with the checklist; peer probing questions | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the ~2–3 min narrative + answer follow-ups | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the narrative; complete Unit-2 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — checklist + prep. Stretch — non-linear telling + a reflective conclusion.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 2):** ~2–3 min narrative with accurate complex past tenses + clear sequence (rubric at B2). **Queue:** recycles into U12.
**Coach notes:** a strong proof-clip candidate — emotionally resonant + linguistically advanced. Compare informally to earlier milestone clips.
