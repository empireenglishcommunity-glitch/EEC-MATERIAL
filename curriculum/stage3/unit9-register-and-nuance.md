## S3-U9-L01 — Formal vs Informal

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 9 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: B1 range.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can recognize and produce formal vs informal choices (vocab, contractions, phrasing, tone).
- The learner can say the same thing in two registers.
- The learner can judge which register a situation needs.

**Target language:** formal/informal pairs (get → receive/obtain; kids → children; want → would like; a lot of → considerable);
contractions/phrasal verbs (informal) vs single-word verbs (formal). **Grammar:** register features. **Accent Lab focus:**
register-appropriate prosody (crisper/formal vs relaxed/informal). **Mindset:** you can code-switch — a confident, adaptable speaker.

**Materials:** a formal/informal comparison card; a two-version clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: same idea said simply | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: an email-style formal message vs a text-style informal one (same content) | 7 min |
| 3 | Notice | Register markers (vocab, contractions, phrasal vs Latinate verbs, directness); brief explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Prosody differs by register (crisp vs relaxed) | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Convert sentences formal↔informal | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say one message in both registers; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check register consistency | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post one message in formal + informal versions | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — comparison card. Stretch — a mixed-register message + fix it.
**Success check:** consistent formal vs informal versions. **Queue:** register recycles this unit.
**Coach notes:** register mismatch (too casual in formal settings or stiff in casual ones) is a top B2 issue — build awareness that *appropriateness*, not "more formal = better," is the goal.

## S3-U9-L02 — Diplomacy & Softening

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 9 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can soften requests, bad news, and disagreement diplomatically.
- The learner can use softening devices (*I was wondering if…, would it be possible…, to be honest…, I'm afraid…*).
- The learner can sound polite and professional under pressure.

**Target language:** softening/diplomatic language (I was wondering if, would it be possible, perhaps, slightly,
I'm afraid, to be honest, with respect). **Grammar:** hedging + indirect forms. **Accent Lab focus:** polite,
warm intonation; softening rise. **Mindset:** diplomacy = strength, not weakness.

**Materials:** a softening-language card; blunt→diplomatic transform set; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: formal/informal (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I was wondering if you could…"; "I'm afraid there's a problem." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Softening devices for requests/bad news/disagreement; brief explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Polite intonation; the softening rise | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Turn blunt statements into diplomatic ones | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Deliver a request + bad news + a disagreement diplomatically; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check softening + tone | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a diplomatic version of a blunt message | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — softening card. Stretch — handle a sensitive scenario (decline a request kindly).
**Success check:** diplomatically softened request/bad news/disagreement. **Queue:** feeds U10 (professional) & U11 (debate).
**Coach notes:** intonation carries at least half of diplomacy — a grammatically polite sentence said flatly still sounds rude. Drill the warm, softening prosody.

## S3-U9-L03 — Collocations & Natural Phrasing

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 9 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use high-frequency collocations (make a decision, take a break, strong opinion, heavy rain).
- The learner can replace "textbook" phrasing with natural word partnerships.
- The learner can sound more idiomatic/native.

**Target language:** common collocations (verb+noun, adj+noun, adverb+adj) + natural phrasing. **Grammar:** (lexical).
**Accent Lab focus:** collocations as stress units/chunks. **Mindset:** collect natural phrases from your input diet.

**Materials:** a high-frequency collocations set; a "textbook vs natural" transform set; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: a few known collocations | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip rich in collocations; learners notice the word partnerships | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Collocations = words that go together; common patterns; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Say collocations as single stress chunks | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Replace weak/textbook phrasing with collocations | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Talk about a topic using ≥5 collocations; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check collocation accuracy + naturalness | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a short text using 5 target collocations | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — collocations set. Stretch — self-source 5 collocations from real input.
**Success check:** ≥5 natural collocations used correctly. **Queue:** collocations recycle into U10/U11/U12.
**Coach notes:** collocations are the biggest "sounds natural" lever at B2 — encourage a personal collocation notebook fed by their input diet.

## S3-U9-L04 — Adapting Register to the Situation

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 9 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can choose the right register for a given situation and audience.
- The learner can shift register mid-interaction as needed.
- The learner can combine register + diplomacy + collocations naturally.

**Target language:** integrated register/diplomacy/collocation. **Grammar:** register control. **Accent Lab focus:**
matching prosody to register/audience. **Mindset:** read the room — adapt confidently.

**Materials:** situation/audience cards (boss, friend, customer, interview); a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: formal/informal + softening + collocations | 5 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: same request to a friend vs a manager | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Matching register to audience/situation; shifting mid-interaction; brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Prosody matches the audience | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Deliver a message to different audiences (register-match) | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Handle a situation card with the right register; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check register-audience fit | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post the same message adapted to 2 audiences | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — situation cards + register cues. Stretch — shift register mid-conversation on cue.
**Success check:** register appropriately matched to audience/situation. **Queue:** feeds U10/U12.
**Coach notes:** this is the practical synthesis of the unit — reward accurate *judgment* of what a situation needs, not just formality.

## S3-U9-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "Same Message, Two Registers" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 9 · ~40 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can deliver the same message in a formal and an informal register (~2 min total), with diplomacy + collocations.
- The learner can explain their register choices.
- The learner records their **Unit-9 two-register performance**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-9 language. **Grammar:** register + diplomacy consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** register-appropriate prosody; each learner's residual sound.
**Mindset:** confident code-switching — one of the clearest signs of an advanced speaker.

**Materials:** a model two-register performance; a checklist card (formal version / informal version / a softening device / collocations / prosody match);
recording prompt; Unit-9 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: formal/informal markers, softening, collocations | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean two-register performance | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills register prosody + a residual sound | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse both versions; peer feedback on appropriateness | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record both register versions + explain the choices | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the performance; complete Unit-9 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — checklist + prep. Stretch — a sensitive message (bad news) in both registers.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 9):** two clear register versions with diplomacy + collocations (rubric at B2). **Queue:** recycles into U10/U12.
**Coach notes:** register + diplomacy + collocations are the "naturalness" trio the flagship conversation needs — reward appropriateness and natural phrasing over complexity.
