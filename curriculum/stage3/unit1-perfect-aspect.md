## S3-U1-L01 — Present Perfect Continuous (Form + Use)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 1 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: B1 present perfect.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can form the present perfect continuous (*have/has been + -ing*).
- The learner can talk about activity that started in the past and continues / has just stopped.
- The learner can emphasize the *activity/duration* rather than the completed result.

**Target language:** present perfect continuous; activity verbs (work, study, learn, wait, try, live, practise).
**Grammar:** *have/has been + -ing*; focus on ongoing activity/duration. **Accent Lab focus:** thought groups
("I've been working // on a new project"); "I've been" contraction/linking. **Mindset:** identity lock-in — you *are* an English speaker, not "trying to learn."

**Materials:** structure slide; a "what have you been up to?" clip; activity-verb set; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: B1 present perfect ("I've worked here for 5 years") | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I've been working on a project all week. She's been studying for exams." | 7 min |
| 3 | Notice | *have been + -ing* = ongoing/recent activity; focus on duration/activity; brief English explanation | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Thought-group phrasing; "I've been" linking | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Substitution + questions ("What have you been doing lately?") | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say 3 things you've been doing recently; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check *been + -ing*; note thought-group phrasing | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "Lately I've been ___, ___, and ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — structure slide + verb set. Stretch — add duration + a result ("…so I'm tired / …and I've almost finished").
**Success check:** 3 correct present perfect continuous sentences. **Queue:** perfect continuous recycles every warm-up this unit.
**Coach notes:** learners with strong B1 may default to the simple perfect ("I've worked") for ongoing activity. Nudge toward the continuous when the *activity/duration* is the point — the contrast is formalized in L02.

## S3-U1-L02 — Perfect Continuous vs Simple Perfect (Activity vs Result)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 1 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can choose the continuous (activity/duration) vs simple (result/completion/quantity) perfect.
- The learner can express both the *how long* and the *how much/result* of an activity.
- The learner can self-correct the choice.

**Target language:** contrast pairs ("I've been reading this book" vs "I've read three books"). **Grammar:**
perfect continuous (activity, ongoing, "how long") vs perfect simple (result, completed, "how many/much").
**Accent Lab focus:** contrastive stress on the key word; thought groups. **Mindset:** progress is activity *and* results — value both.

**Materials:** an activity-vs-result decision card; a mixed-sentence sort; a clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: present perfect continuous (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I've been painting the house (activity) — I've painted two rooms (result)." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Continuous = activity/duration/still going; simple = result/complete/quantity; brief explanation | 8 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Contrastive stress ("I've **been** painting" vs "I've painted **two** rooms") | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Sort sentences; produce paired activity/result statements | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Describe an ongoing effort (activity + result so far); record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check the choice; note habitual errors | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post an activity + result pair about a current project | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — decision card + sorted examples. Stretch — a short monologue moving between both.
**Success check:** correct continuous/simple choice in most items + a clean pair. **Queue:** the contrast re-drilled across the stage.
**Coach notes:** the meaning distinction (activity vs result) matters more than rigid rules — some verbs/contexts allow both. Reward sensible choices; correct clear mismatches.

## S3-U1-L03 — *How Long…?* + Duration Precision

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 1 · ~35 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can ask and answer *"How long have you been …?"* precisely.
- The learner can use *for/since* + refined duration language with the continuous.
- The learner can talk in detail about long-running activities.

**Target language:** *How long have you been…?*; *for/since*; duration nuance (ages, a while, ever since).
**Grammar:** present perfect continuous for duration. **Accent Lab focus:** weak *for/since*; question intonation; thought groups.
**Mindset:** measure your journey in duration + consistency, not perfection.

**Materials:** duration prompt set; a "how long" clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: activity vs result (L02) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "How long have you been learning English?" "Ever since I was a teenager." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *How long…been + -ing*; for/since + nuance; brief explanation | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Weak for/since; natural question intonation | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Interview: *How long have you been…?* + precise answers | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output | Talk about 2 long-running activities with duration precision; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check continuous + duration language | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "I've been ___ for/since ___." (2 activities) | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — duration phrase bank. Stretch — add how it's changed over that time.
**Success check:** accurate *How long…been…?* + duration. **Queue:** duration language recycles into U2/U12.
**Coach notes:** great recycling of B1 for/since in a more precise, natural register — push for varied duration expressions.

## S3-U1-L04 — Talking About Ongoing Projects & Efforts

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 1 · ~40 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can describe an ongoing project/goal (activity + progress + result so far + next steps).
- The learner can combine perfect continuous + simple perfect + future naturally.
- The learner can talk fluently about work/study/personal projects.

**Target language:** project/effort language (working on, making progress, so far, almost done, next step).
**Grammar:** integrated perfect continuous + simple + future. **Accent Lab focus:** phrasing across a longer turn; emphasis on progress words.
**Mindset:** name a real ongoing project and narrate it in English (using > studying).

**Materials:** a "my current project" model clip; a project-talk frame; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: continuous + simple perfect + how long | 5 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I've been developing an app. I've finished the design, and I'm going to launch soon." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Combining tenses to describe a project (activity/result/next); brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Phrase the longer turn in thought groups; stress progress words | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Describe a project using all three time references; peer Q&A | 9 min |
| 6 | Free output | Talk ~1.5 min about a real ongoing project; record | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check tense integration + fluency | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a project update (activity + result + next step) | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — project frame. Stretch — field 2 follow-up questions.
**Success check:** a coherent multi-tense project talk. **Queue:** project talk feeds U12 + professional units.
**Coach notes:** high real-world value (status updates, interviews). Encourage a reusable "current project" narrative.

## S3-U1-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "What You've Been Working On" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 3 / B2 · Unit 1 · ~40 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can talk for ~2 min about recent activity/projects: perfect continuous + simple perfect + duration + next steps.
- The learner can hold a natural exchange (ask + respond + follow up).
- The learner records their **Unit-1 "what I've been up to" talk**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-1 language. **Grammar:** perfect aspect consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** overall clarity + thought-group phrasing; each learner's residual sound.
**Mindset:** identity lock-in — speak as the English-speaking professional you're becoming.

**Materials:** a model talk; a checklist card (perfect continuous / a result / duration / next step / a question to partner);
recording prompt; Unit-1 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: perfect continuous, activity vs result, how long | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean ~2 min "what I've been up to" talk | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a residual sound + thought-group phrasing | 5 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse with the checklist; peer exchange (ask + follow up) | 8 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the ~2 min talk + answer one live question | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 3 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the talk; complete Unit-1 quiz | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — checklist + prep. Stretch — sustain 2+ min with spontaneous follow-ups.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 1):** ~2 min talk with accurate perfect aspect + natural phrasing (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at B2). **Queue:** recycles into U12.
**Coach notes:** B2 opens with precision + naturalness — reward accurate aspect choice *and* natural thought-group phrasing, not just correctness.
