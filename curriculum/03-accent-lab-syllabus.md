# The Accent Lab — Syllabus (v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 1.4 · **Requirement:** R4 (Accent Lab) · **Design:** §5
> **Reads with:** `methodology/01-eec-methodology-handbook.md` (§9 accent pedagogy) and
> `curriculum/01-cefr-roadmap.md` (per-stage accent focus).
> **Purpose:** EEC's signature contrastive American-pronunciation curriculum, built *specifically* for
> Arabic speakers (Egyptian-Arabic first). It systematizes the pronunciation thread woven through the
> lessons into one sequenced syllabus, with per-sound teaching cards, a prosody module, a stage-by-stage
> map, and the scoring/tracking that feeds the speaking rubric and the review engine.

---

## 1. Why the Accent Lab exists

Global apps are **L1-agnostic** — they teach an Egyptian the same way they teach a Spaniard. But Arabic
creates *predictable* interference with English pronunciation. Because it's predictable, it's
**teachable and drillable** — and almost nobody does it systematically. This is a core EEC
differentiator and a flagship free-content engine.

**Honesty guardrail (GC-5):** the Accent Lab targets a **clear, confident, neutral American accent** and
high intelligibility. Native-like is an aspirational ceiling for the most dedicated — never a promise.
We celebrate intelligibility gains; we never shame a residual accent.

**Dialect awareness (important):** our primary audience is **Egyptian**. Egyptian Arabic differs from
other Arabic dialects in ways that change the target list — most notably, Egyptians **already have /g/**
(the letter ج is pronounced /g/), so /g/ is *easy* for them but the English **/dʒ/ ("j")** is often
*hard* (they may say "gob" for "job"). We flag dialect-specific notes on each card.

## 2. The Arabic→English interference map (target inventory)

The full set of features the Accent Lab trains, grouped. Each has a teaching card in §4–§5.

**A. Consonants (segmental)**
1. /p/ vs /b/ — Arabic lacks /p/ → "beople" for "people".
2. /v/ vs /f/ — Arabic lacks /v/ → "fery" for "very".
3. /θ/ and /ð/ — Egyptian Arabic often merges these to /s,z/ or /t,d/ → "sink/tink" for "think".
4. /dʒ/ ("j") — **Egyptian-specific:** tends to become /g/ → "gob" for "job". (Other dialects: reverse.)
5. /ŋ/ (the -ing sound) — not a native phoneme → /n/ ("readin") or /ŋg/ ("readingg").
6. /l/ vs /r/ quality — both exist in Arabic, but English **/r/** (bunched/retroflex, no trill) and the
   English **dark /l/** differ from Arabic; target quality, not confusion.
7. /tʃ/ ("ch") — not native to Egyptian Arabic → may become /ʃ/ ("sheese" for "cheese").
8. The **-s / -es endings** (/s/, /z/, /ɪz/) — plural, 3rd-person, and possessive.

**B. Vowels (segmental)**
Arabic has far fewer vowel qualities, so English contrasts collapse:
9. /ɪ/ vs /iː/ — ship/sheep.
10. /e/ vs /æ/ — bed/bad.
11. /ʌ/ vs /ɑː/ — cup/car; and /ɒ/ vs /ɔː/ — cot/caught.
12. /ʊ/ vs /uː/ — full/fool.
13. The **schwa /ə/** — the most common English vowel; Arabic speakers tend to give full value to
    unstressed vowels (no reduction).

**C. Consonant clusters (phonotactics)**
14. **Epenthesis (vowel insertion)** — the signature Arabic-transfer error: breaking clusters with an
    added vowel → "iskool/sitreet" for "school/street". Onset and coda clusters.

**D. Prosody (suprasegmental) — often matters most for "sounding natural"**
15. **Word stress** — placement changes naturalness/meaning (thirTEEN vs THIRty; PHOtograph vs
    phoTOGrapher).
16. **Sentence stress & rhythm** — English is stress-timed (stressed beats + reduced unstressed
    syllables); Arabic is more syllable-timed → English can sound "flat/even".
17. **Weak forms & reductions** — and, to, of, can → /ən, tə, əv, kən/; gonna/wanna/gotta.
18. **Linking** — joining words (an-apple, go-away).
19. **Intonation** — rising (yes/no questions), falling (statements, wh-questions, instructions).

## 3. How the Accent Lab is taught (pedagogy)

From Handbook §9, formalized. **Per-sound sequence (7 steps):**
1. **Perceive** — hear the contrast (minimal pairs); perception must precede production.
2. **Understand** — the articulation cue (what the mouth/tongue/lips do) — explained in Arabic early.
3. **Produce in isolation** — the sound alone.
4. **Produce in words** — target words.
5. **Produce in phrases/sentences** — controlled → freer.
6. **Produce in free speech** — spontaneous use.
7. **Maintain** — spaced re-drilling of the learner's weak items until stable.

**Core techniques:** minimal-pair listening + production · **shadowing** (speak along with/just behind a
native model) · **record-and-compare** (learner records, compares to model, self-diagnoses) · **targeted
feedback** (name the specific fix) · **spaced re-drilling** (weak sounds resurface via the review engine).

**Tools:** model audio bank (American), minimal-pair sets, mirror/paper-strip tricks, a record-and-compare
setup (phone/Zoom), and the Cohort Tracker weak-sound flags.

## 4. Consonant & cluster cards

> Format: **Target — dialect note — articulation cue — minimal pairs — example words — common error — mini-drill.**

### Card C1 — /p/ vs /b/
- **Cue:** /p/ = lips together + a **puff of air** (انفخ هوا) and no voice; /b/ = same lips, no puff, + voice.
- **Minimal pairs:** pen/Ben, pig/big, pack/back, cap/cab, rope/robe.
- **Words:** people, happy, stop, appen→**happen**, cup.
- **Common error:** /b/ for /p/ ("beople").
- **Drill:** paper-strip test (paper moves on /p/, not on /b/); pairs → words → "A **p**erson **p**ays."
- **Stage:** introduced **Stage 0 / Unit 0**.

### Card C2 — /v/ vs /f/
- **Cue:** both = **top teeth on lower lip**; /v/ has **voice**, /f/ has none.
- **Minimal pairs:** van/fan, vote/… , very/ferry, save/safe, leave/leaf.
- **Words:** very, five, video, love, seven.
- **Common error:** /f/ for /v/ ("fery").
- **Drill:** mirror (watch teeth-lip); hum for voicing on /v/; pairs → "I have **v**ery **f**ew."
- **Stage:** Stage 0 / Unit 0.

### Card C3 — /θ/ and /ð/
- **Dialect note:** Egyptians often merge to /s,z/ or /t,d/.
- **Cue:** **tongue tip lightly between the teeth** (اللسان بين السنان); /θ/ no voice (think), /ð/ voice (this).
- **Minimal pairs:** think/sink, thin/sin, three/tree; this/dis, they/day, breathe/breeze.
- **Words:** think, three, mother, father, this, the, weather.
- **Common error:** "sink/tink" for "think"; "dis/dat" for "this/that".
- **Drill:** exaggerate tongue-out first; pairs → "**Th**is is my **th**ree bro**th**ers." (recycled U2)
- **Stage:** Stage 0 / Units 2, 5; consolidated Stage 1.

### Card C4 — /dʒ/ ("j") — Egyptian priority
- **Dialect note:** Egyptian ج = /g/, so /dʒ/ is the *hard* one for them.
- **Cue:** /dʒ/ = tongue to the ridge behind the teeth + release with a "soft" burst + voice (like the
  start of "jam"); contrast with hard /g/ (go).
- **Minimal pairs:** jam/gam→**(gum)**, joke/…, "job" vs "gob", jail/…; jet vs get.
- **Words:** job, judge, orange, manage, page, engineer, June.
- **Common error:** /g/ for /dʒ/ ("gob", "engineer" with hard g).
- **Drill:** start from "d" + "zh"; pairs jet/get; "My **j**ob is a good **j**ob."
- **Stage:** Stage 0 / Unit 1 (jobs vocab is a natural home) → reinforced Stage 1.

### Card C5 — /ŋ/ (the -ing sound)
- **Cue:** back of the tongue up (like /g/) but nasal, **no hard g at the end**.
- **Minimal pairs:** thin/thing, sin/sing, ban/bang.
- **Words:** doing, reading, morning, going, running.
- **Common error:** "readin" (/n/) or "readingg" (/ŋg/).
- **Drill:** hold the nasal; "I'm doi**ng** and readi**ng**." (recycled U5, U9)
- **Stage:** Stage 0 / Units 5, 9.

### Card C6 — American /r/ and dark /l/
- **Cue:** /r/ = tongue **bunched/curled back, no trill** (مش راء مكررة); dark /l/ (end of words) = back
  of tongue raised (full, feel).
- **Minimal pairs (quality, not confusion):** right/light, road/load; feel/fee.
- **Words:** red, right, sorry, car, world; well, table, people.
- **Common error:** Arabic **trilled/tapped r**; overly "clear" l at word ends.
- **Drill:** sustain the American /r/ ("errr"); "The **r**ed ca**r** is **r**eally fa**r**."
- **Stage:** Stage 0 / Units 7, 8 (left/right clarity).

### Card C7 — /tʃ/ ("ch")
- **Dialect note:** not native to Egyptian Arabic.
- **Cue:** /tʃ/ = "t" + "sh" together (church); contrast with /ʃ/ (ship).
- **Minimal pairs:** chair/share, chip/ship, cheese/… , watch/wash.
- **Words:** chair, cheese, watch, teacher, lunch.
- **Common error:** /ʃ/ for /tʃ/ ("sheese", "wash" for "watch").
- **Drill:** pairs chip/ship; "The **ch**ef **ch**ose **ch**eese."
- **Stage:** Stage 1 (light exposure in Stage 0 vocab).

### Card C8 — the -s / -es endings (/s/, /z/, /ɪz/)
- **Cue:** after voiceless (books /s/), after voiced/vowel (pens /z/), after sibilants (boxes /ɪz/).
- **Words:** books/cats (/s/); pens/dogs/goes (/z/); boxes/watches/washes (/ɪz/).
- **Common error:** adding a vowel ("bookes"), or one flat /s/ everywhere.
- **Drill:** sort words into 3 sound groups; applies to **plurals, 3rd-person -s, possessives**.
- **Stage:** Stage 0 / Units 3 (plurals), 4 (3rd-person). Same rule as past -ed comes in Stage 1.

### Card C9 — Consonant clusters (no epenthesis) ⭐ signature
- **Cue:** say clusters **without adding a vowel** (مفيش حرف علة بين السواكن) — glide through them.
- **Words:** school, street, spring, desk, asked, texts, world, twelfth.
- **Common error:** "iskool/sitreet/deskuh" (vowel insertion).
- **Drill:** build the cluster gradually (s…k…school); slow-then-natural; "**S**chool **s**tarts on the **s**treet."
- **Stage:** Stage 0 / Units 0, 3; reinforced every stage. **This is the highest-value Arabic fix.**

## 5. Vowel & prosody cards

### Card V1 — /ɪ/ vs /iː/ (ship/sheep)
- **Cue:** /ɪ/ short & relaxed; /iː/ long & tense (smile). **Pairs:** ship/sheep, bit/beat, live/leave.
- **Common error:** merging both to one Arabic /i/. **Drill:** short↔long contrast; "I **live** to **leave**."
- **Stage:** Stage 0 / Unit 0 (vowel intro) → Stage 1.

### Card V2 — /e/ vs /æ/ (bed/bad)
- **Cue:** /æ/ = open, jaw down. **Pairs:** bed/bad, men/man, pen/pan. **Stage:** Stage 0→1.

### Card V3 — /ʌ/, /ɑː/, /ɒ/, /ɔː/ (cup/car/cot/caught)
- **Cue:** distinguish the "a/o" family. **Pairs:** cup/cop, cart/caught. **Stage:** Stage 1.

### Card V4 — /ʊ/ vs /uː/ (full/fool)
- **Pairs:** full/fool, pull/pool. **Stage:** Stage 1.

### Card V5 — the schwa /ə/ & vowel reduction
- **Cue:** unstressed vowels **reduce** to a lazy "uh" (banana = bə-NA-nə). **Common error:** giving every
  vowel full value (sounds robotic). **Stage:** Stage 1→2 (core to rhythm).

### Card P1 — Word stress
- **Cue:** one strong syllable per word; stress can change meaning. **Examples:** THIRteen vs thirTY;
  PREsent (n) vs preSENT (v). **Stage:** intro Stage 0 → developed Stage 1–2.

### Card P2 — Sentence stress & rhythm (stress-timing)
- **Cue:** stress the **content words** (nouns, verbs, adjectives); squeeze the function words.
  **Example:** "I **want** to **go** to the **beach**." **Stage:** Stage 1→2.

### Card P3 — Weak forms & reductions
- **Cue:** and→/ən/, to→/tə/, can→/kən/, of→/əv/; gonna/wanna/gotta. **Stage:** Stage 2 (connected speech).

### Card P4 — Linking
- **Cue:** join final consonant to next vowel (an-apple, turn-it-off). **Stage:** Stage 2.

### Card P5 — Intonation
- **Cue:** rising for yes/no questions; falling for statements, wh-questions, and instructions.
  **Stage:** intro Stage 0 (questions, directions) → refined Stage 2–3.

## 6. Syllabus sequence by stage

| Stage (CEFR) | Segmental focus | Prosody focus |
|--|--|--|
| **Stage 0 (Pre-A1→A1)** | /p/–/b/, /v/–/f/, /θ/–/ð/, /dʒ/, /ŋ/, /l/–/r/ quality, -s endings, **clusters**, vowel intro (ship/sheep) | word-stress intro; question vs statement intonation; -teen/-ty |
| **Stage 1 (A2)** | consolidate all consonants; full vowel contrasts (V1–V4); /tʃ/; -ed past endings | sentence rhythm intro; stress patterns; schwa intro |
| **Stage 2 (B1)** | residual segmental clean-up; schwa mastery (V5) | **connected speech**: weak forms, reductions, linking; intonation patterns |
| **Stage 3 (B2)** | fine-tuning individual weak sounds | thought groups, emphasis/contrast stress, naturalness, self-monitoring |
| **Stage 4 (C1)** | near-native polish; idiomatic reductions | prosodic nuance; individual fine-tuning (aspirational ceiling) |

## 7. Assessment & the Pronunciation rubric

- The Accent Lab feeds the **Pronunciation/Accent** dimension of the 5-dimension speaking rubric
  (Handbook §16; formalized in Task 1.6).
- **Scoring 0–5** considers: intelligibility, accuracy on the stage's target sounds, and prosody
  (stress/rhythm/intonation). It is **not** scored as "how native" — it's "how clear + how well the
  stage targets are produced."
- **Milestone evidence:** the Day-1 sample vs later samples show measurable clarity gains — a key part of
  the transformation proof.

## 8. Personalized weak-sound tracking

- Each lesson's Accent Lab element is **tagged**; when a learner misses/mispronounces a target, it's
  flagged in the **Cohort Tracker** (pronunciation notes).
- Flagged sounds **resurface more often** via the spaced-review engine (warm-ups, clinics).
- The **Accent Clinic** (Stage 0, Unit 10 L03) and equivalent clinics each stage target each learner's
  personal top 3.

## 9. Free-content spin-off (the marketing engine)

The most visual/relatable cards become a flagship **free series** — e.g., *"The 5 sounds Arabic speakers
always get wrong"*, *"Why we say 'iskool' (and how to stop)"*, *"job vs gob — the /dʒ/ fix"*. These:
- Demonstrate the method (a real system, not hacks),
- Are highly shareable (recognition + humor + quick win),
- Route viewers to the entry challenge / waitlist (brand foundations §10, funnel design).
