# Stage 1 · Unit 5 — Making Comparisons (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 5.2 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage1/00-stage1-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~35% (fading). **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can compare people, places,
> and things (comparatives + superlatives), express degree with *(not) as…as / too / enough*, and give a
> reasoned preference — recorded.
> **Grammar spine:** comparatives (*-er/more*) + superlatives (*-est/the most*) + irregulars + *as…as/too/enough* ·
> **Accent Lab:** word stress in longer adjectives, /ð/ in *than*, *-er/-est* endings · **Mindset:** compare to your Day-1 self.

**Unit lessons:** L01 comparatives (-er) · L02 longer & irregular comparatives (more/better/worse) ·
L03 superlatives · L04 *as…as*, *too*, *enough* · L05 speaking task + review.

**Assets to produce:** a comparison adjective set, two-item + three-item comparison visuals (cities/phones/foods),
minimal audio for *than* (/ð/) and *-er/-est*, a Google Form per lesson.

## S1-U5-L01 — Comparatives: "Cairo Is Bigger Than…" (-er)

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 5 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Stage-0 adjectives.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can form comparatives of short adjectives (+ *-er* + *than*).
- The learner can compare two things/people/places.
- The learner can produce 3–4 true comparison sentences.

**Target language:** short adjectives (big, small, cheap, old, new, fast, tall, hot, cold) + *-er* + *than*.
**Grammar:** short adj + *-er* + *than*; spelling (big→bigger, easy→easier). **Accent Lab focus:** *than* with /ð/
(voiced th); the *-er* ending (/ər/, not a trilled r). **Arabic support:** ~35%. **Mindset focus:** the only fair comparison is you-today vs you-on-Day-1.

**Materials:** comparison-adjective set; two-item visuals; a comparison clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: 5 Stage-0 adjectives in simple sentences | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "Cairo is bigger than Alexandria. A taxi is faster than a bus." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Rule: short adj + *-er* + *than*; spelling (double consonant; y→ier); brief Arabic note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | /ð/ in *than*; the /ər/ ending (no Arabic trill); stress the adjective | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Compare two visuals; substitution with the adjective set | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Make 3 true comparisons (two cities, two foods, two phones); record one | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check *-er + than*; check /ð/ in *than* | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 comparisons about things in your life | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give the adjective set + frame "___ is ___er than ___." Stretch — add a reason ("…because it has more people").
**Success check:** 3 correct comparative sentences with *than*. **Queue:** comparatives recycle every warm-up this unit.
**Coach notes:** two errors: "more big" (double marking) and dropping *than*. Fix both now; the /ð/ in *than* is a great recycling point for U7's th-work.

## S1-U5-L02 — Longer & Irregular Comparatives: *more expensive, better, worse*

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 5 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can form comparatives of long adjectives (*more + adjective*).
- The learner can use irregular comparatives (good→better, bad→worse, far→further).
- The learner can compare using a mix of short and long adjectives.

**Target language:** long adjectives (expensive, beautiful, comfortable, interesting, difficult, important) + *more*;
irregulars (better, worse, further). **Grammar:** *more + long adj + than*; irregular comparatives. **Accent Lab focus:**
word stress in long adjectives (*ex**pen**sive, com**for**table, **in**teresting*). **Arabic support:** ~30%.
**Mindset focus:** track your reps — consistency is the real "better."

**Materials:** long-adjective set + stress marks; irregular card; comparison visuals; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: 4 short comparatives from L01 | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "This phone is more expensive but the other is better." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Long adj → *more + adj*; irregulars (good/bad/far); the "one syllable = -er, longer = more" rule of thumb | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Stress the right syllable in long adjectives; keep /ð/ in *than* | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Sort adjectives (short → -er / long → more); build comparisons | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Compare two things you own/use with 1 short + 1 long adjective; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check *more* vs *-er* choice + irregulars | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 2 comparisons: one with *more…*, one with *better/worse* | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give the sorted set + irregular card. Stretch — combine two adjectives in one comparison.
**Success check:** correct *more*/irregular comparatives + right form choice. **Queue:** long-adj stress recycles into U7/U9.
**Coach notes:** the "-er vs more" boundary (two-syllable adjectives) can be fuzzy — teach the clear cases; accept reasonable choices for borderline ones.

## S1-U5-L03 — Superlatives: "The Biggest / The Most Beautiful"

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 5 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can form superlatives (*the + -est* / *the most + adj*) and irregulars (the best/worst).
- The learner can say which is the most/least in a group of three+.
- The learner can talk about their favourite/"the best" things.

**Target language:** superlative adjectives; *the biggest, the most expensive, the best, the worst*; "in the world / in my city".
**Grammar:** *the + adj-est* (short) / *the most + adj* (long); irregular superlatives. **Accent Lab focus:** the *-est* ending;
stress; the article *the*. **Arabic support:** ~30%. **Mindset focus:** name your best English win so far.

**Materials:** three-item visuals; superlative slide; a "the best in my city" clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: comparatives (short + long + irregular) from L01–L02 | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "It's the biggest city and the most famous place in Egypt." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Superlative rule: *the + -est* / *the most*; irregulars; always with *the*; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | *-est* ending; don't drop *the*; stress the adjective | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | From 3 items, say the -est/most; then the least; peer check | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say "the best/worst/biggest…" for 3 categories in your life; record one | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check *the* + superlative form | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 superlatives ("the best… in my city / my life") | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give the slide + frame "___ is the ___est ___." Stretch — add "one of the most…" and a reason.
**Success check:** correct superlative forms with *the*. **Queue:** superlatives recycle into U8 (best place to visit) & U10.
**Coach notes:** dropping *the* and double-marking ("the most biggest") are the errors to catch. Keep examples personal and real.

## S1-U5-L04 — *as…as*, *too*, and *enough* (Degree & Preference)

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 5 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can say two things are equal/unequal with *(not) as…as*.
- The learner can express degree with *too* (excess) and *enough* (sufficiency).
- The learner can express and justify a preference.

**Target language:** *as + adj + as*; *not as…as*; *too + adj*; *adj + enough / enough + noun*; *I prefer… because…*.
**Grammar:** equality/degree structures. **Accent Lab focus:** rhythm of *"as … as"*; *too* vs *to* clarity.
**Arabic support:** ~30%. **Mindset focus:** "good enough today" beats "perfect never" — ship your reps.

**Materials:** comparison prompts; *too/enough* picture cards; a preference dialogue; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: one comparative + one superlative | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Dialogue: "This one isn't as expensive." "But it's too small." "Is it big enough?" | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *(not) as…as* (equal/unequal); *too* = more than needed; *enough* = the right amount; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Rhythm "**as** cheap **as**"; distinguish *too* /tuː/ from *to* /tə/ | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Compare pairs with *as…as*; react to items with *too/enough*; state a preference | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Choose between two options, justify with *as…as / too / enough*; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check structures + preference reasoning | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a choice + reason using *too* or *enough* | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — provide frames for each structure. Stretch — combine all three in a short opinion.
**Success check:** correct use of ≥2 of the structures with a justified preference. **Queue:** degree structures recycle into U6 (quantity) & U9 (opinions).
**Coach notes:** *too* vs *enough* placement (adj + enough, but enough + noun) trips learners — give clear model sentences and lots of oral reps.

## S1-U5-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "Compare Two Cities / Two Things You Like" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 5 · ~35 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can compare two things in a connected way (6–8 sentences): comparatives + superlative + degree + preference.
- The learner can respond to a partner's comparison and add their own view.
- The learner records their **Unit-5 comparison talk**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-5 language. **Grammar:** comparatives/superlatives/degree consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** overall clarity; long-adjective stress; /ð/ in *than*; each learner's flagged sound.
**Arabic support:** ~25% (fading). **Mindset focus:** compare your English now to Stage 0 — measurable growth.

**Materials:** a model comparison talk; a checklist card (2+ comparatives / 1+ superlative / 1 degree structure / a preference + reason);
recording prompt; Unit-5 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: -er, more, -est/most, irregulars, as…as/too/enough | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean comparison talk (6–8 sentences) | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist of a good comparison talk | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a flagged sound (long-adj stress / *than* /ð/) | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse with the checklist; peer practice + a reaction each | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the full comparison talk (two cities / two things) | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the talk; complete Unit-5 quiz | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — 6 sentences with the card. Stretch — add a superlative "the best… in the world" + a follow-up.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 5):** connected 6–8 sentence comparison with correct forms + a justified preference (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at A2). **Queue:** comparison language recycles into U10.
**Coach notes:** encourage opinions with reasons — this unit is a natural bridge to A2 discussion. Keep it personal and let them disagree politely.
