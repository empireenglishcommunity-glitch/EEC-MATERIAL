> **Spec task:** 5.2 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage1/00-stage1-unit-map.md`

> **Grammar spine:** adverbs of manner (+ *good/well*) + present continuous vs present simple (extend) +

## S1-U9-L01 — Feelings & Personality

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 9 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Stage-0 adjectives; U1 feelings.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can describe feelings and basic personality with adjectives.
- The learner can ask/answer *"How do you feel? / What's he like?"*.
- The learner can describe someone they know.

**Target language:** feelings (happy, sad, nervous, excited, worried, tired, bored); personality (kind, funny,
serious, friendly, shy, hard-working). **Grammar:** *be + adjective*; *What's he/she like?* (personality). **Accent Lab focus:**
stress + intonation matching the feeling; clear adjective endings. **Arabic support:** ~25%.
**Mindset focus:** thinking in English — name your feeling directly in English, without translating.

**Materials:** feelings/personality flashcards; a description clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: U1 feelings + Stage-0/U7 adjectives | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "How do you feel?" "A bit nervous." "What's your brother like?" "He's funny and kind." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Feelings vs personality; *feel + adjective* vs *be + adjective*; *What…like?*; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Let intonation carry the feeling ("I'm so **excited**!"); clear endings | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Pair: ask feelings + describe a person's personality (from cards) | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Describe how you feel + one person you know (3–4 sentences); record one | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check feeling/personality language | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "Today I feel ___. My friend is ___ and ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — flashcards + frames. Stretch — add "because" + a comparison (from U5).
**Success check:** correct feelings + personality description. **Queue:** feelings/personality recycle into L04/L05 & U10.
**Coach notes:** *What's he like?* (personality) vs *How is he?* (state) confuses learners — clarify with clear examples.

## S1-U9-L02 — Adverbs of Manner: "She Speaks English Well"

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 9 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can form and use adverbs of manner (adjective + *-ly*; irregulars *good→well, fast, hard*).
- The learner can say *how* someone does something.
- The learner can talk about how they do daily activities.

**Target language:** adverbs (carefully, quickly, slowly, quietly, badly; well, fast, hard). **Grammar:** adverb
formation + position (after the verb/object); *good* (adj) vs *well* (adv). **Accent Lab focus:** the *-ly* ending; stress
stays on the verb. **Arabic support:** ~25%. **Mindset focus:** you don't need to speak *fast* — speak *clearly*.

**Materials:** adjective↔adverb cards; an activity clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: feelings/personality adjectives (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "She speaks English well and drives carefully." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | adjective + *-ly* → adverb; irregulars (good→well, fast, hard); position; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | *-ly* ending; keep verb stress ("speaks **well**") | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Turn adjectives into adverbs; describe how people do things | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say how you do 3 things (drive, work, speak English…); record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check adverb form + *good/well* | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "I ___ carefully / quickly / well." (3 adverbs) | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — adjective↔adverb card. Stretch — combine with a comparative ("I speak more slowly than…").
**Success check:** correct adverb formation + *good/well* distinction. **Queue:** adverbs recycle into U10.
**Coach notes:** *good* vs *well* ("I speak English good") is a classic — drill "well" as the adverb repeatedly.

## S1-U9-L03 — *Usually* vs *Now*: Present Simple vs Continuous (Extend)

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 9 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Stage-0 U9 (continuous intro).

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can contrast habits (present simple) with actions now (present continuous).
- The learner can use time markers (*usually/every day* vs *now/at the moment/today*).
- The learner can describe what they usually do vs what they're doing these days.

**Target language:** time markers (usually, every day, always vs now, at the moment, this week, today).
**Grammar:** present simple (habit) vs present continuous (now / temporary). **Accent Lab focus:** -ing /ŋ/ (no hard g);
contrast stress on the time marker. **Arabic support:** ~25%. **Mindset focus:** "these days I'm practicing every day" — say your current habit in English.

**Materials:** simple-vs-continuous sort; a "usually vs now" clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: Stage-0 present simple + continuous (U4/U9) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I usually work in an office, but this week I'm working from home." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Habit (simple + usually) vs now/temporary (continuous + now/this week); brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | -ing endings (/ŋ/); stress the contrast marker (**usually** vs right **now**) | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Sort sentences (habit/now); make paired sentences (usually…, but now…) | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say 2 habits + 2 "these days" actions; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check tense choice + -ing endings | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "I usually ___, but these days I'm ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — the sort + time-marker list. Stretch — add a reason + a feeling about the change.
**Success check:** correct simple/continuous contrast with time markers. **Queue:** the contrast recycles into U10.
**Coach notes:** Arabic speakers may overuse the simple for "now" — the time markers are the clearest signal; anchor the choice to them.

## S1-U9-L04 — On the Phone & Small Talk

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 9 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01–L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can manage a simple phone call (ask for someone, leave/take a message).
- The learner can start and hold light small talk (*How's it going? How was your weekend?*).
- The learner can open and close a conversation naturally.

**Target language:** phone phrases (*Can I speak to…? Who's calling? Can I take a message? Hold on*); small talk
(*How's it going? Not bad. What's new? Anyway, I have to go.*). **Grammar:** integration (requests + feelings + past).
**Accent Lab focus:** friendly intonation; natural rhythm of fixed phrases. **Arabic support:** ~20%.
**Mindset focus:** small talk is a skill — a few phrases make you sound natural fast.

**Materials:** a phone-call dialogue clip; small-talk phrase cards; role-play cards; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: feelings + *How was your…?* (U1/L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: a short phone call + a small-talk opening/closing | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Phone stages (open → ask → message → close); small-talk phrases; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Friendly intonation on fixed phrases ("How's it **going**?") | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Pair role-play a phone call + a small-talk chat; swap | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Make a short phone call (ask for someone, leave a message); record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check phone phrases + natural flow | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a short phone-call voice note or a small-talk opener + closer | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — phrase cards + stages. Stretch — handle "they're not here" + leave a detailed message.
**Success check:** a coherent phone/small-talk exchange with correct fixed phrases. **Queue:** functions recycle into U10.
**Coach notes:** fixed phrases are memorize-and-use chunks — prioritize fluent delivery + intonation over analysis.

## S1-U9-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: A 2-Minute Chat About Your Week & Feelings (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 9 · ~35 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can hold a ~2-minute chat: feelings + how things are going + usually-vs-now + a bit of small talk.
- The learner can ask a partner questions and react naturally.
- The learner records their **Unit-9 chat** — the most conversational task yet.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-9 language. **Grammar:** adverbs + simple/continuous + functions consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** overall clarity; -ing endings; feeling/small-talk intonation; each learner's flagged sound.
**Arabic support:** ~20% (light). **Mindset focus:** this is real conversation — you're becoming a *user* of English.

**Materials:** a model 2-minute chat; a checklist card (open / feelings / usually-vs-now / a question + reaction / close);
recording prompt; Unit-9 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: feelings, adverbs, usually vs now, small-talk phrases | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean ~2-minute chat | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist of a good chat | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a flagged sound (-ing / intonation) | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse in pairs; focus on reacting + asking back | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record a ~2-minute chat (with a partner or as a monologue + Q prompts) | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the chat; complete Unit-9 quiz | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — use the checklist + phrase cards. Stretch — sustain 2+ minutes with follow-ups + reactions.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 9):** a coherent ~2-minute chat with feelings + tense contrast + natural functions (rubric: Fluency + Interaction + Pronunciation at A2). **Queue:** chat skills recycle into U10.
**Coach notes:** this is the closest task to real conversation in Stage 1 — reward interaction (asking back, reacting) as much as accuracy.
