# Stage 1 · Unit 1 — The Past (1): Was & Were (full lessons, v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 5.2 · **Built on:** `curriculum/02-lesson-template.md` · **Map:** `stage1/00-stage1-unit-map.md`
> **Arabic support:** ~40% (moderate), fading; core explanations still in Egyptian Arabic where the grammar is new.
> **Unit goal:** by the end, the learner can talk about the past with *to be* — say where they and others
> were, describe past states/feelings, use past time expressions, and ask/answer *Was/Were…?* — recorded.
> **Grammar spine:** *was/were* (affirmative, negative, questions) + *There was/were* · **Accent Lab:**
> /w/ in *was/were*, weak *was* /wəz/, word-stress review · **Mindset:** momentum into a new level.

**Unit lessons:** L01 *I was, you were* + past time expressions · L02 negatives & questions ·
L03 *There was/were* · L04 How was it? (past experiences) · L05 speaking task + review.

**Assets to produce:** short "last weekend" dialogue videos (American models), a *was/were* conjugation
slide, past-time-expression flashcards (yesterday / last… / ago), minimal audio for weak/strong *was*,
a Google Form per lesson, community voice-note prompts.

## S1-U1-L01 — "I Was, You Were" — Past of *To Be* + Time Expressions

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 1 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Stage 0 (present *to be*).

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use *was/were* to say where someone/something was in the past.
- The learner can use past time expressions (*yesterday, last night/week, two days ago*).
- The learner can produce 3–4 true past sentences about themselves.

**Target language:** was/were; yesterday, last night/week/month/year, … ago, this morning; places & feelings
(home, work, school, tired, happy, busy). **Grammar:** *I/he/she/it **was*** · *you/we/they **were*** (affirmative).
**Accent Lab focus:** /w/ in *was/were* (don't drop it); the **weak** *was* /wəz/ inside a sentence.
**Arabic support:** ~40%. **Mindset focus:** momentum — you're at a new level; lock your daily 20-min slot again.

**Materials:** *was/were* conjugation slide; "yesterday vs now" graphic; time-expression flashcards; short model clip.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: present *to be* — "I am at home. She is a teacher." (Stage-0 recall) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: people saying where they were yesterday/last weekend; text + light Arabic gloss | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Present → past: *am/is → was*, *are → were*; brief Arabic explanation; the time expressions | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Drill /w/ (*was/were*); weak *was* in "I was **tired**" — stress the content word | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Substitution: "I was at ___ yesterday." / "They were ___ last night." (swap places/times) | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say 3 true sentences: where you were yesterday, last night, last weekend; record one | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check was/were choice + /w/; note weak-form attempts | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a voice note: "Yesterday I was ___. Last weekend I was ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give the conjugation card + one time word. Stretch — add a feeling + reason ("I was tired because I worked a lot").
**Success check:** correct *was/were* with a past time expression, intelligible. **Queue:** was/were → recycle every warm-up this unit; past time expressions enter queue.
**Coach notes:** the classic error is *was/were* agreement ("we was"). Fix gently now; it recurs — re-drill across the unit.

## S1-U1-L02 — Negatives & Questions: *Wasn't / Weren't / Were you…?*

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 1 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can make past *to be* negative (*wasn't / weren't*).
- The learner can ask past *to be* questions (*Was he…? Were you…?*) and give short answers.
- The learner can hold a short past Q&A with a partner.

**Target language:** wasn't/weren't; *Was I/he/she/it…? Were you/we/they…?*; short answers (*Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.*).
**Grammar:** negative + question forms of *was/were*; inversion for questions. **Accent Lab focus:** contracted
*wasn't/weren't* (clear final /t/); question intonation (rising for yes/no).
**Arabic support:** ~40%. **Mindset focus:** consistency beats a perfect week — just don't skip your slot.

**Materials:** negative/question form slide; short-answer cards; a Q&A dialogue clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: L01 — "Where were you yesterday?" answered by 2–3 learners | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: a short past Q&A ("Were you at the party?" "No, I wasn't, I was at home.") | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Form: *wasn't/weren't*; question inversion; short answers; brief Arabic note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Drill *wasn't/weren't* (final /t/); rising intonation of *"Were you…?"* | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Pair drill: A asks *"Were you at…?"* B gives a short answer + correction | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Mini-interview: ask a partner 3 past questions; record one exchange | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check negative/question forms + short answers | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: ask a classmate a *Was/Were* question in the community channel and answer one | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — provide the short-answer cards. Stretch — add a follow-up question ("Why? Where were you?").
**Success check:** correct question + short answer + one negative, intelligible. **Queue:** question/negative forms recycle into U3 (did/didn't parallels).
**Coach notes:** learners often keep the statement word order in questions ("You were at home?" only). Model the inversion + accept intonation-questions as a stepping stone.

## S1-U1-L03 — *There Was / There Were* — Describing a Past Place

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 1 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can describe what existed in a past place using *there was / there were*.
- The learner can use it in negative and question forms (*there wasn't / were there…?*).
- The learner can describe a place from their past (their old home, a place they visited).

**Target language:** there was/were (+ wasn't/weren't, *Was/Were there…?*); place & object nouns (recycled from Stage-0 U3);
quantity words (a lot of, some). **Grammar:** *there was* (singular) / *there were* (plural). **Accent Lab focus:**
linking *there was* → "there-wuz"; contrast singular/plural clearly. **Arabic support:** ~40%.
**Mindset focus:** design your environment — one small English cue in your room.

**Materials:** two "then vs now" place images; *there was/were* slide; description model clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: was/were + a past time expression (L01–L02) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Model describes a childhood room/place: "There was a small bed. There were two windows." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *there was* (singular) vs *there were* (plural); negative & question; brief Arabic note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Linking + weak form: "there was a…"; keep singular/plural distinct | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Describe an image: 3 sentences with there was/were; swap with a partner | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Describe a real place from your past (old home/school) in 3–4 sentences; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check singular/plural agreement; note weak forms | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post 3 sentences about a place you remember ("There was… / There were…") | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give a sentence frame + word bank. Stretch — add a feeling ("It was small but I was happy there").
**Success check:** correct there was/were agreement across 3 sentences. **Queue:** there was/were → recycle in U8 (travel descriptions) & U10.
**Coach notes:** agreement with plural nouns is the error to watch ("there was two windows"). Re-drill with clearly plural nouns.

## S1-U1-L04 — How Was It? — Talking About a Past Experience

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 1 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can ask and answer *"How was…?"* about a past event.
- The learner can describe a past experience with feelings/opinion adjectives.
- The learner can react naturally (*"Really? That's great / That's a pity."*).

**Target language:** *How was your…?*; opinion adjectives (great, boring, fun, difficult, delicious, tiring);
reactions (Really?, That's great/nice/a pity). **Grammar:** *was/were* + adjective; consolidation.
**Accent Lab focus:** stress & intonation on opinion words ("It was **great**!"); friendly reactions.
**Arabic support:** ~35%. **Mindset focus:** notice a small win from this week and say it out loud.

**Materials:** reaction cards; opinion-adjective visuals; a "How was your weekend?" clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: there was/were + was/were (U1 so far) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "How was your weekend?" "It was great! I was with my family." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *How was/were…?* + adjective; natural reactions; brief Arabic note only if needed | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Emphatic stress on the adjective; reaction intonation ("**Real**ly?") | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Pair: ask "How was your…?", answer with an adjective, react; swap topics | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Talk for ~1 min about a recent experience (was/were + feelings); record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check was/were + adjective; encourage a reaction | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "My weekend was ___ because ___." + react to one classmate's post | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — one adjective + one reaction. Stretch — add "because" + a reason and a follow-up question.
**Success check:** a coherent past-experience mini-answer with opinion + reaction. **Queue:** opinion adjectives + reactions recycle in U5 (comparisons) & U9 (feelings).
**Coach notes:** push for *because* + reason here — it's the bridge to A2 connected speech. Keep reactions warm and authentic.

## S1-U1-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: "Where Were You Last Weekend?" (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 1 · ~35 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can give a connected past account (~5–7 sentences) using *was/were* + *there was/were* + time + feelings.
- The learner can ask a partner about their weekend and react.
- The learner records their **Unit-1 past account** (a milestone showing new past-tense ability).

**Target language:** integrated Unit-1 language. **Grammar:** *was/were* consolidated (all forms).
**Accent Lab focus:** overall clarity — /w/, weak *was*, opinion stress; each learner's flagged weak sound.
**Arabic support:** ~30% (fading). **Mindset focus:** you can now talk about the past — a real level-up from Stage 0.

**Materials:** a model 6-sentence weekend account; a checklist card (where / who with / there was-were / how it was);
recording prompt; Unit-1 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid chain: was/were, question, there was/were, how was it | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean model: "Last weekend I was… There were… It was…" | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break the model into the checklist of a good past account | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills their **one** weakest sound (from U1) | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse the full account with the checklist; peer practice (pairs) | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the full weekend account (5–7 sentences) | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach gives group-level delayed feedback | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the account; complete Unit-1 quiz; note your progress vs Stage 0 | 1 min |

**Differentiation:** Support — 5 sentences with the card. Stretch — add a *because*-reason and a question to a partner.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 1):** connected 5–7 sentence past account, intelligible, correct was/were (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at A2). **Queue:** whole past account recycles into U3 storytelling and U10 review.
**Coach notes:** this is the first "I can talk about my past in English" moment — make it feel big; it pairs well as a mid-journey proof clip.
