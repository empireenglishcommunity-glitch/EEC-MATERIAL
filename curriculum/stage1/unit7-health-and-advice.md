## S1-U7-L01 — Body & Symptoms: "What's the Matter?"

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 7 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Stage-0 body basics.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can name common body parts and describe symptoms.
- The learner can ask and answer *"What's the matter? / What's wrong?"*.
- The learner can say how they feel today.

**Target language:** body parts (head, throat, stomach, back, tooth/teeth); symptoms (a headache, a cold,
a temperature, a sore throat, I feel sick/tired). **Grammar:** *have (got) + symptom*; *I feel + adjective*.
**Accent Lab focus:** /θ/–/ð/ in health words (*throat, teeth, mouth, health*). **Arabic support:** ~30%.
**Mindset focus:** never miss twice — one low day is normal, two starts a slide.

**Materials:** body/symptom flashcards; a "how do you feel?" clip; the /θ/–/ð/ health-words card; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: *have/has* (Stage-0) + feelings adjectives (U1) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "What's the matter?" "I have a headache and a sore throat." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Symptom language (*have a…*, *I feel…*); question forms; brief Arabic note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | /θ/ (throat, teeth, mouth) vs /ð/ (breathe, the); tongue between teeth | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Pair: "What's the matter?" → describe a symptom (from cards); swap | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say how you feel today / a recent illness (2–3 sentences); record one | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check symptom language + /θ/–/ð/ | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "Today I feel ___." or "Last week I had ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — flashcards + frame "I have a ___." Stretch — add duration ("for two days") + a feeling.
**Success check:** correct symptom description + intelligible /θ/–/ð/. **Queue:** symptoms + th-words recycle this unit.
**Coach notes:** th-sounds are a Stage-0/Stage-1 recurring target — health vocab is a natural, meaningful place to re-drill them.

## S1-U7-L02 — Giving Advice: *should / shouldn't*

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 7 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can give advice with *should/shouldn't + base verb*.
- The learner can ask for advice (*What should I do?*).
- The learner can respond to a problem with suitable advice.

**Target language:** *should/shouldn't*; advice verbs (rest, drink water, see a doctor, take medicine, sleep).
**Grammar:** *should/shouldn't + base verb* (all persons). **Accent Lab focus:** stress on the advice verb;
clear *shouldn't* (/ʃʊdnt/). **Arabic support:** ~30%. **Mindset focus:** what should YOU do daily? Advise yourself.

**Materials:** problem-advice cards; an advice dialogue clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: symptoms (L01) — "What's the matter?" | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I have a cold." "You should rest and drink water. You shouldn't go out." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *should/shouldn't + base verb*; *What should I do?*; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Stress the verb ("You should **rest**"); clear *shouldn't* | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Problem cards → give 2 pieces of advice each; swap | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | A partner states a problem; give advice (2–3 sentences); record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check *should + base verb* | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post advice for a common problem ("If you're tired, you should…") | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — advice-verb bank + frame. Stretch — add a reason + a *shouldn't*.
**Success check:** appropriate advice with correct *should/shouldn't*. **Queue:** should/advice recycles into U9 (small talk) & U10.
**Coach notes:** learners sometimes add *to* ("you should to rest"). Drill "should + base verb, no to" — mirrors the modal pattern.

## S1-U7-L03 — Obligation & Necessity: *have to / don't have to / must*

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 7 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can express obligation/necessity with *have to / must*.
- The learner can express no obligation with *don't have to*.
- The learner can talk about rules and necessities in their life/work.

**Target language:** *have to / has to / don't have to / must*; obligation contexts (work, health, rules).
**Grammar:** *have to + base verb* (necessity/external rule); *must* (strong necessity); *don't have to* (not necessary).
**Accent Lab focus:** *have to* → /hæftə/ (receptive reduction); stress. **Arabic support:** ~30%.
**Mindset focus:** the difference between "I have to" and "I choose to" — own your practice.

**Materials:** obligation cards (work/health rules); a short clip; a "must vs don't have to" sort; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: advice (should) from L02 | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I have to take medicine twice a day. You don't have to come with me." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *have to/must* (necessary) vs *don't have to* (not necessary ≠ shouldn't); brief Arabic note | 7 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | *have to* → "hafta" (hear it); stress the main verb | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Sort statements (necessary / not necessary); make rules for a place | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say 3 things you have to / don't have to do this week; record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check *have to* vs *don't have to* meaning | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "At work/home I have to ___, but I don't have to ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give the sort + frames. Stretch — contrast *must* (strong) vs *should* (advice).
**Success check:** correct obligation vs no-obligation meaning. **Queue:** have to/must recycle into U8 (travel rules) & U10.
**Coach notes:** the trap: *don't have to* (not necessary) vs *mustn't/shouldn't* (prohibited/bad idea). Clarify with clear, real examples.

## S1-U7-L04 — Health Habits & a Visit to the Doctor/Pharmacy

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 7 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can talk about healthy/unhealthy habits.
- The learner can handle a simple doctor/pharmacy exchange (describe symptom → get advice/medicine).
- The learner can combine symptoms + advice + obligation.

**Target language:** health-habit language (exercise, sleep well, eat healthily); pharmacy/doctor phrases.
**Grammar:** integration of symptoms + should + have to. **Accent Lab focus:** connected clarity in the exchange;
th-words. **Arabic support:** ~25%. **Mindset focus:** health = the base of consistency; protect your energy.

**Materials:** doctor–patient role-play card; a pharmacy dialogue clip; habit visuals; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: symptoms + should + have to (L01–L03) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: patient describes a symptom; pharmacist advises + gives instructions | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | The exchange stages (symptom → advice → instruction/obligation); brief note | 5 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clear th-words + advice stress inside the exchange | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Pair role-play (patient/pharmacist) from the card; swap | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Play the patient: describe a symptom, take the advice; record | 4 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check the exchange flow + target grammar | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: describe a symptom + the advice you'd expect | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — use the stages card. Stretch — add a follow-up question ("How often should I take it?").
**Success check:** a coherent health exchange using symptoms + advice + obligation. **Queue:** the exchange recycles into U10.
**Coach notes:** this practical role-play integrates the unit's grammar — prioritize a smooth, communicative exchange over perfect forms.

## S1-U7-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: Give a Friend Health Advice (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 7 · ~35 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can respond to a friend's problem with connected advice (6–8 sentences): symptoms understood + *should* + *have to*.
- The learner can ask about the problem and react with empathy.
- The learner records their **Unit-7 advice talk**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-7 language. **Grammar:** should + have to/must consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** overall clarity; /θ/–/ð/ health words; advice stress; each learner's flagged sound.
**Arabic support:** ~25% (fading). **Mindset focus:** giving advice in English = a real, useful social skill unlocked.

**Materials:** a model advice talk; a checklist card (ask about problem / 2+ pieces of advice / 1 have-to / empathy);
recording prompt; Unit-7 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: symptoms, should/shouldn't, have to/must | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean advice talk (6–8 sentences) | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist of good advice-giving | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a flagged sound (th-words / advice stress) | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse with the checklist; peer practice (one plays the friend) | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the full advice talk to a friend's problem | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the advice talk; complete Unit-7 quiz | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — 6 sentences with the card. Stretch — add *must* vs *should* nuance + a follow-up.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 7):** connected 6–8 sentence advice with correct should/have to (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at A2). **Queue:** advice language recycles into U10.
**Coach notes:** empathy + advice is deeply communicative — reward warmth and natural reactions, not just grammar.
