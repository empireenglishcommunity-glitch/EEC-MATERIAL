## S1-U6-L01 — Countable vs Uncountable; *some / any*

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: Stage-0 food (U6).

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can classify common foods as countable or uncountable.
- The learner can use *some* (positive) and *any* (negative/question) correctly.
- The learner can say what there is/isn't in their kitchen.

**Target language:** food (rice, bread, water, milk, sugar, eggs, apples, tomatoes); *some/any*.
**Grammar:** countable (an apple / apples) vs uncountable (rice, water — no plural); *some* in +, *any* in −/?.
**Accent Lab focus:** clusters in food words (*bread, fruit, vegetables*); no vowel insertion. **Arabic support:** ~35%.
**Mindset focus:** real-world reps — name your fridge contents in English tonight.

**Materials:** countable/uncountable sort; food flashcards; a kitchen image; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: Stage-0 food words + "I like…" | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "There's some rice and some milk, but there aren't any eggs." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Countable (plural OK) vs uncountable (no plural); *some* vs *any*; brief Arabic note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clusters in food words; keep them clean (no "bi-read") | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Sort foods; make "There's some…/ There isn't any…" sentences | 6 min |
| 6 | Free output | Say what's in your kitchen (some/any, 3–4 sentences); record one | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check some/any + countable/uncountable | 1 min |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "In my kitchen there's some ___ but there isn't any ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give the sort + frames. Stretch — add a question ("Is there any…?") to a partner.
**Success check:** correct some/any + countable/uncountable in 3 sentences. **Queue:** some/any recycles every warm-up this unit.
**Coach notes:** the common error is pluralizing uncountables ("breads", "rices"). Re-drill the "no plural" list; treat it as vocabulary to memorize.

## S1-U6-L02 — *much / many / a lot of / a few / a little*

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L01.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can use *many/a few* with countables and *much/a little* with uncountables.
- The learner can use *a lot of* with both.
- The learner can talk about quantities in their diet/day.

**Target language:** *much, many, a lot of, a few, a little*; food + daily-habit contexts. **Grammar:** quantifier +
countable/uncountable matching. **Accent Lab focus:** weak *of* /əv/ in *a lot of*; rhythm. **Arabic support:** ~30%.
**Mindset focus:** a little every day > a lot once — apply it to practice AND habits.

**Materials:** quantifier matching card; quantity visuals; a "my diet" clip; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: some/any + countable/uncountable (L01) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: "I drink a lot of water, a little coffee, and I eat a few eggs." | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *many/a few* (countable) vs *much/a little* (uncountable); *a lot of* (both); brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Weak *of* in "a lot **of**"; smooth rhythm | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Match quantifiers to nouns; describe quantity visuals | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Describe your daily food/drink amounts (3–4 sentences); record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check quantifier–noun matching | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post: "I eat a few ___ and drink a lot of ___." | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — matching card. Stretch — add *too much/too many* (link to U5 *too*).
**Success check:** correct quantifier–noun matching in 3 sentences. **Queue:** quantifiers recycle into U7 (health habits) & U10.
**Coach notes:** *much* in positive statements is rare in natural English ("a lot of" is preferred) — teach *much* mainly for questions/negatives.

## S1-U6-L03 — *How Much? / How Many?* + Quantities & Containers

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L02.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can ask *How much…?* (uncountable/price) and *How many…?* (countable).
- The learner can use containers/units (a bottle of, a cup of, a piece of, a kilo of).
- The learner can ask about and state prices clearly.

**Target language:** *How much/How many*; containers (bottle, cup, glass, piece, kilo, packet); prices (recycled).
**Grammar:** *How much + uncountable/price* vs *How many + countable*. **Accent Lab focus:** clear numbers/prices; question intonation.
**Arabic support:** ~30%. **Mindset focus:** use it in the wild — ask a price in English this week.

**Materials:** container flashcards; a market dialogue; price cards; Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: quantifiers (L02) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Market clip: "How much is the rice?" "How many apples do you want?" | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | *How much* (uncountable/price) vs *How many* (countable); containers; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Clear prices/numbers; rising then falling on the question | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Ask how much/how many about market items; answer with containers + prices | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Role-play a quick market exchange (ask 2 prices/quantities); record | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check much/many choice + clear prices | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a short market Q&A (how much / how many) | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — provide question frames + container cards. Stretch — buy 3 items, total the price.
**Success check:** correct how much/how many + a clear price. **Queue:** how much/many recycles into U8 (tickets) & U10.
**Coach notes:** prices + numbers are a Stage-0 recycle — a good place to re-check number clarity under light pressure.

## S1-U6-L04 — At the Restaurant: Ordering Politely

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 6 · ~30 min · recorded + live · prerequisites: L03.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can order food/drink politely (*Could I have…? / I'll have…*).
- The learner can ask about the menu and make simple requests.
- The learner can handle a basic waiter–customer exchange.

**Target language:** ordering language (*Could I have…, I'll have…, Can I see the menu?, The bill, please*); menu items.
**Grammar:** *Could I…?* (link to U8 requests) + *I'll* (link to U4 will). **Accent Lab focus:** polite rising intonation;
clusters in menu words. **Arabic support:** ~30%. **Mindset focus:** confidence — ordering in English is a real, doable win.

**Materials:** a menu prop; an ordering dialogue clip; role-play cards (waiter/customer); Google Form.

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall | Retrieval: how much/how many + quantities (L03) | 4 min |
| 2 | Input | Clip: full ordering exchange (greet → order → request → bill) | 6 min |
| 3 | Notice | Polite forms (*Could I have… / I'll have…*); the exchange stages; brief note | 6 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab | Polite rising intonation ("Could I have the chicken, please?") | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Pair role-play from the menu; swap waiter/customer | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output | Order a full small meal politely; record the customer turns | 3 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Check politeness + clarity | — |
| 8 | Assignment | Post a short ordering voice note (order + one request + bill) | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — give the exchange stages + frames. Stretch — handle a change ("Sorry, no onions, please").
**Success check:** a polite, intelligible order with a request. **Queue:** *Could I…?* + *I'll* recycle into U8 & U10.
**Coach notes:** politeness = intonation + form. Egyptians can sound abrupt if intonation stays flat — model the friendly rise.

## S1-U6-L05 — Unit Speaking Task: Role-Play Ordering a Meal (+ Review)

**Metadata:** Stage 1 / A2 · Unit 6 · ~35 min · recorded + live (assessment-style) · prerequisites: L01–L04.

**Objectives (can-do):**
- The learner can complete a full restaurant role-play (greet → ask → order → request → pay), politely and clearly.
- The learner can talk about quantities and preferences within it.
- The learner records their **Unit-6 ordering role-play**.

**Target language:** integrated Unit-6 language. **Grammar:** quantity + ordering consolidated.
**Accent Lab focus:** polite intonation; clear prices/numbers; food-word clusters; each learner's flagged sound.
**Arabic support:** ~25% (fading). **Mindset focus:** you can now handle a real-life situation start to finish.

**Materials:** a model restaurant role-play; a checklist card (greet / ask about menu / order politely / a quantity or request / pay);
recording prompt; Unit-6 quiz (Google Form).

**Lesson flow:**

| # | Phase | Content | ~Time |
|--|--|--|--|
| 1 | Warm-up / recall (retrieval-heavy) | Rapid: some/any, quantifiers, how much/many, ordering phrases | 5 min |
| 2 | Input/model | Watch a clean restaurant role-play | 4 min |
| 3 | Notice | Break it into the checklist of a good ordering exchange | 4 min |
| 4 | Accent Lab clinic | Each learner drills a flagged sound (a food cluster / polite rise) | 4 min |
| 5 | Guided output | Rehearse the role-play in pairs; swap roles | 7 min |
| 6 | Free output (task) | Record the full customer side of the role-play | 5 min |
| 7 | Feedback | Self + peer check vs checklist; coach group-level feedback | 2 min |
| 8 | Assignment/queue | Post the role-play; complete Unit-6 quiz | — |

**Differentiation:** Support — use the stages card. Stretch — improvise a problem (wrong dish) and resolve it politely.
**Success check (formative summary of Unit 6):** a complete, polite, intelligible ordering exchange with correct quantity language (rubric: Fluency + Accuracy + Pronunciation at A2). **Queue:** ordering/quantity recycles into U10.
**Coach notes:** this is a highly practical unit — encourage learners to actually use it in a real café for extra reps and a confidence boost.
