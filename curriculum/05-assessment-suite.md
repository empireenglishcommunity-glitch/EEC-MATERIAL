# EEC Assessment Suite (v1.0)

> **Spec task:** 1.6 · **Requirement:** R3 (Assessment & Progress Tracking) · **Design:** §4
> **Reads with:** `curriculum/01-cefr-roadmap.md`, `methodology/01-eec-methodology-handbook.md` (§16),
> `curriculum/03-accent-lab-syllabus.md` (§7), `operations/02-cohort-tracker.md`.
> **Purpose:** The measurement backbone that makes EEC's promise provable. It defines the placement test,
> formative checks, the summative assessment, the **5-dimension CEFR speaking rubric** (the heart of the
> suite), the A1 pass thresholds, and how results are recorded to produce the before/after transformation
> proof. If teaching is speaking-first, assessment is speaking-weighted — the two are one system.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Speaking-weighted.** The primary evidence is a spoken performance, scored on the rubric — because
   speaking is the outcome we promise and the hardest thing for Arabic learners.
2. **CEFR-aligned.** Every result maps to a CEFR level so progress is objective and universal.
3. **Low-anxiety.** Assessment is framed as a friendly conversation, not a trap (protects the affective
   filter → better performance and retention).
4. **Evidence-generating.** Every learner produces a **Day-1** and a **graduation** speaking sample on the
   *same prompt* — the paired clip is both a fair measure and the strongest marketing proof.
5. **Formative ≠ gatekeeping.** In-unit checks give feedback and drive review; the summative certifies the level.
6. **Honest (GC-5).** Pronunciation is scored on **intelligibility + stage targets**, never "how native."

---

## 2. The four assessment types (overview)

| Type | When | Form | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Placement** | Before Unit 0 | Short adaptive quiz + 2–3 min speaking sample | Assign starting level; capture Day-1 baseline |
| **Formative** | End of each unit | Quiz (Google Form) + short speaking task | Feedback + spaced review; flag weak points |
| **Summative** | End of each stage | Mixed quiz + full level speaking task | Certify the CEFR level (e.g., A1 at Stage 0 exit) |
| **Exam Track** | Upper levels (opt.) | TOEFL/IELTS-style mock + scoring | Certification prep for those who need it |

## 3. Placement test

**Goal:** put a new learner at the right stage and record their baseline.

**Part A — Adaptive recognition quiz (~10–15 min, Google Form or platform quiz).**
- Grammar/vocab items spanning A1→B1, arranged by difficulty; stops escalating when the learner starts missing.
- **Score bands → provisional level:**
  - 0–30% → **Pre-A1 / A1** (start Stage 0 from Unit 0)
  - 31–55% → **high A1 / A2** (Stage 0 fast-track or Stage 1 when built)
  - 56–75% → **A2 / B1**
  - 76%+ → **B1+** (upper stages / exam track)

**Part B — Speaking sample (2–3 min) = the Day-1 baseline.**
- **Standard prompt (fixed, reused at graduation):**
  > *"Tell me about yourself, your family, and your typical day."* (with easier fallback prompts for true
  > beginners: *"What's your name? Where are you from? What do you do?"*)
- Scored on the 5-dimension rubric (§4). This is the **before** clip stored in the Cohort Tracker.

**Determination rule:** the speaking sample **overrides** the quiz when they disagree (a learner may
recognize grammar but not speak it — we place on productive ability, not recognition). Round **down** if
between levels, so learners get early wins.

## 4. The 5-Dimension CEFR Speaking Rubric ⭐ (heart of the suite)

Each dimension scored **0–5**. Judged against a **level-appropriate task** (an A1 learner is scored on an
A1 task, etc.). The general descriptors below define the 0–5 quality scale.

### 4.1 Dimension descriptors (0–5)

**① Fluency** — flow, pace, hesitation.
- **0** No connected speech. **1** Isolated words, long silences. **2** Very short phrases, frequent long
  pauses. **3** Simple connected sentences; noticeable but manageable hesitation. **4** Speaks with
  reasonable flow on familiar topics; pauses to plan, not to survive. **5** Smooth, natural pace; hesitation
  only as a native might.

**② Accuracy** — grammar & word choice.
- **0** No control. **1** Errors in almost every utterance; meaning often lost. **2** Frequent basic errors;
  meaning usually clear on simple content. **3** Basic structures mostly correct; errors don't block meaning.
  **4** Good control of a range of structures; occasional slips, self-corrects. **5** Consistently accurate;
  errors rare and minor.

**③ Pronunciation / Accent** — intelligibility + stage targets + prosody (Accent Lab §7).
- **0** Unintelligible. **1** Very hard to understand; heavy L1 interference. **2** Understandable with
  effort; many target-sound errors (e.g., /p/–/b/, clusters). **3** Generally intelligible; some target
  sounds inconsistent; flat rhythm. **4** Clearly intelligible; most target sounds accurate; developing
  natural stress/rhythm. **5** Clear, confident, neutral American-leaning delivery; strong prosody.

**④ Range** — vocabulary & structure variety.
- **0** None. **1** A few isolated words. **2** Very limited; basic memorized phrases. **3** Enough for
  familiar, concrete topics. **4** Good range; some flexibility and paraphrase. **5** Wide range used
  flexibly and appropriately.

**⑤ Interaction** — turn-taking, responding, sustaining a conversation.
- **0** Cannot interact. **1** Responds only to very simple, slow prompts. **2** Manages very short,
  predictable exchanges; needs repetition. **3** Asks/answers simple questions; keeps a short conversation
  going with some help (uses survival phrases). **4** Initiates and responds naturally on familiar topics;
  handles follow-ups. **5** Interacts fluently and spontaneously; manages the conversation comfortably.

### 4.2 Score-to-CEFR mapping (guidance)

The rubric describes *quality on a level-appropriate task*. As a cross-check, the **average across the 5
dimensions on a task pitched at that level** maps roughly:

| Avg (level-appropriate task) | Indicative CEFR performance |
|---|---|
| ~1.0–1.9 | Pre-A1 |
| ~2.0–2.9 | emerging at the target level |
| **~3.0–3.9** | **solid at the target level (pass)** |
| ~4.0–4.5 | strong / ready for the next level |
| ~4.6–5.0 | exceeds; clearly into the next level |

*(Because the task is level-referenced, a "3+" on an A1 task = solid A1; a "3+" on a B2 task = solid B2.)*

## 5. Stage-0 (A1) pass thresholds

To be **certified A1** (Stage-0 completion) on the A1 speaking task (the Unit-10 conversation):
- **Average ≥ 3.0** across the 5 dimensions, **AND**
- **No single dimension below 2**, **AND**
- The learner demonstrates the **A1 exit "can-do" criteria** (roadmap §3): self-intro, family, routine,
  free time, a functional exchange (order/directions), and describing what's happening now.

**If not yet met:** the learner isn't "failed" — they get targeted feedback, extra practice on the weak
dimension(s), and a reassessment. (Consistency + support, not gatekeeping — Mindset track.)

---

## 6. Formative checks (per unit)

Already embedded in each Stage-0 unit ("end-of-unit check"). Standard shape:
- **Quiz (Google Form):** recognition/production of the unit's target grammar + vocab.
- **Speaking task:** the unit's L05 milestone recording, scored *lightly* on the rubric (focus on the
  unit's target dimensions).
- **Function:** feedback + drive spaced review + **flag weak sounds/structures** in the Cohort Tracker
  (feeds the Accent Clinic and re-drilling). Not used to block progress.

---

## 7. Summative assessment (per stage)

**Stage-0 summative (Unit 10, L05):**
1. **Mixed recognition quiz** covering all Stage-0 grammar/vocab.
2. **Full A1 speaking task** (5–8 min conversation): greet → self-intro → family → routine → free time →
   a functional exchange → describe a scene now → close. Scored on the full rubric.
3. **Graduation "after" sample** on the **standard prompt** (identical to Day-1) → the before/after pair.

**Result:** CEFR (A1) determination per §5, recorded in the Cohort Tracker, certificate issued.

*(Later stages follow the same pattern with level-appropriate tasks; B2's summative = the flagship 20–30
min everyday+professional conversation.)*

---

## 8. Exam Track (upper levels, optional)

For learners who need certification (Stage 4 / C1):
- **TOEFL iBT** (academic/US) and **IELTS** (migration/UK) — format familiarization, section strategies,
  timed practice, and **full mock tests** scored to band/score estimates.
- Offered as a specialized add-on, not the core promise.

---

## 9. Scoring workflow & recording

1. Coach scores the speaking task live (or from the recording) on a **rubric sheet** (5 dimensions, 0–5,
   + a one-line note per dimension).
2. Enter into the **Cohort Tracker** (Task 0.5): `placement_level`, `placement_score`, `day1_speaking_score`,
   `final_speaking_score`, `end_level`, `cefr_gain`, `hit_b2_benchmark`, sample links, `consent_to_use`.
3. Give the learner **warm, specific feedback**: 2 strengths + 1 focus (never a wall of corrections).
4. With consent, prepare the **paired Day-1 ↔ graduation clip** for the proof flywheel.

**Inter-rater consistency (as EEC scales):** when more than one coach scores, calibrate periodically on
shared sample recordings so scores mean the same thing across coaches.

## 10. Assets to produce

- **Placement quiz** (adaptive item bank A1→B1) + the fixed **speaking prompt** (+ beginner fallback).
- The **rubric sheet** (printable/Google Form) with the 0–5 descriptors for all 5 dimensions.
- **Per-unit formative quizzes** (Google Forms) — one per Stage-0 unit.
- The **Stage-0 summative** quiz + speaking task script + the graduation prompt.
- A **certificate of completion** template (brand-compliant).
- A short **"how we assess"** explainer for learners (sets expectations; lowers anxiety).
