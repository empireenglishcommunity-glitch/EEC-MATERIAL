# "I'll…" — Decisions, Offers & Predictions with *will*

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 4 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit4-future-plans.md` · **S1-U4-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 4 · Feedback —.
> **Objectives:** use *will* for decisions, offers and predictions · use the contraction *'ll* · use *won't*.
> **Teach *will* by FUNCTION, not as "the future tense".** Learners who meet it as a tense confuse it with *going to* forever. Learners who meet it as *deciding now · offering · predicting* keep the two apart naturally — which is what L04 then formalises.
> **Support:** the three function cards. **Stretch:** predict something about next year and justify it.
> **Arabic ~30%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
*will* is the most **social** future in English. It is what you say when you decide in the moment, when you
offer to help someone, and when you guess about what is coming.

> يعني دي اللي بتستخدمها في المواقف الحياتية السريعة: في مطعم، في محل، ولما حد يحتاج مساعدة.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Three short moments (📹 in the portal).

> **1 — deciding now**
> *Waiter:* Are you ready to order?
> *Customer:* Yes — I **'ll have** the chicken, please.
>
> **2 — offering**
> *A:* These bags are heavy.
> *B:* Don't worry, I **'ll help** you.
>
> **3 — predicting**
> *A:* Do you think it **'ll rain** tomorrow?
> *B:* No, I think it **'ll be** sunny. But it **won't be** hot.

**Notice:** in none of these had the speaker planned anything in advance. They decided, offered, or guessed
**as they spoke**.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| I'll have the chicken. | هاخد الفراخ |
| I'll help you. | هساعدك |
| I think it'll rain. | أظن هتمطر |
| It won't be hot. | مش هتكون حرارة |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play each moment separately and name its function before moving on. Three clean examples beat
> one long dialogue here.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Form is easy: *will* + simple verb, and it never changes for the person.**

| | |
|---|---|
| I / you / he / she / we / they | **will** go · **'ll** go |
| negative | **won't** go |

> ولا حتى حرف واحد بيتغير حسب الفاعل. أسهل من **going to** بكتير من ناحية الشكل.

**The three jobs of *will*:**

| Job | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision** | you decide *now*, while speaking | *I'll take this one.* |
| **Offer / promise** | you offer help | *I'll call you later.* |
| **Prediction** | you guess about the future | *I think it'll be fine.* |

**Contractions are the normal form.** In speech, *I will* is almost always **I'll**:

- I'll · you'll · he'll · she'll · we'll · they'll · **won't**

> ⚠️ **خلي بالك:** *won't* دي اختصار *will not*، ومش ليها علاقة بكلمة *want*. النطق مختلف تمامًا.

**With predictions, add *I think*:** *I think it'll rain.* It softens the guess and sounds natural.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Form first — it is trivially easy and gives them a quick win. Then the three functions with an
> oral example each. Do not contrast with *going to* yet; that is L04's whole job.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Target 1 — the contraction *'ll* is tiny. Do not skip it.**
It is a single /l/ sound joined to the pronoun:

> *I'll* = "ail" · *he'll* = "heel" · *we'll* = "weel" · *they'll* = "thail"

Learners often drop it entirely and say *"I go"* — which sounds like the present.

**Target 2 — *won't* has a long /oʊ/.**

> *won't* = "wohnt" — long, like *don't*. It is **not** *want* /wɒnt/.

> الفرق بين *won't* و*want* بيغيّر المعنى تمامًا، فخد بالك من حرف العلة.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I'll call you tonight, but I won't be late."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Drill *'ll* attached to each pronoun, then the *won't/want* contrast — that pair causes real
> misunderstandings.
