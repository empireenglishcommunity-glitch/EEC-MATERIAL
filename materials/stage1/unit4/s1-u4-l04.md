# *Going To* vs *Will* — Choosing Which

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 4 · **Lesson 4**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L03
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit4-future-plans.md` · **S1-U4-L04***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 4 · Feedback —.
> **Objectives:** choose between the two forms · switch between them in one conversation · justify the choice.
> **The distinction is about WHEN THE DECISION HAPPENED, not about time.** *going to* = decided before you spoke. *will* = decided as you spoke. Every other explanation of this pair leaks; this one holds.
> **Do not chase perfection.** Native speakers overlap them constantly and a wrong choice is almost never misunderstood. Reward correct *meaning* over correct form.
> **Support:** the two-card sort. **Stretch:** a dialogue that uses both naturally.
> **Arabic ~30%.**

- Choose **going to** or **will** and know why.

> الفرق بينهم مش في الوقت — الاتنين عن المستقبل. الفرق في **القرار اتخد امتى**.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
This is the one A2 choice that makes you sound like you are thinking in English rather than translating.
And it is far simpler than most books make it.

And if you pick the wrong one, you will almost never be misunderstood — so speak comfortably and let the
accuracy arrive with time.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Listen for the moment the decision happens (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** What are you doing tonight?
> **B:** I **'m going to study** — I have an exam on Sunday.
> **A:** Oh, I didn't know. Do you want some help?
> **B:** Really? Then I **'ll come** to your place at seven.
> **A:** Perfect. I **'ll make** some coffee.

**Notice the shift.** *studying* was **already planned**, so B used *going to*. Coming at seven and making
coffee were **decided in that moment**, so both used *will*.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| I'm going to study. | كنت ناوي أدرس |
| Then I'll come at seven. | يبقى هاجي سبعة |
| I'll make some coffee. | هعمل قهوة |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second time, stop after each future form and ask: *had they decided this before
> the conversation started?* That single question is the whole lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**One question decides it: had you already decided before you started speaking?**

| | **going to** | **will** |
|---|---|---|
| The decision | made **before** now | made **right now** |
| Feels like | a plan | a reaction |
| Example | *I'm going to travel in June.* | *I'll take this one.* |

> اسأل نفسك: القرار كان موجود قبل ما أفتح بقي؟ لو أيوه، استخدم **going to**. لو لأ، وقررت في اللحظة،
> استخدم **will**. سؤال واحد بيحل الموضوع كله، ومش محتاج تحفظ قوائم.

**The classic pair:**

- *(phone rings)* **I'll get it!** → decided this second
- *I'm going to call the bank tomorrow.* → decided earlier, it is on your list

**Two more places each one owns:**

| Use **going to** for | Use **will** for |
|---|---|
| arrangements — *We're going to get married in June* | offers — *I'll help you* |
| evidence you can see — *Look at those clouds, it's going to rain* | promises — *I won't tell anyone* |

> ⚠️ **حالة تستاهل تتحفظ:** لما تشوف دليل بعينك — سحاب أسود، حد بيقع — بنستخدم **going to**، مش
> **will**. لأن الدليل موجود قبل ما تتكلم.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Lead with the single question, then the classic *I'll get it* against *I'm going to call*.
> Add the evidence case last — it is the one exception learners find genuinely surprising.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Switching between the two changes your rhythm**, and that is worth practising as a pair:

> *going to* is **long** — three beats melted into "gonna".
> *'ll* is **short** — one sound clipped onto the pronoun.

> I'm **gonna** study tonight, so I**'ll** call you tomorrow.

Say that sentence until the long form and the short form sit comfortably beside each other. In real speech
you will switch between them constantly.

> جرّب الجملة دي كتير — فيها الشكلين ورا بعض، وده اللي بيحصل في الكلام الطبيعي.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** the mixed sentence above.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** One mixed sentence, many repetitions. Switching is the skill; either form alone is already
> secure.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — going to or will?**
1. *(the doorbell rings)* — I ______ get it!
2. I've bought the tickets. We ______ leave on Friday.
3. Look at the sky — it ______ rain.
4. *"I'm tired."* — Sit down, I ______ finish the work.
5. She ______ start university in September.

**Exercise 2 — why?** For each answer above, write *decided before* or *decided now*.

**Exercise 3 — respond naturally.**
1. *"This bag is so heavy."* → __________________________
2. *"What are your plans for the summer?"* → __________________________

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** 1) 'll · 2) 're going to · 3) 's going to · 4) 'll · 5) 's going to.
> **Ex2:** now · before · before *(evidence)* · now · before. **Ex3:** an offer with *will*, a plan with
> *going to*.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- One question: **had you decided before you spoke?**
- Yes → **going to** · No → **will**.
- Evidence you can see → **going to**: *It's going to rain.*
- Offers and promises → **will**: *I'll help.*
- A wrong choice is almost never misunderstood — keep talking.
