> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تتكلم عن خططك، وتسأل غيرك، وتقرر وتتوقع.

**The stage pivots here.** Units 1 to 3 were all past. This unit turns you around to face forward — and it
does it with a structure you already own: *am · is · are* from Stage 0, with *going to* bolted on.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | I'm going to… | plans and intentions · future time expressions · hearing *gonna* |
| **L02** | Asking about plans | inversion, *wh-* questions, short answers · *What about you?* |
| **L03** | I'll… | *will* for **deciding now · offering · predicting** · *'ll* and *won't* |
| **L04** | *going to* vs *will* | one question decides it: **had you decided before you spoke?** |
| **L05** | My plans for next month (milestone) 🎙️ | 6–8 sentences with both forms + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** *be going to* (statements, questions, negatives) · *will / 'll / won't* · choosing between
them.
**Accent Lab:** linking *going to* → *gonna* (recognise it) · the contraction *'ll* · *won't* against *want*
· the two question tunes.
**Mindset:** goal-setting — one concrete A2 target, said out loud to a real person.
**Arabic support:** ~35% at L01, fading to ~25% by L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-4 plans recording** — the first time you talk about a future you intend to make happen.
- The **past and the future**, both available to you, four units into the stage.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~35 min, the rest ~30) plus this wrapper.
> **Teach *will* by function, not as "the future tense" (L03).** Learners who meet it as a tense confuse it
> with *going to* permanently. Learners who meet it as *deciding now · offering · predicting* keep them
> apart naturally, which is what L04 then formalises.
> **The L04 rule that actually holds:** the difference is **when the decision happened**, not when the
> action happens. Decided before you spoke → *going to*. Decided as you spoke → *will*. Every other framing
> of this pair leaks somewhere.
> **Do not chase perfection on the contrast.** Native speakers overlap the two constantly, and a wrong
> choice is almost never misunderstood. Reward correct meaning over correct form, or you will make learners
> hesitant about the most useful tense they have.
> **Two pronunciation items with real consequences:** a swallowed *'ll* makes a future sound like a present,
> and *won't* mistaken for *want* reverses the meaning.
