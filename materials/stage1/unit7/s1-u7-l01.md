# Body & Symptoms: "What's the Matter?"

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 7 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 6
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit7-health-and-advice.md` · **S1-U7-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 3 · Feedback 1.
> **Objectives:** name body parts · describe symptoms with *have* · use *I feel* + adjective.
> **The pattern split is the lesson:** English uses **have** for a symptom you possess and **feel** for a state. *I have a headache* against *I feel tired*. Learners who blur them produce *"I have tired"* and *"I feel headache"*.
> **This is also the /θ/–/ð/ consolidation point.** Health vocabulary is unusually thick with those sounds — *throat, teeth, mouth, health, breathe* — which is why the syllabus puts them here.
> **Support:** the body diagram. **Stretch:** describe a symptom you actually had recently.
> **Arabic ~30%.**

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Name the parts of the body you might need to talk about.
- Say what is wrong: *I have a headache.*
- Say how you feel: *I feel tired.* 🎙️

> لو مرضت بره، الجمل دي هي اللي بتوصل مشكلتك. مش رفاهية.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Being unable to say what hurts is one of the few moments where missing vocabulary is genuinely serious. It
is also, at A2, a small and closed set of words — so it is quick to fix.

> وفي الوحدة دي بنرجع لصوتين مهمين: **/θ/** و**/ð/**. كلمات الصحة مليانة بيهم — *throat, teeth, mouth,
> health* — فدي أحسن فرصة تظبطهم فيها. وكلها كلمات معدودة.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A phone call to a friend (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** You don't sound well. **What's the matter**?
> **B:** I **have** a bad **cold**. My **throat** hurts and I **have** a **headache**.
> **A:** Oh no. **Do you have** a **temperature**?
> **B:** A little. I just **feel** very **tired** and **weak**.
> **A:** You **should** rest. Don't go to work tomorrow.

**Notice the two patterns:** *I **have** a headache* — a thing you possess. *I **feel** tired* — a state you
are in.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| What's the matter? | مالك؟ |
| I have a cold. | أنا مبرد |
| My throat hurts. | زوري بيوجعني |
| I feel tired. | حاسس بتعب |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second time learners sort what they hear into *have* sentences and *feel*
> sentences. That sort is the grammar of the lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Three patterns cover almost everything.**

**1) *have* + a symptom** — for a thing you have got:

> *I **have** a headache · a cold · a temperature · a sore throat · a stomach ache*

**2) *feel* + an adjective** — for a state:

> *I **feel** tired · sick · dizzy · weak · better*

**3) *… hurts*** — for a specific part:

> *My **back hurts**. · My **tooth hurts**.*

> ولاحظ إن الفعل هنا بيتغير على حسب العضو نفسه، عضو واحد وعضوين مش زي بعض.

> ⚠️ **دي الغلطة المتوقعة:** خلط الاتنين. بنقول *I have a headache* و*I feel tired*، مش *I have tired*
> ولا *I feel headache*. القاعدة البسيطة: **have** بتيجي مع اسم، و**feel** بتيجي مع صفة.

**Body parts you will need:**

| | |
|---|---|
| head · throat · stomach · back | tooth → **teeth** |
| eye · ear · hand · leg | foot → **feet** |

**And the question that opens all of this:** *What's the matter?* or *What's wrong?*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Teach the three patterns with two oral examples each, then contrast *have* + noun against
> *feel* + adjective directly. Flag the two irregular plurals — *teeth* and *feet* — since both are common
> in health talk.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Two sounds, one tongue position — and health words are full of both.**

| Sound | Voiced? | Words |
|---|---|---|
| **/θ/** | no — no vibration | **th**roat · tee**th** · mou**th** · heal**th** |
| **/ð/** | yes — throat vibrates | **th**is · **th**e · brea**th**e · **th**ere |

The tongue tip sits lightly **between the teeth** for both. The only difference is whether your voice is
switched on.

**The test:** hand on your throat. *teeth* → still. *breathe* → vibrating.

> نفس مكان اللسان في الاتنين، والفرق بس إن الصوت شغال ولا لأ. حط إيدك على رقبتك وجرّب الكلمتين ورا بعض.

**Watch for the two substitutions:** /θ/ becoming /s/ — *"sroat"* — and /ð/ becoming /d/ — *"dis"*.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"My throat and my teeth hurt, and I can't breathe well."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Mirror plus hand-on-throat. This pair was introduced back in Stage 0; this is the
> consolidation, so expect progress rather than a first attempt.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *have* or *feel*?**
1. I ______ a headache.
2. I ______ tired today.
3. She ______ a cold.
4. He ______ much better now.

**Exercise 2 — complete with a body part.**
1. I can't eat — my ______ hurts. (tooth)
2. I sat all day and now my ______ hurts. (back)
3. I can't speak well — my ______ is sore. (throat)

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I have tired.* → __________________________
2. *I feel a headache.* → __________________________
3. *My tooths hurt.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** Describe how you feel right now, in two sentences.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** have · feel · has · feels. **Ex2:** tooth · back · throat.
> **Ex3:** *I feel tired* · *I have a headache* · *My teeth hurt*.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **have** + a noun: *a headache · a cold*.
- **feel** + an adjective: *tired · dizzy · better*.
- **… hurts** for a part: *My back hurts*.
- **tooth → teeth · foot → feet.**
- /θ/ and /ð/ share a tongue position; only the voice differs.
