# Obligation & Necessity: *have to · don't have to · must*

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 7 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit7-health-and-advice.md` · **S1-U7-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 4 · Feedback —.
> **Objectives:** express necessity with *have to* · say something is unnecessary with *don't have to* · use *must*.
> **The single most valuable thing in this lesson is that *don't have to* does NOT mean *shouldn't*.** It means *there is no need*. Learners assume any negative obligation is a prohibition, and the misunderstanding is genuine — *"You don't have to come"* heard as *"You mustn't come"* changes a welcome into a rejection.
> **Support:** the three-card sort. **Stretch:** contrast a rule at their work with a personal choice.
> **Arabic ~30%.**

---

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Say something is necessary: *I have to work on Saturday.*
- Say something is **not** necessary: *You don't have to come.*
- Use *must* for something strong. 🎙️

> **should** كانت نصيحة. النهاردة الكلام عن الضروري واللي مش ضروري — وده فرق مهم.

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
This is how you talk about rules, work, and anything outside your control. *I have to be there at eight. You
don't have to pay. You must take the medicine.*

> والنقطة اللي بتفرق: **don't have to** معناها **مش ضروري**، مش ممنوع.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two colleagues comparing their weeks (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** I **have to** work on Saturday this week. The manager asked everyone.
> **B:** Really? I **don't have to** — my shift changed.
> **A:** Lucky. And I **have to** finish the report before Thursday.
> **B:** You **must** be tired. You **should** ask for a day off.
> **A:** I can't. But I **don't have to** come in early, at least.

**Notice:** *have to* is about a rule from **outside** — the manager, the schedule. *should* was advice from a
person.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| I have to work on Saturday. | لازم أشتغل السبت |
| I don't have to. | مش مضطر |
| You must be tired. | لازم تكون تعبان |
| I don't have to come early. | مش لازم أجي بدري |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second time ask *who decided?* for each obligation. External source is what
> distinguishes *have to* from *should*.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) *have to* + simple verb — it is necessary, usually because of a rule.**

| | |
|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **have to** go |
| he / she / it | **has to** go |

> ⚠️ خد بالك: مع *he* و*she* بتبقى **has to**. دي الحاجة الوحيدة اللي بتتغير.

**2) *don't have to* — it is NOT necessary. You may, if you like.**

> *You **don't have to** come.* → no obligation, you are free
> *He **doesn't have to** pay.* → it is not required

**3) *must* — strong, and usually written or formal.**

> *You **must** take the medicine.* · *Passengers **must** wear a seatbelt.*

**The three compared:**

| | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *You **should** rest.* | advice — my opinion |
| *You **have to** rest.* | necessary — a rule or a real need |
| *You **don't have to** rest.* | not necessary — your choice |

> ⚠️ **أهم نقطة في الدرس:** *don't have to* ≠ *shouldn't*. الأولى معناها **مش ضروري**، والتانية معناها
> **ماينفعش**. لو خلطتهم، بتقول لحد "ممنوع" وإنت قصدك "مفيش إلزام".

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Build the three-way table on the board and drill it by giving a situation and asking which one
> fits. The *don't have to* against *shouldn't* contrast deserves at least four examples of its own.

## 🗡️ Your Arsenal — ذخيرتك

| English | بالعربي | Example |
|---|---|---|
| have to / has to | لازم / مضطر | *I have to go.* |
| don't have to | مش ضروري | *You don't have to wait.* |
| must | لازم (قوي) | *You must stop smoking.* |
| a rule | قاعدة | *It's a rule.* |
| take a day off | تاخد يوم أجازة | *I have to take a day off.* |
| be on time | تكون في الميعاد | *We have to be on time.* |

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Target 1 — *have to* is pronounced /hæftə/.**
The *v* becomes an **f**, and *to* reduces to /tə/:

> *I have to go* → **"I HAF-tə go"**

This is not sloppy — it is the standard pronunciation. And *has to* becomes **/hæstə/**.

> دي مش لهجة عامية، ده النطق الطبيعي. الـ **v** بتتحول لـ **f**، ودي مفاجأة لأغلب المتعلمين.

**Target 2 — the stress goes on the main verb:**

> I have to **WORK** on Saturday.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I have to work, but I don't have to start early."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** The /f/ substitution surprises learners and immediately improves both their listening and their
> naturalness. Worth the full four minutes.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *have to* or *has to*?**
1. I ______ leave at six.
2. She ______ work on Friday.
3. They ______ wear a uniform.

**Exercise 2 — necessary, or not necessary?**
1. *You don't have to bring food.* → ______________
2. *You have to show your ID.* → ______________
3. *You mustn't smoke here.* → ______________

**Exercise 3 — *should*, *have to*, or *don't have to*?**
1. It's a rule at work — I ______ wear a shirt.
2. You look ill. I think you ______ rest.
3. It's free — you ______ pay.

**Exercise 4 — fix the mistake.** *"He have to go."* → __________

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** have to · has to · have to. **Ex2:** not necessary · necessary · prohibited —
> use the third to sharpen the contrast. **Ex3:** have to · should · don't have to. **Ex4:** *He has to go.*

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **have to / has to** + simple verb — necessary, usually an outside rule.
- **don't have to** = **not necessary**, not forbidden.
- **must** = strong, often written.
- **should** = advice · **have to** = necessity.
- *have to* sounds like **/hæftə/**.
