## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 3 you'll **tell stories** — use the ten most common irregular verbs,
> ask and answer past questions, say what didn't happen, and narrate a real event.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تحكي حكاية حقيقية، وتسأل غيرك عن ماضيه.

**Why this closes the block:** Unit 1 gave you past **states**, Unit 2 gave you past **actions** with *-ed*.
This unit adds the verbs that refuse *-ed*, and — more importantly — the ability to **ask**. That is what
turns narration into conversation.

> بعد الوحدة دي تبقى خلصت الماضي بكل صيغه.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-3 story** — the first recording where you *tell* something that happened to you.
- The **did/didn't** rule, which makes every past question you will ever ask correct.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L03 and L05 run ~35 min, the rest ~30) plus this wrapper. This unit closes
> the past-tense block begun in Unit 1 — the single biggest milestone of A2.
> **L03 is the priority lesson.** Keeping the past form after *did* — *"Did you went?"* — is the most
> common A2 error there is, and it appears in every past question a learner will ever ask. Drill it to
> reflex. The mental model that fixes it: **the past is said once in the sentence**, and *did* already said
> it.
> **Expect over-regularisation** in L01–L02 (*goed*, *eated*). It is evidence the *-ed* rule landed, not
> evidence of failure — correct warmly and re-expose. Ten irregulars is the right number for A2; resist
> handing out a fifty-verb table.
> **In L04, correct almost nothing mid-story.** Stories are the fluency engine; interrupting them teaches
> hesitation. Note the two patterned errors and give them as delayed group feedback.
> **L05 is a proof asset,** and the best one so far — play it beside the learner's Stage-0 clip.
