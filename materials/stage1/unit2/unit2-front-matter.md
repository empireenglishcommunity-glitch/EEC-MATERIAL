## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 2 you'll **say what you did** — put *-ed* on any regular verb,
> pronounce all three endings correctly, spell them right, and narrate your day in order.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تحكي عملت إيه، بترتيب، وبنطق وإملاء صح.

**Why this follows Unit 1:** *was* and *were* describe **states** — where you were, how you felt. This unit
gives you **actions**, and actions are what stories are made of. One ending unlocks thousands of verbs.

> قاعدة واحدة، وكل الأفعال العادية بتبقى تحت إيدك.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Yesterday I worked | verb + *-ed* for all persons · *ago* |
| **L02** | The *-ed* sound ⭐ | hear and produce **/t/ · /d/ · /ɪd/** · sort any verb into its group |
| **L03** | Spelling rules | *stopped · studied · lived* — doubling, *y→ied*, drop-*e* |
| **L04** | Sequencing a day | *first · then · after that · finally* · narrate 4–6 sentences in order |
| **L05** | "What did you do yesterday?" (milestone) 🎙️ | a 6–8 sentence narration + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** past simple **regular** (affirmative) · *-ed* spelling · *ago* · sequence words.
**Accent Lab:** ⭐ **the three *-ed* endings** — this is the flagship pronunciation lesson of the stage.
**Mindset:** habit-stacking — attach English to something you already do every day.
**Arabic support:** ~40% at L01, fading to ~30% by L05.

---

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-2 "my day" narration** — the first recording where you *tell* rather than *describe*.
- The three *-ed* endings, which every past sentence you ever say will use.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L02 and L05 run ~35 min, the rest ~30) plus this wrapper.
> **L02 is the highest-value lesson in the stage.** Arabic speakers insert a full vowel into every *-ed*,
> so *worked* becomes three syllables. Because regular verbs appear in every past sentence they will ever
> speak, that one habit repeats a hundred times a day. Teach perception before production — an ending they
> cannot hear yet is an ending they can only guess at.
> **The errors to hunt, in order:** dropping *-ed* entirely (L01), the inserted vowel (L02), *y→ied* and
> the doubling rule (L03), and endings collapsing once sentences get longer (L04–L05).
> **Do not hold L05 hostage to perfect endings.** They need many more reps across later units; the
> milestone is the narration.
> **L05 is a proof asset.** Capture it with consent and hold it beside the Unit-1 recording — states
> against actions is an easy difference for a learner to hear in themselves.
