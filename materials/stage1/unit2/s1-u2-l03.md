## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Everything you write gets read by someone — a work message, a comment, an exam. A slip like *"stoped"* reads
as beginner even when your speaking is solid.

> والحاجة الحلوة إن القواعد **تلاتة بس**، وأغلب الأفعال مش محتاجة أي واحدة فيهم.

Three small rules, and then everything else is simply *+ed*.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Read this short paragraph and **underline every past verb**.

> Last weekend I **stopped** working early. I **studied** for two hours, then I **tidied** my room.
> Later I **lived** the quiet life — I **cooked**, I **watched** a film, and I **planned** my week.

**Notice:** *stopped* has two **p**s. *studied* and *tidied* end in **-ied**. *lived* dropped its **e**.
*cooked* and *watched* just added *-ed*.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Learners underline, then group the verbs by what happened to the spelling *before* you name
> the rules. The categories are visible in the data.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Rule 1 — double the last letter** when the verb ends *consonant · vowel · consonant* and the last part
is stressed.

| | |
|---|---|
| sto**p** → sto**pp**ed | pla**n** → pla**nn**ed |
| sto**p** = C-V-C | st**o**p carries the stress |

> القاعدة بتشتغل لما الفعل يخلص بـ **ساكن + علة + ساكن**، والمقطع الأخير عليه نبرة.

**Why the letter doubles:** it protects the short vowel inside. Written *"stoped"*, a reader would say it
with a long vowel, like *"hoped"*. The double letter is a signal that the vowel stays short.

**Rule 2 — *y* becomes *ied*** after a consonant.

- stud**y** → stud**ied** · tr**y** → tr**ied** · tid**y** → tid**ied**
- **But:** after a *vowel*, keep the y — pla**y** → pla**yed** · sta**y** → sta**yed**

> خلي بالك: الفرق كله في الحرف اللي **قبل** الـ y. لو ساكن، الـ y بتتحول لـ **i** وبنكتب **ied**. لو
> حرف علة، بنسيبها زي ما هي ونضيف **-ed** عادي. بص على الحرف اللي قبلها قبل ما تكتب، وماتخمنش.

**Rule 3 — drop the silent *e***.

- liv**e** → liv**ed** · arriv**e** → arriv**ed** · decid**e** → decid**ed**

> الـ **e** الساكتة في الآخر بتوقع، عشان ماينفعش نكتب **ee** ورا بعض. فبنشيلها ونحط **-ed** مكانها.

**Everything else — just add *-ed*.** *work → worked · clean → cleaned · visit → visited*

Most verbs land here, so the three rules above are the exceptions rather than the norm.

> وأسهل طريقة إنك تحفظ مثال واحد لكل قاعدة، وترجع له دايمًا وقت الشك بدل ما تخمّن.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Teach Rule 1 with the Arabic explanation of *why* the letter doubles — learners who
> understand it stop guessing. Rules 2 and 3 are mechanical and need only examples.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق
**Changing the spelling does not change the sound.**

- *stopped* still ends in **/t/** — the extra **p** is silent.
- *studied* still ends in **/d/**.
- *decided* still ends in **/ɪd/**, because the verb ends in **d**.

> الحرف المزدوج مش بيتنطق مرتين. الإملاء اتغيرت، الصوت زي ما هو.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I stopped, I studied, and I decided."* — /t/, /d/, /ɪd/.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~3 min.** Short and pointed. Learners commonly start pronouncing the doubled letter once they see it.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **C-V-C + stress** → double the last letter: *stopped · planned*.
- **consonant + y** → **-ied**: *studied*. **vowel + y** → keep it: *played*.
- **silent e** → drop it: *lived*.
- Everything else: **just add -ed**.
- Spelling changes; the **sound** does not.
