# The *-ed* Sound: /t/ · /d/ · /ɪd/ ⭐

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 2 · **Lesson 2** — *ACCENT LAB FOCUS*
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit2-past-regular-verbs.md` · **S1-U2-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Perceive 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 8 · Guided 5 · Your Turn 3 · Feedback 1 · Assign 1.
> **Objectives:** hear and produce the three endings · sort verbs into the three groups · use them correctly in sentences.
> **This is the highest-value pronunciation lesson in the stage.** Arabic speakers add a full vowel to every *-ed*, so *worked* becomes "work-ed" — three syllables where there should be one. It affects every regular verb they will ever say, so it is worth the extra five minutes.
> **Perception before production.** Do not let them produce until they can reliably hear the difference; producing an ending you cannot hear just installs a guess.
> **Support:** master killing the extra vowel first, then /t/ against /d/. Ignore /ɪd/ until those land. **Stretch:** self-correct inside connected speech.
> **Arabic ~35%** — the voiced/voiceless idea needs Arabic, the drilling does not.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
English writes the past **one** way and says it **three** ways. Most Arabic speakers add a full vowel to
every ending, so *worked* comes out as three syllables instead of one.

> والمشكلة إن الأفعال دي بتيجي في كل جملة ماضي، فالغلطة بتتكرر مية مرة في الكلام. وأصعب حاجة إنك
> ماتسمعهاش في نفسك، فبتفضل تتكرر من غير ما تحس.

Regular verbs appear in almost every past sentence you will ever speak. Fix this once and all of them get
clearer at the same time — which is why this lesson gets its own star, and five extra minutes.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف (listen only)
Three groups. Just **listen** and feel the difference (📹 in the portal).

> **Group 1:** work**ed** · watch**ed** · stopp**ed** · help**ed** · talk**ed**
> **Group 2:** play**ed** · clean**ed** · listen**ed** · call**ed** · stay**ed**
> **Group 3:** visit**ed** · want**ed** · start**ed** · need**ed** · wait**ed**

Group 1 ends in a **/t/**. Group 2 ends in a **/d/**. Group 3 adds a **whole extra syllable**, /ɪd/.

**Count the syllables:** *worked* = 1 · *played* = 1 · *visited* = 3.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Perception only — hold them back from repeating. Play each group twice, then mix and have
> them point to a group. They must be able to *hear* it before they try to say it.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The ending you use depends on the sound just before it.**

| If the verb ends in… | the *-ed* is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| a **voiceless** sound (k, p, s, sh, ch, f) | **/t/** | worked · stopped · watched · helped |
| a **voiced** sound or a vowel (l, n, r, v, b, g, y…) | **/d/** | played · cleaned · called · listened |
| **/t/ or /d/** | **/ɪd/** | visited · wanted · started · needed |

> **يعني إيه voiced وvoiceless؟** حط إيدك على رقبتك وقول **s** طويلة — مفيش رعشة، فده صوت **مهموس**.
> بعدين قول **z** — هتحس رعشة، فده صوت **مجهور**. والقاعدة إن آخر الفعل بيفرض على الـ **-ed** تمشي معاه.

Your mouth already does this — it is easier to keep the voicing the same than to switch it mid-word.

> يعني إنت مش بتحفظ قاعدة، إنت بتلاحظ حاجة بقّك بيعملها لوحده.

> ⚠️ **الجروب التالت بس** هو اللي بياخد مقطع زيادة، لأن الفعل خالص بـ **t** أو **d**. في أي حالة تانية مفيش حركة.

Two identical consonants cannot sit together, so a vowel has to separate them. Everywhere else, the ending is
a single consonant with no vowel at all.

**The quick test:** can you say the ending **without** opening your mouth again? If yes, it is /t/ or /d/.
If the word would jam, it is /ɪd/.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Teach voiced/voiceless with the hand-on-throat test in Arabic — it is a physical idea and
> lands instantly. Then let them predict the group for five new verbs before you confirm. Emphasise that
> /ɪd/ is the **narrow** case, not the default.

## 🗡️ Your Arsenal — ذخيرتك (recall)

| Group | Sound | Verbs |
|---|---|---|
| after voiceless | **/t/** | worked · watched · stopped · helped · talked · finished |
| after voiced / vowel | **/d/** | played · cleaned · called · listened · stayed · studied |
| after t / d | **/ɪd/** | visited · wanted · started · needed · waited · decided |

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق (the sort)

**Step 1 — sort these fifteen verbs into the three columns.** Say each one as you place it.

> cooked · played · wanted · walked · cleaned · started · helped · listened · visited
> talked · called · needed · finished · stayed · waited

**Step 2 — say each column as a chain**, keeping the rhythm even:
- /t/ · *cooked, walked, helped, talked, finished*
- /d/ · *played, cleaned, listened, called, stayed*
- /ɪd/ · *wanted, started, visited, needed, waited*

**Step 3 — the mixed sentence.** This is the real test:

> *I **worked**, then I **played** with my son, and later I **visited** my mother.*

Three verbs, three different endings, one breath.

> 🎧 **Record & compare** the mixed sentence. Listen for the extra vowel you are trying to remove.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** The full sort, out loud, then the mixed sentence with record-and-compare. Circulate during
> step 1 — the sorting is where you hear who has the rule and who is guessing.
> **Answers:** /t/ cooked, walked, helped, talked, finished · /d/ played, cleaned, listened, called, stayed
> · /ɪd/ wanted, started, visited, needed, waited.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — which sound?** Write /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/.
1. stopped ______ 2. decided ______ 3. cleaned ______ 4. watched ______ 5. waited ______ 6. studied ______

**Exercise 2 — count the syllables.**
- worked ______ · wanted ______ · played ______ · started ______

**Exercise 3 — say it, then check.** Read aloud, then verify each ending:
> *She called me, I waited, and then we walked home.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** **Ex1:** /t/ · /ɪd/ · /d/ · /t/ · /ɪd/ · /d/. **Ex2:** 1 · 2 · 1 · 2.
> **Ex3:** called /d/, waited /ɪd/, walked /t/ — all three groups in one sentence.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Voiceless → **/t/** · voiced or vowel → **/d/** · after **t/d** → **/ɪd/**.
- Only the **third** group adds a syllable.
- *worked* is one syllable. *visited* is three.
- Hand on the throat tells you voiced from voiceless.
- *(These endings get re-drilled in every unit from here — they need many reps.)*
