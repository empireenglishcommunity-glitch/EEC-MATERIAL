# Feelings & Personality: *"What's He Like?"*

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 9 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 8 · Stage-0 adjectives · Unit 1 (feelings)
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit9-people-feelings-everyday.md` · **S1-U9-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 3 · Feedback 1.
> **Objectives:** describe feelings and basic personality · ask and answer *How do you feel?* and *What's he like?* · describe someone you know.
> **Two confusions carry this lesson, and both are worth real time.**
> **1) *What's he like?* against *How is he?*** The first asks about **personality** — permanent. The second asks about **state** — right now. Learners answer *"What's your brother like?"* with *"He's fine"*, which is a non-answer, and they cannot see why.
> **2) *-ed* against *-ing* adjectives.** *I'm bored* means you feel nothing is happening. ***I'm boring* means you are a dull person.*** This is the single most embarrassing error at A2 and learners make it constantly. Teach the rule as a direction: **-ed** is how **I** feel, **-ing** is what the **thing** does to me.
> **Support:** feelings and personality flashcards plus the frames. **Stretch:** add *because* and a comparison from Unit 5.
> **Arabic ~25%** — mostly English now.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Most learners have two words for how they feel: *good* and *tired*. But feelings are the most common thing
people talk about, and English separates *feeling* something from *being* something. Get that separation and
you also stop making the one mistake that gets a laugh in the wrong way.

> والفرق مهم: **bored** زهقان، و**boring** شخص ممل.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two short exchanges (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** **How do you feel** about the exam tomorrow?
> **B:** A bit **nervous**, honestly. But also kind of **excited**.
> **A:** You'll be fine. Hey — **what's** your new manager **like**?
> **B:** She's really **kind**, and very **hard-working**. A bit **serious**, but **friendly** when you know her.
> **A:** And **how is** she today? I heard she was ill.
> **B:** Much better, thanks.

**Notice the two different questions.** *What's she **like**?* asked about her **personality** — and the
answer was *kind, hard-working, serious*. *How **is** she?* asked about **right now** — and the answer was
*better*. Different questions, different answers.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| How do you feel? | حاسس بإيه؟ |
| a bit nervous | متوتر شوية |
| What's she like? | شخصيتها إيه؟ |
| How is she? | عاملة إيه؟ (دلوقتي) |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. On the second pass learners label each question *personality* or *now*. That sort
> is the lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) Feelings — how you are right now.**

> *I **am** nervous. · I **feel** nervous.* — both correct, both natural

| | |
|---|---|
| good ones | *happy · excited · relaxed · proud* |
| hard ones | *sad · nervous · worried · angry · bored · tired* |

**Soften or strengthen with one word:** *a bit tired · really happy · so excited*

**2) Personality — what someone is always like.**

> *He **is** kind. · She **is** hard-working.*

| | |
|---|---|
| warm | *kind · funny · friendly · generous* |
| serious | *serious · hard-working · quiet · shy* |

**3) The two questions people confuse.**

| Question | Asks about | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| ***What's* he *like*?** | personality — always | *He's funny and kind.* |
| ***How is* he?** | state — now | *He's fine. · He's much better.* |

> ⚠️ **خد بالك:** لو حد سألك *"What's your brother like?"* وقلت *"He's fine"*، تكون ما جاوبتش. هو بيسأل عن
> شخصيته، مش عن حالته.

**4) *-ed* or *-ing*? This is the important one.**

| | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-ed** | how **I** feel | *I'm **bored**. · I'm **interested**.* |
| **-ing** | what the **thing** does | *The film is **boring**. · The book is **interesting**.* |

> ⚠️ **الغلطة المشهورة:** *"I'm boring"* معناها إنك شخص ممل، مش إنك زهقان. اللي بيحسّ ياخد **-ed**، واللي
> بيسبّب الإحساس ياخد **-ing**.

| I feel | The thing is |
|---|---|
| bored · tired · excited | boring · tiring · exciting |
| worried · interested | worrying · interesting |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on the adjective sets, two on *What…like?*, two on *-ed/-ing*. For the last one,
> the fastest fix is a forced choice: point at a learner, say *"boring"*, and have them tell you whether that
> describes the person or the film.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Let the voice carry the feeling.** At A2 the words are usually right and the delivery is flat, so the
feeling never arrives. English marks emotion with **pitch** and **length**, not extra words.

> *I'm **so** ex-**CI**-ted!* — high, and stretch the stressed syllable
> *I'm a bit **wor**-ried.* — lower, quieter, shorter

Say *"I'm excited"* three times: bored, normal, thrilled. Same three words. If a listener cannot tell them
apart, the sentence is not doing its job.

> جرب تقول نفس الجملة بتلات إحساسات مختلفة. الكلمات مش بتتغير، الصوت هو اللي بيتغير.

**And keep the endings on.** These adjectives are long, and the ends are where the meaning sits:

> *nervous* /ˈnɜːvəs/ · *worried* /ˈwʌrid/ · *interested* /ˈɪntrəstɪd/ — three syllables, not four

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I'm really excited, but I'm also a bit nervous."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** The three-emotions-same-sentence drill is the one to keep. It works instantly and learners
> remember it.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *-ed* or *-ing*?**
1. That lesson was very ______. (interest)
2. I'm ______ in history. (interest)
3. The journey was ______. (tire)
4. I'm ______ — I need to sleep. (tire)

**Exercise 2 — *What's he like?* or *How is he?***
1. You want to know about your friend's new flatmate's personality. → __________________
2. Your friend has been ill and you want to know about today. → __________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I'm boring in this class.* → __________________________
2. *— What's your sister like? — She's fine, thanks.* → __________________________
3. *I'm exciting about the trip.* → __________________________
4. *He is a hard-working person and very kindly.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** Describe **one person** you know in two sentences: personality, plus one
reason with *because*.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** interesting · interested · tiring · tired. **Ex2:** *What's he like?* · *How is he?*
> **Ex3:** *bored* · *She's funny and kind* · *excited* · *kind*. Ex3.2 is the one to discuss — ask why
> *"She's fine"* does not answer the question.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- ***I am*** *nervous* and ***I feel*** *nervous* are both fine.
- ***What's* he *like*?** = personality. ***How is* he?** = right now.
- **-ed** = how I feel · **-ing** = what the thing does to me.
- ***I'm boring*** means you are dull. Say ***I'm bored***.
- Pitch and length carry the feeling — flat delivery loses it.
