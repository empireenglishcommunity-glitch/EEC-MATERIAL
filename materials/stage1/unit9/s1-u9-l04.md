# On the Phone & Small Talk

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 9 · **Lesson 4**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L03
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit9-people-feelings-everyday.md` · **S1-U9-L04***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 7 · Your Turn 3 · Feedback —.
> **Objectives:** manage a simple phone call · leave or take a message · open, sustain, and close light small talk naturally.
> **Teach chunks, not grammar.** A phone call is one of the few situations where people use nearly identical fixed phrases every time. Fluency here comes from retrieving a whole line — *Can I speak to…?* — not constructing it word by word.
> **The missing visual channel is the difficulty.** Learners cannot point, read a face, or repair with a gesture. Give them explicit repair phrases: *Could you say that again? Could you speak more slowly?*
> **Small talk has a social shape:** open → answer + return question → one follow-up → close. Learners who answer *How's it going?* with *Fine* and silence are grammatically correct but conversationally unavailable. The return question is the lesson.
> **Support:** phone-stage and small-talk phrase cards. **Stretch:** handle an unavailable person and leave a detailed message with name, number, and reason.
> **Arabic ~20%** — fixed English phrases should carry the lesson.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
On the phone, your English loses its safety net: no face, no pointing, no gesture. But the conversation is
more predictable than it feels. Learn its stages and the pressure drops.

Small talk works the same way. It is not empty speech; it is how people signal *I am open to talking to you*.
One returned question changes the whole exchange.

> الرد لوحده مش كفاية. جاوب واسأل نفس السؤال للشخص التاني.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A short phone call (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Hello, Empire Travel. How can I help?
> **B:** Hi. **Can I speak to** Ms Hassan, please?
> **A:** **Who's calling**, please?
> **B:** It's Kareem Ali.
> **A:** Thanks. **Hold on** a moment… I'm sorry, she's not available right now. **Can I take a message?**
> **B:** Yes, please. Could you ask her to call me back about my booking?
> **A:** Of course. Does she have your number?
> **B:** Yes, she does. Thanks for your help.
> **A:** You're welcome. Goodbye.

Later, two colleagues meet:

> **C:** Hey, **how's it going**?
> **D:** Not bad, thanks. **How about you?**
> **C:** Pretty good. **How was your weekend?**
> **D:** Quiet, but nice. I visited my family. What did you do?
> **C:** I worked, unfortunately! **Anyway, I have to go. Good talking to you.**
> **D:** You too. See you later.

**Notice the work done by fixed lines:** the phone call moves through open → ask → message → close. The small
talk answer always comes back with a question.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| Can I speak to…? | ممكن أكلم…؟ |
| Who's calling? | مين بيتكلم؟ |
| Can I take a message? | أبلغه حاجة؟ |
| How's it going? | الدنيا ماشية إزاي؟ |
| Anyway, I have to go. | عمومًا، لازم أمشي |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners identify each phone stage and circle every return question.
> Do not translate every phrase — these are memorize-and-use chunks.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**A phone call has four stages.**

| Stage | Useful line |
|---|---|
| **1 Open** | *Hello, this is Noor.* |
| **2 Ask** | *Can I speak to Ahmed, please?* |
| **3 Connect or message** | *Hold on. · Can I take a message?* |
| **4 Close** | *Thanks for your help. Goodbye.* |

**If the person is unavailable:**

> *I'm sorry, she isn't available right now.*
> *Could you ask her to call me back?*
> *Can I leave a message?*

**If you did not hear:**

> *Could you say that again? · Could you speak more slowly? · Did you say fifteen or fifty?*

> استخدام جملة إصلاح مش فشل. دي مهارة المكالمة نفسها. ولو مسمعتش، اطلب الإعادة بدل ما تخمّن وتضيّع
> الرسالة أو الرقم المهم.

**Small talk has four moves too.**

| Move | Example |
|---|---|
| open | *How's it going? · How was your weekend?* |
| answer | *Not bad. · Pretty good. · A bit busy.* |
| **ask back** ⭐ | *How about you? · And you?* |
| close | *Anyway, I have to go. · Good talking to you.* |

**The rule that makes it a conversation:** answer + **ask back**.

> *How's it going?* — *Pretty good, thanks. **How about you?***

A one-word answer is not rude, but it closes the door. The returned question keeps it open.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Keep the phone and small-talk stages visible. Drill complete chunks, then remove one line at a
> time from the board. Spend the final two minutes forcing answer-plus-return-question pairs.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Fixed phrases need one rhythm, not one beat per word.**

> *How's it **GO**ing?* → /haʊzɪtˈɡəʊɪŋ/
> *Can I **SPEAK** to Ahmed?* → the small words compress
> *Could you ask her to **CALL me BACK**?* → stress the action

Say *How · is · it · going?* with four separate beats. It sounds like a language exercise. Now let the first
three words slide together and land on **go**. That sounds like a greeting.

**Friendly intonation rises into the question and relaxes through the answer.** A flat *How's it going?*
sounds as if you do not want to know.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"Hi, can I speak to Mariam, please?"* Then: *"How's it going? Pretty good — how about you?"*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Contrast mechanical word-by-word delivery with chunk rhythm. Prioritise fluent delivery and
> warmth over analysis; every line here is functional language.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — put the phone call in order.**
- a) *Can I take a message?*
- b) *Can I speak to Mr Adel, please?*
- c) *Thanks for your help. Goodbye.*
- d) *Hello, this is Rania from Empire English.*

**Exercise 2 — choose the line.**
1. You did not hear the number. → __________________________
2. The person is not there. Offer to record information. → __________________________
3. You want the person to contact you later. → __________________________
4. You need two seconds to connect the call. → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — keep the small talk alive.** Add an answer **and** a return question.
1. *How's it going?* → __________________________
2. *How was your weekend?* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — close naturally.** Replace the abrupt ending.
> *OK. Bye.* → _________________________________________________

**Exercise 5 — role-play.** Caller: ask for Salma and leave your name, number, and reason. Receiver: she is
not available; take the message. Swap roles.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** d → b → a → c. **Ex2 models:** *Could you say the number again? · Can I take a
> message? · Could you ask them to call me back? · Hold on a moment.* **Ex4:** *Anyway, I have to go. Good
> talking to you.* In Ex5, remove the phrase card for the second round.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Phone shape: **open → ask → connect/message → close**.
- *Can I speak to…? · Who's calling? · Hold on. · Can I take a message?*
- Repair is a skill: *Could you say that again?*
- Small talk: **open → answer + ask back → follow-up → close**.
- *Anyway, I have to go. Good talking to you.* closes without cutting someone off.
