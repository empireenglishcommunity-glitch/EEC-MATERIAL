# Adverbs of Manner: "She Speaks English Well"

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 9 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit9-people-feelings-everyday.md` · **S1-U9-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 7 · Your Turn 3 · Feedback —.
> **Objectives:** form adverbs of manner (*-ly* plus the irregulars) · say **how** someone does something · describe how you do daily activities.
> ***good* against *well* is the classic.** ❌ *"I speak English good."* It survives to C1 if it is not killed at A2, because it is never misunderstood — so nobody corrects it. Drill *well* as the adverb until it is automatic.
> **Watch the trap: *hardly* is not the adverb of *hard*.** *He works hard* = a lot. *He hardly works* = almost not at all. Opposite meanings. Teach *hard* as its own irregular and flag *hardly* explicitly, or a learner will build it by rule and say the reverse of what they mean.
> **Position matters and is easy:** after the verb, or after the whole object. *He speaks English well*, not ❌ *"He speaks well English"*.
> **Support:** the adjective↔adverb card. **Stretch:** combine with a Unit-5 comparative — *"I speak more slowly than my brother."*
> **Arabic ~25%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
*He drives* tells me a fact. *He drives **carefully*** tells me about him. One word, and the sentence carries
information about character, effort, and skill.

> والغلطة المستهدفة: *"I speak English good"*. لأنها مفهومة، ممكن تفضل سنين.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Describing a colleague (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** What's Omar like at work?
> **B:** He's great. He speaks English **well** — really **well**, actually — and he works very **hard**.
> **A:** Is he fast?
> **B:** He doesn't work **quickly**, he works **carefully**. He reads everything **slowly** and he **hardly
> ever** makes a mistake.
> **A:** That sounds better than fast.
> **B:** Much better.

**Notice where the adverbs sit:** *speaks English **well**· works **hard** · reads everything **slowly***.
They come **after** the verb, or after the whole object — never between them.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| He speaks English well. | بيتكلم إنجليزي كويس |
| He works hard. | بيشتغل بجد |
| carefully | بعناية |
| slowly | ببطء |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners underline every adverb and mark what comes immediately before
> it. The position rule emerges from the data rather than being announced.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) The rule: adjective + *-ly*.**

| Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|
| quick | quick**ly** |
| careful | careful**ly** |
| quiet | quiet**ly** |
| bad | bad**ly** |

**Two spelling changes:**

| | |
|---|---|
| *-y* → *-ily* | happy → happ**ily** · easy → eas**ily** |
| *-ble* → *-bly* | possible → possi**bly** |

**2) Three irregulars — no *-ly* at all.**

| Adjective | Adverb | |
|---|---|---|
| good | **well** | *She sings well.* |
| fast | **fast** | *He drives fast.* |
| hard | **hard** | *They work hard.* |

> ⚠️ **دي الغلطة الكلاسيكية:** *good* صفة، و**well** هي الظرف. بنقول *I speak English **well***، مش
> *English good*.

**3) One word to be careful with:** ***hardly*** exists, but it does **not** mean *hard*.

| | Means |
|---|---|
| *He works **hard**.* | a lot 💪 |
| *He **hardly** works.* | almost never ❌ |

> ⚠️ **دي فخ حقيقي:** **hardly** معناها "تقريبًا مش"، فهي عكس **hard** بالظبط. متبنيهاش بالقاعدة.

**4) Where it goes:** after the verb, or after the whole object.

> *He speaks **well**.* ✅ · *He speaks English **well**.* ✅ · *He speaks **well** English.* ❌

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes forming adverbs, two on *good/well*, one on *hardly*, one on position. Do
> *good/well* as rapid-fire oral correction — they need to hear themselves say *well* many times.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *-ly* is a real syllable, and it disappears first.** Under speed, learners drop it and the adverb
turns back into an adjective:

> *care-ful-**ly*** — three syllables · *quick-**ly*** — two · *eas-i-**ly*** — three

Say *carefully* and count on your fingers. If you get two, the *-ly* is being swallowed.

**Part 2 — the stress does not move to the adverb.** The verb keeps the emphasis:

> *She **SPEAKS** English well.* ✅
> *She speaks English **WELL**.* — only if you are correcting someone

> ولاحظ إن النبرة بتفضل على الفعل. الظرف بييجي بعده هادي، مش مضروب.

**And a word worth its own moment:** *well* is one syllable with a clean /w/ — not *"vell"*.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"He works very hard and he speaks English really well."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Finger-counting syllables is the fastest way to expose a swallowed *-ly*. Two minutes there,
> two on keeping stress on the verb.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — make the adverb.**
1. slow → __________ 2. happy → __________ 3. good → __________
4. careful → __________ 5. fast → __________ 6. easy → __________

**Exercise 2 — *good* or *well*?**
1. She's a ______ driver.
2. She drives ______.
3. My English isn't very ______.
4. I don't speak French ______.

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I speak English good.* → __________________________
2. *He speaks well English.* → __________________________
3. *She drives very careful.* → __________________________
4. *He works hardly every day.* (he works a lot) → __________________________
5. *I sleeped very bad last night.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** Say **how** you do three things: drive, work, and speak English.

> Ex3.4 is the one to slow down on — ask what the original sentence actually said.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- adjective + **-ly** → adverb. *happy → happily · easy → easily*.
- Irregulars: **good → well · fast → fast · hard → hard.**
- **hardly** ≠ **hard**. *He hardly works* means almost never.
- Position: after the verb, or after the object. Never *speaks **well** English*.
- **-ly** is a syllable — keep it alive at speed.
