## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 6 you'll **handle a restaurant on your own** — talk about food and
> amounts, ask how much and how many, and order politely from start to finish.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تدير موقف مطعم كامل بالإنجليزي، من أول ما تقعد لحد ما تدفع.

**Why this unit feels different:** the earlier units gave you language for *talking about* things. This one
gives you language for **doing** something. At the end you will be able to walk into a café and handle it.

> دي أول وحدة نتيجتها مهارة تستخدمها بره الفصل فورًا.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Countable & uncountable · *some/any* | which foods take a plural · *some* against *any* |
| **L02** | *much · many · a lot of · a few · a little* | say how much without a number |
| **L03** | *How much? / How many?* | ask amounts and **prices** · containers |
| **L04** | At the restaurant | *Could I have…, please?* · polite intonation |
| **L05** | Role-play a meal (milestone) 🎙️ | the whole transaction + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** countable and uncountable nouns · *some/any* · quantifiers · *How much / How many* ·
polite requests with *Could*.
**Accent Lab:** food-word clusters with no inserted vowel · the reduced *of* · **thirTEEN against THIRty**,
because it is money · rising intonation on requests.
**Mindset:** real-world reps — ask a price, order a coffee, use it outside the classroom.
**Arabic support:** ~35% at L01, fading to ~25% by L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-6 restaurant recording** — a complete transaction, not a description.
- A skill you can use **this week**, outside any lesson.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~35 min, the rest ~30) plus this wrapper.
> **L01's error is structural, not careless.** Arabic pluralises many nouns English treats as uncountable, so
> *"some breads"* and *"many informations"* are transfer. Teach the countable test as *"can you count them
> one by one?"* and the container workaround, rather than as a grammatical category to memorise.
> **In L02, give *a lot of* first.** It works with everything, so it is the safe option — and a learner who
> always has a safe option keeps talking, which is the actual goal. Refine into *many/much* and
> *a few/a little* afterwards.
> **L03's teen-versus-ty stress is not a detail — it is money.** *Thirteen* and *thirty* differ only in
> stress placement, and learners are usually unaware they are producing it ambiguously.
> **L04 carries the most important social point in the stage:** English politeness lives in **grammar and
> intonation**, not in extra words. *Give me a coffee* is grammatically perfect and socially rude, and
> learners almost never realise they sound blunt. Demonstrate the same sentence rising and falling and let
> them hear it.
> **L05 is scored with Interaction included** — it is a two-way task, so introduce one unscripted turn
> ("we don't have any fish today") and see them adapt.
