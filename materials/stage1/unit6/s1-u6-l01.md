# Countable vs Uncountable; *some / any*

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 6 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 5
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit6-food-quantity-restaurants.md` · **S1-U6-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 3 · Feedback 1.
> **Objectives:** sort food into countable and uncountable · use *some* and *any* correctly.
> **Teach it as "can you count them one by one?"** not as a grammatical category. *Three rices* is wrong for the same reason *three waters* is — you count the container, not the substance.
> **The Arabic transfer to expect:** Arabic pluralises many of these freely, so *"I bought some breads"* and *"many informations"* are structural, not careless.
> **Support:** the sort card. **Stretch:** name ten fridge items in the right column.
> **Arabic ~35%.**

---

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Tell which food words take a plural and which never do.
- Use **some** in statements and **any** in questions and negatives.
- Talk about what is in your kitchen right now. 🎙️

> في الإنجليزي فيه كلمات أكل بتتعد وكلمات مابتتعدش، والفرق ده بيغيّر الجملة كلها.

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Get this wrong and every sentence about shopping, cooking or ordering sounds off. Get it right and a whole
unit of quantity language becomes straightforward.

> والمشكلة إن العربي بيجمع كلمات كتير الإنجليزي مابيجمعهاش، زي "خبزات" و"معلومات".

So we translate directly and produce *breads* and *informations*, which are wrong. This is not carelessness —
it is a real difference between the two languages, and it only has to be learned once.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Someone checking the kitchen before shopping (📹 in the portal).

> We have **some rice** and **some bread**, but we don't have **any milk**.
> There are **some eggs** — four, I think — and **some tomatoes**.
> Do we have **any sugar**? … No, there isn't **any**. And we need **some water**.

**Notice:** *eggs* and *tomatoes* have an **-s**. *rice*, *bread*, *milk*, *sugar* and *water* never do.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| some rice | شوية رز |
| some eggs | شوية بيض |
| We don't have any milk. | مفيش لبن |
| Do we have any sugar? | فيه سكر؟ |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second time learners sort what they heard into two columns. Let the pattern emerge
> before you name it.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The test: can you count them one by one?**

| **Countable** — you can count them | **Uncountable** — you cannot |
|---|---|
| an egg → two eggs | rice |
| an apple → three apples | bread |
| a tomato → tomatoes | water · milk · sugar |

> اسأل نفسك: أقدر أعدها واحدة واحدة؟ لو أيوه، تبقى معدودة وبتتجمع. لو لأ، تفضل زي ما هي. والفكرة إن
> الحاجات غير المعدودة بنتعامل معاها كمادة واحدة، مش كقطع منفصلة، وعشان كده مالهاش جمع.

**Uncountable words follow three fixed habits:**

1. **No plural.** *rice*, never *rices*.
2. **No *a* or *an*.** Not *a bread* — say *some bread*.
3. **They take a singular verb.** *The water **is** cold.*

**To count them, count the container instead:**

> *two **bottles of** water* · *three **pieces of** bread* · *a **kilo of** rice*

**Now *some* and *any*:**

| | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ statements | **some** | *We have some bread.* |
| ❌ negatives | **any** | *We don't have any bread.* |
| ❓ questions | **any** | *Do we have any bread?* |

> ⚠️ **استثناء تستاهل تعرفه:** لما تطلب حاجة أو تعرض حاجة، بنستخدم **some** في السؤال:
> *"Would you like **some** tea?"* — لأن ده عرض، مش سؤال عن وجود الحاجة.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Lead with the counting test, then the three habits, then *some/any*. The offer exception is
> worth including — learners meet it constantly and it otherwise looks like a broken rule.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Target — consonant clusters in food words, with no vowel pushed inside.**

Arabic does not usually allow these clusters, so a vowel tends to slip in:

| Word | ✅ | ❌ |
|---|---|---|
| bread | *bred* — one syllable | "be-read" |
| fruit | *froot* — one syllable | "fe-root" |
| vegetables | **VEJ**-ta-bulz — three | "ve-ge-ta-bles" |
| milk | *milk* — one | "mi-lik" |

> الحرفين الساكنين في أول الكلمة بيتنطقوا ورا بعض على طول، من غير حركة بينهم.

Note that *vegetables* is **three** syllables, not four — the second *e* disappears entirely.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I bought some bread, fruit and vegetables."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** *vegetables* is the highest-value fix — learners almost universally give it four syllables.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — countable or uncountable?**
> rice · egg · water · apple · bread · tomato · sugar · milk

**Exercise 2 — *some* or *any*?**
1. We have ______ rice at home.
2. I don't want ______ sugar in my tea.
3. Do you have ______ eggs?
4. Would you like ______ coffee?

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I bought some breads.* → __________________________
2. *She drank a water.* → __________________________
3. *There are many informations.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — your kitchen.** Three things you have, one thing you don't.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** countable — egg, apple, tomato · uncountable — rice, water, bread, sugar, milk.
> **Ex2:** some · any · any · some *(the offer)*. **Ex3:** *some bread* · *some water / a glass of water* ·
> *a lot of information*.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Can you count them one by one? Then they take a plural.
- Uncountables: **no plural · no *a/an* · singular verb**.
- Count the **container**: *a bottle of water*.
- **some** in statements · **any** in negatives and questions · **some** in offers.
- *bread · fruit · milk* are one syllable. *vegetables* is three.
