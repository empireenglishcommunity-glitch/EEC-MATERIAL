# *There Was / There Were* — Describing a Past Place

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 1 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit1-was-and-were.md` · **S1-U1-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 4 · Feedback 1.
> **Objectives:** describe what existed in a past place with *there was/were* · use its negative and question forms · describe a real place from their own past.
> **Watch for:** plural agreement — *"there was two windows"* is the error to hunt. Drill with obviously plural nouns.
> **Support:** give a sentence frame plus a word bank. **Stretch:** add a feeling — *"It was small but I was happy there."*
> **Arabic ~40%.** This lesson is emotionally warm — people enjoy describing a childhood place. Use that.

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Say what existed somewhere in the past: *There was a small bed.*
- Use the plural: *There were two windows.*
- Describe **a real place from your own past** in 3–4 sentences. 🎙️

> هتوصف مكان من ماضيك — بيتك القديم، مدرستك، أو مكان زرته.

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
This is how people tell stories. *"There was a small café near our house…"* Describing a place is the
frame that memories hang on, and it is one of the most common things you will ever be asked to do in
English.

> لما تحكي عن ذكرى، أول حاجة بتوصفها هي المكان.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Someone describes the room they grew up in (📹 in the portal).

> **There was** a small bed near the window. **There were** two old chairs and a wooden desk.
> **There wasn't** a television — we watched TV in the living room with everyone.
> It **was** a simple room, but I **was** happy there.

**Notice:** *there was* for **one** thing, *there were* for **more than one**.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| There was a small bed. | كان فيه سرير صغير |
| There were two chairs. | كان فيه كرسيين |
| There wasn't a television. | ماكانش فيه تليفزيون |
| It was simple. | كان بسيط |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice; second time learners count how many things were singular and how many plural.
> That counting is what makes the agreement rule stick.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The rule is only about the noun that follows.**

| | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **one** thing | **there was** | *There was a chair.* |
| **more than one** | **there were** | *There were three chairs.* |

> القاعدة بتبص على الاسم اللي بعدها: مفرد يبقى **there was**، جمع يبقى **there were**. مالهاش دعوة بالفاعل.

**Negative and question — same pattern as L02:**

- *There **wasn't** a lift in the building.*
- *There **weren't** any shops nearby.*
- *__Was__ there a garden?* → **Yes, there was.**
- *__Were__ there many people?* → **No, there weren't.**

**Quantity words that pair with it:** *a lot of · some · any · many · two, three…*

> في العربي بنقول "كان فيه" مع المفرد والجمع، فمفيش حاجة في لغتك بتنبهك للفرق ده. عشان كده الغلطة دي
> بتتكرر كتير مع المتعلمين العرب. الحل إنك تبص على الاسم اللي بعد الفعل قبل ما تتكلم: لو أكتر من
> واحد، لازم **there were**.

> ⚠️ **أشهر غلطة:** *"there was two windows"*. لو الاسم جمع، لازم **there were**. اسمع نفسك وانت بتتكلم.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Teach the table in English, then use Arabic only for the agreement warning. Drill five quick
> prompts with obviously plural nouns (*windows, chairs, people, books, cars*) — the error is a habit, not
> a misunderstanding, so it needs repetitions rather than explanation.

## 🗡️ Your Arsenal — ذخيرتك

| English | بالعربي | Example |
|---|---|---|
| There was / There were | كان فيه | *There was a garden.* |
| There wasn't / weren't | ماكانش فيه | *There weren't any shops.* |
| a lot of / some / any | كتير / شوية / أي | *There were a lot of people.* |
| near / next to | جنب / قريب من | *There was a café near us.* |
| old / small / simple | قديم / صغير / بسيط | *It was a simple room.* |
| garden / building / shop | جنينة / عمارة / دكان | *There was a garden.* |

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Target 1 — linking *there was* into one smooth sound.**
Native speakers do not say three separate words. They say something close to **"there-wuz-uh"**:

> *There was a…* → **there-wuz-a** · *There were a lot of…* → **there-wer-a-lot-of**

**Target 2 — keep the singular and plural clearly different.**
Because *was* and *were* both shrink in fast speech, learners often blur them into the same sound. Keep
the vowel distinct: *was* /wəz/ against *were* /wər/.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"There was a bed, and there were two chairs."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Drill the linking first, then immediately contrast the two forms in one sentence so the
> distinction survives the linking.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — was or were?**
1. There ______ a big tree in front of the house.
2. There ______ four people in the car.
3. There ______ a lot of noise.
4. There ______ two schools in our street.

**Exercise 2 — make it negative.**
1. There was a lift. → __________________________
2. There were any shops. → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — ask the question.**
1. (a garden) → __________________________
2. (many people) → __________________________

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** was · were · was · were. **Ex2:** There wasn't a lift · There weren't any shops.
> **Ex3:** Was there a garden? · Were there many people? Check agreement on every single answer.
