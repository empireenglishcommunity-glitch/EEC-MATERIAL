# Longer & Irregular Comparatives: *more expensive · better · worse*

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 5 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit5-making-comparisons.md` · **S1-U5-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 4 · Feedback —.
> **Objectives:** use *more* with long adjectives · use *better* and *worse* · choose the right form.
> **Give them the syllable test, not a list of "long adjectives".** One syllable takes *-er*; three or more take *more*. Two-syllable adjectives are genuinely mixed and not worth policing at A2 — accept either.
> **Watch for the double comparative:** *"more cheaper"*, *"more better"*. It comes from over-applying the new form and disappears with correction plus reps.
> **Support:** the two-column sort card. **Stretch:** compare three things in one turn.
> **Arabic ~35%.**

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Compare with long adjectives: *more expensive · more beautiful*.
- Use the two that break every rule: *good → **better*** and *bad → **worse***.
- **Choose** between *-er* and *more* without guessing. 🎙️

> في الدرس اللي فات كل الصفات كانت قصيرة. النهاردة الصفات الطويلة، وليها طريقة تانية.

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Most of the adjectives you actually want in a real opinion are long ones — *interesting*, *comfortable*,
*important*, *difficult*. Without *more* you are stuck comparing only sizes and prices.

And long adjectives are actually **easier**: you change nothing about the word itself. You put *more* in
front and stop — no spelling change, no doubling, no letters swapped. The only difficulty is knowing which
form to pick.

> الصعوبة الوحيدة إنك تعرف تختار.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two friends choosing a phone (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** This one is **more expensive than** that one, but it's **better**.
> **B:** Is it? The cheaper one looks **more beautiful** to me.
> **A:** Maybe, but the battery is **worse**. And this screen is **more comfortable** for reading.
> **B:** OK, you're right. It's **more important** to have a good battery.

**Notice:** the long adjectives do not change at all — *more* simply goes in front. And *better* and *worse*
look nothing like *good* and *bad*.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| more expensive than | أغلى من |
| more beautiful | أجمل |
| better | أحسن |
| worse | أسوأ |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second time learners split what they hear into *-er* forms, *more* forms and
> irregulars — three columns, discovered rather than given.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Long adjective → *more* + adjective + *than*. The adjective itself never changes.**

| Adjective | Comparative |
|---|---|
| expensive | **more expensive** |
| beautiful | **more beautiful** |
| difficult | **more difficult** |
| important | **more important** |

**How do you know which form to use? Count the syllables.**

| Syllables | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **one** | *-er* | cheap → cheaper |
| **three or more** | *more* | expensive → more expensive |
| **two** | either is usually fine | quiet → quieter *or* more quiet |

> عُدّ المقاطع، ماتحفظش قوائم.

Two-syllable adjectives genuinely accept both forms, so there is nothing to worry about there.

**Two adjectives ignore all of this:**

| | |
|---|---|
| good | **better** |
| bad | **worse** |

> ⚠️ **الغلطة المتوقعة:** *"more better"* أو *"more cheaper"*. اختار **واحدة** بس — إما **more** أو
> **-er**، وعمرك ما تجمع بينهم. والسبب إن الاتنين بيعملوا نفس الشغل، فلو جمعتهم بتبقى بتقول نفس المعنى
> مرتين في نفس الكلمة، وده اللي بيخلي الجملة تبان غلط على ودن السامع فورًا.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Lead with the syllable test — it is a rule they can apply, unlike "long adjectives". Then the
> two irregulars, then block the double comparative explicitly.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Target 1 — long adjectives have a fixed stressed syllable. Put it in the wrong place and the word stops
being recognisable.**

| Adjective | Stress |
|---|---|
| ex**PEN**sive | on the middle |
| **BEAU**tiful | on the first |
| **DIF**ficult | on the first |
| im**POR**tant | on the middle |
| **COM**fortable | on the first |

**Target 2 — *more* is unstressed.** It shrinks and hands the stress to the adjective:

> It's more ex**PEN**sive.

> النبرة في الكلمة الطويلة مش اختيارية — لو حطيتها غلط، الكلمة بتتغير وممكن ماتتفهمش.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"It's more expensive, but it's more comfortable."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Clap the stress for each adjective, then have them clap it back. Misplaced stress in long
> adjectives is a bigger intelligibility problem at A2 than any vowel.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *-er* or *more*?**
1. cheap → ______ 2. interesting → ______ 3. old → ______ 4. comfortable → ______ 5. difficult → ______

**Exercise 2 — good or bad?**
1. My English is ______ than last year. (good)
2. The traffic today is ______ than yesterday. (bad)

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *This book is more better.* → __________________________
2. *Cairo is more big than Tanta.* → __________________________
3. *My car is expensiver than yours.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — mark the stress.** ex-pen-sive · beau-ti-ful · im-por-tant

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** cheaper · more interesting · older · more comfortable · more difficult.
> **Ex2:** better · worse. **Ex3:** *better* · *bigger* · *more expensive*.
> **Ex4:** exPENsive · BEAUtiful · imPORtant.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- One syllable → **-er** · three or more → **more** · two → either.
- The adjective **never changes** after *more*.
- **good → better** · **bad → worse**.
- Never *more better* or *more cheaper*. Pick one.
- Long adjectives have a **fixed stress** — get it right or the word blurs.
