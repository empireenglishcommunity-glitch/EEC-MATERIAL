## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 8 you'll **get yourself there and back** — find your route, ask a
> stranger for help politely, buy a ticket, know what is required and what is forbidden, and then tell the
> story of the trip.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: توصل لأي مكان، وتطلب مساعدة بأدب، وتشتري تذكرة، وتحكي الرحلة بعد كده.

**Why this unit feels different:** Unit 7 taught you to help someone else. This one is about **not needing
help** — and, when you do need it, asking for it in a way that gets a yes. It is the most portable unit in
Stage 1: the language works in every airport on earth.

> ودي أكتر وحدة هتستخدمها بره. الكلام اللي جواها بيشتغل في أي مطار وأي محطة.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Getting around | *How do I get to…?* · **take the** / **by** · *get off at* |
| **L02** | *Can / Could I…?* | ask permission, make requests — and sound polite doing it |
| **L03** | Booking & rules | buy a ticket · **have to / don't have to / can't** |
| **L04** | A past trip | narrate a journey — the past tense finally carrying a real story |
| **L05** | Tell me about a trip (milestone) 🎙️ | a 7–9 sentence narration + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** *can/could* for requests, permission and past ability · *have to* and *can't* for rules ·
past simple recycled from Units 1–3 inside a real narration.
**Accent Lab:** linking in fixed phrases (*get off at* → *ge-TOF-fat*, *could you* → /kʊdʒə/) · rising
intonation on requests · **/kən/ against /kænt/** · English stress in place names · narrative rhythm.
**Mindset:** the input diet — put your maps, tickets and travel apps into English.
**Arabic support:** ~30% at L01, fading to ~25% by L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-8 trip narration** — a strong candidate for your mid-A2 proof clip.
- The genuine ability to travel without someone translating for you.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~35 min, the rest ~30) plus this wrapper.
> **L01's real error set is the two transport patterns.** *Go **by** bus* takes no article; *take **the*** bus
> does. Crossed, they give ❌ *"I go by the bus"* — very common, very audible. And *on foot* breaks the *by*
> pattern, so teach it as its own item rather than hoping it lands.
> **L02 is Unit 7's softening lesson in a new setting,** and worth naming as such. *Should* needed *I think*;
> requests need *could* plus a friendly rise. Intonation is the whole lesson — said flat, *Could you help me*
> is an order, and learners cannot hear that about themselves until they hear both versions back to back.
> **L03 introduces no new grammar and is still the hardest lesson here.** *Have to* is from Unit 7, *can't*
> from Stage 0; the difficulty is assembly under conversational pressure. Protect the three-way meaning
> check — **required / not required / forbidden** — because *don't have to* and *can't* look alike and mean
> opposite things.
> **/kən/ against /kænt/ is the priority sound of this unit, and arguably of the stage.** A weakly released
> final /t/ inverts the meaning of the sentence. Test it with eyes-closed pair work; do not accept "close
> enough", because no other pronunciation error on the list changes what you said.
> **L04 is the spaced-review payoff and should be framed that way.** Units 1–3 built the past tense as
> exercises; here it carries a story the learner wants to tell. Say out loud that this was impossible six
> weeks ago — they consistently underestimate it. Watch for *didn't* + past (❌ *"I didn't went"*), which
> returns under narrative load even in learners who had it clean in Unit 3.
> **L05's difficulty is integration, not any single part.** Under a 7–9 sentence load, two of the three
> strands slip. Correct the assembly, and ask your follow-up question unscripted.
