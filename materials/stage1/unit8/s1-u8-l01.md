# Transport & Getting Around: "How Do I Get To…?"

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 8 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 7 · Stage-0 directions (U8)
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit8-travel-and-transport.md` · **S1-U8-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 3 · Feedback 1.
> **Objectives:** name common transport and travel places · ask *How do I get to…?* and follow the answer · describe how you usually travel.
> **Teach the formula, do not parse it.** *How do I get to…?* is one chunk. Learners who try to analyse *get to* stall; learners who memorise the frame use it on day one.
> **The real error set is the two patterns for transport:** *go **by** bus* (no article) against ***take the** bus*. Cross them and you get ❌ *"I go by the bus"* — extremely common and very audible.
> ***on foot*, not *by foot*.** It is the one member of the set that breaks the pattern, so teach it as an exception rather than hoping it lands.
> **Support:** transport flashcards + the question frame. **Stretch:** give a two-step route with sequence words.
> **Arabic ~30%.**

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Asking a stranger for a route (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Excuse me. **How do I get to** the **airport**?
> **B:** **Take the metro** to Sadat, then **take the** number 27 **bus**.
> **A:** And where do I **get off**?
> **B:** **Get off at** the second **stop** after the bridge. It's about forty minutes.
> **A:** Thank you very much.
> **B:** No problem.

**Notice the shape of the answer:** *Take the …* → *then take the …* → *Get off at …*. Almost every answer
you will ever hear is built from those three moves.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| How do I get to…? | أوصل لـ… إزاي؟ |
| Take the metro. | اركب المترو |
| Get off at… | انزل في… |
| It's about forty minutes. | بتاخد حوالي ٤٠ دقيقة |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. On the second pass learners write only the three route moves, not the words — the
> point is that the answer has a predictable skeleton.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) The question is one fixed chunk. Memorise it whole.**

> ***How do I get to*** + a place?
> *How do I get to the station? · How do I get to the airport?*

**2) The answer has three moves.**

| Move | English |
|---|---|
| which transport | *Take the bus / the metro / the train.* |
| where to change | *Then take the number 27.* |
| where to leave | *Get off at the third stop.* |

> والتلات حركات دي هي كل الرد، فمش لازم تفهم كل كلمة.

**3) Now the part learners get wrong — there are two ways to name your transport, and they do not mix.**

| Pattern | Example | |
|---|---|---|
| **by** + transport, **no *the*** | *I go to work **by bus**.* | ✅ |
| **take the** + transport | *I **take the** bus to work.* | ✅ |
| the two crossed | *I go to work by the bus.* | ❌ |

> ⚠️ **دي أشهر غلطة في الدرس:** إما **by** لوحدها من غير **the**، أو **take the**. ماينفعش تخلط الاتنين.

**4) One exception, and it is worth learning as its own item:** walking is ***on* foot**, never *by foot*.

> *I go **on foot**.* ✅ · *I go by foot.* ❌

> والمشي هو الاستثناء الوحيد في القايمة.

**Places you will need:** *a station · a (bus) stop · a platform · an airport · a taxi rank*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Drill the frame chorally first, then the two transport patterns as a contrast pair, then *on
> foot* alone so it does not get absorbed into the *by* set.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — three words that become one.** *Get off at* is never said as three separate words:

> **get off at** → *ge-TOF-fat* — the /t/ jumps forward and joins *off*.

Say it slowly three times, then at speed. If a listener hears three words, it is too slow to sound natural.

**Part 2 — your own city's name in English.** This is the one that costs people. English stresses these
differently from Arabic:

| Place | English says | not |
|---|---|---|
| Cairo | **KY**-roh | *KAY-roh* |
| Alexandria | a-lig-**ZAN**-dree-uh | *a-lex-AN-dria* |
| Dubai | doo-**BYE** | *DOO-bye* |
| Doha | **DOH**-huh | *do-HA* |

> ودي مهمة أكتر من شكلها: لو قلت اسم بلدك بالنطق العربي، اللي بيسمعك ساعات مش بياخد باله إنه اسم مكان
> أصلاً، فالجملة كلها بتتوه.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"How do I get to the airport? I want to get off at the last stop."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes on the linking, two on place names. Have every learner say their own city out
> loud — it is the place name they will use most and the one they have never heard said in English.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *by*, *the*, or *on*?**
1. I go to school ______ bus.
2. I take ______ metro every morning.
3. My office is close, so I go ______ foot.
4. She goes to Alexandria ______ train.

**Exercise 2 — build the answer.** Someone asks *"How do I get to the museum?"* Give a three-move answer.

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I go to work by the car.* → __________________________
2. *How I get to the station?* → __________________________
3. *I go by foot every day.* → __________________________
4. *Get off in the third stop.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** How do you usually travel to work or school? Two sentences.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** by · the · on · by. **Ex3:** *by car* · *How **do** I get to the station?* · *on
> foot* · *get off **at***. Ex2 is the one to spend time on — check for all three moves.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- ***How do I get to…?*** — one chunk, learn it whole.
- The answer is three moves: **take the …** → **then take …** → **get off at …**
- **by bus** (no *the*) **or** **take the bus** — never both.
- **on foot**, not *by foot*.
- *get off at* sounds like **ge-TOF-fat**.
