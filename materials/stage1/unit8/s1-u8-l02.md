# Requests & Permission: *Can / Could I…?*

**Empire English** · Stage 1 — **Citizen** (A2) · Unit 8 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage1/unit8-travel-and-transport.md` · **S1-U8-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 7 · Your Turn 3 · Feedback —.
> **Objectives:** make polite requests and ask permission with *Can/Could I…?* and *Could you…?* · respond to a request · handle common travel requests.
> **The split that carries the lesson:** ***I*** asks about **me** — permission. ***you*** asks the other person to **act** — a request. Same modal, two jobs, and learners mix them: ❌ *"Could I help me with my bag?"*
> **Intonation is not decoration here.** Said flat, *Could you help me* is a command. This is the single most consequential thing in the lesson and it cannot be taught on paper — model the rise, out loud, repeatedly.
> **This is Unit 7's softening lesson again in a new setting.** *Should* needed *I think*; requests need *could* plus a friendly rise. Name that link — it is the same social skill twice.
> **Support:** the request frames card. **Stretch:** *"Excuse me, could you possibly…?"*
> **Arabic ~30%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
When you travel you need things from strangers: help with a bag, a different seat, a repeated instruction.
The words are easy. What decides whether you get help is **how the request sounds**.

> والفرق بسيط ومحسوس: **could** أدب أعلى من **can**، والصوت الطالع لفوق في آخر السؤال بيحوّل الطلب من أمر
> لطلب.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two small requests on a train (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Excuse me, **could you** help me with my bag? It's very heavy.
> **B:** **Of course.** Here you go.
> **A:** Thank you so much. And **can I** sit here?
> **B:** **Sorry, you can't** — someone is sitting there. But that seat is free.
> **A:** Great, thanks. **Could I** open the window?
> **B:** **Sure**, no problem.

**Notice:** *could **you*** asks **B** to do something. *can/could **I*** asks whether **A** is allowed. And
notice that the refusal is still friendly — *Sorry, you can't* plus an alternative.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| Could you help me? | تقدر تساعدني؟ |
| Can I sit here? | أقدر أقعد هنا؟ |
| Of course. / Sure. | طبعًا |
| Sorry, you can't. | آسف، مش ممكن |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners label each request *I* or *you*. That sort is the grammar.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**One rule of form, and it never changes:** *can* and *could* are followed by the **simple verb**. No *to*,
no *-s*.

**1) Asking about yourself — permission.**

> ***Can I*** *sit here?* · ***Could I*** *open the window?*

**2) Asking the other person — a request.**

> ***Could you*** *help me?* · ***Can you*** *say that again?*

> ⚠️ **الفرق كله في الضمير:** **I** معناها إذن لنفسي، و**you** معناها طلب من الشخص التاني. لو خلطتهم بتقول
> جملة مالهاش معنى زي ❌ *"Could I help me?"*

**3) *could* is more polite than *can*.** Both are correct. *Could* is safer with a stranger.

| | |
|---|---|
| *Can you help me?* | fine — friendly, direct |
| *Could you help me?* | more polite — use with strangers |
| *Excuse me, could you possibly help me?* | very polite ⭐ |

**4) The answers, which you need as much as the questions.**

| Yes | No |
|---|---|
| *Sure. · Of course. · No problem.* | *Sorry, you can't. · I'm afraid not.* |

> وخد بالك: الرفض بالإنجليزي بييجي معاه **sorry** واقتراح بديل. الرفض الجاف لوحده بيبان وحش.

**Two form errors to avoid:** ❌ *Could you to help me?* · ❌ *Can she helps me?*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on form — it is trivial. Four on the *I*/*you* split and the refusal frame, both
> of which are where the real errors live.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the rise. This is the lesson.**

A request goes **up** at the end. Flat means an order.

> *Could you help me?* ↗️ — a request
> *Could you help me.* ➡️ — an instruction

Say the same four words both ways until you can hear yourself switch.

> نفس الكلمات بالحرف، والفرق بس في الصوت آخر الجملة. طالع لفوق يبقى طلب، ومستوي يبقى أمر.

**Part 2 — *could you* becomes one word.**

> **could you** → /kʊdʒə/ — *"koo-juh"*
> **could I** → /kʊdaɪ/ — the /d/ links straight into *I*

Native speakers almost never separate them. *"Could you"* said as two clean words sounds careful, not wrong —
but hearing /kʊdʒə/ is what lets you understand the reply.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"Excuse me, could you help me with my bag?"* — with the rise.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Do the flat-versus-rising contrast as a whole group, exaggerated, then normal. Learners
> genuinely cannot hear that they sound blunt until they hear both versions back to back.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *Could I* or *Could you*?**
1. ______ open the window? (I want to open it)
2. ______ pass me the water? (I want them to pass it)
3. ______ say that again? (I didn't understand)
4. ______ pay by card? (I want to know if it's allowed)

**Exercise 2 — make it more polite.**
1. *Give me a pen.* → __________________________
2. *Speak slowly.* → __________________________
3. *Move your bag.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *Could you to help me?* → __________________________
2. *Can I helps you?* → __________________________
3. *Could I help me with my bag?* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — refuse politely.** Someone asks *"Can I sit here?"* but the seat is taken. Answer in two
sentences: refuse, then offer something else.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** Could I · Could you · Could you · Could I. **Ex3:** drop *to* · *Can I help you?* ·
> *Could you help me with my bag?* Ex4 matters most — insist on *sorry* plus an alternative.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **can / could** + simple verb. No *to*, no *-s*.
- **Can/Could I…?** = permission for **me**. **Could you…?** = a request of **you**.
- **could** is politer than **can**; *Excuse me, could you possibly…* is politer still.
- Refuse with **sorry** and an alternative, never bare.
- The rise ↗️ is what makes it a request. Flat is an order.
