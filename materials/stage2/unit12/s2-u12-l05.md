## 2️⃣ The Summative Quiz
Complete the Stage-2 quiz independently. It samples recognition and short production across everything the
stage built:

- present perfect for experience and for recent events, and the perfect-versus-past choice
- narrative past: past simple, past continuous, *used to*, and *had* + participle for what came earlier
- first and second conditionals, and the *will*-after-*if* trap
- relative clauses, defining and non-defining, including the doubled-subject error
- reported speech: backshift, pronoun and time shifts, reported questions
- the passive, including *by* and the agentless passive
- connectors, discussion functions, opinion framing and hedging

> [!TEACHER]
> **~12 min.** Use the committed portal or Google Form instrument and its set band. Preserve the raw score
> **and** the production responses — the short production items are the part that actually predicts speaking.
> A quiz that submits successfully is not evidence of speaking ability; it is one component of five.

## 3️⃣ Speaking Task Setup
The discussion runs **8–12 minutes** in three connected parts:

1. **Opinion and justification**, with at least one genuine push-back from the assessor.
2. **A detailed story** used as evidence for your position, including something that had happened earlier.
3. **A hypothetical and a reported opinion**, then one question from you.

You are scored on five dimensions: **Fluency · Coherence · Accuracy · Range · Interaction**.

You may ask for repetition, ask for thinking time, or repair a sentence you started badly. All three are part
of the Interaction score rather than deductions from it.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~3 min.** Explain the format and the rubric only. The support route may hold brief preparation notes and
> run at the shorter end of the range while still covering every criterion; record that accommodation
> alongside the result so the determination stays interpretable later.

## 5️⃣ The B1 Discussion

### Part A — Opinion and justification
The assessor opens with one broad question, for example: *Should university be free for everyone? · Is working
from home better for most people? · Should there be a limit on what anyone can earn? · Has technology made us
less patient?*

State your position, justify it, and hold or adjust it under one push-back.

### Part B — A story as evidence
Tell a detailed story that supports or complicates your position. Set the scene, connect the events, include
one thing that had already happened, and return to the point it proves.

### Part C — Hypothetical and reported
Answer two closing questions: what you would do if the opposite were true, and what other people have told you
about this and whether you agreed. Then ask the assessor one question of your own.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~14 min, recorded.** Follow the assessment suite's prompt and scoring procedure exactly. Push back once,
> genuinely, for every learner — a discussion that is never challenged cannot evidence Interaction. Do not
> silently simplify after one error or escalate after a strong opening. Score the whole sample against all five
> dimensions and preserve the evidence notes, quoted.

## 6️⃣ The B1 Milestone Sample 🎬
Record the standard prompt under the before/after protocol's comparable conditions — same prompt, same phone
or microphone position, same quiet room, unread and unrehearsed:

> **"Tell me about yourself, your family, and your typical day."**

Then add the Stage-2 extension:

> **"Give me an opinion you hold and your reason for it, tell me a story that shows why, and tell me what you
> would do if that situation happened again."**

Speak naturally. Do not memorise anything and do not read. This recording exists to sit beside your Day-1,
Stage-0 and A2 samples; it does not change the assessment result, and nothing you say in it is scored.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Capture as a **separate file** from the assessment. Match the prompt and conditions as closely
> as the protocol requires — an unmatched recording proves nothing, because any difference could be the
> conditions. Log the file link, the date, the level point and the consent tier. Never publish, edit into
> marketing, or transfer the clip beyond that tier without explicit authorisation.

## 7️⃣ Result and Feedback
The coach gives, in this order:

1. the rubric evidence across all five dimensions, quoted from what you said
2. the **B1 determination** against the assessment suite's threshold
3. two specific wins, quoted
4. one language or interaction target for Stage 3
5. one residual pronunciation target carried from the tracker

Evidence comes before any label. If a component needs repeating, that is a scheduled piece of work with a
date on it, not a verdict about you.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~3 min.** Do not round up for motivation or down for strictness — both destroy the value of the record.
> If the evidence is insufficient, or a technical failure affected capture, write that down and reschedule the
> affected component rather than estimating it.
