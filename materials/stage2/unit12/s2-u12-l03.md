## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Your individual sounds are largely fine by now. What still marks you out is **rhythm** — where the beat falls, which
words get squeezed, how a list or a contrast is shaped. That is what makes a speaker easy or tiring to follow, and it
is almost never taught.

There is a second reason this lesson exists. A teacher cannot follow you into every future conversation, but
self-monitoring can. Once you can hear a feature in your own speech, feel what your mouth is doing differently, and
repair it inside a sentence without stopping, your improvement stops depending on a course timetable at all.

Be clear about the target: **clearer and easier to follow than your own earlier recordings.** Not native. Accent is
identity, not error, and nobody in this programme is asking you to lose yours.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع (perception before production)
For each of your targets, listen to a clear version and a flat version. Mark which is which. **Do not speak yet.**

| Common Stage-2 target | Contrast to hear |
|---|---|
| the **beat** | every word equal · content words stressed |
| **weak forms** | *for* /fɔːr/ · /fə/ |
| ***'ll*** vs ***'d*** | *I'll go* · *I'd go* |
| **list intonation** | all flat · rise-rise-**fall** |
| **contrast stress** | *cheap but slow* flat · both words stressed |
| ***however*** | run on · full stop, comma |
| **turn-end** | rising · **falling** |
| **linking** | *asked me to* separated · *as-kmi-tə* |

**If you cannot reliably hear the difference, slow down there.** The mouth cannot fix what the ear does not yet
track — and prosody is harder to hear in your own speech than any individual sound.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** A/B listening with eyes closed. A target only advances when the learner identifies four of five
> correctly. Perception failure is information, not failure.

## 🧩 Decode it — الطريقة (cue cards)

Choose only the cards matching your targets.

| Target | Physical cue | Quick test |
|---|---|---|
| **the beat** | tap the table on content words only | can a listener follow at speed? |
| **weak forms** | let the vowel collapse to /ə/ | does *for* sound like *four*? |
| ***'ll* / *'d*** | one clean consonant, never dropped | can a partner tell real from unreal? |
| **list contour** | hand rises, rises, then **drops** | does your last item fall? |
| **contrast stress** | push **both** contrasted words | is the contrast audible? |
| ***however*** | stop, say it, comma, continue | two pauses present? |
| **turn-end** | drop the pitch and stop | does anyone else take the floor? |
| **linking** | no gap between the words | one shape or three words? |

**One cue per target.** In real speech you will not recall an explanation, but you can recall a tap, a drop, or a
hand movement. A cue works because it is physical and instant: it survives the moment when you are also choosing
a word, tracking what the other person said, and deciding whether you agree. An explanation does not survive
that moment, however well you understood it at the time.

Keep each cue tied to its own test, and check the test rather than your feeling. Feeling clearer is not evidence
of being clearer, and the two come apart most often on exactly the features that matter — because a rhythm you
produce yourself always sounds more natural to you than it does to anyone listening.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Re-cue, do not re-lecture. The learner states the cue and demonstrates the test. It must be usable
> without you present.

## 🗡️ Your Arsenal — ذخيرتك (the repair language)

| When you notice… | Do this |
|---|---|
| the sentence sounded flat | repeat the **phrase**, not the word, with the beat |
| a weak form came out strong | carry on; fix it on the next occurrence |
| you dropped a *'ll* or *'d* | repair immediately — it changes meaning |
| your turn ended on a rise | add a short falling tag: *…anyway.* ↘️ |
| you are losing the thread | *What I mean is…* and restart the clause |

A repair should be small and forward-moving: *"…which is cheaper — sorry, **cheaper but slower**."* Correct, then
continue. Never restart the whole turn.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — عيادة النطق (the nine-minute core)

For **each** target, run the same ladder:

1. **Hear** the contrast.
2. **Set** the cue.
3. Say a **word** or short phrase.
4. Say one **sentence**.
5. Say a **long sentence** — where prosody actually lives.
6. **Record and compare** with the model.
7. Repeat once, changing only the target.

**Required lane for everyone — the beat:**

> *I'**VE** been **WORK**ing there fər a**BOUT** three **YEARS**.*
> *If **I** were **YOU**, I**'d** **ASK** for **MORE**.*
> *The **BUILD**ing where I **WORK** wəz **REN**ovated **LAST** year.*

Then personalise lanes two and three from your own list.

> 🎧 **Checkpoint:** save your first and final attempt. Improvement you cannot compare is only a feeling.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~9 min.** Three minutes per target. Support route: six minutes on the highest-impact target, three on the beat.
> Stretch: the final sentence carries all three targets, unscripted.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب (seeded passage)

Read once for meaning, once for your targets:

> **If** I **were** starting again, I**'d** choose differently. **However**, the job I **have** now **was** offered
> to me by someone I**'d** met **for** about ten minutes at a conference — **which**, honestly, is not a strategy.
>
> The role **was created** because two people **had left** in the same month. **Firstly**, nobody explained that.
> **Secondly**, nobody explained the salary either. **Finally**, I **found out** from a colleague **who** **had
> been** there **for** nine years.
>
> **It's** cheap**er** than commuting, quick**er** than my last job, **and much less stressful**. **Although** I
> **wouldn't** call it a career.

Underline every instance of your targets. Read again, then speak for **ninety seconds** about your own work without
the passage. When you notice a slip, repair the phrase and continue.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min passage + 4 min free speech.** The passage seeds *'d*, *were*, *however*, weak *for*, a list contour, a
> contrast, two passives and a *had* form. Score only each learner's selected targets. Count spontaneous self-repairs
> as success.
