## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Units 4 to 8 gave you the grammar for complex ideas: hypotheticals, definitions inside a sentence, reporting other
people, and describing processes without a doer. Individually they are exercises. Together they are the difference
between simple English and adult English.

The summative does not ask you to produce a second conditional on demand. It asks you to talk about something real
for several minutes, and the structures either turn up when they are needed or they do not. That is what this lesson
tests.

There is also a practical reason to check them as a group rather than one at a time. Under pressure, learners fall
back to the safest structure they know and avoid the others entirely — which produces accurate but flat speech that
sits below its owner's real level. Retrieving all four in one session makes the avoidance visible, to you and to
them.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — النموذج
A short talk in which all four cooperate (📹 in the portal).

> The building **where** I work **was renovated** last year, and it **was done** badly.
>
> The company **that** did it **was chosen** because it was cheapest — **which**, in hindsight, explains everything.
> Our office manager **told us** the work **would take** six weeks. **She asked us whether we could** work from home
> for that period, and we all **said** yes.
>
> It **took** five months. **If** they **had planned** it properly, it **would have taken** six weeks — but **if** I
> **were** honest, I think nobody **was** managing it at all.
>
> **If** the same thing **happens** again, I**'ll** simply refuse to come in. **The people who** suffered most were
> the ones **whose** desks **were moved** four times.

**Notice the cooperation.** Two passives and a relative clause in the first two sentences. Reported statement and
reported question in the second paragraph. A second conditional, a *were*, and a first conditional in the last two.
None of it is announced — each structure appears because the meaning required it.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners identify all four structure types. Then ask which sentence would be
> impossible without its structure — that is the argument for having them.

## 🧩 The Five-Error Card — كارت الخمس أخطاء

Repair each before reading the correction.

| Wrong | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *If I will see him, I'll tell him.* | *If I **see** him…* | present after *if* |
| *If I would have money, I'd travel.* | *If I **had** money…* | past after *if* in the unreal |
| *A doctor is a person who he helps.* | *…a person **who helps**…* | the pronoun **is** the subject |
| *She asked me where do I live.* | *…where I **lived**.* | a reported question is a statement |
| *Coffee grown in Brazil.* | *Coffee **is grown**…* | the passive needs *be* |

**And two judgement points, which matter more than form:**

> **1) *by* is usually omitted.** *My bike was stolen* — not *by someone*. Include the agent only when it is
> surprising, specific, or important.
> **2) Drop the relative pronoun** when another subject follows it: *the film I saw*. It is more natural, and
> keeping it is never wrong.

**The four structures and what each is for:**

| Structure | Job |
|---|---|
| **conditionals** | reason about the possible and the imaginary |
| **relative clauses** | pack information into one sentence |
| **reported speech** | pass on what others said and asked |
| **passive** | describe processes and facts with no doer |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Learners repair all five aloud, then state one judgement point in their own words. Any structure that
> stays wrong becomes an individual priority before L05.

## 🗡️ Your Arsenal — ذخيرتك (retrieval)

| Job | Frame |
|---|---|
| real condition | *If I …, I'll …* |
| unreal condition | *If I …ed, I'd …* |
| hypothetical advice | *If I were you, I'd …* |
| define a person | *the person **who** …* |
| define a place | *the place **where** …* |
| reduced clause | *the thing I …* |
| report a statement | *He said (that) … · She told me …* |
| report a question | *She asked me if / where …* |
| report a request | *He asked me **to** …* |
| present passive | *It **is made** …* |
| past passive | *It **was built** …* |

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Weak forms and reductions across all four structures.** This is the Stage-2 thread, and here it is under maximum
load:

> ***'ll*** /l/ and ***'d*** /d/ — one phoneme separating real from unreal
> ***that*** → /ðət/, or dropped entirely
> ***to*** → /tə/ in *asked me to*
> ***is / are / was / were*** — weak but never absent in a passive
> ***by*** — unstressed, with the agent stressed

**The minimal pair that carries the most meaning:**

> *I**'ll** go* — real · *I**'d** go* — unreal

**Then the beat across a mixed sentence.** Content words only:

> *The **BUILD**ing where I **WORK** wəz **REN**ovated **LAST** year.*

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"If they'd planned it properly, it would have taken six weeks."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes on the *'ll* / *'d* pair, two on weak auxiliaries in the passive. Both are meaning-carrying
> rather than cosmetic.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Present after *if* in a **real** condition; past after *if* in an **unreal** one.
- The relative pronoun **is** the subject — never add another.
- **Drop** the pronoun when another subject follows it.
- A **reported question is a statement**: no inversion, no *do*.
- The passive always needs ***be***; *by* is usually **omitted**.
- ***'ll*** against ***'d*** — one sound, real against imaginary.
