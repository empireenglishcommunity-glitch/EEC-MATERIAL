# B1 Mock Discussion — The Full 8–12 Minutes

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 12 · **Lesson 4** — *FULL MOCK*
⏱️ ~40 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L03
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit12-putting-it-together.md` · **S2-U12-L04***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~40 min):** Retrieval 5 · Model 5 · Notice 4 · Accent 3 · Mock + peer rubric 15 · Feedback 3 · Self-assess 2 · Queue 3.
> **Objectives:** sustain an 8–12 minute discussion with opinions, justification, (dis)agreement and narration · manage turns and fluency strategies · self-assess readiness for the L05 summative.
> **This is the exact summative format.** Same length, same prompt types, same rubric. A mock only removes anxiety when nothing about it is a surprise. Do not raise the difficulty to "prepare" them or lower it to "encourage" them.
> **English-only, and say so warmly.** Stage 2 ends in full immersion. If a learner stalls, feed a phrase in English rather than switching language — that is the strategy they will actually need in the room.
> **Score the discussion, not a speech.** Turn-taking, following up, challenging, conceding and recovering all count. B1 is not error-free; it is coherent, justified, and sustained.
> **Low stakes, high honesty.** Today produces one short polish list, not a label. Quote evidence: *"You justified three opinions with reasons; two conditionals lost the 'd."*
> **Support:** prompt card, phrase bank, two minutes' prep, 8 minutes. **Stretch:** chair the discussion, no phrase bank, 12 minutes, handle a direct challenge.
> **Arabic 0%** — full immersion.

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
Today you run the whole thing:
- **Part 1:** state and justify an opinion, and respond when someone disagrees.
- **Part 2:** tell a detailed story that supports your point.
- **Part 3:** handle a hypothetical and a reported opinion.
- Score yourself on the rubric and leave with **two** things to polish. 🎙️

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Uncertainty costs more than difficulty. Almost everyone who underperforms in a speaking assessment does so
because the shape of the event is unfamiliar, not because the language is missing. You spend the first two
minutes working out what is being asked, the next two recovering, and by the time you sound like yourself the
assessment is over. Running the format once, in full, at full length, removes that entire category of loss.

There is a harder reason too. Eight to twelve minutes is long enough that memorised material runs out. A
rehearsed answer covers ninety seconds; after that you are simply having a conversation, and the discussion
starts asking for the things this whole stage was built on — a reason attached to an opinion, a story with a
shape, a hypothetical you did not see coming, and a way to disagree without ending the exchange. That is why
the mock is not shortened. The length is the test.

So treat today as a rehearsal with real stakes and no consequences. Prepare topics, not scripts. Find out
which structures desert you under pressure while there is still a lesson left to fix them, and go into the
summative already knowing what the room feels like.

## 🔁 Warm-up — سخّن (rapid mixed retrieval)
Sixty seconds each, no preparation:

1. An opinion about remote work, **plus a reason**.
2. Something you **have** done that changed how you think.
3. What you **would** do with an unexpected free month.
4. Something a friend **told** you that turned out to be wrong.
5. A change that **was made** somewhere you study or work.

Your partner responds to each with **agreement, partial agreement, or disagreement** — never just *"OK."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Speed over polish. This wakes up tense switching, opinion framing and the reflex to respond
> rather than wait. Note anyone who answers without a reason; that is the single most common B1 shortfall.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — النموذج
Watch the model discussion excerpt in the portal, then read the transcript slice:

> **Coach:** Some people say social media has made us worse at conversation. What do you think?
> **Learner:** I'd partly agree, actually. I think it's made us better at short exchanges and much worse at long ones. If I have a serious conversation now, I notice I want to check my phone after five minutes — which never used to happen.
> **Coach:** Is that the platform's fault, though?
> **Learner:** That's a fair challenge. I wouldn't say it's entirely the platform. But the apps are designed to interrupt you, so I'd say the responsibility is shared.
> **Coach:** Has anything changed for you personally?
> **Learner:** Yes. I've deleted two apps since January. The interesting thing is what happened afterwards — for the first week I was genuinely bored, and I kept picking the phone up anyway. Then around day ten I started reading again, which I hadn't done properly for years.
> **Coach:** And if you got a job that required you to be online all day?
> **Learner:** Then I'd have to separate the two. My brother works in marketing, and he told me he keeps a second phone with nothing on it. I thought that sounded extreme, but I understand it now.

Notice what the learner does **not** do: deliver a monologue, avoid the challenge, or answer with a bare
opinion. Every turn carries an opinion plus a reason, a concession, or a piece of evidence.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Learners identify one opinion+reason, one concession, one narrative stretch, one hypothetical
> and one reported statement. Then ask where the coach introduced something unmemorisable.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة (what a strong B1 discussion sounds like)

The rubric has five dimensions. Score each **0–5** from evidence across the whole discussion, never from an
impression formed in the first minute.

| Dimension | At-threshold evidence |
|---|---|
| **Fluency** | speaks in connected stretches; hesitation does not repeatedly halt the discussion |
| **Coherence** | ideas are ordered and signposted; a listener can follow the argument without asking |
| **Accuracy** | Stage-2 structures usually carry the intended meaning and time |
| **Range** | draws on perfect, narrative past, conditionals, relatives, reported speech, passive and connectors as the topic requires |
| **Interaction** | responds to what was actually said; follows up, concedes, challenges, and repairs |

Four habits separate a discussion that reaches the threshold from one that does not, and none of them is about
knowing more grammar.

**One: every opinion arrives with a reason.** An unjustified opinion is a dead turn — there is nothing for the
other person to take hold of. Attach the reason in the same breath, not after being asked for it.

**Two: agreement is graded, not binary.** Total agreement ends the topic and blank disagreement stalls it.
Partial agreement is the engine of a real discussion, because it obliges you to say which part and why.

**Three: a story is used as evidence, not as filler.** The strongest discussions move between the abstract and
the concrete. You make a claim, you show the incident that produced it, and then you return to the claim. That
movement is also what naturally recruits the whole tense system.

**Four: a challenge is an invitation.** When someone pushes back, the weak move is to abandon the position or
repeat it louder. The strong move is to concede the reasonable part and then hold the rest — *"That's a fair
point, but I'd still say…"* — which is the single clearest marker that you are discussing rather than reciting.

**Length is not the same as talking a lot.** A twelve-minute discussion where you speak sixty percent of the
time and never respond to anything scores below an eight-minute one where every turn connects.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Read the actual bands from `curriculum/05-assessment-suite.md` when scoring. This card explains
> the dimensions to learners; do not invent a threshold in the room.

## 🗡️ Your Arsenal — ذخيرتك (the strategies, not the script)

| Function | Lines |
|---|---|
| **Justify** | *The reason I say that is… · mainly because… · which matters because…* |
| **Partly agree** | *I'd partly agree. · Up to a point. · That's true of X, less so of Y.* |
| **Disagree softly** | *I see it differently, actually. · I'm not sure I'd go that far.* |
| **Concede then hold** | *That's a fair point, but I'd still say… · Fair enough — although…* |
| **Buy time honestly** | *That's a good question, let me think. · I've never been asked that.* |
| **Bring evidence** | *Something happened last year that changed my mind…* |
| **Hypothesise** | *If that happened, I'd probably… · Supposing it did…* |
| **Report** | *A colleague told me she'd already tried it.* |
| **Hand the floor over** | *What's your take? · Would you say the same?* |
| **Repair** | *Sorry — what I mean is… · Let me put that another way.* |

Use these as **strategies you can reach for**, not sentences to recite. A memorised phrase delivered at the
wrong moment is more obvious than a hesitation.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — عيادة النطق (top-one tune-up)
Take the **single** highest-impact target from your L03 clinic — the one most likely to affect whether you are
easy to follow. Run one short ladder and stop:

> cue → phrase → one discussion sentence → one unrehearsed sentence

> *I'd **PROB**ably say it dependz ən the **CON**text.*

Then put the card away. Three minutes should settle your mouth, not fill your head with monitoring. During the
mock, meaning and interaction come first.

> 🎧 Record the discussion sentence once. No perfection loop on mock day.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~3 min.** Prioritise a meaning-affecting or follow-ability target over a cosmetic one. Never introduce a
> new feature today.

## 🏋️ Train — البروفة الكاملة (the full mock)

In pairs or threes. One learner discusses, the others assess with the rubric card, then rotate.

### Part 1 — Opinion + challenge *(~4 min)*
The assessor picks one:
- Should university be free for everyone?
- Is working from home better for most people?
- Do phones make us less patient?
- Should there be a limit on how much people can earn?
- Is it better to specialise early or stay broad?

State your position, justify it, and answer **two** follow-ups. The assessor must **push back at least once**.

### Part 2 — Story as evidence *(~4 min)*
Tell a detailed story that supports or complicates your position. Set the scene, connect the events, include
one thing that had happened before, and land on the point it proves.

### Part 3 — Hypothetical + reported *(~4 min)*
Answer two closing questions:
- *What would you do if the opposite were true?*
- *What have other people told you about this, and did you agree?*

Then ask the assessor **one** question back.

**Assessors:** one evidence note plus one next action per dimension. No adjectives without a quotation.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~15 min.** Keep assessor behaviour identical for everyone: two follow-ups, one genuine push-back, two
> closing questions. Support route may hold the prompt card and phrase bank and run eight minutes; record that
> accommodation. Stretch route chairs the discussion and manages the topic change itself. Time it visibly —
> learners need to feel how long twelve minutes is.

## 🎤 Your Turn — دورك (self-assessment from evidence)
Score your own discussion **0–5** on all five dimensions, then write:

- one dimension you can **prove** is at threshold, with the words you used
- one dimension that is **not** there yet
- the **two** things you will polish before L05 — and nothing more

> 🎙️ **Optional recording for private comparison — this is not the milestone clip.**

> [!TEACHER]
> **~3 min feedback + 2 min self-assessment.** Compare peer scores with your own evidence and resolve the gap
> out loud; learners calibrate by hearing why. Give a maximum of **two** polish targets.
> **Success check:** sustains 8–12 minutes at or near the B1 threshold, justifies opinions unprompted, handles
> a push-back without abandoning or repeating the position, and leaves with a specific, manageable L05 plan.

## 📜 Your Orders — مهمتك
- Complete the rubric self-assessment from evidence, not from how the discussion felt.
- Prepare **topics and examples**, never memorised answers. Twelve minutes will find a script.
- Practise only your two polish targets. Reviewing all of Stage 2 the night before adds noise and removes control.
- Bring one story you know well. A story you have actually lived buys you three minutes of fluent speech.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- The format: **opinion + challenge → story as evidence → hypothetical + reported**.
- The rubric: **Fluency · Coherence · Accuracy · Range · Interaction**.
- Every opinion travels with a **reason**; partial agreement keeps the discussion alive.
- A push-back is an invitation: **concede the fair part, hold the rest**.
- B1 is coherent, justified and sustained — not error-free.
- The summative is the same shape as today. There is no trap waiting.
