the practical definition of B1.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Past continuous | *was/were + -ing* — a line, not a dot |
| **L02** | *while* / *when* | background + interruption in one sentence |
| **L03** | *used to* | habits and states that are **not true anymore** |
| **L04** | Setting a scene | layer all three so a listener can picture it |
| **L05** | A memorable moment (milestone) 🎙️ | a 6–8 sentence story + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** past continuous · past continuous against past simple with *while/when* · *used to* for past
habits and states · all three integrated into narrative.
**Accent Lab:** weak ***was*** /wəz/ and ***were*** /wə/ — present but never dropped · *-ing* linking with clean
/ŋ/ · ***used to*** → /ˈjuːstə/ as one word · narrative rhythm: flat background, sharp event · *-ed* endings
surviving at speed.
**Mindset:** stop translating — build the sentence directly, especially when describing a scene.
**Arabic support:** ~15% through L01–L03, easing to ~10% for L04–L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-3 memorable moment** — a strong proof-clip candidate, and audibly beyond the A2 trip narration.
- The ability to open a story so people actually listen to it.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **L01 must hold one meaning only: *in progress*.** The form takes two minutes; the meaning is the work.
> Learners who meet the past-simple contrast here will use the continuous for everything — that contrast is
> **L02**. Watch the dropped auxiliary (❌ *"I working at eight"*), which is exactly where a legitimately weak
> *was* becomes an absent one.
> **L02 should be anchored to the timeline, not to the words.** ▬▬ line = continuous, ▪️ dot = simple. Learners
> who memorise "*while* takes continuous" fail the moment a sentence is reordered. Show both mirror errors
> together — simple for the background, continuous for the event — and note that **either clause can come
> first**; only the comma moves.
> **L03 teaches *used to* and nothing else.** *Be used to* (accustomed) is a different structure; name the
> difference in one sentence if asked and move on. Two form traps: the **plain verb** (❌ *used to lived*) and the
> vanishing *d* in *didn't **use** to*. Make sure learners hear the built-in *not anymore* — that implication is
> the reason to choose the structure at all. Then prevent over-generalisation: one dated event takes the past
> simple.
> **L04 is integration, so correct by delayed feedback.** The predictable error is **continuous overuse** — a
> whole story in the background tense that never arrives at an event. Exercise 3 exists to expose exactly that.
> Prioritise layering over form accuracy here.
> **L05 is a proof-clip candidate.** Pair it with the A2 trip narration where consent allows; the gap is the
> clearest evidence of the stage. Score the **scene** and the **point**, not only the tenses.
