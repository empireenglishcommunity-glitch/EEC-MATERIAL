# Setting a Scene in a Story

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 3 · **Lesson 4**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L03
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit3-narrative-past.md` · **S2-U3-L04***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** open a story by setting the scene · layer background, habit and event across the three tenses of this unit · keep a short narrative smooth and connected.
> **Prioritise a well-layered story over perfect forms.** This is the integration lesson: correct **patterned** errors by delayed feedback after the story, never mid-sentence. A learner interrupted while narrating stops narrating.
> **The teachable insight is that stories open wide and then narrow.** Scene (continuous) → context or habit (*used to*) → the event (simple) → result. Learners who begin with the event have no story, just news.
> **Watch for continuous overuse.** Having just met a new tense, learners put the whole story in it. The background is continuous; the **events are simple**. Two or three continuous verbs at the start is plenty.
> **Support:** the four-layer skeleton card. **Stretch:** open with a sound or a feeling instead of a time.
> **Arabic ~10%** — mostly English now.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Two people can describe the same incident and only one of them is worth listening to. The difference is rarely
vocabulary — it is that the good storyteller **builds a scene first**, so the event lands somewhere. You now have
exactly the tenses that scene-building requires.

## 🔁 Warm-up — سخّن
One sentence in each of this unit's three forms: a *was/were + -ing*, a *while/when* pair, and a *used to*.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Confirms all three are available before asking learners to combine them under narrative pressure.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A short story, well opened (📹 in the portal).

> It **was** about ten at night and it **was raining** — properly raining, the kind we hardly ever get here. I
> **was driving** home from my sister's, and the roads **were** completely empty. I **used to** take the ring
> road every time, but that night something **told** me to go through the old town instead.
>
> **While** I **was waiting** at a red light, a man **knocked** on my window. He **was holding** a phone and he
> **looked** worried. His car **had** a flat tyre and his phone **was** dead.
>
> So I **drove** him to a garage. It **took** twenty minutes. He **offered** me money and I **said** no, and then
> he **gave** me a bag of oranges from his boot. I still **don't** know why he **had** oranges.

**Notice the layers.** Paragraph one is almost all **background** — weather, time, roads, plus one *used to* for
context. Paragraph two brings the **interruption**. Paragraph three is a run of **simple** past events. The scene
is wide, then it narrows.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play once for the story. Second pass: learners label each paragraph *scene · interruption ·
> events*. Ask where the first past-simple verb appears — surprisingly late, and that is the craft.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**A story has four layers, and each one has its tense.**

| Layer | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 **Scene** — when, where, weather, mood | past **continuous** + *was/were* | *It was raining and I was driving home.* |
| 2 **Context** — what normally happened | ***used to*** | *I used to take the ring road.* |
| 3 **Event** — what cut in | past **simple** | *A man knocked on my window.* |
| 4 **Result** — what you did, how it ended | past **simple** | *I drove him to a garage.* |

**Scene-setting openers:**

> *It was about ten at night… · It was raining… · I was on my way home… · Nobody was around…*

**Words that move a story forward:**

> *Suddenly · At that moment · Just as · Then · So · In the end · A few minutes later*

> ⚠️ خد بالك: المشهد بس هو اللي بياخد المستمر. الأحداث نفسها بتاخد الماضي البسيط.

**The most common structural mistake** is starting at layer 3:

> *"A man knocked on my window."* — no story yet; the listener has nothing to picture.
> *"It was raining and I was waiting at a light when a man knocked on my window."* — now it is a story.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Board the four layers as a skeleton and leave it up for Train. The bad-versus-good opening
> comparison is the part learners remember.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Narrative rhythm: the scene is smooth, the event is sharp.**

The background runs on with weak forms and linking, almost hurried. Then the event verb gets a clear beat and a
small pause before it:

> *It wəz raining ənd I wəz driving home* ⟋ *when a man **KNOCKED** on my window.*

Say a story with every word equally stressed, then again with the background flattened and the event pushed. The
second version sounds like storytelling; the first sounds like a police statement.

**Keep the endings alive under speed** — this is where *-ed* and *-ing* die:

> *knock**ed*** /t/ · *offer**ed*** /d/ · *want**ed*** /ɪd/ · *wait**ing*** /ŋ/

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"It was raining and I was waiting at the light when someone knocked on my window."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on the flat-background/sharp-event contrast, three on endings at speed. Both are
> immediately audible in the recording, so flag them there rather than in isolation.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — build the scene.** Turn each bare event into a two-part sentence with a background.
1. *The phone rang.* → __________________________
2. *I lost my wallet.* → __________________________
3. *We met by accident.* → __________________________

**Exercise 2 — choose the tense.**
1. It ______ (be) late and everyone ______ (sleep).
2. I ______ (used to / walk) to school when I ______ (be) a child.
3. While we ______ (wait), the manager ______ (come) out.
4. In the end, we ______ (get) a refund.

**Exercise 3 — fix the story.** This is all background and never arrives:
> *It was raining. I was walking. I was thinking about work. I was feeling tired. I was going home.*

Rewrite it so something **happens**.

**Exercise 4 — build one.** Use the four layers to tell a real small incident in five or six sentences.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex2:** was / were sleeping · used to walk / was · were waiting / came · got. Ex3 is the key
> exercise — it isolates continuous overuse, which is this lesson's predictable error. Ex4 rehearses L05.

## 📜 Your Orders — مهمتك
- Post a five-or-six-sentence story with a proper opening.
- **Steal openings.** Next time you watch something in English, note the first two lines of any anecdote. Scene
  first is a habit, and habits are copied more easily than they are taught.

---

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Four layers: **scene → context → event → result.**
- Scene = **continuous**. Events = **simple**. Context = ***used to***.
- Do not start at the event; the listener has nothing to picture.
- Movers: *suddenly · just as · then · in the end*.
- Flat background, sharp event — and keep the endings alive.
