# Past Continuous: "I Was Doing…"

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 3 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 2
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit3-narrative-past.md` · **S2-U3-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** form the past continuous (*was/were + -ing*) in all three sentence types · describe a past situation **in progress** · use *at 8 o'clock · this time yesterday · all morning*.
> **Keep one meaning today: *in progress*, not completed.** The past continuous describes the situation **around** an event, never the event itself. The explicit contrast with the past simple is **L02** — introduce it here and learners will use the continuous for everything.
> **The form is easy; the meaning is the work.** Learners can build *was working* in two minutes and then use it to mean *worked*. Spend your time on "was it finished, or was it going on?"
> **Do not let the auxiliary drop.** ❌ *"I working at eight"* — and note that *was* is weak in a statement, which makes it easy to swallow entirely.
> **Support:** the timeline picture + the frame. **Stretch:** two things in progress at once — *"I was cooking while my brother was studying."*
> **Arabic ~15%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Until now every past sentence you made described a **finished** action. But most stories need a background too:
the weather, what people were doing, what was going on when the interesting part happened. That background is
what this tense is for.

> والماضي البسيط بيقول الحاجة خلصت، والزمن ده بيقول كانت مستمرة.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Describing an evening (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** What **were** you **doing** at eight last night? I called twice.
> **B:** Sorry! I **was cooking**, and the kids **were watching** something loud.
> **A:** Ah. I **wasn't sure** if you **were working**.
> **B:** No, not last night. **This time yesterday** I **was sitting** in traffic for an hour.
> **A:** All that time?
> **B:** **All morning**, honestly. Nothing **was moving**.

**Notice:** not one of those actions is presented as finished. Every one was **in progress** at the moment being
described — that is the whole meaning of the tense.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| What were you doing? | كنت بتعمل إيه؟ |
| I was cooking. | كنت بطبخ |
| They were watching TV. | كانوا بيتفرجوا |
| Nothing was moving. | مفيش حاجة كانت بتتحرك |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: for each verb, learners answer one question — *finished, or going on?*
> Every answer is "going on".

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The form: *was / were* + verb**-ing**.**

| Person | | |
|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it | **was** | *I **was working**.* |
| you / we / they | **were** | *They **were working**.* |

**Negative — add *n't* to the auxiliary:**

> *I **wasn't** listening.* · *They **weren't** waiting.*

**Question — swap the auxiliary to the front:**

> ***Was*** *he sleeping?* · ***Were*** *you working?* · ***What were*** *you doing?*

> ⚠️ **خد بالك:** الفعل المساعد لازم يبقى موجود قبل الفعل. مينفعش تقول *I working*.

**The meaning: in progress at that moment.** Picture a line, not a dot:

| | |
|---|---|
| *I **worked** until six.* | ▪️ finished — a completed action |
| *I **was working** at six.* | ▬▬ in progress — it started before six and continued after |

**Time phrases that go with it:**

> *at eight o'clock · this time yesterday · all morning · all day · at that moment*

**And two things at once** both take the continuous:

> *I **was cooking** while my brother **was studying**.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on form, four on the line-against-dot image. Do not open the *when/while* contrast —
> that is L02. Today the continuous simply describes what was going on.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *was* and *were* are weak.** In a normal statement they almost disappear:

> *was* → /wəz/ · *were* → /wə/
> *"I **wəz** working"* · *"They **wə** waiting"*

They only go strong when you contradict somebody: *"I **WAS** listening!"*

**Part 2 — but never delete them.** Weak is not absent. *"I working"* is not a sentence, and this is exactly
where a weak form becomes a dropped one.

**Part 3 — link the *-ing* into the next word**, and keep /ŋ/ clean with no hard /g/:

> *was cooking and* → *was-cookin-**gand*** · *were waiting outside* → *were-waitin-**goutside***

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I was working all morning and they were waiting outside."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes weak forms, one on not deleting them, two on *-ing* linking. The weak-but-present
> balance is the target: learners either over-pronounce or drop entirely.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *was* or *were*?**
1. I ______ sleeping. 2. They ______ talking. 3. She ______ reading.
4. You ______ driving. 5. We ______ eating. 6. It ______ raining.

**Exercise 2 — make it negative, then a question.**
1. *He was studying.* → neg: __________ / question: __________
2. *They were listening.* → neg: __________ / question: __________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I working at eight o'clock.* → __________________________
2. *They was waiting for me.* → __________________________
3. *What you were doing?* → __________________________
4. *She were not listening.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** What were you doing at these times yesterday: 8am · 2pm · 10pm?

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** was · were · was · were · were · was. **Ex3:** *I **was** working* · *They **were**
> waiting* · *What **were you** doing?* · *She **wasn't** listening.* Ex3.3 is the word-order one worth
> repeating aloud.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **was / were + verb-ing.**
- Meaning: **in progress**, not finished — a line, not a dot.
- Negative: *wasn't · weren't*. Question: auxiliary first.
- *was* /wəz/ and *were* /wə/ are weak — but **never dropped**.
- Two things at once: both continuous.
