# *While* / *When*: Interrupted Actions

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 3 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit3-narrative-past.md` · **S2-U3-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** combine past continuous and past simple in one sentence · use *while* for the background and *when* for the event · tell a short interrupted-action story.
> **Anchor everything to the timeline, not to the words.** ▬▬ long line = **continuous** (the background). ▪️ dot = **simple** (the event that cuts in). Learners who memorise "*while* takes continuous" fail as soon as a sentence is reordered; learners who see the timeline do not.
> **The two classic errors are mirror images:** simple for the background — ❌ *"I walked when it started to rain"* — and continuous for the event — ❌ *"while I was walking, it was starting to rain."* Show both, side by side, as wrong for the same reason.
> **Either clause can come first.** *"I was walking when it started"* and *"When it started, I was walking"* are both correct; only the comma moves. Learners assume the order is fixed.
> **Support:** the timeline graphic stays visible all lesson. **Stretch:** three-part stories — background, interruption, result.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Stories are not lists of finished events. Something is going on, and then something cuts across it — that is what
makes an event worth telling. This one sentence pattern is the engine of almost every anecdote you will ever
tell.

> والقاعدة في صورة: الخلفية خط طويل، والحدث نقطة بتقطع الخط.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Three small disasters (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** I **was walking** home **when** it **started** to rain — no jacket, of course.
> **B:** That's nothing. **While** I **was cooking** last night, the electricity **went** off.
> **A:** In the middle of cooking?
> **B:** In the middle of everything. And **when** the lights **came** back, the food **was** burnt.
> **A:** My phone **died while** I **was talking** to my manager.
> **B:** What **did** you **do**?
> **A:** I **called** him back. He **was laughing when** he **answered**.

**Notice the shape.** In every sentence one verb is a **long background** (*was walking · was cooking · was
talking*) and one is a **short event** (*started · went · died · came*). The background is continuous; the event
is simple.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| I was walking when it started to rain. | كنت ماشي لما الدنيا مطرت |
| While I was cooking… | وأنا بطبخ… |
| The electricity went off. | النور قطع |
| My phone died. | تليفوني فصل |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners draw the timeline for each sentence — a line and a dot. Drawing
> it is worth more than naming it.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**One picture explains the whole lesson.**

> ▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬▬ **background** → past **continuous**
> ⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀▪️⠀⠀⠀⠀⠀ **event that cuts in** → past **simple**

**1) The default pairing:**

> *I **was walking*** *home* ***when*** *it **started** to rain.*
> ***While*** *I **was cooking**, the electricity **went** off.*

**2) Which word goes where.**

| Word | Usually introduces | |
|---|---|---|
| **while** | the **long** background | *While I was driving…* |
| **when** | the **short** event | *…when my phone rang.* |

**3) Either clause can come first.** Put the *while*/*when* clause at the front and add a comma:

> *I was reading **when** she called.* = ***When** she called*, I was reading.*
> *The lights went out **while** I was cooking.* = ***While** I was cooking*, the lights went out.*

> ⚠️ **الغلطتين المتوقعتين:** إنك تحط الماضي البسيط للخلفية، أو المستمر للحدث. الخلفية دايمًا خط، والحدث نقطة.

**4) Two backgrounds at once → both continuous, joined by *while*:**

> *I **was studying** while my sister **was watching** television.*

**5) Two quick events one after another → both simple, joined by *when*:**

> *When the bell **rang**, everybody **stood** up.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Board the timeline first and leave it up. Then run sentences aloud and have learners point: line
> or dot? Only after that give the *while/when* tendency — as a tendency, not a law.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the two clauses are two rhythm groups, with a small pause between them.**

> *I was walking home* ⟋ *when it started to rain.*

Said as one flat run, the listener cannot hear where the background ends and the event begins. The pause does
the punctuation.

**Part 2 — the background often rises slightly; the event falls.**

> *While I was cooking* ↗️ *the electricity went off.* ↘️

**Part 3 — keep the weak forms from L01 inside the background**, then hit the event verb clearly:

> *I wəz **WALKING** home when it **STARTED** to rain.*

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I was driving home when my phone rang."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Model the pause exaggeratedly, then normally. Learners who run the clauses together usually also
> lose the tense contrast, so the prosody drill supports the grammar directly.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — choose the tense.**
1. I ______ (read) when the phone ______ (ring).
2. While she ______ (drive), it ______ (start) to snow.
3. They ______ (wait) outside when I ______ (arrive).
4. When the film ______ (finish), we ______ (go) home.

**Exercise 2 — *while* or *when*?**
1. I was cooking ______ the doorbell rang.
2. ______ I was walking to work, I saw an accident.
3. He broke his leg ______ he was playing football.
4. ______ she called, I was asleep.

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I walked home when it started to rain.* → __________________________
2. *While I was cooking, the lights were going out.* → __________________________
3. *When I was arriving, they were leaving already.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — tell it.** Three sentences: something that was going on, something that interrupted it, and what
you did next.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** was reading / rang · was driving / started · were waiting / arrived · finished / went.
> **Ex2:** when · While · while · When. **Ex3:** *I **was walking** home when…* · *…the lights **went** out* ·
> *When I **arrived**…*. Ex1.4 is the useful exception: two short events, so both simple.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **Background = continuous** ▬▬. **Event = simple** ▪️.
- **while** usually takes the background; **when** usually takes the event.
- Either clause can come first — front it and add a comma.
- Two backgrounds → both continuous. Two quick events → both simple.
- Pause between the clauses; it is the audible punctuation.
