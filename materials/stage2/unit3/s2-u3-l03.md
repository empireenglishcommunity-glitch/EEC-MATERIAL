# *Used to*: Past Habits & States

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 3 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit3-narrative-past.md` · **S2-U3-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** use *used to* for past habits and states that are no longer true · form the negative and question · contrast then with now.
> **Teach only *used to*.** ***Be* used to** (= accustomed) is a different structure and will be met later. If a learner raises it, name the difference in one sentence and move on — merging them here reliably breaks both.
> **The form error is the base verb:** ❌ *"I used to lived there."* *Used to* is followed by the **plain** verb, always. And in the negative and question the *d* disappears: *didn't **use** to · Did you **use** to…?*
> **The meaning carries an implication learners must hear: *not anymore*.** *"I used to smoke"* tells the listener you stopped. That implication is the reason to choose this structure over the past simple.
> **Support:** the then/now card. **Stretch:** three changes with a reason for each.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Change is one of the most natural things to talk about: your city, your job, your habits, who you were at
eighteen. *Used to* is the structure English reaches for whenever the point is that **something is different
now**.

> ولو استخدمت الماضي البسيط بس، اللي بيسمعك مش هيعرف إن الحاجة اتغيرت ولا لأ.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Talking about how things changed (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** This street **used to** be so quiet. There **used to** be a bakery right there.
> **B:** I remember. I **used to** buy bread there every morning.
> **A:** **Did** you **use to** live near here?
> **B:** For years. I **didn't use to** like the area at first, honestly — but it grew on me. I **used to**
> complain about the noise, and **now** I miss it.
> **A:** Things change. I **used to** walk everywhere; **these days** I drive.
> **B:** Me too. I **don't** walk **anymore**.

**Notice:** every single one of those sentences means *and that is no longer the case*. The bakery has gone, he
has moved, she drives now. The structure carries the change.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| It used to be quiet. | كانت هادية زمان |
| I used to buy bread there. | كنت بشتري عيش من هناك |
| I didn't use to like it. | ماكنتش بحبها |
| I don't walk anymore. | مابقيتش بمشي |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: after each sentence, learners say what is true **now**. That inference is
> the meaning of the structure.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) Positive: *used to* + the plain verb.**

> *I **used to** smoke.* · *She **used to** live in Tanta.* · *There **used to** be a cinema here.*

Nothing changes for person — *I / you / he / she / we / they* all take **used to**.

> ⚠️ **خد بالك:** الفعل بعد **used to** بييجي أساسي خالص. مينفعش *used to lived* ولا *used to living*.

**2) Negative and question: the *d* disappears.**

| | |
|---|---|
| negative | *I **didn't use to** like coffee.* |
| question | ***Did** you **use to** play football?* |

Because *did* already carries the past, *use* goes back to its plain form. This is the same rule you learned at
A2 for *didn't go*.

**3) It works for habits **and** states.**

| | |
|---|---|
| habit — a repeated action | *I **used to** train every day.* |
| state — a lasting situation | *I **used to** have long hair.* · *There **used to** be a shop here.* |

**4) The built-in meaning: *not anymore*.** Pair it with change language:

> *I used to play a lot. **Now** I don't.* · ***These days** I drive.* · *I don't smoke **anymore**.*

**5) When *not* to use it.** For a single finished event, use the past simple:

> *I **went** to Rome in 2019.* ✅ — one event
> *I used to go to Rome in 2019.* ❌ — not a habit, and it has a date

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on the three forms, two on habit-against-state, two on the *not anymore*
> implication. Point 5 prevents over-generalisation, which is the predictable next error.

## 🗡️ Your Arsenal — ذخيرتك

| English | بالعربي | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I used to… | كنت بـ… | *I used to swim a lot.* |
| I didn't use to… | ماكنتش بـ… | *I didn't use to like it.* |

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *used to* is one reduced chunk.** It is never said as two careful words:

> **used to** → /ˈjuːstə/ — *"YOOS-tuh"*

The /d/ vanishes completely into the /t/. Learners who say *"yoozd too"* sound like they are reading, and
listeners often miss the structure entirely.

**Part 2 — the stress lands on *used*, and the following verb keeps its own beat:**

> *I **YOOS**-tuh **PLAY** football.*

**Part 3 — the negative keeps the same reduction:**

> *didn't use to* → /ˈdɪdəntjuːstə/ — *"didn-choos-tuh"*

> ولاحظ إن الحرف **d** بيختفي في النطق، فالكلمتين بيبقوا كلمة واحدة.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I used to live there, but I didn't use to like it."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Drill /ˈjuːstə/ chorally until it is one word. This single reduction does more for intelligibility
> here than any grammar correction.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — complete with *used to* + the verb.**
1. I ______ (play) volleyball at school.
2. There ______ (be) a cinema on this street.
3. She ______ (have) very long hair.

**Exercise 2 — negative and question.**
1. *I used to like coffee.* → negative: __________________________
2. *You used to work here.* → question: __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I used to lived in Cairo.* → __________________________
2. *I didn't used to like it.* → __________________________
3. *Did you used to play football?* → __________________________
4. *I used to go to Rome in 2019.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — then and now.** Three things that were true about you five years ago and are not true now. Add
what is true **now** for each.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** used to play · used to be · used to have. **Ex3:** *used to **live*** · *didn't **use**
> to* · *Did you **use** to* · *I **went** to Rome in 2019*. Ex3.4 needs the reason said aloud: one event with a
> date is not a habit.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **used to + plain verb** — no person changes.
- Negative **didn't use to**, question **Did you use to…?** — the *d* goes.
- Works for habits **and** states: *used to have · there used to be*.
- It always implies **not anymore**.
- One dated event → past simple, not *used to*.
- Say it as one word: **/ˈjuːstə/**.
