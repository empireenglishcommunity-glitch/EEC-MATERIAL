## 🔁 Warm-up — سخّن (سلسلة)
Rapid chain: a *was/were + -ing* → a *while* clause → a *when* event → a *used to* → a scene-setting opener.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** One each, fast, no repeats. This sweeps the unit before the narrative load rises.

---

## 👂 Watch & Listen — النموذج
A model memorable moment (📹 in the portal).

> This **happened** about four years ago. I **was working** in a small company then, and I **used to** stay late
> almost every night — nobody **asked** me to, I just **did**. It **was** a Thursday and the office **was**
> nearly empty.
>
> I **was finishing** a report **when** the fire alarm **went** off. Everyone **was walking** out slowly,
> half-laughing, because it **had** happened twice that month for nothing. But **while** we **were standing**
> outside, we **saw** actual smoke coming from the kitchen window.
>
> The fire brigade **arrived** in about six minutes. Nobody **was hurt** — someone **had left** a towel on a hot
> plate. But I **remember** standing in the car park at nine at night, holding a report nobody **read**, and
> thinking: I **don't** want to be the person who stays late anymore.
>
> I **left** that job four months later. So it **was** memorable, but not for the fire.

**Notice:** scene and habit first (*was working · used to stay*), the interruption (*when the alarm went off*),
events in the simple past, and a **point** at the end. The last line is what makes it a story rather than an
incident.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Play once for the story, once for structure. Ask what the final sentence adds — it converts an
> anecdote into a memorable moment, which is the task word.

## 🧩 The Checklist — تشيك ليست الحكاية
1. ✅ **Set the scene** — when, where, what was going on (continuous)
2. ✅ One ***used to*** for context or habit
3. ✅ One clear **event** in the past simple
4. ✅ One ***while*** or ***when*** pairing
5. ✅ A **result** or ending
6. ⭐ A **point** — why you remember it
7. ⭐ Handle one **question** about the story you did not prepare

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Board it and hand out the card. Items 1 and 6 separate a B1 narrative from an A2 sequence of
> events.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — عيادة النطق
Drill **your** weakest from this unit:

- **weak *was / were*** — /wəz/, /wə/ — present but never dropped
- ***-ing* linking** and clean /ŋ/ with no hard /g/
- ***used to*** → /ˈjuːstə/ as one word
- **narrative rhythm** — flat background, sharp event
- ***-ed* endings** surviving at speed

> 🎧 **Record & compare** your weakest one only.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** One personal target each, from your L01–L04 notes. A dropped auxiliary is the priority — it
> removes the tense that carries the whole scene.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر (Unit 3 wrap)
- **was / were + -ing** = in progress; a line, not a dot.
- **Background continuous, event simple** — *I was walking when it started*.
- ***while*** usually the background, ***when*** usually the event; either clause can come first.
- **used to + plain verb** = a habit or state that is **not true anymore**.
- Stories open with a **scene** and end with a **point**.

## ✅ Self-check — Unit 3 mini-quiz
1. *I ___ (work) when she called.* → **was working**
2. *They ___ (wait) outside.* → **were waiting**
3. *While I ___ (cook), the lights went out.* → **was cooking**
4. *When the bell ___ (ring), we stood up.* → **rang**
5. *I ___ live in Tanta.* (habit, not now) → **used to**
6. *I didn't ___ to like coffee.* → **use**
7. *Did you ___ to play football?* → **use**
8. *I used to ___ there.* (live) → **live**
9. Which tense sets the scene? → **past continuous**
10. Which tense carries the events? → **past simple**
