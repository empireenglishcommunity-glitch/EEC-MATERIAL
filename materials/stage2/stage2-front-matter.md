## Welcome, Legionary — أهلاً بالليجيونري

You arrive with A2. You can narrate a familiar past event, make a plan, compare two options, give advice, and
keep an ordinary conversation alive. Stage 2 is where that stops being a set of tasks you can complete and
starts being a language you can think in.

> إنت داخل المرحلة دي وإنت قادر تحكي وتخطط وتقارن. الجديد إنك هتبقى تناقش، وتقول رأيك بسبب، وتتخيل.

**By the end of Stage 2 you will be able to express and justify an opinion, tell a detailed story, handle a
hypothetical, report what someone else said, and sustain a discussion of eight to twelve minutes — largely in
English.**

That last phrase is the real content of this stage. B1 is called *independence* for a reason: the measure is not
how much grammar you have collected but whether you can cope when the conversation stops being predictable.

> الهدف الحقيقي مش عدد القواعد. الهدف إنك تكمل الكلام لما الموقف يخرج عن المتوقع.

## Your rank — رُتبتك

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rank** | Legionary — الليجيونري |
| **CEFR** | B1 — Intermediate |
| **Entry** | A2 achieved through Stage 1 or placement evidence |
| **What it means** | you can handle most everyday and work situations, and give reasons for what you think |

The rank path: Recruit (A1) → Citizen (A2) → **Legionary (B1)** → **Confident** (B2, the Coronation) →
Sovereign (C1).

A Citizen participates in a conversation. A Legionary can hold a position in one — state it, support it, adjust
it when challenged, and stay in the exchange instead of ending it.

> الرُتبة معناها إنك بقيت تقدر تمسك موقف في نقاش، مش بس تشارك فيه.

## The roadmap — خريطة المرحلة

**12 units · 60 lessons · roughly 150–200 guided hours, about 16–24 weeks at around 7 hours a week.**

| Unit | Theme | What you take from it |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Present Perfect (1) — Experiences | talk about life experiences; *ever/never*; *been* against *gone* |
| **2** | Present Perfect (2) — Recent & Unfinished | *just/already/yet*; *for/since*; the perfect-against-past contrast |
| **3** | Narrative Past — Continuous & *used to* | set a scene, interrupt an action, describe a habit that ended |
| **4** | First Conditional & Future Time | real conditions and their results; *when/as soon as/unless* |
| **5** | Hypotheticals — Second Conditional | imagine, advise with *If I were you*, and wish |
| **6** | Relative Clauses | say who, which, where and whose in one sentence instead of three |
| **7** | Reported Speech | report statements, questions, advice and requests accurately |
| **8** | The Passive | describe processes, facts and results when the doer does not matter |
| **9** | Connecting Ideas & Opinions | *because/so/although/however*; state and justify an opinion |
| **10** | Discussion Skills | agree, disagree politely, hedge, and give reasons under pressure |
| **11** | Storytelling & Real-World Topics | the whole stage in use — work, technology, city life, the unexpected |
| **12** | Putting It Together | retrieval, an accent clinic, the full mock, and the **B1 summative** |

**The grammar spine:** present perfect for experience → recent and unfinished time, and the contrast with the
past simple → past continuous and *used to* → first conditional and future time clauses → second conditional
and *wish* → defining relative clauses → reported speech → the present and past passive → connectors and
opinion structure → discussion functions → all of it integrated.

Stage 2 is built in three movements. **Units 1–3 complete the time system** that Stage 1 opened, because
storytelling is what the perfect and the narrative past exist for. **Units 4–8 add the reasoning tools** —
conditions, hypotheticals, relative clauses, reported speech and the passive — which are what let you talk
about complicated things without breaking them into short flat sentences. **Units 9–11 build the discourse
layer**, where the grammar becomes discussion. Unit 12 consolidates and assesses.

**Vocabulary target:** roughly **2,000–2,500 words cumulative** — opinions, work and study, connectors,
collocations, and topics that begin to leave the concrete behind. All of it is collected in the generated
glossary at the back of this book.

**Accent Lab priorities:**
- **linking** — a final consonant joining the next vowel, so words stop arriving separately
- **weak forms** — *and, to, of, for, can, was, were* collapsing toward /ə/
- **reductions** — *gonna, wanna, gotta*, and the *'ve / 's / 'll / 'd* contractions
- **intonation** — questions, lists, contrast, emphasis, and how a turn ends
- **stress-timing**, the American beat: content words carry the beat, function words get squeezed
- your own residual sounds, identified from your recordings rather than assumed from a list

> النطق هنا بقى إيقاع، مش حروف. الفرق بين كلام سهل المتابعة وكلام متعب هو الإيقاع.

## The Arabic fade — الدعم العربي بيقف

Stage 1 faded Arabic gradually. Stage 2 finishes the job.

| Units | Arabic support | What that looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **1–5** | ~15% | short glosses on new concepts and on the one contrast that prevents a durable error |
| **6–8** | ~12% | glosses on the hardest form only |
| **9** | ~10% | reassurance and mindset lines |
| **10–11** | ~5% | near-immersion; almost everything arrives in English |
| **12** | **0%** | full immersion — English only, including the assessment |

This is not help being taken away. Familiar lesson structure, worked examples, tables and repeated English
instructions gradually do the work that translation used to do. By Unit 12 you will not need the Arabic,
and the last week of a stage is the wrong moment to discover you still do.

> لو الشرح بقى إنجليزي أكتر، ده مش لأننا سحبنا الدعم. ده لأنك بقيت تستقبل اللغة باللغة.

## The plateau — الهضبة

There is one thing about this stage you should hear before it happens rather than after.

Somewhere in the middle of B1, most learners feel they have **stopped improving**. Stage 0 and Stage 1 produced
visible jumps: you could not order food, then you could. B1 does not work like that. The gains move into
fluency, accuracy, range and confidence — real improvements that are hard to feel from the inside, arriving at
the same time as the Arabic support fades and the tasks get longer. So the work gets harder and the sense of
progress gets quieter, in the same month.

**This is the normal shape of intermediate learning, not evidence of failure, and it is the point where most
self-taught learners quit.** The answer is not to study harder or start a new method. It is to keep the routine,
keep recording, and compare like with like: your ninety-second Unit-11 recording against your Stage-1 milestone,
not against how fluent you wish you sounded.

> الإحساس إنك واقف مكانك طبيعي في المرحلة دي، ومش دليل إنك بتفشل. ده أشهر مكان الناس بتسيب فيه.

## How to use this book — إزاي تستخدم الكتاب

Every teaching lesson keeps the same route. Review, milestone and assessment lessons adapt the middle to the
task.

| Section | What you do there |
|---|---|
| 🎯 **Your Conquest** | name the ability you are building |
| 👑 **Why this matters** | connect the language to a real reason to use it |
| 🔁 **Warm-up** | retrieve earlier language before adding more |
| 👂 **Watch & Listen** | meet the language inside a complete situation |
| 🧩 **Decode it** | notice the form, the meaning, and the contrast that matters |
| 🗡️ **Your Arsenal** | collect the words and functional phrases |
| 🎙️ **Accent Lab** | perceive, cue, produce, record, and compare |
| 🏋️ **Train** | practise accurately before the speaking load rises |
| 🎤 **Your Turn** | connect the lesson into meaningful speech |
| 📜 **Your Orders** | carry one small action into real life |
| 🧠 **Remember** | compress the lesson into what must survive |
| ✅ **Self-check** | verify and correct without waiting for a teacher |

**Four rules make Stage 2 work:**

1. **Attach a reason to every opinion.** An unjustified opinion is a dead turn at B1 — there is nothing for the
   other person to hold on to, and it is the single most common thing that keeps a learner below threshold.
2. **Stop translating in your head.** Translation is a bridge, and at some point standing on a bridge is slower
   than walking. Build English-only zones — one podcast, one hour, one topic — and grow them.
3. **Talk through the gap, never around it.** When the word is missing, describe it, approximate it, or say what
   it is not. Silence and switching language are the two habits this stage exists to remove.
4. **Record before you feel ready.** In a stage where progress is hard to feel, a recording is the only honest
   evidence you have. Keep them.

> ⭐ **القاعدة الذهبية:** الاستمرار أهم من الشدّة. ارجع بسرعة، حتى لو رجعت بعشر دقايق بس.

## What gets recorded — إيه اللي بيتسجل

Each unit ends in a speaking task. Those recordings do three jobs: they give the coach evidence for specific
feedback, they let you compare your current speech with your own earlier speech, and they build the proof
timeline that reaches four points at the end of this stage — Day 1, Stage 0, A2, and **B1**.

Recording for learning is not permission to publish. Storage, comparison and any public use follow the consent
tier in the before/after protocol, that tier is yours to choose, and you can withdraw it later. You can
complete this entire course without ever granting marketing use.

> التسجيل دليل ليك وأداة تعليم. أي استخدام تاني محتاج موافقة منفصلة وواضحة، ومن حقك تسحبها.

## How you are assessed — التقييم

- **Entry:** A2 evidence through the Stage-1 summative or the placement process.
- **End of every unit:** a short quiz plus a speaking task — formative checks that direct feedback and the
  review queue, not gates.
- **Unit 12, L03:** a personalised accent clinic built from your own recorded evidence, targeting the three
  features that most affect how easy you are to follow.
- **Unit 12, L04:** a full mock discussion in the exact shape of the summative, without a pass or fail label.
- **Stage-2 summative:** a mixed quiz plus an extended **opinion and discussion** task of 8–12 minutes, scored
  on **Fluency · Coherence · Accuracy · Range · Interaction** at the committed B1 threshold, in English only.
- **B1 milestone sample:** a separate standard-prompt recording, so Day 1, Stage 0, A2 and B1 can be compared
  fairly.

A green quiz is not proof of speaking ability, and a strong discussion is not proof that every structure is
stable. The determination uses the required evidence together, and it begins with the evidence rather than the
label.

> التقييم مش كمين. شكله معروف، ومعاييره ثابتة، والنتيجة تبدأ بالدليل قبل الاسم.

## An honest promise — وعد صادق

We promise structured practice toward **clear, confident, intelligible communication**. We do not promise a
native accent, effortless fluency in a fixed number of days, or progress without sustained use. Pronunciation
is scored for clarity and follow-ability. Your accent is your identity, not an error to be removed.

> مانوعدكش بلكنة أصلية ولا بطلاقة سريعة. نوعدك بمسار واضح، وتدريب حقيقي، ودليل تسمعه بنفسك.

**What Stage 2 can honestly deliver:** you can state and justify opinions, tell detailed stories with a shape,
handle hypotheticals and report what others said, describe how things are done, disagree without ending the
conversation, and keep a discussion going for eight to twelve minutes. You will still search for words, make
errors in longer sentences, and find fully abstract or fast professional discussion difficult. That is not a
failed B2; it is a real B1 — and B2 is the next climb, not a standard you have missed.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Stage overview for the coach.** 12 units, 60 lessons, ~150–200 guided hours, normally 16–24 weeks at about
> 7 hours a week. Entry is evidenced A2. Arabic runs ~15% → 12% → 10% → 5% → **0%** by Unit 12; the fade is
> committed per unit and gated by the Arabic-dial audit, so do not restore translation merely because
> productive struggle appears.
> **Stage structure:** U1–U3 complete the tense system, U4–U8 add the reasoning grammar, U9–U11 build the
> discourse and discussion layer, U12 is retrieval + clinic + mock + summative.
> **Pre-teach the plateau, do not wait for it.** Unit 1 names it and Unit 2 revisits it. When a learner says
> they are getting worse, play two of their own recordings, name the increased task difficulty, and compare
> like with like before changing anything about the plan.
> **The immersion is the deliverable, not a side effect.** From U10 the live sessions are English-only. When a
> learner stalls, feed an English phrase rather than switching language — that is the strategy they need in the
> assessment and in every conversation after it.
> **Non-negotiables:** every requested recording, the Accent Lab, retrieval before support, a reason attached
> to every opinion, and interaction evidence. A correct monologue is not a discussion; reward following up,
> conceding, challenging, clarifying and repairing.
> **Persistent errors to recycle:** *will* after *if*; present perfect with a finished-time expression; *for*
> against *since*; *been* against *gone*; a doubled subject in a relative clause (*the man who he…*); missing
> backshift and reported word order; a missing *be* in the passive; *however* punctuated as a conjunction;
> second-conditional *'d* dropped so real and unreal collapse together.
> **Assessment integrity:** U12-L04 must match L05 exactly in length, parts and rubric. Push back once,
> genuinely, for every learner — a discussion that is never challenged cannot evidence Interaction. Rubric
> bands and determination come from `curriculum/05-assessment-suite.md`; prompt, conditions, storage and
> consent come from `curriculum/06-before-after-protocol.md`. The assessment recording and the milestone sample
> are two separate files. Never round a determination up for motivation or down for strictness.
> **Carry residuals forward.** Ending a stage is not a claim that every feature is finished; hand the
> unresolved ones to Stage 3 in writing.
