> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 5 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 9 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** describe a sequenced process with the passive · choose between active and passive on purpose · sequence with *first · then · next · after that · finally*.
> **Processes make the passive purposeful, which is the whole point of this lesson.** Reward a clear, well-sequenced description over form perfection — a learner who sequences six steps with one wrong participle has met the objective.
> **Teach the choice, not just the transformation.** By now learners can convert; what they need is judgement. **Active** when the doer is interesting; **passive** when the process is. Most instructions and most news headlines are passive for exactly that reason.
> **The natural context is anything they already know how to do** — their job, cooking, a bureaucratic procedure. Familiar content plus a new structure is the easiest productive combination there is.
> **Support:** the sequencer card plus the participle card. **Stretch:** describe a process from their actual work with six steps.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Explaining a process is a professional skill. Instructions, procedures, recipes, reports and news are written this
way because the steps matter and the person performing them usually does not. Being able to do it in English moves
you from describing your life to explaining your work.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
How a simple process works (📹 in the portal).

> **First**, the applications **are collected** online — nothing **is accepted** on paper anymore. **Then** they
> **are checked** by an assistant, who removes anything incomplete.
>
> **After that**, the complete files **are sent** to two reviewers. Each application **is read** twice, and the
> scores **are compared**. If the two reviewers disagree, the file **is passed** to a third.
>
> **Next**, the shortlist **is published** on the website. Candidates **are contacted** by email, and interviews
> **are arranged** for the following month.
>
> **Finally**, the decisions **are announced** — usually late, if I'm honest.

**Notice two things.** The sequencers carry the structure — *first · then · after that · next · finally*. And the
only *by* in the whole description is the assistant, because that step is the one where the person matters.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| First, they are collected. | الأول بيتجمعوا |
| Then they are checked. | بعدين بيتراجعوا |
| After that, they are sent. | بعد كده بيتبعتوا |
| Finally, the decisions are announced. | وفي الآخر النتايج بتتعلن |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners list the sequencers, then count the passives. The ratio is the
> lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**A process is sequencers plus passives.**

| Step | Sequencer |
|---|---|
| 1 | *First, · To begin with,* |
| 2 | *Then, · Next, · After that,* |
| 3 | *Once it's ___, · When it's been ___,* |
| last | *Finally, · In the end,* |

> *First, the beans **are picked**. Then they **are dried**. After that, they **are roasted**.*

**1) Choosing active or passive.**

| Choose | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **active** | the doer is interesting | *My grandfather **built** this house.* |
| **passive** | the **process** is interesting | *This house **was built** in 1940.* |

> ⚠️ خد بالك: الاختيار على حسب المهم في الجملة — اللي عمل الحاجة، ولا الحاجة نفسها.

**2) News headlines are passive for the same reason** — the event outranks the agent:

> *Three people **were injured**. · A decision **is expected** tomorrow. · The road **has been closed**.*

**3) Mixing them is normal and good.** A real description moves between the two:

> *They **collect** the applications online, then the files **are checked**, and an assistant **removes** anything
> incomplete.*

**4) Keep the tense consistent** across the process. A present-tense procedure stays present throughout; a history
stays past.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Keep this short — sequencers and the choice table. Train is where the lesson happens.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the sequencer gets its own small group, then a pause.**

> *First,* ⟋ *the beans are **PICKED**.* ⟋ *Then,* ⟋ *they are **DRIED**.*

That rhythm — signpost, pause, step — is what makes a long process followable. Without the pauses the steps blur
into one sentence and the listener loses count.

**Part 2 — the auxiliaries stay weak and contracted across the whole description:**

> *they**'re** picked · it**'s** dried · they**'re** sent*

**Part 3 — but every participle ending survives.** This is the hardest part at speed, and it is where a process
description falls apart:

> *pick**ed** · dri**ed** · roast**ed** · pack**ed** · check**ed** · sen**t** · publish**ed***

Say the whole chain in one breath and keep all seven endings.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"First they're picked, then they're dried, and finally they're packed."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on the signpost-pause-step rhythm, three on the participle chain at speed. The chain is
> the best diagnostic in the unit.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — active or passive?** Choose and say why.
1. Telling a friend who made your cake.
2. Explaining how bread is made in a factory.
3. A news headline about a road closure.
4. Praising the architect of a building.

**Exercise 2 — put the process in order and make it passive.**
> *bake it · mix the flour and water · leave it to rise · sell it · shape the dough*

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *First, the beans pick. Then they dry.* → __________________________
2. *The applications are collect online.* → __________________________
3. *Then it is leave to cool.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — your process.** Describe something you actually do at work, at home, or in a routine — five steps,
mostly passive, properly sequenced.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~9 min.** **Ex1:** active · passive · passive · active. **Ex2:** *First the flour and water are mixed, then the
> dough is shaped, then it's left to rise, then it's baked, and finally it's sold.* **Ex3:** add *are/is* +
> participles. Give Ex4 at least four minutes.
