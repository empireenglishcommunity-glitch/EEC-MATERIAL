# *by* + Agent — When to Include It

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 8 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit8-passive.md` · **S2-U8-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** add *by* + agent when the doer matters · leave it out when it does not · judge which case applies.
> **The error here is over-use, not under-use.** Having just learned *by*, learners attach it to everything — ❌ *"My bike was stolen by someone"*, ❌ *"The bins are emptied by the bin men."* Both are grammatical and both are wrong in the sense that matters: nobody would say them.
> **State the principle plainly: omitting the agent is the *point* of the passive.** You include *by* only when the doer is **surprising, specific, or important** — usually a named creator.
> **The reliable context for *by* is authorship**: books, buildings, paintings, discoveries, inventions. Teach it there and the judgement becomes concrete.
> **This is a judgement lesson, not a form lesson.** The form takes two minutes. Spend the rest on decide-and-justify.
> **Support:** the include/omit decision card. **Stretch:** rewrite a paragraph removing every unnecessary *by*.
> **Arabic ~12%.**

- Add **by** when the doer matters.
- Leave it out when it does not — which is most of the time.
- Explain which case you are in. 🎙️

> الأصل إننا بنشيل اللي عمل الحاجة. بنضيف **by** بس لما يكون مهم أو مفاجئ.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
The passive exists to remove the doer. So adding *by* every time undoes the reason you chose the structure — and
it produces sentences that are correct but that no native speaker would utter.

> ولو حطيت *by someone*، الجملة تبقى صح نحويًا بس محدش بيقولها.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two people in a gallery (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** This one **was painted by** Mahmoud Said, wasn't it?
> **B:** Yes — and the one behind you **was painted by** his student. You can see it.
> **A:** Who's the building **designed by**?
> **B:** An Italian architect. The interior **was redesigned** in the seventies, though — badly.
> **A:** By whom?
> **B:** Nobody remembers. It just **was redesigned**, and everyone regrets it.
> **A:** My coat **was taken** from the cloakroom last week, by the way.
> **B:** Taken? Or stolen?
> **A:** I prefer *taken*.

**Notice the pattern.** *By* appears for the **painters** and the **architect** — specific, important people. It
does **not** appear for the seventies redesign (nobody knows) or the coat (nobody knows either).

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| It was painted by Mahmoud Said. | رسمها محمود سعيد |
| Who was it designed by? | مين اللي صممه؟ |
| It was redesigned in the seventies. | اتعمل تعديل في السبعينات |
| My coat was taken. | جاكيتي اتاخد |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners mark which sentences have *by* and say why. The pattern is
> authorship against anonymity.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The form is trivial: *be* + participle + *by* + doer.**

> *The book **was written by** an Egyptian journalist.*
> *These photos **were taken by** my grandfather.*

**When to include *by* — the doer is surprising, specific, or important:**

| Situation | Example |
|---|---|
| a named creator | *It was written **by** Naguib Mahfouz.* |
| a surprising doer | *The window was broken **by** the cat.* |
| the point of the sentence | *The law was passed **by** the previous government.* |

**When to leave it out — which is most of the time:**

| Situation | Say |
|---|---|
| the doer is unknown | *My bike **was stolen**.* — not *by someone* |
| the doer is obvious | *The bins **are emptied** on Tuesdays.* — not *by the bin men* |
| the doer is people in general | *English **is spoken** here.* — not *by people* |

> ⚠️ **دي الغلطة هنا:** إنك تزود **by** على كل حاجة. *stolen by someone* مالهاش معنى — لو مش عارف مين، سيبها.

**1) Never say these:**

> *by someone · by somebody · by people · by them* (when *them* is nobody in particular)

**2) Asking about the agent:**

> ***Who** was it written **by**?* — natural in speech
> ***By whom** was it written?* — formal, and rare in conversation

**3) Note *by* against *with*.** *By* is the doer; *with* is the tool:

> *It was cut **by** a machine.* — the machine did it
> *It was cut **with** a knife.* — the knife was the instrument

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on form and the two lists, two on the never-say list, two on *by* against *with*. The
> instrument distinction is a genuine and useful extra.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *by* is weak when the name is what matters:**

> *by* → /baɪ/ but quick and unstressed
> *It was written **bay-**na-**GEEB** ma-**FOOZ***

The **name** takes the stress, not *by*.

**Part 2 — but *by* goes strong when you are contrasting doers:**

> *It wasn't written by **HIM** — it was written **BY HER**.*

**Part 3 — the whole agentless passive is smoother, and that is a clue.** Say both aloud:

> *My bike was stolen.* — smooth, natural
> *My bike was stolen by someone.* — clumsy, and the clumsiness is the signal

If a phrase feels like it is dragging, that is often because it should not be there.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"It was painted by Mahmoud Said, but the frame was made locally."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on unstressed *by* with a stressed name, one on contrastive *by*, two on the
> smooth-versus-clumsy comparison — which teaches the judgement through the ear.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **Omitting the doer is the point of the passive.**
- Include ***by*** only when the doer is **surprising, specific, or important**.
- Never: *by someone · by somebody · by people*.
- ***by*** = the doer · ***with*** = the tool.
- Ask: *Who was it written **by**?*
- Unstressed *by*, stressed name.
