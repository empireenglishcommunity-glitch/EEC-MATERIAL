> **Objectives:** form the present simple passive · describe facts and processes where the doer is unknown or irrelevant · notice when the passive is the natural choice.
> **The participle is the engine, and it came from Unit 1's third column.** If participles are shaky, re-drill them **here** — the passive simply does not work without them. This is the second time that column pays off, as promised in U1-L02.

> **Support:** the *be + participle* frame plus the participle card. **Stretch:** describe a three-step process.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Sometimes the person who did something is unknown, obvious, or simply not the point. *"My bike was stolen"* — I do
not know who. *"English is spoken here"* — by everybody, so who is irrelevant. The passive is how English keeps the
focus on the **thing** rather than the doer.

> ولاحظ إن الفعل المساعد لازم يبقى موجود.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A short factual description (📹 in the portal).

> Coffee **is grown** in more than fifty countries, but most of it **is produced** in Brazil, Vietnam and
> Colombia. The beans **are picked** by hand in many places, then they **are dried**, **roasted** and **packed**.
>
> In my city, coffee **is sold** on almost every corner. It **is usually served** very sweet, and it **isn't
> drunk** quickly — people sit with it for an hour.
>
> The word *coffee* **is thought** to come from Arabic. Nobody knows for certain.

**Notice:** nobody is named. We are not told **who** grows, picks, dries or sells — because it does not matter, or
nobody knows. The focus stays entirely on the coffee.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| Coffee is grown in Brazil. | البن بيتزرع في البرازيل |
| The beans are picked by hand. | الحبوب بتتقطف بالإيد |
| It is served very sweet. | بيتقدم حلو أوي |
| It isn't drunk quickly. | مابيتشربش بسرعة |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners try to name the doer for each verb. They cannot — which is the
> reason the passive exists.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The form: *am / is / are* + past participle.**

| Subject | *be* | participle |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee | **is** | grown |
| The beans | **are** | picked |
| I | **am** | paid |

> *English **is spoken** here.* · *These cars **are made** in Japan.*

> ⚠️ **أشهر غلطة:** إنك تنسى الفعل المساعد. لازم **is** أو **are** قبل التصريف التالت.

**1) The negative and the question:**

> *Rice **isn't grown** here.* · ***Are** these **made** locally?*

**2) Compare active and passive.** The object of the active becomes the subject of the passive:

| | |
|---|---|
| active | *They **make** these cars in Japan.* |
| passive | *These cars **are made** in Japan.* |

**3) When to use it — three situations:**

| Reason | Example |
|---|---|
| the doer is **unknown** | *My bike **is** often **stolen** from here.* |
| the doer is **obvious** | *The bins **are emptied** on Tuesdays.* |
| the doer is **unimportant** | *Coffee **is grown** in Brazil.* |

**4) You usually leave the doer out entirely.** That is the whole point — and it is why *by* is optional (L03).

**5) Only verbs with an object can be passive.** *Sleep · arrive · happen* cannot: ❌ *"The accident was happened."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on form, two on active↔passive transformation, two on the three reasons. Point 5
> prevents a common over-generalisation with *happen*.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *is* and *are* are weak, and they get contracted:**

> *It**'s** made* /ɪts/ · *They**'re** grown* /ðer/

But weak is not absent. Drop them entirely and the sentence has no verb — which is exactly the error the grammar
section warns about, arriving through pronunciation.

**Part 2 — the participle carries the beat:**

> *Coffee is **GROWN** in Brazil.* · *These are **MADE** in Japan.*

**Part 3 — the participle endings must survive.** This is Unit 1's work again:

> *pick**ed*** /t/ · *dri**ed*** /d/ · *roast**ed*** /ɪd/ · *pack**ed*** /t/

Say the four-verb chain from the clip at speed and keep all four endings alive:

> *picked, dried, roasted and packed*

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"The beans are picked, dried, roasted and packed."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on weak-but-present auxiliaries, three on the participle chain. That chain is a
> genuinely hard piece of connected speech and an excellent diagnostic.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — make it passive.**
1. *They grow rice here.* → __________________________
2. *People speak English in Malta.* → __________________________
3. *They don't sell it in shops.* → __________________________
4. *Somebody cleans the office every night.* → __________________________

**Exercise 2 — complete with *is/are* + participle.**
1. These shoes ______ (make) in Italy.
2. The bins ______ (empty) on Tuesdays.
3. Breakfast ______ (serve) from seven.
4. That word ______ (not / use) anymore.

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *Coffee grown in Brazil.* → __________________________
2. *These cars are make in Japan.* → __________________________
3. *The accident was happened yesterday.* → __________________________
4. *English speaks here.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — describe a process.** Three steps of something familiar — how tea is made, how bread is baked, how
your work gets done.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** *Rice is grown here · English is spoken in Malta · It isn't sold in shops · The office is
> cleaned every night.* **Ex3:** add *is* · *made* · *The accident happened* · *English is spoken here*. Ex3.3 is
> the intransitive trap.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- ***am / is / are* + past participle.**
- Never drop *be*: ❌ *Coffee grown in Brazil.*
- Use it when the doer is **unknown, obvious, or unimportant**.
- The active object becomes the passive subject.
- Verbs with no object cannot be passive: ❌ *was happened*.
- Weak auxiliary, **stressed participle**.
