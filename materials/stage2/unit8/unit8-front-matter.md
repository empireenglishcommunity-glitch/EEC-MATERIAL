## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 8 you'll **explain how things work** — describe facts, histories and
> processes without naming who did them, and add the doer only when it matters.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تشرح إزاي حاجة بتتعمل، والتركيز على الحاجة نفسها.

**Why this is the professional unit:** instructions, procedures, reports and news are all written this way, because
the steps matter and the person performing them usually does not. This is the structure that moves you from
describing your own life to explaining your work.

> ودي الوحدة اللي بتخليك تشرح، مش بس توصف.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Present passive | *is/are* + participle — facts and processes |
| **L02** | Past passive | *was/were* + participle — history and results |
| **L03** | *by* + agent | include the doer only when it earns its place |
| **L04** | Processes & news | sequence a procedure, and choose active or passive |
| **L05** | How it's made (milestone) 🎙️ | an 8–10 sentence explanation + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** present and past simple passive · *by* + agent and when to omit it · active/passive choice ·
process sequencing.
**Accent Lab:** weak but present ***is/are/was/were*** · participle endings surviving at speed · the final clusters
in *built · found · closed · sent* · unstressed *by* with a stressed agent · the signpost-pause-step rhythm.
**Mindset:** progress is not perfection — every mistake tells you exactly what to drill next.
**Arabic support:** ~12% for L01–L03, easing to ~10% for L04–L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-8 process explanation** — a genuinely professional skill in English.
- The end of the heavy grammar block: U6–U8 complete the structures for complex ideas.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **The participle is the engine, and it came from U1's third column.** If participles are shaky, re-drill them in
> L01 — the passive cannot work without them. This is the second payoff from that table, exactly as promised in
> U1-L02.
> **L01's error is a missing *be*:** ❌ *"Coffee grown in Brazil."* A participle alone is not a verb. Note that this
> error often arrives through **pronunciation** — a weak auxiliary becoming a deleted one — so treat the Accent Lab
> as grammar support, not decoration. Teach the **why** (doer unknown, obvious, or unimportant) before the form, or
> learners produce correct sentences in contexts where nobody would use them.
> **L02 is L01 with one word changed** — say so. The error is the base verb (❌ *was build*), because learners hear
> the past auxiliary and stop transforming. Anchor it: **the auxiliary carries the tense, the participle never
> changes.** Spend the real time on participles that differ from the past simple — *done · forgotten · written ·
> taken · broken*.
> **L03's error is over-use, not under-use.** Having just met *by*, learners attach it to everything: ❌ *stolen by
> someone*, ❌ *emptied by the bin men*. Both are grammatical and neither is said. State the principle plainly —
> **omitting the agent is the point of the passive** — and teach the judgement through authorship contexts. The
> *by* against *with* distinction (doer against tool) is a useful extra.
> **L04 makes the passive purposeful.** Processes are its natural home. Reward clear sequencing over form
> perfection, and teach the **choice** rather than the transformation, since conversion is already automatic by
> now.
> **L05 closes the U6–U8 block.** Tell the cohort the heavy grammar is behind them and that U9–U11 — discourse and
> opinion — is where the B1 "cope independently" outcome is actually won.
