# Disagreeing Politely

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 10 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit10-discussion-skills.md` · **S2-U10-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** disagree politely · acknowledge before objecting · always attach a reason.
> **Intonation is the lesson, not the phrases.** *"I'm not sure I agree"* said flat is colder than *"No"* said warmly. Learners reach for the right words and deliver them harshly, then conclude English speakers are unfriendly. Model the softening rise repeatedly.
> **Acknowledge first — always.** *"I see your point, but…"* is the single most useful discussion move in the language. Disagreement that arrives without acknowledgement reads as an attack regardless of the words used.
> **A reason is compulsory.** Bare disagreement is a wall; disagreement with a reason is a contribution. This links straight back to U9's *opinion → reason* shape.
> **Make it low-stakes and practise it a lot.** Many learners avoid disagreeing in English entirely, which quietly removes them from every discussion. Say explicitly that disagreeing is safe and normal here.
> **Support:** the disagreement phrase card, ordered by strength. **Stretch:** disagree, give a reason, then offer a middle position.
> **Arabic ~5%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Learners often avoid disagreeing in English because they are not sure how to do it politely — and avoiding it
removes them from the most interesting half of every conversation. The phrases are easy. The delivery is what makes
them safe.

> والنبرة أهم من الكلمات.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A disagreement that stays friendly (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Honestly, I think remote work makes people lazy.
> **B:** **I see your point** — I've seen it happen. **But** in my experience it's more about management than the
> people. **I'd say** badly-managed teams are lazy wherever they sit.
> **A:** Maybe. Still, you lose something without an office.
> **B:** **I'm not sure I agree with that.** You lose the corridor conversations, **although** you gain three hours
> a day. **For me**, that's a good trade.
> **A:** For you. Not for a junior who needs to learn.
> **B:** **That's a fair point, actually.** I hadn't thought about juniors. **So maybe** it depends on where you
> are in your career.
> **A:** That I'd agree with.

**Notice the order every time.** Acknowledge → object → give a reason. And notice B **concedes** at the end — the
disagreement improves the idea rather than winning.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners find the acknowledgement in each of B's turns. It is always there,
> and it is what keeps the exchange comfortable.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Three moves, in this order.**

> **1 Acknowledge** → *I see your point…*
> **2 Object** → *…but I'd say…*
> **3 Reason** → *…because…*

**1) Acknowledgements — never skip this:**

> *I see your point. · I know what you mean. · That's true, but… · I understand that, however…*

**2) Objections, from soft to direct:**

| Strength | Phrase |
|---|---|
| **very soft** | *I'm not sure I agree. · I'm not convinced.* |
| **soft** | *I'd say… · Actually, I think… · For me, …* |
| **direct** | *I disagree. · I don't think that's right.* |
| **too direct** | *No. · You're wrong.* ❌ avoid in a discussion |

**3) *Actually* is your friend.** It softly signals a different view:

> ***Actually**, I found the opposite.*

**4) Disagree with the **idea**, not the person:**

> *I don't agree **with that**.* ✅
> *I don't agree **with you**.* — noticeably sharper

**5) The move that raises the level — offer a middle position:**

> *Maybe **it depends on**… · Perhaps **both are true**… · **So maybe** the real issue is…*

> ⚠️ اعترف الأول.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on the three moves, two on the strength ladder, two on idea-not-person plus the middle
> position. Insist on the acknowledgement in every practice turn.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the softening rise. This is the whole lesson.**

> *I'm not sure I agree* ↗️ — gentle, open, invites a reply
> *I'm not sure I agree* ➡️ — flat, closed, sounds like contempt

The words cannot save a flat delivery. Say the same sentence twice — once as a friend who is curious, once as
someone ending the conversation — and hear how little the words matter.

**Part 2 — the acknowledgement is warm and slightly slower:**

> *I **see** your point…* — unhurried, then a small pause before *but*

Rushing through the acknowledgement makes it sound like a formality, which is worse than omitting it.

**Part 3 — *but* is unstressed.** Learners hit it hard, which turns the sentence adversarial:

> *I see your point, bət I'd say…* ✅
> *I see your point, **BUT** I'd say…* — combative

**Part 4 — *actually* is quick and low**, three syllables in English speech: /ˈæktʃəli/.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I see your point, but I'm not sure I agree — in my experience it's the opposite."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Three minutes on the rise, two on the unstressed *but* and the unhurried acknowledgement. This is the
> highest-value Accent Lab in the unit: the grammar is trivial and the delivery is everything.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — soften it.** Rewrite each as a polite disagreement with a reason.
1. *No, that's wrong.* → __________________________
2. *I disagree completely.* → __________________________
3. *That makes no sense.* → __________________________

**Exercise 2 — add the missing move.** Each is missing one of the three.
1. *But I'd say it's the management.* → missing: __________
2. *I see your point, but I disagree.* → missing: __________

**Exercise 3 — disagree, three ways.** Take this opinion and disagree softly, then moderately, then directly:
> *"Everyone should learn to code."*

**Exercise 4 — six-turn discussion.** In pairs, take a topic. You must **disagree at least twice**, always
acknowledging first and always giving a reason. Then find a middle position.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex2:** 1) an acknowledgement · 2) a reason. Give Ex4 four minutes — and listen specifically for the
> acknowledgement, which is the move that disappears under pressure.

## 🎤 Your Turn — دورك

- **Disagreeing in English is safe.** Practise it here, low-stakes, until it stops feeling risky. A learner who
  cannot disagree is only ever half in the conversation.

---

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **Acknowledge → object → reason.** In that order, every time.
- *I see your point, but…* is the most useful move in the language.
- Ladder: *I'm not sure I agree* → *I'd say* → *I disagree*. Avoid *You're wrong*.
- Disagree with **the idea**, not the person: *with that*, not *with you*.
- Offer a **middle position** to raise the level.
- The **rise** ↗️ is what makes it polite; keep *but* unstressed.
