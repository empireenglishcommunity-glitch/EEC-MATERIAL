# Agreeing & Adding

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 10 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 9
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit10-discussion-skills.md` · **S2-U10-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** agree naturally and at the right strength · add a related point rather than starting a new one · respond instead of waiting for a turn.
> **The behaviour to build is *listen → react → add*.** Learners at this level state opinions competently and then go silent, treating discussion as a queue of monologues. That is the single biggest gap between B1 on paper and B1 in a room.
> **Agreement has strength levels, and learners only own the weakest.** *I agree* is neutral; *exactly* and *absolutely* are strong; *that's a good point* concedes something. Using one flat phrase for everything makes a speaker sound uninterested.
> **Adding is what keeps a discussion alive.** Agreement alone closes a topic. *"I agree — and what's more…"* hands something back.
> **Near-immersion from here.** Instructions in English; Arabic only where a contrast genuinely prevents an error.
> **Support:** the agreement phrase card. **Stretch:** agree, add a point, then ask the other person a question.
> **Arabic ~5%.**

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Agree at the **right strength** — not always *I agree*.
- **Add** a point instead of just approving.
- Respond to someone rather than waiting to speak. 🎙️

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Most learners can state an opinion. Far fewer can **respond** to one — and a discussion is nothing but responses.
If you only ever deliver your own view and stop, you are taking turns, not having a conversation.

> والمناقشة مش أدوار كلام. لازم ترد وتضيف.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two colleagues agreeing — and building (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** I think open-plan offices are a mistake. Nobody can concentrate.
> **B:** **Exactly.** And **what's more**, the people who designed them don't sit in them.
> **A:** That's a very good point.
> **B:** **I think so too.** **Plus**, they're sold as collaborative, but everyone wears headphones all day.
> **A:** **True.** **Although** I'd say it depends on the job — designers seem to like it.
> **B:** **Fair enough.** **I'd agree with that**, actually. For focused work it's hopeless; for a team sketching
> something, maybe not.
> **A:** So the problem isn't the layout, it's applying one layout to everything.
> **B:** **Absolutely.** That's better put than what I said.

**Notice:** nobody merely agrees. Every agreement is followed by **something new** — a further point, a limit, or a
reformulation. The conversation moves because each person adds.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners underline every agreement phrase and then the thing added after
> it. The pairing is the lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Agreement comes in strengths. Choose deliberately.**

| Strength | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **strong** | *Exactly. · Absolutely. · That's exactly what I think.* |
| **neutral** | *I agree. · I think so too. · True.* |
| **partial** | *That's a good point, although… · Fair enough, but…* |
| **warm** | *I couldn't agree more. · You're absolutely right.* |

**1) Then add. These four do most of the work:**

> ***And what's more*** *… · **Plus**, … · **Also**, … · **On top of that**, …*

**2) The three-move pattern to internalise:**

> **1 React** → *Exactly.*
> **2 Add** → *And what's more, the designers don't sit in them.*
> **3 Hand back** → *Don't you think?*

**3) Agreeing with part of something** is more useful than total agreement, and sounds more thoughtful:

> *I agree **with the first part**, but I'm less sure about…*
> *That's true **up to a point**.*

**4) Do not just repeat their sentence.** Echoing is not adding:

> *— Open-plan offices are noisy. — Yes, they are noisy.* ❌ the topic dies
> *— Yes, and the noise is worst in the afternoon.* ✅

**5) Short reactions keep the channel open** while you think: *Right. · Mm. · Sure. · Good point.* These are not
filler; they tell the speaker you are listening.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on strengths, two on adders, two on the three-move pattern. Drill it live: you state an
> opinion, each learner reacts **and** adds.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — agreement lives on warmth, not words.**

> *Exactly.* ↘️ said high then falling — genuine
> *Exactly.* ➡️ flat — sarcastic, or bored

Say *"Exactly"* three ways: enthusiastic, neutral, unimpressed. If a partner cannot tell them apart, the phrase is
not doing its job — and in a discussion, flat agreement reads as disagreement.

**Part 2 — the strong ones take real emphasis:**

> ***ex**-ACT-ly* · ***AB**-so-lute-ly*

Both stress the first syllable hard. Under-stressed, they sound like polite noise.

**Part 3 — *and what's more* is one fast chunk:**

> **and what's more** → /ənˈwɒtsmɔːr/ — the stress lands on *more*

**Part 4 — short reactions are quiet and quick.** *Right · Mm · Sure* should barely interrupt the other speaker.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"Exactly. And what's more, nobody actually uses it."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** The three-way *"Exactly"* drill is the keeper. Flat agreement is extremely common among learners and
> is routinely misread as indifference.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — react and add.** Agree, then add something new.
1. *"Public transport here is too expensive."* → __________________________
2. *"Learning a language as an adult is harder."* → __________________________
3. *"Everyone spends too much time on their phone."* → __________________________

**Exercise 2 — choose the strength.**
1. You completely agree. → __________
2. You agree with part of it. → __________
3. You accept their point but will add a limit. → __________

**Exercise 3 — fix the response.** Each one closes the topic. Rewrite so it opens it.
1. *— The traffic is terrible. — Yes, the traffic is terrible.*
2. *— This café is expensive. — I agree.*

**Exercise 4 — chain of three.** In a group, one person gives an opinion. Each next person must **react, add, and
hand back**. Keep it going for six turns.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** Give Ex4 at least four minutes — it is the lesson. Insist on all three moves; the handing back is the
> part learners drop first.

## 🎤 Your Turn — دورك
Your partner gives an opinion. **React, add a point, and hand it back** — three times, on three topics.

> 🎙️ **Record it.**

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** **Success check:** varied agreement strength, a genuinely new point each time (not an echo), a
> handover, and warm rather than flat delivery.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **Listen → react → add.** Agreement alone closes a topic.
- Strengths: *Exactly · Absolutely* (strong) · *I agree · True* (neutral) · *Good point, although…* (partial).
- Adders: *and what's more · plus · also · on top of that*.
- **Never echo** their sentence back — add something new.
- Short reactions — *Right · Mm · Sure* — keep the channel open.
- Flat agreement sounds like **disagreement**.
