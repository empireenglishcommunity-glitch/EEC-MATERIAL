## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 10 you'll **hold a real discussion** — react to what people actually say,
> disagree without offending anyone, soften your claims, take your turn, and close the conversation.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تناقش فعلاً — ترد، وتعترض بأدب، وتحافظ على دورك.

**Why this is the "cope independently" unit:** Unit 9 taught you to state and justify an opinion. That is a
monologue. This unit is about what happens when somebody answers you — and that is where B1 is actually won or
lost.

> والوحدة دي بالإنجليزي بس. لو ضاعت كلمة، وصّفها أو لفّ حولها.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Agreeing & adding | *listen → react → add* — never just approve |
| **L02** | Disagreeing politely | acknowledge → object → reason, with a softening rise |
| **L03** | Hedging & softening | make claims defensible **and** buy thinking time |
| **L04** | Structured discussion | manage the turn: get in, hold, come back, summarise |
| **L05** | Discuss with a partner (milestone) 🎙️ | a 3–4 minute discussion + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** functional language — agreement strengths, polite disagreement, hedging, and turn management,
layered on Unit 9's connectors and opinion frames.
**Accent Lab:** warm agreement intonation · the **softening rise** on disagreement · unstressed *but* · unstressed
hedges · interrupting **higher, not louder** · the falling turn-end that hands over the floor.
**Mindset:** speaking under pressure without freezing — hedges and fillers keep you in the room.
**Arabic support:** ~5% throughout — near-immersion, English-only instructions.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-10 discussion** — an unscripted conversation in a second language.
- The three phrase sets that make you a participant rather than a spectator.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **The behaviour to build across the whole unit is *listen → react → add*.** Learners here state opinions
> competently and then go silent, treating discussion as a queue of monologues. Closing that gap is the unit's real
> objective, and it is worth more than any phrase list.
> **L01: agreement has strengths and learners own only the weakest.** One flat *I agree* for everything sounds
> uninterested. Also forbid echoing — repeating the other person's sentence is not adding, and it kills the topic.
> **L02 is an intonation lesson wearing a phrasebook.** *"I'm not sure I agree"* delivered flat is colder than a
> warm *"No"*. Learners get the words right, deliver them harshly, and conclude that English speakers are
> unfriendly. Model the softening rise repeatedly, keep *but* unstressed, and insist the acknowledgement comes
> first every single time. Say out loud that disagreeing here is safe — many learners avoid it entirely and quietly
> drop out of every discussion.
> **L03: push hedging hard, and frame it as a fluency tool.** It makes a claim defensible **and** buys processing
> time inside a natural phrase. Learners over-commit because absolutes are grammatically simpler. Warn against
> over-hedging too — a turn made only of hedges says nothing.
> **L04 is the summative format in miniature.** Set the topic, enforce English, then get out of the way. Manage the
> two failure modes — the dominant speaker and the silent one — by assigning the summariser role to the quietest
> person. The new content is **turn management**, which learners almost never have.
> **L05 scores interaction above accuracy.** Record the **pair**; the evidence is the exchange. This is a flagship
> confidence moment — name what it proves.
