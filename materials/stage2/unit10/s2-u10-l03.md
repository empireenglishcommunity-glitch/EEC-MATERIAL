# Hedging & Softening

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 10 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit10-discussion-skills.md` · **S2-U10-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** soften claims with hedges · use hedging to buy thinking time · avoid over-committing to absolute statements.
> **Encourage hedging heavily — it is a fluency tool, not weakness.** Two things happen at once: the claim becomes defensible, and the speaker gains a second of processing time inside a natural-sounding phrase. Learners who believe hedging is timid are losing both benefits.
> **Many learners over-commit** because absolute statements are grammatically simpler. *"People are lazy"* is easier to build than *"some people tend to be a bit lazy"* — but the first invites an argument the learner cannot yet defend.
> **Hedges are unstressed and fast.** Delivered slowly and heavily they sound uncertain rather than measured, which is the opposite of the intent.
> **This is also the anti-freeze tool.** *"I suppose it depends…"* keeps the turn while the learner thinks. Frame it that way and resistance disappears.
> **Support:** the hedging phrase card. **Stretch:** hedge a strong claim three different ways.
> **Arabic ~5%.**

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Soften a claim so it is defensible.
- Use hedges to **buy thinking time**.
- Sound measured instead of absolute. 🎙️

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
An absolute statement is easy to build and easy to attack. *"Social media is bad"* invites an immediate objection.
*"Social media **tends to** be bad **for** teenagers, **I think**"* is harder to argue with and gives you a moment to
think while you say it. That is why fluent speakers hedge constantly.

> والتحفّظ مش ضعف.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Someone giving a careful opinion (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Do you think university is still worth it?
> **B:** **Hmm. I suppose it depends on** the subject. **In my experience**, it's **kind of** essential for medicine
> or engineering, but **probably** less so for something like marketing.
> **A:** So it's a waste for some people?
> **B:** **I wouldn't go that far.** **I'd say** it's **maybe** less necessary than it was. **A lot of** employers
> **seem to** care more about a portfolio now — **at least** in the fields I know.
> **A:** That's quite hedged.
> **B:** **Well**, I only really know one industry. **I could be wrong**, but that's **more or less** how it looks
> from here.

**Notice what the hedges do.** Every claim is limited — by subject, by field, by experience. B never says anything
that could simply be shown to be false, and she is never lost for words either.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners remove every hedge and re-read B's turns aloud. The unhedged version
> sounds arrogant and is much easier to attack — that contrast is the lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Hedges do three different jobs. Learn one from each column.**

| Soften the claim | Limit the scope | Buy time |
|---|---|---|
| *I think · I guess · maybe* | *in my experience* | *Well, …* |
| *probably · it seems* | *at least for me* | *Hmm, let me think* |
| *tends to · kind of* | *in most cases* | *I suppose…* |
| *I'd say · I wouldn't say* | *as far as I know* | *That's a good question* |

**1) Softening a verb:**

> *Social media **is** bad.* → *Social media **tends to be** bad.*
> *People **don't** read.* → *People **seem to** read less.*

**2) Limiting the scope** — the most useful hedge of all, because it makes you honest:

> *…**in my experience**.* · *…**at least in my field**.* · *…**as far as I know**.*

**3) Buying time.** These are complete, natural utterances that commit you to nothing:

> *Well… · Hmm, that's a good question. · I suppose it depends.*

Use them **instead of** silence, and instead of switching to Arabic.

**4) Admitting uncertainty raises your credibility:**

> *I could be wrong, but… · I'm not sure, but I think…*

**5) The one caution — do not hedge everything.** A turn made entirely of hedges says nothing. One or two per claim
is plenty:

> *Maybe possibly I kind of think perhaps…* ❌

> ⚠️ تحفّظ واحد أو اتنين كفاية.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on the three jobs, two on scope-limiting (the highest-value column), two on
> time-buying. Warn against over-hedging or the next lesson fills with vagueness.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — hedges are unstressed and fast.** This is what separates *measured* from *unsure*:

> *I **SUPPOSE** it depends…* ❌ heavy — sounds reluctant
> *I spose it **DEPENDS**…* ✅ quick — sounds thoughtful

*I suppose* → /aɪspəʊz/, almost one word. *Kind of* → /ˈkaɪndə/. *Sort of* → /ˈsɔːtə/.

**Part 2 — the time-buyers are slower and lower**, and that is deliberate:

> *Hmm…* ⟋ *well…* ⟋ *I suppose it depends on the subject.*

Those two beats are legitimate thinking time. A native speaker uses them constantly; a learner who refuses them
just stops talking instead.

**Part 3 — keep the **claim** stressed, not the hedge:**

> *It tends to be **QUIETER** in the mornings.*

**Part 4 — *I could be wrong, but…*** runs fast and lands on *wrong*:

> /aɪkʊdbiˈrɒŋbət/

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"Hmm — I suppose it depends. In my experience it tends to be quieter."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on unstressed hedges, three on the time-buyers as legitimate speech. Learners often need
> explicit permission to pause aloud rather than silently.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — hedge these claims.**
1. *Young people don't read.* → __________________________
2. *Online learning is worse.* → __________________________
3. *Everyone hates their job.* → __________________________

**Exercise 2 — limit the scope.** Add a scope hedge to each.
1. *Public transport is unreliable.* → __________________________
2. *Employers don't care about degrees.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — buy time.** Your partner asks a hard question. Respond with a time-buyer, then answer. Three rounds.

**Exercise 4 — fix the over-hedge.**
> *Well, maybe I kind of possibly think that perhaps it might sort of depend, I suppose.*

Rewrite it with **two** hedges and one clear claim.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex1 (models):** *Young people seem to read less · Online learning tends to be harder for some ·
> A lot of people aren't happy at work.* Ex3 is the anti-freeze rehearsal and matters most. Ex4 makes the
> over-hedging warning concrete.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Hedges do three jobs: **soften · limit scope · buy time**.
- Soften: *tends to · seems to · probably · kind of*.
- Limit: *in my experience · as far as I know · at least in my field*.
- Buy time: *Well… · Hmm, that's a good question · I suppose…*
- *I could be wrong, but…* raises credibility.
- **One or two per claim.** Never a whole turn of hedges.
- Hedges are **unstressed**; the claim keeps the beat.
