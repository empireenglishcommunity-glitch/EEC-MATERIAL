# Reported Questions

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 7 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit7-reported-speech.md` · **S2-U7-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** report *yes/no* questions with *if* or *whether* · report *wh-* questions in statement order · drop the auxiliary *do*.
> **The top error in the unit is keeping question order:** ❌ *"She asked me where **do** I live."* Drill the anchor relentlessly — **a reported question is a statement**. No inversion, no *do*, no question mark.
> **Give them the two-step procedure.** 1) Choose the link word: *if/whether* for yes/no, the *wh-* word otherwise. 2) Then write a **statement**. Learners who follow those two steps in order get it right; learners who translate get it wrong.
> **The intonation must fall.** It is no longer a question, so a rising tune contradicts the grammar and is a reliable sign the learner is still thinking of it as a question.
> **Support:** the two-step card. **Stretch:** report a four-question exchange in one turn.
> **Arabic ~12%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Reporting questions is how you re-tell an interview, a phone call, a doctor's appointment, or an argument. It is
also the single most error-prone structure in this unit — and the errors are very audible, because the word order
is visibly wrong.

> والمنقول جملة، مش سؤال.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Reporting a job interview (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** How did it go? What did they ask you?
> **B:** They asked me **why I wanted** the job — the obvious one.
> **A:** And?
> **B:** They asked me **if I had** any experience with their software, and **whether I could** start in March.
> **A:** Did they ask about salary?
> **B:** They asked me **what I was** earning now, which I thought was a bit direct. Then the second one asked me
> **where I had studied** and **how long I had been** at my current company.
> **A:** That's a lot of questions.
> **B:** I asked them **when they would** decide. They said Friday.

**Notice:** every reported question reads like a **statement** — *why I wanted · if I had · what I was earning ·
when they would decide*. There is no inversion and no *do* anywhere.

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *"Why do you want the job?"* | *…why I wanted the job* |
| *"Do you have experience?"* | *…if I had experience* |
| *"Where did you study?"* | *…where I had studied* |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners reconstruct two original questions. The contrast between the two
> word orders is the lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Two steps. Do them in order.**

> **Step 1 — choose the link word.**
> **Step 2 — write a statement.**

**1) Yes/no questions → *if* or *whether*** (they are interchangeable):

> *"**Are** you tired?"* → *He asked me **if I was** tired.*
> *"**Do** you live here?"* → *She asked me **whether I lived** here.*

**2) *Wh-* questions → keep the *wh-* word**, then a statement:

> *"**Where do** you live?"* → *She asked me **where I lived**.*
> *"**What did** you say?"* → *He asked me **what I had said**.*

> ⚠️ **أهم قاعدة في الدرس:** بعد كلمة السؤال، الترتيب بيبقى ترتيب جملة عادية. مفيش **do** ومفيش قلب.

**3) The three things that disappear:**

| Gone | Because |
|---|---|
| the **inversion** | it is a statement now |
| the auxiliary ***do/does/did*** | statements do not need it |
| the **question mark** | there is no question being asked |

**4) The tense still shifts, exactly as in L02:**

> *"Where **are** you?"* → *He asked where I **was**.*

**5) The reporting verb is usually *ask*** — and it takes a person directly, like *tell*:

> *She **asked me**…* ✅ · *She asked **to** me…* ❌

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on the two steps, two on the three disappearing elements, two on transformation
> drilling. Insist learners say the link word out loud **before** the rest of the sentence.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the tune falls.** This is the audible half of the grammar:

> *She asked me where I **LIVED**.* ↘️ — a statement
> *Where do you **LIVE**?* ↗️ — a question

If the reported version rises, the listener hears a question and waits for an answer.

**Part 2 — *asked* is one syllable ending in /kt/.**

> **asked** → /æskt/ — not *"ask-ed"*

That cluster is hard, and inserting a vowel is the commonest error here. Build it backwards: /t/ → /kt/ → /skt/ →
*asked*.

**Part 3 — link the frame:**

> *asked me* → *as-**kmee*** · *asked him* → *as-**ktim***

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"She asked me where I lived and whether I could start in March."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes on /æskt/ — genuinely difficult and worth the drill — and two on the falling tune. The
> tune is diagnostic: a rise means the learner still conceives of it as a question.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **A reported question is a statement.** No inversion, no *do*, no question mark.
- Yes/no → ***if*** or ***whether***. *Wh-* → keep the *wh-* word.
- Two steps: **link word first, then statement order**.
- The tense still shifts one step back.
- *ask* takes a person directly: *asked **me***, never *asked to me*.
- The tune **falls**. ↘️
