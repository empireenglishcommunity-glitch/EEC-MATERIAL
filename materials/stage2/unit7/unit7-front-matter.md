## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 7 you'll **pass on what other people said** — statements, questions,
> requests and advice, with the right verb and the right word order.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تنقل كلام الناس، مش بس تحكي كلامك.

**Why this unlocks a lot of speaking:** half of ordinary conversation is repeating what somebody else said. And
the content already exists — you are not inventing anything, only re-shaping it. That makes this the cheapest
speaking practice available at B1.

> والحلو إن الكلام موجود أصلاً. إنت بس بتنقله، فالتدريب بيبقى أسهل.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | *say* vs *tell* | one rule: *tell* needs a person, *say* doesn't |
| **L02** | Backshift | move the tense one step back — and know when not to |
| **L03** | Reported questions | statement order · no *do* · no question mark |
| **L04** | Requests & advice | *asked me to · told me not to* — no tense shift at all |
| **L05** | Report a conversation (milestone) 🎙️ | an 8–10 sentence report + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** *say* against *tell* · the backshift set · reported yes/no and *wh-* questions · reporting with
*to* + infinitive.
**Accent Lab:** weak ***that*** /ðət/ · *told me / told him* linking with a disappearing /h/ · **/æskt/** as one
syllable · the **falling tune** of a reported question · weak ***to*** /tə/ with a stressed ***not***.
**Mindset:** the output ramp — say slightly more than feels comfortable, every session.
**Arabic support:** ~12% through L01–L03, easing to ~10% for L04–L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-7 reported conversation** — a real narrative skill, not a grammar exercise.
- A daily practice habit that needs no preparation: re-tell what you were told.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **L01 is the foundation — do not rush it.** Every later lesson sits on a reporting frame, so a shaky *say/tell*
> choice contaminates the whole unit. The two classic errors are mirror images (❌ *said me* / ❌ *told that*) and
> one rule fixes both, so drill them **as a pair**. Teach *that* as optional; learners who think it is required
> sound stilted.
> **L02 must not demand perfection.** The B1 target is the **main shifts** only: present→past and *will*→*would*.
> Chasing every modal produces learners who stop reporting rather than learners who report well. Say plainly that
> **native speakers often keep the present when the fact is still true** — *"She said she lives in Cairo"* is
> normal. And watch pronouns and time words, which are forgotten more often than tenses.
> **L03 carries the unit's top error:** ❌ *"She asked me where **do** I live."* Anchor it hard — **a reported
> question is a statement.** Give the two-step procedure (link word, then statement order); learners who follow
> it in that order succeed. The **falling tune** is diagnostic: a rise means they are still thinking of it as a
> question. */æskt/* is genuinely difficult and worth its own drill.
> **L04 is the confidence lift, so schedule it as one.** There is no tense shift — just *verb + person + to +
> base*. Learners who struggled with L02 need this win. The real content is that the **verb carries the force**:
> *asked · told · advised · warned · reminded*. Note that *not* takes a beat, because swallowing it reverses the
> instruction.
> **L05 scores followability, not flawless backshift.** The one item to hold the line on is word order in reported
> questions.
