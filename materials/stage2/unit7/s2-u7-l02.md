# Reported Statements (Backshift)

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 7 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit7-reported-speech.md` · **S2-U7-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** shift the tense back when reporting · handle the main shifts reliably · adjust pronouns and time words.
> **Do not demand perfect backshift.** The B1 target is the **main shifts** — present→past and *will*→*would*. Chasing every modal produces learners who stop reporting at all rather than learners who report accurately.
> **Reassure them explicitly: native speakers often keep the present** when the fact is still true — *"She said she lives in Cairo"* is entirely normal. Teaching backshift as an absolute rule creates anxiety and is also inaccurate.
> **Pronouns and time words shift too**, and learners forget them more often than tenses: *"I'll see you tomorrow"* reported the next week becomes *"he said he'd see me the next day."*
> **Five shifts are enough** for this lesson. Put them on a card and drill those only.
> **Support:** the shift card stays visible. **Stretch:** report a three-line exchange with pronouns and time words adjusted.
> **Arabic ~12%** — the backshift idea glossed once.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
When you report, the moment of speaking has already passed — so English pulls the tense backwards to mark that
distance. Once you see it as *one step back* rather than a list of pairs, most of it becomes predictable.

> وخد بالك: الضمائر وكلمات الوقت بتتغير كمان.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Reporting a phone call (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** What did the doctor say?
> **B:** He said the results **were** fine. He told me I **needed** to rest for a week.
> **A:** Did he say anything about the medication?
> **B:** He said he **would** send a prescription, and that I **could** stop the tablets.
> **A:** So what did you say?
> **B:** I told him I **was** feeling much better. He said that **was** normal — but he also said I **shouldn't**
> go back to the gym yet.

**Notice the shift.** The doctor actually said *"the results **are** fine · you **need** to rest · I **will** send
· you **can** stop."* Reported, every one of those moved one step back.

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *are* | *were* |
| *need* | *needed* |
| *will* | *would* |
| *can* | *could* |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners reconstruct the doctor's original words. Working backwards makes
> the shift concrete.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Everything moves one step back.**

| Direct speech | → | Reported |
|---|---|---|
| present simple — *works* | → | past simple — *worked* |
| *am / is* | → | *was* |
| *are* | → | *were* |
| past simple — *worked* | → | past perfect — *had worked* |
| *will* | → | *would* |
| *can* | → | *could* |

> ⚠️ **الفكرة:** الزمن بيرجع خطوة واحدة لورا، لأن لحظة الكلام خلصت.

**1) The five you actually need at B1** are the shaded ones above: *is→was · are→were · present→past ·
will→would · can→could*. Master those and you can report almost anything.

**2) Pronouns change with the speaker.**

> *"**I** lost **my** keys," said Ali.* → *Ali said **he**'d lost **his** keys.*

**3) Time and place words change too:**

| Direct | Reported |
|---|---|
| *today* | *that day* |
| *tomorrow* | *the next day* |
| *yesterday* | *the day before* |
| *now* | *then* |
| *here* | *there* |

**4) An honest exception, and a common one.** If the fact is **still true**, speakers often keep the present:

> *She said she **lives** in Cairo.* ✅ perfectly normal — she still does
> *She said she **lived** in Cairo.* ✅ also correct

Do not worry about which. Both are used.

**5) Some things never shift:** *would · could · should · might* are already back, so they stay.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on the shift table — five items only — two on pronouns and time words, two on the
> still-true exception. That exception prevents over-correction later.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the reporting frame is fast and flat; the reported content carries the meaning.**

> *He said the results were **FINE**.*
> *She told me she'd **FINISHED**.*

**Part 2 — *would* and *had* reduce to *'d*, and they sound identical.**

> *he**'d** call* = *he would call*
> *he**'d** finished* = *he had finished*

Context tells them apart: *'d* + base verb is *would*; *'d* + participle is *had*. Learners need this for
listening more than for speaking.

**Part 3 — keep the past endings clean** under the extra length of a reported sentence:

> *need**ed*** /ɪd/ · *work**ed*** /t/ · *finish**ed*** /t/

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"He said the results were fine and he'd send a prescription."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes on frame-versus-content stress, two on the *'d* ambiguity. The second is a genuine
> comprehension unlock.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Reporting moves the tense **one step back**.
- The five that matter: *is→was · are→were · present→past · will→would · can→could*.
- **Pronouns** and **time words** shift too: *tomorrow → the next day*.
- If the fact is **still true**, keeping the present is fine.
- *would · could · should · might* do not shift.
