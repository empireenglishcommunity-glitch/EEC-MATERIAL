# *where* / *whose*

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 6 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit6-relative-clauses.md` · **S2-U6-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** use *where* for places and *whose* for possession · choose between *where* and *which* correctly · describe your own world in detail.
> **The trip-up is *which* for a place:** ❌ *"the place which I live."* *Where* already contains *in which*, so nothing else is needed. Contrast the two directly — *the place **where** I live* against *the place **which** I love* — because both are correct with different structures underneath.
> **Do not teach *whose* as possession in the abstract.** Teach it as the answer to *"whose?"*: *the man **whose** car was stolen*. One clear question makes it usable.
> ***whose* is not *who's*.** They sound identical, which matters for writing only — mention it once, do not dwell.
> **This lesson is where learners describe their own city, family and workplace.** Personal content, so expect real engagement and let them talk.
> **Support:** the two-column *where/whose* card. **Stretch:** three sentences describing your city with both pronouns.
> **Arabic ~12%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Now that you can define people and things, the two missing pieces are **places** and **belonging** — and those are
exactly what you need to describe your own city, home and family, which is the most common thing anybody asks you
about.

> و**where** للمكان، و**whose** للملكية. وأهم نقطة إن **where** جواها معنى "في"، فمفيش حرف جر بعدها.
> والاختبار: لو الجملة محتاجة "في" يبقى **where**.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Describing a neighbourhood (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** I grew up in a small town **where** everyone knew everyone.
> **B:** That sounds nice, actually.
> **A:** It was — but there was nothing to do. The café **where** we used to sit is a phone shop now.
> **B:** Do you remember the neighbour **whose** dog barked all night?
> **A:** How could I forget? And the family **whose** house had that huge tree?
> **B:** They moved to Alexandria. Their son is the one **who** became a doctor.
> **A:** It's strange going back to a place **where** nothing looks the same.

**Notice:** *where* introduces a **place** and never needs a preposition after it. *Whose* answers the question
*whose?* — the neighbour's dog, the family's house.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| a town where everyone knew everyone | بلد الكل فيها يعرف الكل |
| the café where we used to sit | الكافيه اللي كنا نقعد فيه |
| the neighbour whose dog barked | الجار اللي كلبه كان بينبح |
| the family whose house… | العيلة اللي بيتها… |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners note that no *where* clause contains *in it* or *there*. That
> absence is the rule.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) *where* = for places, and it already means *in which*.**

> *This is the house **where** I was born.*
> *I work in an office **where** nobody talks.*

Because *where* contains the preposition, you do not add one:

> *the town **where** I live* ✅
> *the town where I live **in*** ❌
> *the town **which** I live* ❌ — *which* is not a place word here

> ⚠️ **خد بالك:** **where** جواها "في". ماتقولش *the place where I live in*، ولا *the place which I live*.

**2) But *which* is still right when the place is the object of the verb:**

| | |
|---|---|
| *the city **where** I grew up* | I grew up **in** it |
| *the city **which** I love* | I love **it** — no preposition needed |

The test: does the sentence need *in / at / on*? If yes → **where**.

**3) *whose* = possession, for people and sometimes things.**

> *the man **whose** car was stolen* — his car
> *a book **whose** ending I hated* — its ending

**The question test:** if you could ask *"whose ___?"*, use *whose*.

**4) *whose* is followed directly by the noun** it belongs to — no *the*:

> *the woman **whose** son plays football* ✅ · *the woman whose **the** son…* ❌

**5) Spelling only:** *whose* (possession) and *who's* (= who is) sound the same. In writing they are different
words.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on *where* and the no-preposition rule, two on the *where/which* test, two on *whose*
> with the question test. Keep the *who's* note to fifteen seconds.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *whose* is /huːz/**, with a clear long vowel and a voiced /z/:

> **whose** → /huːz/ — not /hoʊs/, and the final sound is **z**, not **s**

Say *whose · shoes · lose* — same vowel, same ending.

**Part 2 — *where* is /wer/**, and the /w/ must be there:

> **where** → /wer/ — round the lips first; without the /w/ it collapses toward *air*

**Part 3 — the grouping from L01, over a longer clause.** These clauses tend to be longer, so the pause discipline
matters more:

> *I grew up in a small town ⟋ **where everyone knew everyone**.*

**Part 4 — keep *where* and *whose* unstressed.** The stress falls on the noun or verb that follows:

> *the man whose **CAR** was stolen* · *the town where I **GREW UP***

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I grew up in a town where everyone knew the family whose house had the big tree."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on /huːz/ — the /z/ is genuinely often wrong — one on /wer/, two on grouping the longer
> clause.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *where* or *whose*?**
1. the restaurant ______ we had dinner
2. the student ______ phone rang
3. a city ______ the buses are free
4. the author ______ books I collect

**Exercise 2 — join the sentences.**
1. *That's the school. I studied there.* → __________________________
2. *I met a woman. Her brother is famous.* → __________________________
3. *This is the café. We first met here.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *the town where I live in* → __________________________
2. *the place which I was born* → __________________________
3. *the man who's car was stolen* → __________________________
4. *the woman whose the son plays football* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — describe your world.** Four sentences: your street, your city, one relative, one neighbour — using
*where* twice and *whose* twice.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** where · whose · where · whose. **Ex3:** drop *in* · *where I was born* · *whose* · drop
> *the*. Ex4 is personal and usually generates the best language of the lesson.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **where** = for places, and it already means *in which* — add no preposition.
- Use **which** when the place is the object: *the city which I love*.
- **whose** = possession; test it with the question *whose ___?*
- **whose** is followed by a bare noun — no *the*.
- /huːz/ with a **z**; /wer/ with a real **w**.
