## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
When you do not know a word — which happens constantly, at every level — you have two options: stop, or **define
your way around it**. That skill is called circumlocution, and it is one of the most practical things an
intermediate learner can own. It is built almost entirely from relative clauses.

> ودي مهارة عملية: لو الكلمة ضاعت، وصّفها.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Someone describing a word they cannot remember (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** What's the word for the thing **that** you use to open a bottle of wine?
> **B:** A corkscrew?
> **A:** That's it. And the shop **where** you buy medicine?
> **B:** A pharmacy.
> **A:** Right. I was talking to a man **whose** job is to fix those machines **which** print bank cards, and I
> couldn't remember any of the words.
> **B:** You managed, though. You described everything.
> **A:** That's the trick, isn't it? If you don't know a word, you describe the thing **it** does.

**Notice:** she never gets stuck. Each time a word is missing, she builds a definition instead — *the thing that
you use to… · the shop where you buy… · the man whose job is to…*

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| the thing that you use to… | الحاجة اللي بتستخدمها عشان… |
| the shop where you buy… | الدكان اللي بتشتري منه… |
| the man whose job is… | الراجل اللي شغلته… |
| I couldn't remember the word. | مش قادر أفتكر الكلمة |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners note the three definition frames. Those frames are the takeaway
> tool, more than any pronoun rule.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Three frames cover almost any definition.**

| Frame | For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *a person **who**…* | people and jobs | *A vet is a person who treats animals.* |
| *a thing / something **that**…* | objects | *It's a thing that keeps food cold.* |
| *a place **where**…* | places | *It's a place where you borrow books.* |

**And two extras when you need them:**

> *…**whose** job is to…* · *the one **which** …*

**1) Add a purpose with *you use … to*:**

> *It's a thing **that you use to** cut paper.* — scissors

**2) Stack two clauses when one is not enough — but stop at two:**

> *It's the shop **where** you buy medicine **that** stays open all night.* ✅
> Three clauses in one sentence normally collapses; start a new sentence instead.

> ⚠️ خد بالك: جملة فيها وصفين كفاية. لو زودت التالت، المعنى بيتوه.

**3) The escape sentence, worth memorising.** When a word vanishes mid-conversation:

> *Sorry — what's the word for the thing that…?*

That single sentence keeps you in the conversation instead of stopping it.

**4) Precision is the goal.** *"A thing that you use"* defines nothing. Add the **purpose**, the **place**, or the
**material**.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Keep it short — the three frames plus the escape sentence. Train is where the learning happens
> today.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Longer sentences need grouping, not slowing.** The way to sound fluent across a long sentence is to break it
into groups, keeping each group fast:

> *It's the shop ⟋ **where you buy medicine** ⟋ that stays open all night.*

Three groups, each quick, with light edges. Learners who instead slow the whole sentence down sound laboured; the
speed is fine, the **grouping** is what was missing.

**Keep the relative pronouns unstressed** — they are signposts, not content:

> *the thing thət you **USE** to **CUT** paper*

**And keep /huːz/ and /wer/ clean** under the extra length, since they are the first things to blur when a sentence
grows.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"It's a place where you buy medicine that's open late."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Demonstrate slow-and-flat against fast-and-grouped. This is the clearest fluency lever available in
> the unit.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — define these in one sentence each.**
1. a dentist 2. an umbrella 3. a library 4. a mechanic 5. a fridge 6. a passport

**Exercise 2 — guessing game.** Define something without naming it; your partner guesses. Three rounds each. Use
a different pronoun every time.

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *It's a place where you can buy medicine there.* → __________________________
2. *A vet is a person which treats animals.* → __________________________
3. *It's a thing that you use it to cut paper.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — define an abstract idea.** Try one: *a deadline · patience · homesickness · a bargain.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~10 min.** Give Ex2 at least five minutes — it is the heart of the lesson. **Ex3:** drop *there* · *who/that* ·
> drop *it*. Ex4 stretches strong learners without extra input.

## 📜 Your Orders — مهمتك
- Post three definitions and let the group guess.
- **Use the escape sentence when a word disappears** — *"what's the word for the thing that…?"* Stopping is the
  only real failure; describing your way around a gap is what fluent speakers do constantly.

---

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Three frames: *a person **who** · a thing **that** · a place **where***.
- Add purpose with ***you use it to***…
- **Two** clauses per sentence is the practical limit.
- Never repeat the object or the place: ❌ *…that you use **it*** · ❌ *…where you buy **there***.
- Group long sentences; do not slow them down.
