# Leaving Out the Relative Pronoun

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 6 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit6-relative-clauses.md` · **S2-U6-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** recognise object relative clauses · drop the pronoun where English allows it · deliver the reduced clause smoothly.
> **Do not over-drill the rule.** The goal is natural-sounding speech, not a subject/object analysis learners can recite. Model many examples and let them imitate; the pattern is absorbed far faster than it is explained.
> **The one-line test is enough:** if another **subject follows** the pronoun, you can drop it. *The film **I** saw* — *I* is there, so *that* is optional. *The film **that won*** — nothing follows but the verb, so *that* must stay.
> **Nothing is ever wrong by keeping it.** Say that explicitly, because learners get anxious about a rule they cannot see. Dropping it sounds more fluent; keeping it is always correct.
> **This is a listening problem before it is a speaking one.** Learners often fail to parse *"the man I met"* because they are waiting for a pronoun that never comes. Practise hearing it too.
> **Support:** paired sentences with and without the pronoun. **Stretch:** a short description using three reduced clauses.
> **Arabic ~12%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
This is one of the few places where **removing** something makes your English better. Native speakers drop the
pronoun constantly, so you need it twice: to sound natural when you speak, and — more urgently — to understand
people when they do it to you.

> ولو مستنّي كلمة رابطة وهي مش جاية، ذهنك بيتوقف.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Talking about a weekend (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Did you like the film **we saw**?
> **B:** The one **you chose**? Honestly, no.
> **A:** Why not? Everyone **I know** loved it.
> **B:** It was too long. The book **I read** last year was much better.
> **A:** That's the thing **I don't understand** about you — you always prefer the book.
> **B:** Because the book has the parts **they cut**. Anyway, the restaurant **you booked** was excellent.
> **A:** See? Something **I chose** worked.

**Notice what is missing.** *The film **we saw**. Everyone **I know**. The book **I read**.* There is no *that* or
*which* anywhere — and every sentence is completely correct.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| the film we saw | الفيلم اللي شوفناه |
| everyone I know | كل اللي أعرفهم |
| the book I read | الكتاب اللي قريته |
| the restaurant you booked | المطعم اللي حجزته |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners insert *that* into three of the lines and confirm the meaning is
> unchanged. Both versions are correct — that is the reassurance they need.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**One test: is there another subject right after the pronoun?**

| | Test | Pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| *the film **(that) I** saw* | *I* follows → yes | **optional** |
| *the film **that won*** | only a verb follows | **required** |

**1) When you can drop it** — the noun is the **object** of the clause:

> *the man **(who) I** met* · *the book **(that) she** wrote* · *the thing **(which) we** need*

**2) When you cannot** — the noun is the **subject** of the clause:

> *the man **who** lives here* ✅ · *the man lives here* ❌ — now it is just a sentence
> *the bus **that** goes to town* ✅

> ⚠️ **الفرق في سطر:** لو بعد الكلمة الرابطة فاعل تاني، تقدر تشيلها.

**3) Keeping it is never wrong.**

> *the film **that** I saw* ✅ correct, slightly more careful
> *the film I saw* ✅ correct, more natural

**4) *where* and *whose* are not dropped.** They carry meaning that nothing else supplies:

> *the place **where** I live* — *where* stays.

**5) Watch the double-object trap.** Do not leave the old object behind:

> *the film I saw* ✅ · *the film I saw **it*** ❌

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on the test, two on many spoken examples for imitation, one on "keeping it is fine", one
> on the leftover-object error. Resist a full subject/object grammar lecture.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the reduced clause has no pause at all.** With the pronoun gone, the two nouns run straight together:

> *the film we saw* → *the-**film**-we-**saw*** — one smooth group, no gap where *that* used to be

Learners often leave a small silence where the pronoun was. That silence is what makes it sound hesitant rather
than fluent.

**Part 2 — the stress pattern tells the listener what is happening:**

> *the **FILM** we **SAW*** — two beats, everything else squeezed

**Part 3 — train the ear, not just the mouth.** Listen for these shapes; they are extremely common and easy to
miss:

> *the guy I work with · the thing you said · the place we went · everything I do*

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"The film we saw was worse than the book I read."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes eliminating the phantom pause, one on stress, two on listening. Say the four phrases at
> natural speed and have learners repeat immediately — imitation beats explanation here.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — can the pronoun be dropped? Yes or no.**
1. *the woman who called me*
2. *the woman who I called*
3. *the bus that goes to the airport*
4. *the bus that I take every day*

**Exercise 2 — remove the pronoun where possible.**
1. *the letter that she sent* → __________________________
2. *the man who lives upstairs* → __________________________
3. *the food which we ordered* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *The film I saw it was long.* → __________________________
2. *The man lives next door is friendly.* → __________________________
3. *the place I live in it* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — describe.** Three sentences about things in your week, all with the pronoun dropped: something you
watched, someone you met, something you bought.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex1:** no · yes · no · yes. **Ex2:** *the letter she sent* · cannot drop · *the food we ordered*.
> **Ex3:** drop *it* · restore *who* · *the place where I live*. Ex1 is the diagnostic and worth doing aloud
> together.
