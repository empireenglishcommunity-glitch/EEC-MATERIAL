# *who / which / that* — People & Things

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 6 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 5
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit6-relative-clauses.md` · **S2-U6-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** join two sentences with *who*, *which* or *that* · define people and things · say the clause as one intonation group.
> **The error to hunt is the doubled subject:** ❌ *"A doctor is a person who **he** helps people."* The relative pronoun **is** the subject — it replaces it. Once learners see the replacement, the extra pronoun disappears.
> **Frame this as sentence-joining, not as new grammar.** Two short sentences become one longer one. That framing also explains *why* to bother: relative clauses are the single fastest way to stop sounding like a list of short statements.
> **This is a visible complexity win.** Celebrate the longer sentences explicitly — learners hear their own English get richer in one lesson, which is rare and motivating on the plateau.
> ***That* covers both** people and things in defining clauses, and is the commonest choice in speech. Teach *who/which* as the precise forms, *that* as the natural default.
> **Support:** the joining card with two-sentence pairs. **Stretch:** define an abstract noun.
> **Arabic ~12%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Learners at this level often know enough words but still sound simple, because every sentence is short and
separate. Relative clauses fix that in one structure: they let you attach information **inside** a sentence
instead of starting a new one.

> ودي أسرع طريقة تخلي جملك أطول وأنضف، من غير قواعد جديدة كتير.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Describing colleagues and things (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Who's the woman **who** was at the meeting yesterday?
> **B:** That's Nadia — she's the engineer **who** designed the new system.
> **A:** And the app **that** everyone keeps talking about, is that hers too?
> **B:** The one **which** won the award, yes. She's the kind of person **who** never mentions it.
> **A:** I need to meet her.
> **B:** She's the only person here **that** understands the old database.

**Notice:** each *who / which / that* introduces information that **defines** which person or thing is meant —
not extra decoration, but the identifying detail.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| the woman who was there | الست اللي كانت هناك |
| the engineer who designed it | المهندسة اللي صممته |
| the app that everyone talks about | الأبليكيشن اللي الكل بيتكلم عنه |
| the one which won | اللي كسب |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners identify the noun each clause defines. Point out that Arabic uses
> one word — *اللي* — where English chooses between three.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**You are joining two sentences. The pronoun replaces the repeated noun.**

> *I know a man. **He** repairs bikes.*
> → *I know a man **who** repairs bikes.*

**1) Which word to use:**

| Pronoun | For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | people | *the doctor **who** helped me* |
| **which** | things and animals | *the book **which** changed my mind* |
| **that** | **both** — and the most common in speech | *the person **that** called · the car **that** broke* |

**2) The pronoun *is* the subject — do not add another one.**

> *A teacher is someone **who** explains things.* ✅
> *A teacher is someone who **he** explains things.* ❌

> ⚠️ **دي أشهر غلطة:** إنك تضيف ضمير تاني. كلمة **who** هي نفسها الفاعل، فمفيش داعي لـ**he** بعدها.

**3) Put the clause **immediately after** the noun it defines.**

> *The man **who lives next door** has three dogs.* ✅
> *The man has three dogs who lives next door.* ❌ — now the dogs live next door

**4) These clauses are *defining*** — they answer *which one?* and take **no commas**:

> *The students **who finished early** left.* — only those students

**5) Useful definition frames:**

> *A ___ is a person **who** … · A ___ is a thing **which** … · It's the kind of place **that** …*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on joining pairs of sentences aloud, two on the doubled subject, two on clause
> placement. The placement error is funny in examples and therefore memorable.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the clause is one intonation group, not a new sentence.**

> *The man ⟋ **who lives next door** ⟋ has three dogs.*

Say it with a full stop before *who* and the sentence breaks. Say it as one flowing group with light edges and it
holds together. This grouping is what makes long sentences sound easy rather than laboured.

**Part 2 — *who* and *that* are weak.** They are grammar words, so they get squeezed:

> *that* → /ðət/ · *who* → /hu/ — quick, unstressed

The stress goes on the **verb** inside the clause:

> *the person that **CALLED** me*

**Part 3 — *which* keeps its /w/ and its final /tʃ/** clean, or it blurs into the next word.

> ولاحظ إن الجملة الطويلة بتبان سهلة لما تنطقها كوحدة واحدة، مش لما تقطعها.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"She's the engineer who designed the system that everyone uses."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Model the broken version against the grouped version. Learners who master the grouping stop fearing
> long sentences, which matters more than any single form here.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *who* or *which*?**
1. the woman ______ works upstairs
2. the phone ______ I bought last year
3. the students ______ passed
4. the film ______ won the prize

**Exercise 2 — join the sentences.**
1. *I have a friend. He speaks five languages.* → __________________________
2. *This is the book. It changed my life.* → __________________________
3. *She's the teacher. She taught me English.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *A nurse is a person who she looks after patients.* → __________________________
2. *The man has a dog who lives next door.* → __________________________
3. *I know someone which can help.* → __________________________
4. *The book who I read was long.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — define five things.** Define these in one sentence each: *a dentist · a fridge · a mechanic · a
library · a passport.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** who · which · who · which. **Ex3:** drop *she* · move the clause after *man* · *who/that* ·
> *which/that*. Ex4 is the fluency payoff — take answers around the room quickly.
