## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 6 you'll **stop speaking in short sentences** — define people, things and
> places inside one sentence, show possession, and describe your way around any word you cannot remember.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تضيف معلومة جوّه الجملة، فكلامك يبقى أغنى وأقل تقطيع.

**Why this unit changes how advanced you sound:** most learners at this point know enough words but still sound
simple, because every sentence is short and separate. Relative clauses are the fastest available fix — one
structure, and your sentences get visibly longer and richer.

> ودي أوضح علامة على التقدم، لأنها بتتسمع فورًا.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | *who · which · that* | join two sentences into one, defining people and things |
| **L02** | *where · whose* | places and possession — and *where* against *which* |
| **L03** | Dropping the pronoun | *the film I saw* — sound fluent by saying less |
| **L04** | Defining in detail | describe your way around any missing word |
| **L05** | Describe a person/place (milestone) 🎙️ | an 8–10 sentence description + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** defining relative clauses — subject relatives (*who/which/that*), *where* and *whose*, and
object relatives with the pronoun omitted.
**Accent Lab:** saying each clause as **one intonation group** · unstressed relative pronouns · */huːz/* for
*whose* · */wer/* for *where* · no phantom pause where a dropped pronoun used to be.
**Mindset:** upgrade your input diet — pick sources one notch above comfortable.
**Arabic support:** ~12% through L01–L03, easing to ~10% for L04–L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-6 description** — audibly longer, richer sentences than Unit 1.
- Circumlocution: the ability to keep talking when a word disappears.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **Frame the whole unit as sentence-joining**, not as new grammar. Two short sentences become one longer one —
> which also answers the learner's *why bother?*
> **L01's error is the doubled subject:** ❌ *"a person who **he** helps people."* The pronoun **is** the subject.
> Also watch clause placement — *"The man has three dogs who lives next door"* is funny and therefore memorable.
> Teach *who/which* as the precise forms and *that* as the natural spoken default.
> **L02's trip-up is *which* for a place.** *Where* already contains *in which*, so ❌ *"the place where I live in"*
> and ❌ *"the place which I live"* are both wrong — but *"the city which I love"* is right, because there is no
> preposition. Give them the test: does the sentence need *in/at/on*? Teach *whose* through the question *whose
> ___?* and note it takes a **bare** noun.
> **L03 should not be over-drilled.** The goal is natural speech, not a subject/object analysis. One test —
> *another subject after the pronoun means you can drop it* — plus many imitable examples. Say clearly that
> **keeping it is never wrong**, because learners get anxious about an invisible rule. And treat it as a
> **listening** skill first: learners stall on *"the man I met"* because they are waiting for a pronoun that never
> arrives.
> **L04 is a fluency lesson in grammar clothing.** The guessing game is the lesson — give Train its full ten
> minutes, because precision suddenly has a purpose beyond the teacher. Teach the natural ceiling of **two**
> clauses per sentence rather than rewarding maximum length.
> **L05 is where you celebrate visible complexity.** Put it beside a Unit-1 recording; sentence length and richness
> are the most audible progress markers in Stage 2, and this is the unit that produces them.
