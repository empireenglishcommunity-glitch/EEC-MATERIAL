# *For* and *Since* — "How Long Have You…?"

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 2 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit2-present-perfect-recent.md` · **S2-U2-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 1.
> **Objectives:** use *for* (a period) and *since* (a starting point) with the present perfect · ask and answer *How long have you…?* · describe situations still true now.
> **This is the highest-value fix in the unit.** Arabic marks an ongoing situation with the present, so learners produce ❌ *"I live here since 2019"* — and because it is perfectly understandable, nobody corrects it and it survives for years. Anchor it: **unfinished time reaching now → present perfect.**
> **The *for/since* choice itself is mechanical** — duration against point. Do not spend the lesson there; spend it on the tense that carries them.
> **Note the meaning:** *I've lived here for five years* means **I still live here**. That is what "unfinished" means, and it is why the past simple is wrong.
> **Support:** the timeline card + a phrase sort. **Stretch:** interview a partner with three *How long…?* questions.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
*How long have you…?* is one of the first questions anybody asks you — about your job, your city, your English.
Answering it correctly marks you instantly as beyond A2. Answering it with the present tense is the single most
common intermediate error there is.

> والعربي بيستخدم المضارع هنا، فالجملة مفهومة بس غلط.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Meeting a new colleague (📹 in the portal).

> **A: How long have** you **worked** here?
> **B:** I**'ve worked** here **for** about three years. And you?
> **A:** I**'ve** only **been** here **since** March, so I'm still learning.
> **B: How long have** you **known** Layla? You seemed familiar.
> **A:** Oh, I**'ve known** her **since** we were at university — **for** nearly ten years.
> **B:** That's a long time.

**Notice:** every one of those situations is **still true right now** — he still works here, she still knows
Layla. That is why English uses this tense and not the past simple.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| How long have you worked here? | بقالك قد إيه بتشتغل هنا؟ |
| for three years | بقالي تلات سنين |
| since March | من مارس |
| I've known her since… | أعرفها من… |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: for each sentence, learners answer *is it still true?* Every answer is
> yes — that is the pattern the tense encodes.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) The tense first: unfinished time takes the present perfect.**

If a situation **started in the past and is still true now**, English uses **have/has + participle** — not the
present, and not the past simple.

| | |
|---|---|
| *I**'ve lived** here for five years.* | ✅ and I still live here |
| *I live here for five years.* | ❌ |
| *I **lived** here for five years.* | ✅ but I have **moved away** |

> ⚠️ **أهم غلطة في الوحدة:** الحالة اللي لسه مستمرة بتاخد الزمن الجديد. بنقول *I've lived here for
> five years*، مش *I live here for five years*.

**2) Then the easy part — *for* or *since*?**

| | Takes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **for** | a **period** — how long | *for two hours · for a week · for ten years · for a long time* |
| **since** | a **point** — when it started | *since Monday · since 2019 · since I was a child · since we met* |

**The test:** could you answer *"how long?"* → **for**. Could you answer *"starting when?"* → **since**.

**3) The question.**

> ***How long have*** *you* + participle**?**
> *How long have you had that car?* · *How long has she been here?*

**4) The verbs this happens with** are mostly states: *live · work · know · have · be · study*.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Four minutes on the tense — that is the real content — and three on the *for/since* sort. Test
> with a phrase sort, then push straight into *How long…?* pairs.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *for* is weak.** It almost never sounds like the word *four*:

> *for* → /fə/ — *"I've worked here **fə** three years"*

Say it strong and the sentence sounds like a list of separate words. It only becomes /fɔːr/ when you stress it
for contrast.

**Part 2 — *since* keeps its /s/ endings.** Both of them:

> *since* /sɪns/ — not *"sin"*. The final /s/ matters before a vowel: *since April* → *sin-**s**April*.

**Part 3 — link the auxiliary into the participle**, as in Unit 1:

> *I've lived* → *I-**vlived*** · *I've known* → *I-**vknown***

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I've lived here for five years and I've known her since 2019."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes on weak *for* — it is the most audible improvement available in this lesson — then
> *since*, then the linking.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *for* or *since*?**
1. ______ three months  2. ______ Tuesday  3. ______ a long time
4. ______ 2015  5. ______ I graduated  6. ______ half an hour

**Exercise 2 — put the verb in the right tense.**
1. I ______ (know) him for ten years.
2. She ______ (work) here since January.
3. We ______ (live) in Cairo for a while, then we moved to Alexandria.
4. How long ______ you ______ (have) that phone?

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I live here since 2019.* → __________________________
2. *I've worked here since three years.* → __________________________
3. *She knows me for a long time.* → __________________________
4. *How long do you study English?* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** How long have you: lived where you live · done your job or studies · known your
oldest friend?

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** for · since · for · since · since · for. **Ex2:** have known · has worked · lived (finished
> — they moved) · have you had. **Ex3:** *I've lived* · *for three years* · *She's known me* · *How long **have**
> you studied…?* Ex2.3 is the useful trap: *for a while* with a finished situation genuinely takes the past.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Still true now → **have/has + participle**, never the present simple.
- **for** + a period · **since** + a starting point.
- *How long have you…?* — the question that proves you are past A2.
- Past simple means it **stopped**: *I lived there for five years* (and left).
- **for** is weak: /fə/.
