# Present Perfect **vs** Past Simple — the Hinge

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 2 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~40 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L02 · Stage 1 past simple
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit2-present-perfect-recent.md` · **S2-U2-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~40 min):** Warm-up 5 · Input 6 · Decode 8 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Feedback 1 · Assign 1.
> **Objectives:** choose present perfect for indefinite or unfinished time and past simple for a specific finished time · switch to the past simple when a detail is added · narrate an experience then give its specifics correctly.
> **This is the single most important grammar lesson in Stage 2, and it will not be mastered today.** Say that to yourself before you start. The realistic goal is that learners leave with **one reliable question** they can ask themselves, plus many contrastive repetitions. Mastery comes from re-drilling it in every remaining unit.
> **Teach the trigger, not the event.** Learners try to decide from the meaning of what happened, which is unreliable. The **time expression decides**: a specific finished time forces the past simple, and nothing else does.
> **The "announce, then detail" chain is the productive pattern** and the one that transfers to real speech: *"I've got a new job — I started last week."* Drill the chain, not isolated sentences.
> **Support:** the decision card stays on the desk all lesson. **Stretch:** a monologue that moves between the tenses without prompting.
> **Arabic ~15%** — the rule glossed once, then English.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
English has two ways to talk about the past and they are not interchangeable. Choosing wrongly rarely stops
communication, which is exactly why the error lasts: it is comfortable, understandable, and permanently
noticeable to a fluent listener.

> دي أهم قاعدة في المرحلة، والزمن بيتحدد من كلمة الوقت.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
An experience turning into details (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Have you ever **been** to Italy?
> **B:** Yes, I**'ve been** twice, actually.
> **A:** Nice — when?
> **B:** I **went** the first time **in 2019**, and I **went** again **last summer** with my sister. We
> **stayed** in Naples and we **ate** far too much pizza.
> **A:** Have you **been** to Rome?
> **B:** No, I **haven't** — not **yet**.

**Watch the pivot.** The conversation opens in the present perfect (*have you been · I've been*). The moment
**"when?"** is answered, every verb switches to the past simple (*went · stayed · ate*). Then the last question
returns to experience — and returns to the present perfect.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| I've been twice. | رحت مرتين |
| I went in 2019. | رحت سنة ٢٠١٩ |
| We stayed in Naples. | قعدنا في نابولي |
| I haven't been yet. | لسه ما رحت |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners mark where the tense changes and what caused it. The cause is
> always a time expression.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Ask one question: *is there a specific finished time?***

| Answer | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes** — a finished time is named or implied | **past simple** | *I **went** yesterday.* |
| **No** — no time, or the time is still open | **present perfect** | *I**'ve been** there.* |

**1) These expressions force the past simple.** They close the time:

> *yesterday · last week · in 2019 · two days ago · when I was young · at school · this morning* (if it is over)

**2) These sit comfortably with the present perfect.** The time is open or absent:

> *ever · never · before · already · yet · just · recently · today · this week · so far · in my life*

**3) So the same event takes either tense, depending only on the time phrase.**

| | |
|---|---|
| *I**'ve lost** my keys.* | they are still lost — it matters now |
| *I **lost** my keys yesterday.* | a finished moment |
| *She**'s broken** her arm.* | it is still broken |
| *She **broke** her arm in March.* | a finished moment |

> ⚠️ **الخلاصة:** لو الوقت محدد وخلص، ماضي بسيط. لو مفيش وقت أو الوقت لسه مفتوح، الزمن الجديد.
> والكلمة اللي بتحدد الوقت هي اللي بتقرر.

**4) The pattern you will actually use — announce, then detail.**

> *I**'ve got** a new job.* → *I **started** on Monday.*
> *We**'ve moved** house.* → *We **moved** in April.*
> *I**'ve read** that book.* → *I **read** it when I was at school.*

**Announce with the perfect. Explain with the past.** That is how real conversations run.

**5) The error to kill.** Never put a finished time with the present perfect:

> *I've been to Paris last year.* ❌ → *I **went** to Paris last year.* ✅

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** Board the one question and the two word-lists, then spend the rest on the announce-then-detail
> chain. Do not add exceptions today — *this morning* is enough nuance for one lesson.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Switching tense mid-turn is a pronunciation task as much as a grammar one.** The two forms sound completely
different, and the switch has to stay clean at speed:

> *I**'ve** start**ed**…* → contraction + /ɪd/ ending
> *I start**ed**…* → no auxiliary, same /ɪd/ ending

**Drill the chain aloud.** Say each pair as one breath, so the switch happens under pressure:

> *"I've finished the course — I finished last week."*
> *"I've seen that film — I saw it twice."*
> *"We've moved — we moved in April."*

Two things to protect: the **contraction** in the first half (drop it and both halves become past simple) and the
**-ed / irregular form** in the second.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I've been to Italy — I went in 2019 and I stayed for a week."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** The chained pairs are the drill. Listen specifically for a swallowed *'ve*, which collapses the
> whole contrast the lesson is teaching.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — choose the tense.**
1. I ______ (see) that film three times.
2. I ______ (see) it last Friday.
3. She ______ (live) in Cairo since 2018.
4. She ______ (live) in Cairo when she was a child.
5. ______ you ever ______ (eat) sushi?
6. What time ______ you ______ (get) home last night?

**Exercise 2 — announce, then detail.** Add a past-simple second sentence.
1. *I've bought a new phone.* → __________________________
2. *We've finished the project.* → __________________________
3. *He's passed his exam.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I've seen him yesterday.* → __________________________
2. *Did you ever go to Japan?* (experience) → __________________________
3. *I've finished my degree in 2020.* → __________________________
4. *When have you arrived?* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — talk.** Give an experience with no time, then let your partner ask *"when?"* and answer in the
past simple.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex1:** have seen · saw · has lived · lived · Have … eaten · did … get. **Ex3:** *I saw him
> yesterday* · *Have you ever been to Japan?* · *I finished my degree in 2020* · *When did you arrive?*
> Ex3.4 is worth naming: ***when*** is a specific-time question, so it never takes the present perfect.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- One question: **is there a specific finished time?** Yes → past simple. No → present perfect.
- The **time expression** decides, not the type of event.
- *yesterday · last week · in 2019 · ago* → past simple, always.
- *ever · never · yet · already · recently · so far* → present perfect.
- **Announce with the perfect, explain with the past.**
- ***When…?*** always takes the past simple.
