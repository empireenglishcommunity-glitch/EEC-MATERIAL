# *Just · Already · Yet*

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 2 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 1 (present perfect form)
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit2-present-perfect-recent.md` · **S2-U2-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 7 · Your Turn 3 · Assign 1.
> **Objectives:** use *just* (very recent), *already* (sooner than expected) and *yet* (until now, in negatives and questions) · talk about recently finished actions · ask *Have you … yet?* and answer.
> **The slip is *yet* in a positive sentence** — ❌ *"I've done it yet."* Contrast it directly against *already*, which is the positive twin. Teaching the two separately is what lets learners cross them.
> **Position is the grammar here, not meaning.** *Just* and *already* go **between auxiliary and participle**; *yet* goes at the **end**. Learners who know the meanings still put them in the wrong slot.
> ***Already* carries surprise.** *"She's already left"* is not neutral — it means sooner than I expected. Learners who use it as a plain past marker sound oddly astonished by everything.
> **Support:** the position card. **Stretch:** add a past-simple detail — *"I've already done it; I finished an hour ago"* — which previews L03.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

---

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Say something happened **a moment ago** — *I've just finished*.
- Say it happened **sooner than expected** — *She's already left*.
- Ask whether something has happened **up to now** — *Have you eaten yet?* 🎙️

> تلات كلمات صغيرة بتغيّر إحساس الجملة بالكامل.

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
These three words are how English marks *recency* — and they are everywhere in ordinary speech: at work, in
messages, at the door. Without them you can say *what* happened but not *how recently*, which is usually the
part the other person cares about.

> التلاتة بيوصفوا حاجة قريبة، والفرق في مكان الكلمة.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Three short exchanges (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Are you ready to order?
> **B:** Sorry, we haven't decided **yet**. Give us two minutes.
> **A:** Where's Omar? I need to talk to him.
> **B:** He's **already** gone — he left at four.
> **A:** Already? That's early.
> **B:** I know. Oh — I've **just** heard from him, actually. He's on the train.
> **A:** **Have** you **eaten yet**?
> **B:** No, not **yet**. Have you?

**Notice the slots.** *Just* and *already* sit **before the participle**. *Yet* sits at the **very end**, and it
only appears in the negative and the question.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| I've just heard. | سمعت حالًا |
| He's already gone. | مشي خلاص |
| We haven't decided yet. | لسه ما قررنا |
| Have you eaten yet? | أكلت؟ (لسه؟) |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. On the second pass learners mark each adverb's position. The distribution — two in the
> middle, one at the end — is the lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) The meanings are distinct.**

| Word | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **just** | a moment ago | *I've **just** finished.* |
| **already** | sooner than expected | *She's **already** left.* |
| **yet** | up to now — still waiting | *He hasn't arrived **yet**.* |

**2) The positions are fixed.**

> *I've **just** / **already** + participle* — between auxiliary and participle
> *… participle … **yet***. — at the end of the sentence

**3) The sentence type decides which one you may use.**

| | already | yet |
|---|---|---|
| positive | ✅ *I've **already** eaten.* | ❌ |
| negative | ❌ | ✅ *I haven't eaten **yet**.* |
| question | sometimes (surprise) | ✅ *Have you eaten **yet**?* |

> ⚠️ **خد بالك:** **yet** للنفي والسؤال، و**already** للإيجابي.

**4) *Already* is not neutral.** It carries mild surprise — *earlier than I thought*. If nothing is surprising,
leave it out.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Two minutes on meaning, three on position, two on the sentence-type grid. Drill by transformation:
> give a positive with *already* and ask for the question with *yet*.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *just* and *already* are unstressed and fast.** They are grammar words, so they get squeezed:

> *I've just finished* → *I've-**jəs**-finished* — the /t/ often disappears before a consonant
> *He's already gone* → *He's-**ɔːlredi**-gone*

The stress stays on the **main verb**, not the adverb.

**Part 2 — *yet* is different: it is final and it is clear.** Sitting at the end of the sentence, it carries the
falling beat and its /t/ is released:

> *I haven't finished **yet**.* ↘️

**Part 3 — the question rises, and *yet* still lands last:**

> *Have you eaten **yet**?* ↗️

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I've just eaten, but she hasn't arrived yet."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes squeezing *just/already*, two making *yet* final and audible. Learners who stress
> *just* sound emphatic in a way they do not intend.

**Exercise 2 — *already* or *yet*?**
1. I've ______ seen this film — twice.
2. He hasn't woken up ______.
3. Have you spoken to her ______?

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I've done it yet.* → __________________________
2. *She has yet finished.* → __________________________
3. *I've eaten already my lunch.* → __________________________
4. *Have you already the report sent?* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** Two things you have **just** or **already** done today, and one you have **not
done yet**.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **just** = a moment ago · **already** = sooner than expected · **yet** = up to now.
- **just / already** go **before the participle**.
- **yet** goes at the **end**, and only in negatives and questions.
- **already** is the positive twin of **yet** — they never swap.
- *just* and *already* are squeezed; *yet* is final and clear.
