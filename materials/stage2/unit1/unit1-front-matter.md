## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 1 you'll **talk about your life, not your day** — say what you have
> done, ask anybody what they have done, say what you have never done, and tell a listener whether somebody
> is back or still away.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تحكي خبرة حياتك كلها في جمل، من غير ما تحدد وقت.

**Why this is the doorway to B1:** every tense you have used so far pointed at a moment — *now*, *yesterday*,
*next week*. The present perfect points at a **span**: everything up to now. That is the grammar of experience,
and experience is what adults actually talk about.

> ودي أول وحدة نتيجتها إنك تتكلم عن نفسك ككل، مش عن روتينك.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | The form | **have/has + participle** · contractions · *no specific time* |
| **L02** | Past participles | the **third column** — and why *I've saw* is wrong |
| **L03** | *Have you ever…?* | open a conversation with anyone · *ever* and *never* |
| **L04** | *Been* vs *gone* | one image: back, or still away |
| **L05** | Life experiences (milestone) 🎙️ | a 7–9 sentence talk + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** present perfect for indefinite experience — form, participles, questions and negatives,
and the *been/gone* contrast.
**Accent Lab:** the contractions ***'ve / 's*** (present but never dropped) · linking *have-you-ever* →
/həvjuˈevər/ · the *-en* participles · *-ed* participles recycled · *been* /ɪ/ against *gone* /ɒ/.
**Mindset:** the **intermediate plateau** — progress stops being visible around B1, and that is normal.
**Arabic support:** ~15% at L01, easing to ~10% by L05. The new concept is glossed; everything else is English.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-1 experiences talk** — the first recognisably B1 recording in your timeline.
- One question, *Have you ever…?*, that will get you real conversation practice for free.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, L04 ~30, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **Hold the line at L01: *no specific time*.** That single constraint makes the tense teachable. The explicit
> present-perfect-against-past-simple contrast is **U2-L03** — bringing it forward means learners juggle two
> rules and lose both. Flag the pivot in L03, model it once, and leave it.
> **L02 carries the unit's top error and two units' worth of value.** ❌ *"I've saw / I've went"* is column two
> standing where column three belongs — not carelessness, since A2 never needed a third column. Drill column
> three relentlessly: **it also powers the passive in U8.** Teach eleven high-frequency participles, not a long
> list; a list produces recognition, not production.
> **L03's double negative is an Arabic interference point.** ❌ *"I haven't never been"*. *Never* is already
> negative and takes a **positive** auxiliary. Also note that *have* is weak everywhere except a short answer,
> where it carries the whole meaning and takes the beat.
> **L04 is one image, not a rule.** Two arrows: *been* = back, *gone* = still away. Thirty minutes is
> deliberate — analysis dissolves this contrast, situations fix it. The usual error is *gone* where *been*
> belongs, which tells a listener you are abroad.
> **L05 is the first clearly-B1 task, and the past-simple detail is the discriminator.** A learner who says
> *"I went in 2022"* has understood the pivot early; one who says *"I've gone in 2022"* has not. Note it, do
> not correct at length mid-recording.
> **Pre-teach the plateau in L01 and reassure in L05.** This is the stage learners conclude they have stopped
> improving. They have not — the gains simply moved from visible to invisible.
