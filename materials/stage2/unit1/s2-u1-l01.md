# Present Perfect Form: *have / has + past participle*

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 1 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Stage 1 (A2 past simple)
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit1-present-perfect-experiences.md` · **S2-U1-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 7 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 6 · Your Turn 4 · Feedback 1 · Assign 1.
> **Objectives:** form the present perfect for every person · understand that it links past to now · produce three or four experience sentences.
> **Hold one line today: *no specific time*.** That single constraint is what makes the tense learnable. The explicit present-perfect-against-past-simple contrast is **U2-L03** — do not bring it forward, or learners will try to hold two rules at once and lose both.
> **The predictable error is a dropped auxiliary.** A learner who means "sometime in my life" produces *"I visited many countries"*, which is a different tense with a different meaning. Gloss the concept in Arabic **once**, then drill in English.
> **This is where the plateau talk belongs.** B1 is the stage where progress stops being visible week to week. Say so now, before they conclude they have stopped improving.
> **Support:** participle card + frame. **Stretch:** add *once · a few times · never* without naming a date.
> **Arabic ~15%** — the new concept only.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
At A2 you could say *what happened yesterday*. That is a closed box: a finished event at a finished time. This
tense opens a different box — **your whole life up to now**. It is how people ask what you have tried, where you
have travelled, and what you have achieved.

> الجديد إن الزمن ده بيربط الماضي بالنهاردة، والوقت مش بنقوله.

**A note about this stage.** Around B1, progress stops being obvious. You are not slowing down — the things you
are learning are simply less visible than the alphabet was. That is the plateau, and everybody meets it.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Three people describing experiences (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** I**'ve visited** Turkey twice. I**'ve tried** every kind of kebab there is.
> **B:** She**'s travelled** all over Europe, and she**'s met** two famous footballers.
> **C:** We**'ve eaten** at that restaurant before. We**'ve seen** the chef on television.

**Notice what is missing:** no *yesterday*, no *last year*, no date at all. Every sentence means *sometime
before now* — and that is the whole point.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| I've visited Turkey. | زرت تركيا (في حياتي) |
| She's met him before. | قابلته قبل كده |
| We've eaten there. | أكلنا هناك قبل كده |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Play twice. On the second pass learners hunt for a time expression and find none. Let them
> discover the absence rather than announcing it.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The form never changes. Two auxiliaries, one participle.**

| Person | Auxiliary | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | **have** | + past participle | *I **have visited** Spain.* |
| he / she / it | **has** | + past participle | *She **has visited** Spain.* |

**In real speech it is almost always contracted:**

> *I**'ve** · you**'ve** · we**'ve** · they**'ve** · he**'s** · she**'s** · it**'s***

**The negative:**

> *I **haven't** been there.* · *He **hasn't** finished.*

> ⚠️ **الغلطة المتوقعة:** لازم الفعل المساعد يبقى موجود قبل التصريف التالت.

**The one rule that defines this tense:** ***no specific time***. The moment you name a time, English switches
to the past simple.

| | |
|---|---|
| *I**'ve been** to Paris.* | ✅ experience, no date |
| *I **went** to Paris **in 2019**.* | ✅ a date, so past simple |
| *I've been to Paris in 2019.* | ❌ both at once |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Build the auxiliary table, then spend the rest on the no-time rule using the three-row contrast
> above. Do not open the full tense comparison — that is U2-L03.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the contraction is the normal form, not the lazy one.**

> *I have visited* → *I**'ve** visited* — /aɪv/
> *She has tried* → *She**'s** tried* — /ʃiːz/

Saying every *have* in full does not sound careful; it sounds like reading aloud. But **never drop it
entirely** — *"I visited"* is a different tense.

**Part 2 — link the contraction into the participle.** The two words become one shape:

> *I've eaten* → *I-**veaten*** · *We've always* → *We-**valways***

**Part 3 — hear the difference that matters:**

> *I**'ve** worked here.* (still true now) — *I worked here.* (finished, and I left)

> ⚠️ لو الـ**'ve** مش مسموعة، اللي بيسمعك بيفهم زمن تاني خالص.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I've eaten there before, and I've always liked it."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on the contraction, two on linking, one on the meaning-changing contrast. The last
> one is the reason this matters — a swallowed */v/* changes the tense.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *have* or *has*?**
1. I ______ visited Lebanon.
2. She ______ tried scuba diving.
3. They ______ met my brother.
4. My sister ______ travelled to China.

**Exercise 2 — contract it.**
1. *I have seen that film.* → __________________
2. *He has finished his work.* → __________________
3. *We have never tried it.* → __________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I visited Turkey twice in my life.* → __________________________
2. *She have tried sushi.* → __________________________
3. *I've been to Paris last year.* → __________________________
4. *He hasn't finish yet.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** Three real experiences, no dates.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** have · has · have · has. **Ex3:** *I've visited* · *She **has*** · drop *last year* (or
> switch to *I went*) · *finished*. Ex3.3 is the one to linger on.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **have / has + past participle.** *has* only for *he · she · it*.
- Contract it — **I've · she's** — but never drop it.
- **No specific time.** Name a time and you must use the past simple.
- Negative: **haven't · hasn't** + participle.
- A dropped **'ve** is heard as a different tense.
