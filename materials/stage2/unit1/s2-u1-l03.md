# *Have You Ever…?* + *ever / never*

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 1 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit1-present-perfect-experiences.md` · **S2-U1-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Feedback 1.
> **Objectives:** ask about experience with *Have you ever…?* · answer short · use *ever* in questions and *never* in negatives · sustain an experiences mini-interview.
> **This is the highest-value question form at B1.** It opens a conversation with a stranger, and every answer it produces is personal. Learners who own this one frame get real practice for free.
> **Watch the double negative:** ❌ *"I haven't never been"*. In Arabic the negation can be doubled; in English *never* is already negative and takes the **positive** auxiliary — *I have never been*.
> **Preview the pivot lightly, do not teach it.** When an answer gains a **time**, English switches to the past simple: *"Yes — I went in 2019."* Flag it, model it once, and leave it. U2-L03 formalises it.
> **Support:** prompt cards + short-answer cards. **Stretch:** every answer carries a follow-up detail.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Most learner conversations die because the questions run out. *Have you ever…?* never runs out: it works on
food, travel, work, films, fears, anything. And unlike *"What did you do yesterday?"*, it invites a story
rather than a fact.

> والإجابة القصيرة مهمة زي السؤال.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A short interview (📹 in the portal).

> **A: Have you ever been** to Europe?
> **B:** No, I **haven't** — but I**'ve been** to Turkey twice.
> **A: Have you ever tried** Japanese food?
> **B:** Yes, I **have**! I love it. **Have you**?
> **A:** I**'ve never tried** it, actually. **Have you ever eaten** something you regretted?
> **B:** Yes — I **ate** a whole chilli in Bangkok **last year**. Terrible idea.

**Notice three things:** *ever* appears only in the questions · *never* replaces the negative auxiliary · and
in that last answer, the moment a **time** appeared (*last year*), the tense switched to **ate**.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| Have you ever been to…? | عمرك رحت…؟ |
| Yes, I have. | أيوه |
| No, I haven't. | لأ |
| I've never tried it. | عمري ما جربته |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners mark every *ever*, every *never*, and the one past-simple verb.
> Ask why that verb changed — most will spot *last year* themselves.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) The question: auxiliary first.**

> ***Have* you ever** + past participle**?**
> ***Has* she ever** + past participle**?**

> *Have you ever **flown** in a helicopter?* · *Has he ever **been** here?*

**2) Short answers keep the auxiliary and drop everything else.**

| | |
|---|---|
| *Yes, I **have**.* | *No, I **haven't**.* |
| *Yes, she **has**.* | *No, she **hasn't**.* |

Never answer with the main verb: ❌ *"Yes, I been."*

**3) *ever* and *never* sit in the same slot — between the auxiliary and the participle.**

| | |
|---|---|
| question | *Have you **ever** been…?* |
| negative experience | *I have **never** been…* |

> ⚠️ **خد بالك:** كلمة **never** لوحدها بتنفي الجملة، فالفعل المساعد بيفضل إيجابي. بنقول *I have never been*،
> مش *I haven't never been*.

**4) The pivot to watch for.** Add a time and the tense changes:

> *— Have you ever been to Rome?*
> *— Yes, I **have**.* → still experience
> *— Yes, I **went** in 2019.* → a date, so past simple

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on question form, two on short answers, two on the *ever/never* slot. Model the
> pivot once and move on — the full contrast is U2-L03.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *Have you ever* is one word in speech.** Three words, one shape:

> **have you ever** → /həvjuˈevər/ — *"hu-vyu-**EV**-er"*

The stress lands on **ever**. Said as three separate words it sounds like an exam question, not a
conversation.

**Part 2 — the question rises.**

> *Have you ever been to Japan?* ↗️

**Part 3 — the short answer falls, and the auxiliary is stressed.**

> *Yes, I **HAVE**.* ↘️ · *No, I **HAVEN'T**.* ↘️

This is the one place *have* is **not** weak. In a statement it reduces to /əv/; in a short answer it carries
the whole meaning, so it gets the beat.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"Have you ever been to Japan?" — "No, I haven't. Have you?"*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Drill the linked question chorally until it sounds like one word, then the rise, then the
> stressed short answer. The strong-versus-weak *have* contrast is worth naming explicitly.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — build the question.**
1. you / ever / ride a camel → __________________________
2. she / ever / work abroad → __________________________
3. they / ever / eat Korean food → __________________________

**Exercise 2 — short answers.**
1. *Have you ever been to London?* (no) → __________________
2. *Has he ever driven a truck?* (yes) → __________________
3. *Have they ever met you?* (no) → __________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *Have you ever went to Spain?* → __________________________
2. *I haven't never tried it.* → __________________________
3. *Yes, I been.* → __________________________
4. *Have you ever be there?* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — mini-interview.** Ask your partner **three** *Have you ever…?* questions. After each answer, ask
one follow-up.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** *Have you ever ridden a camel? · Has she ever worked abroad? · Have they ever eaten
> Korean food?* **Ex3:** *gone/been* · *I have never tried it* · *Yes, I have* · *been*. Ex4 is the lesson —
> insist on the follow-up, or it becomes a form drill.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- ***Have* you ever + participle?** — auxiliary first.
- Short answers: *Yes, I **have**. / No, I **haven't**.* Never the main verb.
- ***ever*** in questions, ***never*** in negatives — same slot.
- **never** is already negative: *I have never been*, not *haven't never*.
- Add a time and switch to the past simple.
