# *Been* vs *Gone*

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 1 · **Lesson 4**
⏱️ ~30 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L03
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit1-present-perfect-experiences.md` · **S2-U1-L04***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~30 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 6 · Your Turn 3 · Assign 1.
> **Objectives:** separate *has been to* (went and came back) from *has gone to* (went and is still there) · describe where people are · use both in short exchanges.
> **Teach this as one image, not a rule.** *Been* = the person is **back**. *Gone* = the person is **still away**. Draw the two arrows once and the distinction holds; explain it in prose for five minutes and it dissolves.
> **Do not over-teach.** This is a thirty-minute lesson on purpose. The contrast is small, concrete, and fixed by situations — not by analysis.
> **The error is almost always *gone* where *been* belongs**, because *gone* looks like the obvious participle of *go*. It produces *"I've gone to Paris"* from somebody sitting in the room, which is genuinely confusing to a listener.
> **Support:** the contrast graphic stays visible. **Stretch:** a mini-story that needs both.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

By the end, **you'll be able to:**

- Say where someone **has gone** — and is still.

> فرق صغير في الكلمة، بس بيغيّر مكان الشخص في ذهن اللي بيسمعك.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
These two participles come from the same verb and mean opposite things about **where the person is now**. Say
the wrong one and your listener pictures you in another country.

> **been** معناها رحت ورجعت، و**gone** معناها رايح ولسه هناك.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two people looking for a colleague (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Is Layla here?
> **B:** No, she**'s gone** to the bank. She'll be back in twenty minutes.
> **A:** Again? She**'s been** to the bank three times this week.
> **B:** I know. And I**'ve been** there myself twice — the queue is unbelievable.
> **A:** Have you ever **been** to the branch downtown? It's much faster.
> **B:** No, I haven't. Where has everyone **gone** today, anyway? The office is empty.

**Notice:** every ***gone*** describes somebody **not here now**. Every ***been*** describes a completed visit
by somebody who is **back**.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| She's gone to the bank. | رايحة البنك (لسه هناك) |
| She's been to the bank. | رحت البنك ورجعت |
| Where has everyone gone? | الناس كلها رايحة فين؟ |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: for each verb, learners answer one question only — *is that person here
> or not?* That is the entire grammar.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Two arrows. That is the whole lesson.**

| | Meaning | Where they are now |
|---|---|---|
| *He**'s been** to Cairo.* | went **and came back** | 🏠 here |
| *He**'s gone** to Cairo.* | went **and is still there** | ✈️ away |

**1) *been* is the experience word.** It is what you use for your life list, which is why every *Have you ever…?*
question in L03 used it:

> *I**'ve been** to four countries.* · *Have you ever **been** to Europe?*

**2) *gone* is the whereabouts word.** It answers *where is that person?*

> *She**'s gone** home.* · *They**'ve gone** out.* · *My keys have **gone**.*

> ⚠️ **الغلطة الشائعة:** إنك تقول *I've gone to Paris* وإنت قاعد قصاد الشخص. لو رجعت، الصح **been**.

**3) The test that always works.** Ask yourself: **is the person back?**

| Situation | Choose |
|---|---|
| I visited Rome in 2018 and I'm home now | *I've **been** to Rome.* |
| My brother left for Rome this morning | *He's **gone** to Rome.* |

**4) A useful pairing:** *been to* takes a **place**. *gone* often stands with a direction or nothing at all —
*gone home · gone out · gone away*.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Board the two arrows and leave them up. Then run situations aloud and have learners choose. Do
> not accept a choice without the reason — *"because he's back"*.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *'s been* links into one sound.**

> *She's been* → /ʃiːzbɪn/ — the /z/ runs straight into *been*
> *He's gone* → /hiːzɡɒn/

**Part 2 — the two vowels are genuinely different, and they carry the meaning.**

| | Vowel | |
|---|---|---|
| **been** | /ɪ/ — short, or /iː/ in careful speech | *bin · been* |
| **gone** | /ɒ/ — open, back of the mouth | *gone* |

Say them back to back: *been · gone · been · gone*. If they sound similar, your listener cannot tell whether
you are home or abroad.

> والفرق في الحركة هو اللي بيوصل المعنى، فخلي الاتنين واضحين.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"She's gone to the shop, but I've been there already."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes on the linked contraction, two on the vowel contrast. Eyes-closed pair testing works
> well here.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *been* or *gone*?**
1. My sister isn't here — she's ______ to the market.
2. I've ______ to Turkey twice.
3. Where's Ahmed? He's ______ home.
4. Have you ever ______ to a live match?
5. The children have ______ out; the house is quiet.

**Exercise 2 — answer with the right one.**
1. Your friend asks if you know Rome. You visited in 2019. → __________________
2. Your manager asks where Sara is. She left for the airport. → __________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I've gone to Spain three times.* (you're home) → __________________________
2. *She's been to the bank — she'll be back soon.* → __________________________
3. *Have you ever gone to Japan?* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** Two places you have **been**, and one person who has **gone** somewhere today.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** **Ex1:** gone · been · gone · been · gone. **Ex3:** *been* · *gone* · *been*. Ex3.2 is the subtle
> one — *been* contradicts *she'll be back*.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **been** = went **and came back** (or: ever, in your life).
- **gone** = went **and is still there**.
- The test: **is the person back?**
- *Have you ever **been** to…?* — always *been*, never *gone*.
- **been** /ɪ/ against **gone** /ɒ/ — keep the vowels apart.
