# *because · so · so that* — Reason & Result

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 9 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 8
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit9-connecting-ideas-opinions.md` · **S2-U9-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** link a reason with *because* · link a result with *so* · express purpose with *so that* · build longer, connected sentences.
> **Reward smoother, longer sentences — that is the actual objective.** This unit is about discourse, not accuracy. A learner joining three clauses with one clumsy connector has done better than one producing three short perfect sentences.
> ***so* against *so that* is subtle; do not over-explain it.** *So* = result (what happened). *So that* = purpose (why you did it). Two clear examples land it; a lecture does not.
> **The direction of the two main connectors is the whole grammar:** *because* points **backwards** to the cause, *so* points **forwards** to the effect. Learners who see them as a mirrored pair stop swapping them.
> **This begins the run-up to the summative.** U9–U11 are the discourse units, and the B1 speaking task is opinion-plus-discussion. Frame the unit that way from lesson one.
> **Support:** the connector card. **Stretch:** one sentence carrying a reason **and** a result.
> **Arabic ~10%** — mostly English now.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
From here to the end of the stage, the target is no longer new tenses — it is **connected speech**. Two short
sentences say the same thing as one joined sentence, but the joined one sounds like an adult thinking, and it is
what the B1 assessment listens for.

> والهدف بقى إنك تربط، مش إنك تحفظ زمن جديد.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Explaining a decision (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Why did you leave the last job?
> **B:** Mainly **because** the commute was three hours a day, **so** I never saw my family.
> **A:** That's a good reason.
> **B:** I stayed two years **because** I wanted the experience, but in the end I was exhausted, **so** I applied
> somewhere closer.
> **A:** And now?
> **B:** Now I work fifteen minutes away. I took a small pay cut **so that** I could keep the time — and honestly
> it was the best decision I've made.
> **A:** So the money mattered less than the hours.
> **B:** Exactly. I moved **so that** I'd have a life, not a better salary.

**Notice the directions.** *Because* always introduces the **cause** — the commute, the experience. *So* always
introduces the **result** — never seeing family, applying elsewhere. And *so that* gives the **purpose** of a
deliberate choice.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| because the commute was long | لأن الطريق كان طويل |
| so I never saw my family | فمكنتش بشوف عيلتي |
| so that I could keep the time | عشان أحافظ على وقتي |
| in the end | في الآخر |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners mark each connector *cause · result · purpose*. The three-way sort
> is the lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Two connectors, opposite directions.**

| Connector | Introduces | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **because** | the **cause** | ⬅️ backwards |
| **so** | the **result** | ➡️ forwards |

> *I was tired **because** I worked late.* — cause after
> *I worked late, **so** I was tired.* — result after

**Both sentences describe the same two facts.** Only the order and the connector change.

> ⚠️ خد بالك: الاتنين بيوصفوا نفس الحقيقتين، بس الترتيب بيتقلب مع الكلمة.

**1) *so that* = purpose.** It answers *what for?* and usually takes *can · could · would*:

| | |
|---|---|
| *I left early, **so** I caught the train.* | result — what happened |
| *I left early **so that** I could catch the train.* | purpose — my intention |

**2) Punctuation.**

> *…, **so** …* — comma before *so*
> *… **because** …* — usually no comma
> ***Because** …, …* — comma when it starts the sentence

**3) *because of* + a noun.** No verb follows:

> *I was late **because of** the traffic.* ✅ — noun
> *I was late **because** the traffic was bad.* ✅ — clause
> *I was late because of the traffic was bad.* ❌

**4) Answering *why?* in speech.** A short *because* answer is completely natural:

> *— Why didn't you come? — **Because** I was working.*

**5) Do not double the connector.** Arabic can mark both halves; English marks one:

> *Because I was tired, **so** I went home.* ❌ → pick one.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on the mirrored pair, two on *so that*, one on *because of*, one on the doubled
> connector — which is a direct interference error and worth naming.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — a small pause **before** the connector, not after.**

> *I worked late* ⟋ *so I was tired.*
> *I was tired* ⟋ *because I worked late.*

The pause marks the join. Learners who pause *after* the connector — *"because … I worked late"* — sound
hesitant, as if searching for the reason.

**Part 2 — connectors are unstressed.** They are signposts:

> *I was **TIRED** bikəz I worked **LATE**.*

*because* → /bɪkəz/, and in fast speech often just /kəz/.

**Part 3 — keep the two halves at the same speed.** The commonest fluency problem here is a strong first clause
followed by a mumbled second one. The reason is usually the interesting part — do not throw it away.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I left the job because the commute was too long, so now I work nearby."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on pause placement, one on /bɪkəz/, two on keeping both halves audible. Pause placement
> is the highest-value item.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — *because* or *so*?**
1. It was raining, ______ we stayed in.
2. We stayed in ______ it was raining.
3. I was hungry, ______ I made a sandwich.
4. I made a sandwich ______ I was hungry.

**Exercise 2 — join them both ways.**
> *The bus was late. I missed the meeting.*
1. with *because*: __________________________
2. with *so*: __________________________

**Exercise 3 — *so* or *so that*?**
1. I set two alarms ______ I wouldn't oversleep.
2. I overslept, ______ I missed the train.
3. She spoke slowly ______ everyone could understand.

**Exercise 4 — fix the mistake.**
1. *Because I was tired, so I went home.* → __________________________
2. *I was late because of the traffic was bad.* → __________________________
3. *I stayed home so that I was tired.* → __________________________

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** so · because · so · because. **Ex3:** so that · so · so that. **Ex4:** drop one connector ·
> *because the traffic was bad* · *because I was tired*. Ex4.1 is the interference error — worth saying aloud
> twice.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **because** → the **cause**, pointing backwards. **so** → the **result**, pointing forwards.
- Same two facts, different order.
- **so that** = purpose, usually with *can · could · would*.
- **because of** + noun · **because** + clause.
- Never double them: ❌ *Because …, so …*
- Pause **before** the connector; keep it unstressed.
