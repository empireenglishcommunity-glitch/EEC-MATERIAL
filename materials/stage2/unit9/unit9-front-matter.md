## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 9 you'll **argue a point** — connect your ideas smoothly, state an opinion,
> justify it with a reason, illustrate it with an example, and concede a point without losing your position.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تربط أفكارك، وتقول رأيك بسبب ومثال.

**Why this is the keystone unit of the stage:** the B1 summative is **opinion plus discussion**. Units 1–8 built the
grammar; this unit turns it into argument. If you leave here with one reliable three-part shape — opinion, reason,
example — the rest of the stage is winnable.

> والقواعد خلصت. من هنا الشغل على إنك تربط وتقنع.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | *because · so · so that* | give a reason, a result, and a purpose |
| **L02** | *although · but · however* | contrast — and punctuate *however* properly |
| **L03** | Adding & sequencing | organise a turn so it sounds planned |
| **L04** | Opinion & justification ⭐ | **opinion → reason → example**, the keystone shape |
| **L05** | Give your opinion (milestone) 🎙️ | an 8–10 sentence argued turn + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** the four discourse relations — **reason · result · contrast · addition** — plus opinion frames,
hedging, and concession.
**Accent Lab:** list intonation (items rise, the last one **falls**) · double stress on a contrast · the double
pause around *however* · unstressed frames and connectors · protecting the reason so it does not fade.
**Mindset:** from facts to opinions — your view matters, said imperfectly and clearly.
**Arabic support:** ~10% for L01–L04, easing to ~5% at L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-9 opinion turn** — the closest thing yet to the B1 summative.
- The clearest before/after in the programme: routine description at Stage 0, argued position here.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **Reward longer, connected sentences — that is the objective.** This is a discourse unit, not an accuracy unit. A
> learner joining three clauses clumsily has done better than one producing three short perfect sentences.
> **L01's grammar is the mirrored pair:** *because* points **backwards** to the cause, *so* points **forwards** to
> the result. Learners who see them as a pair stop swapping them. Keep *so* against *so that* to two clear examples
> — result against purpose — and do not lecture. Watch the doubled connector (❌ *Because …, so …*), which is direct
> Arabic interference and appears again in L02.
> **L02's *however* is the "sounds educated" upgrade**, and its punctuation is the whole difficulty: **full stop →
> *However* → comma.** Model it visibly. The concede-then-counter move (*I agree that…, however…*) is the
> transferable skill and it reappears in L05 and U10.
> **L03 is the cheapest fluency gain in the stage.** Sequencers add no grammatical difficulty and immediately make
> a turn sound planned. Teach the announce-the-number frame explicitly: it tells the listener the shape **and**
> buys the speaker thinking time. Note the form constraint on *as well as* (+ noun or *-ing*, never a clause), and
> drill the rise-rise-**fall** list contour — without the final fall, learners cannot signal that their turn is
> over, which causes real trouble in U10.
> **L04 is the keystone. Drill opinion → reason → example until it is automatic.** The **example** is the part
> learners skip and the part that convinces. Tell them plainly that the content of the opinion is not assessed, so
> they should pick the side they can explain fastest rather than the side they believe. Teach hedging — absolute
> statements sound aggressive, not confident.
> **L05 is a dress rehearsal for the summative.** Score organisation and justification above accuracy, refuse
> unsupported opinions, and give a **real** counter-argument rather than a prompt.
