# Expressing & Justifying an Opinion

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 9 · **Lesson 4**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L03
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit9-connecting-ideas-opinions.md` · **S2-U9-L04***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** state an opinion with a clear frame · justify it with a reason · support it with an example.
> **This is the keystone skill of Stage 2.** The B1 summative is opinion-plus-discussion, so drill **opinion → reason → example** until it is automatic. If a learner leaves this unit with one reliable three-part shape, the summative is winnable.
> **The three-part shape is the whole lesson.** An opinion alone is a preference. An opinion with a reason is an argument. An opinion with a reason **and an example** is convincing — and the example is the part learners skip.
> **Do not let them hunt for the "right" opinion.** At B1 the content is not assessed; the structure and clarity are. Say so, because learners waste effort deciding what to think instead of practising how to say it.
> **Hedging matters.** *I think · in my opinion · it seems to me* soften a claim appropriately. A learner who states everything as absolute fact sounds aggressive rather than confident.
> **Support:** the opinion-frame card. **Stretch:** opinion, reason, example, then acknowledge the other side with *however*.
> **Arabic ~10%** — mostly English now.

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
This is the sentence shape the B1 assessment is built on, and it is also how educated adults actually argue. An
opinion on its own is just a preference. Add a reason and it becomes an argument; add an example and it becomes
convincing.

> والمثال هو اللي بيفرق: من غيره الكلام يبقى مجرد تفضيل.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two people disagreeing well (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** **In my opinion**, exams are a bad way to measure learning, **because** they test memory under pressure
> rather than understanding. **For example**, I had a colleague at university who was brilliant in every seminar
> and failed every written exam.
> **B:** **I see your point**, **however** I'd argue that exams are at least fair — everyone gets the same
> questions. **Personally**, I think the problem isn't exams, it's what we choose to examine.
> **A:** **That's true**, actually. **Although** I still think coursework tells you more.
> **B:** **It depends on** the subject, doesn't it? **I'd say** for medicine you want an exam. For literature, you
> don't.
> **A:** **Fair enough.** I hadn't thought about it that way.

**Notice the pattern.** Every opinion arrives inside a frame, every frame is followed by a **reason**, and the
strongest turn includes a concrete **example**. Notice also that they acknowledge each other — *I see your point ·
that's true · fair enough*.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| In my opinion… | في رأيي… |
| because they test memory | لأنها بتقيس الحفظ |
| For example… | مثلًا… |
| I see your point. | فاهم قصدك |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners find the three parts in A's first turn. Then ask which turn was
> most convincing and why — the answer is always the one with the example.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The three-part shape. Learn it as one unit.**

> **1 Opinion** → *In my opinion, exams are unfair.*
> **2 Reason** → *…**because** they only test memory.*
> **3 Example** → *…**For example**, my colleague failed every exam but understood everything.*

**1) Opinion frames, from neutral to personal:**

| Frame | Feel |
|---|---|
| *I think…* | the everyday default |
| *In my opinion…* | slightly more formal |
| *I believe…* | stronger conviction |
| *Personally, I…* | marks it as your own view |
| *I'd say…* | soft, tentative |
| *It seems to me that…* | cautious, thoughtful |

**2) Reasons and examples:**

> **reason:** *because · the reason is · that's why*
> **example:** *for example · for instance · such as · like* (casual)

> ⚠️ خد بالك: المثال هو اللي الناس بتنساه، وهو اللي بيخلي رأيك مقنع.

**3) Hedging keeps you credible.** Absolute statements invite argument:

> *Exams are useless.* — an attack
> *In my opinion, exams **tend to** be a poor measure.* — an argument

Useful hedges: *tend to · often · in most cases · it depends on*.

**4) Agreeing and disagreeing politely** — you will need these in L05 and U10:

| Agree | Partly | Disagree |
|---|---|---|
| *I agree. · Exactly. · Fair enough.* | *I see your point, however… · That's true, but…* | *I'm not sure I agree. · I'd disagree, actually.* |

**5) The content does not matter.** Nobody is scoring your view. Pick the side you can explain fastest.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Three minutes on the three-part shape — drill it out loud, several learners, same topic — two on
> frames and hedging, one on the agree/disagree table.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the frame is fast; the opinion is stressed.**

> *In my opinion,* ⟋ *exams are **UNFAIR**.*

*In my opinion* is scaffolding. Stressing it makes you sound as if the fact that it is your opinion is the news.

**Part 2 — the reason gets its own beat, and it must not fade.**

> *…because they only test **MEMORY**.*

The commonest fluency failure in this lesson is a confident opinion followed by a mumbled reason — which loses
exactly the half that makes it an argument.

**Part 3 — *for example* is one chunk with a pause after it:**

> *for example* → /fərɪɡˈzɑːmpl̩/ ⟋

**Part 4 — hedges are said low and quick**, which is what makes them sound reasonable rather than uncertain:

> *It **tends** to be…* — quiet, unhurried

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"In my opinion, exams are unfair, because they only test memory. For example…"*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on frame-versus-opinion stress, two on protecting the reason, one on *for example*. The
> fading reason is the thing to listen for.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — complete the three parts.**
1. *In my opinion, social media is ______ because ______. For example, ______.*
2. *I'd say learning grammar is ______ because ______. For instance, ______.*

**Exercise 2 — add the missing part.** Each has two of the three.
1. *I think homework is useless. For example, I never did mine.* → missing: __________
2. *In my opinion cities are stressful, because they're crowded.* → missing: __________

**Exercise 3 — hedge it.** Make each less absolute.
1. *Exams are useless.* → __________________________
2. *Everyone hates commuting.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — three rounds.** Your partner names a topic; you give opinion, reason and example within twenty
seconds. Swap. Topics: *working from home · public transport · learning a language as an adult · social media.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex2:** 1) a reason · 2) an example. **Ex3:** *tend to be a poor measure · most people find
> commuting tiring*. Give Ex4 at least four minutes — the twenty-second limit forces the shape to become
> automatic.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **Opinion → reason → example.** Drill it until it is automatic.
- The **example** is the part people skip, and the part that convinces.
- Frames: *I think · In my opinion · Personally · I'd say · It seems to me*.
- **Hedge** to stay credible: *tends to · often · it depends on*.
- Acknowledge others: *I see your point, however…*
- Fast frame, **stressed opinion**, and never let the reason fade.
