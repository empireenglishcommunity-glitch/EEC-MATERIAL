# Adding & Sequencing Ideas

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 9 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit9-connecting-ideas-opinions.md` · **S2-U9-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** add ideas with *also · in addition · as well as* · sequence arguments with *firstly · secondly · finally* · organise a longer turn.
> **Structure lowers cognitive load, and that is the real benefit — say it explicitly.** A learner who announces *"there are two reasons"* has bought themselves thinking time and told the listener what to expect. Praise clear organisation; it directly supports the summative.
> **Sequencers are the cheapest fluency gain in the stage.** They add no grammatical difficulty and immediately make a turn sound planned. Learners who resist them usually just have not been told how much they help.
> ***As well as* is followed by a noun or *-ing***, not a clause: ❌ *"as well as it is cheap"* → ✅ *"as well as being cheap."*
> **Watch list intonation.** Items rise, the last one falls. Without that, a list sounds like it never ends — and the listener cannot tell when the turn is over.
> **Support:** the connector card with all four categories from L01–L03. **Stretch:** three organised reasons with an example each.
> **Arabic ~10%** — mostly English now.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Announcing your structure buys you two things at once: the listener knows where you are going, and **you** get a
moment to think. *"There are two reasons"* is a complete sentence that commits you to nothing and gives you time —
which is why confident speakers use it constantly.

> وإنك تقول عدد الأسباب بيوفر لك وقت تفكير.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Someone arguing a point in an organised way (📹 in the portal).

> I think remote work suits some jobs and not others, and there are **three** reasons.
>
> **Firstly**, it depends on the work itself. Writing and analysis are easier at home. **Secondly**, it depends on
> the person — I know people who need an office to function at all. **In addition**, there's the question of
> experience: junior staff learn by overhearing things, which you simply cannot do on a video call.
>
> **As well as** those, there's the commute. I save two hours a day, and I **also** save money.
>
> **Finally**, and this is the part people forget, it depends on your home. Not everyone has a room to work in.
> **So**, overall, I'd say it works — but not for everyone.

**Notice the frame.** He announces **three** reasons at the start, marks each one, adds two more with *in addition*
and *as well as*, and closes with *finally* plus a summary. You always know where you are.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| there are three reasons | فيه تلات أسباب |
| Firstly… Secondly… | أولًا… ثانيًا… |
| In addition… | وكمان… |
| Finally, … | وأخيرًا… |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners count the signposts. Ask what the opening sentence does for the
> speaker — it announces a plan and buys time.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) Adding an idea.**

| Connector | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **also** | before the main verb, or after *be* | *I **also** think… · It's **also** cheaper.* |
| **too** | end of the sentence | *It's cheaper **too**.* |
| **as well** | end of the sentence | *It's cheaper **as well**.* |
| **in addition** | starts a sentence + comma | ***In addition**, it's cheaper.* |
| **as well as** | + noun or **-ing** | ***As well as** saving money…* |

> ⚠️ خد بالك: بعد **as well as** بييجي اسم أو فعل بـ**-ing**، مش جملة كاملة.

> *As well as **being** cheap, it's fast.* ✅
> *As well as it is cheap, it's fast.* ❌

**2) Sequencing an argument.**

> ***Firstly*** *… **Secondly*** *… **Finally*** *…*

Or more casually: *For a start … Then … And the last thing is …*

**3) The frame that does the most work — announce the number:**

> *There are **two** reasons for that. **Firstly**, … **Secondly**, …*

Say the number and the listener relaxes, because they know the shape of what is coming.

**4) Closing a list matters as much as opening it:**

> *So, **overall**, … · **All in all**, … · That's why I think …*

Without a closing signal, listeners keep waiting for another item.

**5) You now have all four relations.** This is the complete toolkit:

| Relation | Words |
|---|---|
| reason | *because · because of* |
| result | *so · that's why · as a result* |
| contrast | *but · although · however* |
| addition | *also · in addition · as well as* |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on addition (with the *as well as* form warning), two on sequencing and the
> announce-the-number frame, two on closing. Keep the four-relation table visible for L04 and L05.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**List intonation: items rise, the last one falls.**

> *Firstly,* ↗️ *… Secondly,* ↗️ *… **Finally**,* ↘️ *…*

That final fall is the audible signal that your turn is finished. Without it, listeners wait — and in a discussion
they will not interrupt a rising tune, so you end up talking longer than you meant to.

**Within a short list, the same shape applies:**

> *It's cheap,* ↗️ *fast,* ↗️ *and **reliable**.* ↘️

**Signposts get their own group and a small pause after them:**

> *Firstly,* ⟋ *it depends on the work.*

**And *also* is unstressed** — it is a joining word, not the news:

> *I **ALSO** think…* ❌ over-stressed
> *I also **THINK** it's better…* ✅

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"Firstly, it's cheaper. Secondly, it's faster. Finally, it's more reliable."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Three minutes on the rise-rise-fall shape — it is the most practically useful prosody in the unit —
> and two on signpost grouping.

  makes you sound more organised **and** gives you time to think — both at once.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **also** before the main verb · **too / as well** at the end · **in addition** starts a sentence.
- **as well as** + noun or ***-ing***, never a clause.
- **Firstly · Secondly · Finally** — and **announce the number** first.
- Close the list: *overall · all in all*.
- Items **rise**, the last one **falls**. ↘️
- You now have all four relations: reason · result · contrast · addition.
