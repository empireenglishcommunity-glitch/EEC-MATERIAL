> **Objectives:** contrast two ideas with *but*, *although* and *however* · place and punctuate *however* correctly · hold two sides of a point.
> ***However*** **is the "sounds educated" upgrade of this unit,** and its punctuation is the tricky bit. It starts a **new sentence** and takes a **comma after it** — not the middle-of-sentence slot where *but* lives. Model it visibly; learners who master it sound noticeably more advanced.
> **The classic error is treating *however* as *but*:** ❌ *"It was expensive however I bought it."* Give them the physical rule — full stop, then *However*, then comma.
> **The other error is doubling, exactly as in L01:** ❌ *"Although it was late, but I stayed."* Arabic marks both halves; English marks one.
> **Nuance is a fluency marker, so name it.** A learner who can concede a point and then disagree is doing something an A2 speaker cannot. That is worth saying out loud.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Real opinions are rarely one-sided. *"The pay was good, **however** the hours were impossible"* is a more honest and
more adult sentence than either half alone. This is also where written English starts to matter — *however* is one
of the clearest signals of an educated register.

> ولاحظ إن **however** بتبدأ جملة جديدة، وبعدها فاصلة. دي أهم نقطة في الدرس.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two views on a city (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** How was Dubai?
> **B:** Impressive, **but** exhausting. Everything works, **although** it doesn't feel very real.
> **A:** In what way?
> **B:** The infrastructure is extraordinary. **However**, you can't walk anywhere. **Although** I was only there
> for a week, I never once left a building without getting into a car.
> **A:** Would you live there?
> **B:** The salaries are excellent. **However**, I'd miss having a street to walk down. **Although** plenty of
> people manage perfectly well, it wouldn't suit me.
> **A:** So a good place to work, not to live.
> **B:** That's it exactly.

**Notice the positions.** *But* sits **inside** a sentence. *Although* introduces a **clause**, at the start or the
middle. *However* begins a **new sentence** and is followed by a **comma**.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| impressive but exhausting | مبهر بس متعب |
| although it doesn't feel real | مع إنه مش بيبان حقيقي |
| However, you can't walk. | لكن، مش بتقدر تمشي |
| it wouldn't suit me | مش هتناسبني |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners mark where each connector sits. The three different positions are
> the grammar.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Three ways to contrast, three different positions.**

| Connector | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **but** | joins two clauses, mid-sentence | *It was cheap **but** slow.* |
| **although** | starts a clause — front or middle | ***Although** it was cheap*, it was slow.* |
| **however** | starts a **new sentence** + comma | *It was cheap. **However**, it was slow.* |

**1) *however* punctuation — the item to get right:**

> *It was expensive. **However**, I bought it.* ✅
> *It was expensive, however I bought it.* ❌
> *It was expensive however I bought it.* ❌

**The physical rule:** full stop → ***However*** → comma.

> ⚠️ خد بالك: **however** بتبدأ جملة جديدة وبعدها فاصلة. مش زي **but** اللي بتيجي وسط الجملة.

**2) *although* can go in either half:**

> ***Although** it was raining*, we walked.* — comma after the clause
> *We walked **although** it was raining.* — no comma

**3) Never double the connector:**

> *Although it was late, **but** I stayed.* ❌ → *Although it was late, I stayed.* ✅

**4) A very useful move — concede, then counter:**

> *I agree that it's expensive. **However**, I think it's worth it.*
> ***Although** I understand the argument*, I don't agree with it.*

This is the shape of a mature opinion, and it is exactly what the summative rewards.

**5) *though*** is the casual spoken version, and it often goes at the **end**:

> *It was expensive. I bought it, **though**.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Two minutes on the three positions, three on *however* punctuation, one on doubling, one on
> concede-then-counter. The concession move is the transferable skill.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *however* takes a real pause after it.** The comma is audible:

> *It was expensive.* ⟋ *However,* ⟋ *I bought it.*

Two breaks: one before *however*, one after. That double break is what makes it sound deliberate rather than
bolted on.

**Part 2 — the contrast gets stress on **both** contrasted words:**

> *It was **CHEAP** but **SLOW**.*
> *The pay was **GOOD**. However, the hours were **IMPOSSIBLE**.*

If only one is stressed, the contrast disappears and the sentence sounds like a list.

**Part 3 — *although* is unstressed and fast:**

> *although* → /ɔːlˈðəʊ/, but squeezed in a sentence — the /ð/ still needs to be there

**Part 4 — end-position *though* falls away quietly:**

> *I bought it, though.* ↘️ — low, quick, almost an afterthought

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"The pay was good. However, the hours were impossible."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on the double pause around *however*, two on double-stressing the contrast, one on
> *though*. The double stress is what makes contrast audible.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **but** mid-sentence · **although** starts a clause · **however** starts a **new sentence** + comma.
- The rule: **full stop → However → comma.**
- Never double: ❌ *Although …, but …*
- **though** is the casual version and often sits at the end.
- Stress **both** contrasted words, and pause twice around *however*.
- Concede, then counter: *I agree that…, however…*
