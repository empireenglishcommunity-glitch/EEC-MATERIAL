## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 5 you'll **talk about the life you don't have** — imagine situations,
> advise by putting yourself in someone's place, say what you wish were true, and then step back and make a real
> plan.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تتكلم عن المتخيل، وتفرّق بينه وبين الواقع.

**Why this completes the reasoning toolkit:** Unit 4 covered what **will** happen. This unit covers what
**would**. Together they let you argue, advise, dream, and plan — which is most of what adults use language for.

> والمفتاح إن الفعل الماضي هنا معناه "مش حقيقي"، مش "حصل".

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Second conditional | *if* + past, *would* — the past form means **unreal** |
| **L02** | Real vs unreal | choose by likelihood, and hear *'ll* against *'d* |
| **L03** | *If I were you…* | advise as imagination, not instruction |
| **L04** | *I wish…* | say what isn't true now — then convert it into a plan |
| **L05** | Dreams (milestone) 🎙️ | an 8–10 sentence talk + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** second conditional · first against second conditional · *were* in unreal conditions ·
*wish* + past for present wishes.
**Accent Lab:** the ***'d*** reduction and its survival · the ***'ll*** / ***'d*** minimal pair · stressed
*wouldn't* · *I wish I* → /aɪˈwɪʃaɪ/ · content-word stress.
**Mindset:** picture the fluent version of yourself — then reverse-engineer the plan.
**Arabic support:** ~15% through L01–L04, easing to ~10% at L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-5 dreams talk**, ending in a real plan.
- The range to say both *"if I finish early, I'll come"* and *"if I had the time, I'd come"* — and to mean
  different things.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **L01's hurdle is conceptual, not formal.** The past tense here is not about the past — *If I had a car* is
> about an untrue **now**. Gloss that once in Arabic, then keep every example **unmistakably imaginary**. If an
> example could be real, learners cannot tell which conditional they are practising and L02's contrast collapses
> before you teach it. Expect U4 interference in both directions: *would* in the *if*-clause, or a present tense
> there.
> **L02 must be anchored to meaning, not form.** The question is *how likely is it?* Show early that the **same**
> situation can take either conditional with different meanings — that single pair converts the rule from
> arbitrary to sensible. And treat the ***'ll*** / ***'d*** minimal pair as an accuracy matter: one phoneme
> carries the entire real/unreal distinction, so an inaudible auxiliary makes the grammar unverifiable.
> **L03 needs an honesty note about *was*.** *"If I was you"* is extremely common among native speakers and
> accepted informally. Teach *were* as the careful standard; do **not** over-penalise *was* in speech. Frame the
> lesson as Stage 1's advice lesson at a higher register: *should* points at the listener, *If I were you* puts
> the speaker in their place.
> **L04 stays on present wishes only.** *Wish* + past perfect is B2; if a learner produces it, confirm it is
> correct and defer it. Do not skip the **wish → plan** conversion at the end — it is ninety seconds and it is the
> mindset content of the unit.
> **L05 closes the conditionals block.** Score the **switching** between real and unreal, not just the second
> conditional, and name the range gain out loud. This is the most concrete progress evidence available while the
> plateau still feels flat.
