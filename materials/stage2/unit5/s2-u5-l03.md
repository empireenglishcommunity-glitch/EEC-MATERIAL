# *If I Were You…* — Hypothetical Advice

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 5 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit5-second-conditional.md` · **S2-U5-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** give advice with *If I were you, I'd…* · use *were* for all subjects in unreal conditions · advise warmly rather than bluntly.
> **On *was* against *were*: teach *were* as the standard, and do not over-penalise *was* in speech.** *"If I was you"* is extremely common among native speakers and is accepted informally. Marking it as an error damages confidence for no communicative gain — name it as the careful form and move on.
> **This is Stage 1's advice lesson again at a higher level.** *Should* was direct; *If I were you, I'd…* is softer because it frames the advice as imagination rather than instruction. Name the link — learners who see the progression use both deliberately.
> **The structure is a fixed chunk.** Do not analyse the subjunctive. *If I were you* is one unit of language, and *I'd* is its automatic partner.
> **Support:** the advice-frame card. **Stretch:** advise, then give the reason and one alternative.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

## 🎯 Your Conquest — هدفك
By the end, **you'll be able to:**
- Give advice with **If I were you, I'd…**
- Use *were* for every subject in an imagined situation.
- Advise without sounding like you are giving orders. 🎙️

> الجملة دي بتقول نصيحتك من غير ما تبقى أمر، لأنك بتتخيل نفسك مكانه.

---

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
In Stage 1 you learned to advise with *should*. It works, but it points at the other person. *If I were you*
does something socially cleverer: it puts **you** in their place, so the advice arrives as empathy instead of
instruction.

> والفرق إنك مش بتأمره، إنت بتتخيل نفسك مكانه.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A friend with a decision (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** They've offered me the job, but it's a two-hour commute.
> **B:** Hmm. **If I were** you, I**'d ask** whether they allow remote days.
> **A:** I hadn't thought of that.
> **B:** And **if I were** in your position, I **wouldn't decide** this week. Take the weekend.
> **A:** My father says I should take it immediately.
> **B:** He might be right. But **if it were** me, I**'d want** to see the contract first.
> **A:** What **would** you **do** about the salary?
> **B:** Honestly? **If I were** you, I**'d ask** for ten percent more. They expect it.

**Notice:** *If I **were** you* — never *was*, in this careful form. And every result uses ***'d*** or
***wouldn't***, because the whole frame is imaginary.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| If I were you… | أنا في مكانك… |
| I'd ask for more. | كنت هطلب أكتر |
| I wouldn't decide yet. | مكنتش هقرر لسه |
| If it were me… | لو كنت أنا… |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners note how many pieces of advice are given without a single
> imperative or *should*. That absence is the point.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**The frame is fixed. Learn it whole.**

> ***If I were you, I'd*** + plain verb.

| | |
|---|---|
| *If I were you, I**'d take** the job.* | ✅ |
| *If I were you, I **wouldn't wait**.* | ✅ negative advice |

**1) *were* for every subject.** In unreal conditions English uses *were* even where you would expect *was*:

| | |
|---|---|
| *If I **were** rich…* | not *was* |
| *If he **were** here…* | not *was* |
| *If it **were** cheaper…* | not *was* |

> ⚠️ **ملاحظة صادقة:** كتير بيقولوا *If I was you* وده مقبول، بس **were** هي الشكل الصح.

**2) Useful variations of the same idea:**

| Phrase | Feel |
|---|---|
| *If I were you…* | the standard, warm |
| *If I were in your position/shoes…* | slightly more formal |
| *If it were me…* | casual, personal |
| *What would you do?* | asking for it |

**3) Compare the registers you now own:**

| | |
|---|---|
| *Take the job.* | an instruction |
| *You **should** take the job.* | advice, direct |
| ***If I were you, I'd** take the job.* | advice, softened by imagination |

**4) Strengthen it with a reason** — advice without a reason is just an opinion:

> *If I were you, I'd ask for more — **because** they clearly need you.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on the frame, two on *were*, two on the register ladder. Drill by giving a problem and
> taking advice around the room; require a reason each time.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the frame is said fast and low, then the advice is stressed.**

> *If-I-were-you* ⟋ *I**'d ASK** for more.*

The frame is grammar, not content, so it gets squeezed. The verb of the advice carries the beat.

**Part 2 — protect the *'d*.** As in L02, one phoneme distinguishes advice from a plain statement:

> *I**'d** ask* /aɪd/ — advice · *I ask* — a habit

**Part 3 — advice needs warm intonation.** A falling, flat delivery makes even a softened frame sound like a
verdict:

> *If I were you,* ↗️ *I'd wait.* ↘️ — gentle rise, then settle

Say it twice: once as a friend, once as an impatient boss. Same words.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"If I were you, I'd ask for more — but it's up to you."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes squeezing the frame, one on *'d*, two on warmth. The friend-versus-boss contrast lands
> instantly and transfers to all advice-giving.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — complete with *were* or the right form.**
1. If I ______ you, I'd apologise.
2. If he ______ here, he'd help us.
3. If it ______ cheaper, I'd buy it.

**Exercise 2 — give advice with the frame.**
1. *My laptop keeps crashing.* → __________________________
2. *I'm always tired in the mornings.* → __________________________
3. *My neighbour plays loud music at night.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *If I were you, I will take the job.* → __________________________
2. *If I were you, I'd to wait.* → __________________________
3. *If I would be you, I'd ask.* → __________________________
4. *If I were you, I'd asked for more.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — advise with a reason.** Your partner describes a real small problem. Give advice with the frame,
a reason, and one alternative.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex1:** were · were · were. **Ex3:** *I**'d** take* · drop *to* · *If I **were** you* · *I'd
> **ask***. Ex4 is the assessment: frame + reason + alternative.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- ***If I were you, I'd*** + plain verb. Learn it as one chunk.
- **were** for every subject in an unreal condition; *was* is common but informal.
- Alternatives: *in your position · in your shoes · if it were me*.
- Register ladder: *Take it* → *You should* → *If I were you, I'd*.
- Attach a **reason**, and keep the delivery warm.
