# Real vs Unreal: 1st vs 2nd Conditional

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 5 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01 · Unit 4
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit5-second-conditional.md` · **S2-U5-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 3 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** choose between the first and second conditional · anchor the choice to likelihood, not to form · switch cleanly between *'ll* and *'d*.
> **Anchor to meaning, not form.** The mixing errors — ❌ *"If I will…"*, ❌ *"If I would…"* — come from treating this as a form choice. The real question is **how likely is it?** Give them that question and the forms follow.
> **Both conditionals can be correct for the same sentence** — with different meanings. *"If it rains, I'll stay"* (it might) against *"If it rained, I'd stay"* (unlikely today). Show a pair like that early; it converts the rule from arbitrary to meaningful.
> **The tell is *'ll* against *'d*.** One phoneme carries the entire real/unreal distinction, which makes this as much an Accent Lab lesson as a grammar one.
> **Support:** the two-column comparison card. **Stretch:** say the same situation both ways and explain the difference in meaning.
> **Arabic ~15%** — the rule glossed once.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
You now have two conditionals, and they answer different questions: *what will happen* and *what would happen*.
Choosing between them is a reasoning skill, not a grammar exercise — and it is one of the clearest markers that a
speaker has arrived at B1.

> ولو خلطت الاتنين، مش هيعرف إنت بتخطط ولا بتتخيل.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Planning, and dreaming, in the same conversation (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** **If** the weather **is** good on Friday, we**'ll go** to the coast.
> **B:** And **if** it **rains**?
> **A:** Then we**'ll stay** here. It's fine either way.
> **B:** **If** I **had** a bigger car, I**'d drive** us all.
> **A:** You don't have a car at all.
> **B:** Exactly. **If** I **did**, I**'d be** very generous with it.
> **A:** **If** you **get** that promotion, you**'ll** be able to buy one.
> **B:** **If** I **got** it, I**'d** buy a boat instead.

**Notice the switch.** Friday's weather and the promotion are **real possibilities** — first conditional. The
bigger car and the boat are **imagined** — second conditional. Same speakers, same conversation, different worlds.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| If it's good, we'll go. | لو الجو حلو، هنروح |
| If I had a car, I'd drive. | لو كان عندي عربية، كنت هسوق |
| If you get it, you'll be able to… | لو جبتها، هتقدر… |
| If I got it, I'd buy a boat. | لو جبتها، كنت هشتري قارب |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners mark each sentence **real** or **imagined**, then check the form
> matched. The last two lines are the same situation both ways — point at them.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Ask: *how likely is this?***

| | **First** conditional | **Second** conditional |
|---|---|---|
| meaning | real, possible | imaginary, unlikely, or untrue |
| *if*-clause | **present** — *If I have time* | **past** — *If I had time* |
| result | ***'ll*** — *I'll call* | ***'d*** — *I'd call* |
| feels like | a plan | a daydream |

> ⚠️ **القاعدة في سطر:** واردة تحصل → مضارع و**will**. متخيلة → ماضي و**would**.

**1) The same situation, both ways — and both correct:**

| | Means |
|---|---|
| *If it **rains**, I**'ll** take a taxi.* | it may well rain |
| *If it **rained**, I**'d** take a taxi.* | it probably will not |

Nothing about the weather changed. The speaker's **estimate** changed.

**2) The two mixing errors, and where they come from:**

> *If I **will** have time…* ❌ — U4's error: future after *if*
> *If I **would** have time…* ❌ — U5 leaking into the *if*-clause

In both conditionals, the *if*-clause never contains *will* or *would*.

**3) A quick decision test.** Could you reasonably bet on it?

| Situation | Choose |
|---|---|
| *I might finish work early today.* | first — *If I finish early, I'll come.* |
| *I am not a millionaire.* | second — *If I were rich, I'd travel.* |
| *There is a real chance of rain.* | first |
| *I do not speak Japanese.* | second |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Three minutes on the table, two on the same-sentence-both-ways pair, two on the two mixing errors.
> Drill by naming a situation and asking which conditional and why — always demand the *why*.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**One phoneme separates real from imaginary. That is the whole pronunciation lesson.**

> *I**'ll** go* → /aɪl/ — the real one
> *I**'d** go* → /aɪd/ — the imagined one

**The minimal pair drill.** Say each pair until a partner can tell them apart with eyes closed:

> *I'll help · I'd help*
> *We'll stay · We'd stay*
> *He'll come · He'd come*

If your listener cannot hear which one you said, they cannot tell whether you are making a plan or a wish — and
there is no context that reliably rescues it.

**Then switch mid-turn**, which is what real conversation requires:

> *"If it rains, I**'ll** take a taxi — but if it snowed, I**'d** just stay home."*

> ولاحظ إن حرف واحد هو الفرق بين خطة وحلم، فخليه واضح.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** the three minimal pairs, then the switching sentence.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Eyes-closed pair testing is the only honest check here. This is the highest-value four minutes in
> the unit: the grammar is useless if the auxiliary is inaudible.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — first or second? Complete it.**
1. If I ______ (finish) early today, I ______ (call) you. (possible)
2. If I ______ (be) taller, I ______ (play) basketball. (untrue)
3. If it ______ (rain) tomorrow, we ______ (cancel). (possible)
4. If I ______ (know) her number, I ______ (give) it to you. (I don't)

**Exercise 2 — say it both ways.** Give the real and the imagined version.
1. *(get the job)* → real: __________ / imagined: __________
2. *(have a free week)* → real: __________ / imagined: __________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *If I will see him, I'll tell him.* → __________________________
2. *If I would have money, I'd travel.* → __________________________
3. *If I was you, I'll wait.* → __________________________
4. *If she had time, she will come.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — decide and justify.** For each, choose a conditional and say **why**: *the bus is late · you are
president for a day · your friend calls tonight · you could speak ten languages.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex1:** finish/'ll call · were/'d play · rains/'ll cancel · knew/'d give. **Ex3:** *If I **see***
> · *If I **had** money* · *If I **were** you, I**'d** wait* · *she **would** come*. Ex4 is the assessment —
> insist on the justification, not just the choice.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **Likely → present + *will*. Imaginary → past + *would*.**
- The same situation can take either; the **speaker's estimate** decides.
- No *will* and no *would* inside the *if*-clause, ever.
- ***'ll*** against ***'d*** is one sound carrying the whole difference.
- Demand the *why* of your own choice.
