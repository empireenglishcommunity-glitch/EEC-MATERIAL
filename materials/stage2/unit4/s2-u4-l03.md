# *Unless* / *if not*

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 4 · **Lesson 3**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01–L02
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit4-first-conditional.md` · **S2-U4-L03***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 4 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** use *unless* + present as the equivalent of *if … not* · give conditions and warnings · convert between the two forms.
> **The trap is the double negative:** ❌ *"unless you don't hurry."* *Unless* is **already** negative, so the verb after it stays **positive**. Keep every example positively conditioned and the trap never opens.
> **Teach it as a rewrite, not as new grammar.** *Unless* = *if … not*. Learners who can convert in both directions own it; learners who learn a separate "meaning" guess.
> **The tense rule is unchanged from L01 and L02** — present after *unless*. That is now three lessons of the same rule, and worth naming as such.
> ***Unless* is common in warnings and conditions**, so the natural contexts are rules, deadlines and consequences. Keep the examples concrete.
> **Support:** the conversion card with both forms side by side. **Stretch:** rewrite a whole set of rules using *unless*.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
*Unless* compresses a whole negative condition into one word. It appears constantly in rules, deadlines,
warnings and instructions — and understanding it wrongly reverses the meaning of the sentence, which in a
workplace or an airport is expensive.

> ولو حطيت النفي مرتين، المعنى بيتقلب. القاعدة: بعد **unless** فعل إيجابي.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A manager briefing a team (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** The deadline is Thursday. **Unless** we **finish** the testing, we **can't** release.
> **B:** We'll finish. **Unless** something **breaks**, we're on track.
> **A:** Good. And remember — **unless** you **book** leave in advance, it **won't** be approved.
> **B:** Understood. What about the client call?
> **A:** It goes ahead **unless** they **cancel**. They usually don't.
> **B:** So, **unless** I **hear** otherwise, I'll prepare the slides.
> **A:** Exactly.

**Notice:** every verb after *unless* is **positive** — *finish · breaks · book · cancel · hear* — and every one
of those sentences could be rewritten with *if … not*.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| unless we finish | إلا لو خلّصنا |
| unless they cancel | إلا لو ألغوا |
| It won't be approved. | مش هتتوافق |
| unless I hear otherwise | إلا لو سمعت غير كده |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners rewrite two of the lines with *if … not* and confirm the meaning
> is unchanged. The equivalence is the lesson.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**One equation.**

> ***unless*** **=** ***if … not***

| With *unless* | With *if … not* |
|---|---|
| *Unless you **hurry**, we'll be late.* | *If you **don't hurry**, we'll be late.* |
| *I won't go unless you **come**.* | *I won't go if you **don't come**.* |
| *Unless it **rains**, we'll walk.* | *If it **doesn't rain**, we'll walk.* |

**1) The verb after *unless* is positive.** The negative is already inside the word:

> ⚠️ **الغلطة المتوقعة:** *unless you don't hurry* — ده نفي مرتين والمعنى بيتقلب. الصح *unless you hurry*.

**2) The tense rule has not changed** — present after *unless*, *will* in the other half:

> *Unless he **calls**, I **won't** wait.*

**3) Clause order flips as usual:**

> *Unless you book, it won't be approved.* = *It won't be approved unless you book.*

**4) A useful fixed phrase:**

> *unless I hear otherwise · unless something changes · unless you'd prefer…*

**5) When to prefer *unless*.** It sounds crisper in rules and warnings. *If … not* is fine everywhere and is the
safer choice when you are unsure.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Four minutes on the equation and the conversion drill in both directions, two on the double
> negative, one on the fixed phrases. Conversion is the assessment.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — *unless* is stressed on the second syllable.**

> **unless** → /ənˈles/ — *"un-**LESS**"*

The first syllable is a schwa and nearly disappears; the /s/ at the end must be clear, or it blurs into the next
word.

**Part 2 — the clause break stays where it was in L01 and L02.**

> *Unless we **FINISH*** ⟋ *we **CAN'T** release.*

**Part 3 — the negative in the main clause takes the beat**, because it carries the consequence:

> *Unless you book, it **WON'T** be approved.*

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"Unless we finish the testing, we can't release."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes on /ənˈles/ with a clear final /s/, two on the stressed negative consequence.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **unless = if … not.**
- The verb after *unless* is **positive** — the negative is already in the word.
- Present after *unless*; *will* in the other half.
- Clause order flips; comma only after a leading *unless*-clause.
- /ənˈles/ — stress the second syllable, keep the final /s/.
