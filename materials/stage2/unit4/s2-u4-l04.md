> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** make realistic plans with conditions · make and soften promises · commit, hedge, or decline clearly.
> **This is the unit's integration lesson.** Correct only **patterned** conditional errors, and by delayed feedback. The target is connected, realistic planning, not sentence-level perfection.
> **The social content is the strength of commitment.** *I'll try* and *I promise I'll* are not interchangeable, and learners routinely over-commit because the stronger phrase is the one they know. Give them a hedge they can actually use.
> **Reward realism.** A plan with a condition and a fallback is better evidence of B1 than a confident plan with none — and it is what real speech sounds like.
> **Support:** the commitment-strength ladder card. **Stretch:** decline something politely while offering an alternative condition.

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Adults negotiate constantly: what you will do, by when, and what happens if something changes. The grammar for
that is already in your hands after three lessons — this one adds the phrases that make it sound committed,
honest, or appropriately cautious.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Agreeing who does what (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Can you send the figures today?
> **B:** I**'ll try**, but I can't promise — I have two meetings. **If** I **don't finish** by five, I**'ll send**
> them first thing tomorrow.
> **A:** That works. **As soon as** you **send** them, I**'ll start** the report.
> **B:** **I promise I'll** send them before nine tomorrow at the latest.
> **A:** Perfect. And **unless** something **changes**, we**'ll present** on Thursday.
> **B:** Agreed. **I'll definitely** be ready by then.
> **A:** If anything comes up, just tell me — I**'d rather** know early.

**Notice the ladder.** *I'll try* is cautious. *I'll send them* is a plan. *I promise I'll* and *I'll definitely*
are strong. Each one tells the listener how much to rely on it.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| I'll try. | هحاول |
| I can't promise. | مش أقدر أوعد |
| I promise I'll… | أوعدك هـ… |
| I'll definitely… | أكيد هـ… |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners rank the commitments from weakest to strongest. That ranking is
> the lesson's real content.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**1) The commitment ladder.** Same future tense, very different weight:

| Strength | Phrase |
|---|---|
| weak | *I'll try. · I'll see what I can do. · I might be able to.* |
| neutral | *I'll send it. · I'll be there.* |
| strong | *I'll definitely… · I promise I'll… · You have my word.* |

**2) A realistic plan has three parts:**

> **the plan** — *I'll send the figures today.*
> **the condition** — *If I don't finish by five,*
> **the fallback** — *I'll send them first thing tomorrow.*

**3) Softeners that keep you honest:**

> *I'll try, but I can't promise. · I should be able to. · Probably, but let me check. · As far as I know…*

**4) Declining well** — refuse the request, not the person, and offer a condition:

> *I can't do it today, but **if** you **can** wait until Monday, I**'ll** do it properly.*

> ⚠️ خد بالك: لو وعدت بحاجة مش متأكد منها، الثقة بتضيع. استخدم *I'll try* وإنت مرتاح.

**5) The grammar underneath is unchanged** — present in the condition, *will* in the result. Three lessons, one
rule.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Three minutes on the ladder, three on the plan-condition-fallback shape. Do not re-teach the
> conditional; only correct it if a pattern appears.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — commitment lives on stress.** The strong words must actually be strong:

> *I**'ll DEFINITELY** be there.* · *I **PROMISE** I'll call.*

Said flat, a promise sounds like a maybe — and the listener will plan accordingly.

**Part 2 — the hedge is quieter and faster.** That contrast is what makes it a hedge:

> *I'll try, but I can't promise.* — lower pitch, quicker, no push

**Part 3 — keep the *'ll* alive under the emphasis.** Learners stressing *definitely* often drop the auxiliary
before it:

> *I**'ll** definitely* — not *"I definitely"*

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I'll try, but I can't promise — I promise I'll send it tomorrow."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Have learners say the same sentence as a firm promise and as a hedge. If a partner cannot tell
> which is which, the delivery is not doing its job.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — rank the commitments** from weakest (1) to strongest (4).
> a) *I promise I'll do it.* b) *I might do it.* c) *I'll do it.* d) *I'll try to do it.*

**Exercise 2 — add a condition and a fallback.**
1. *I'll finish the report today.* → __________________________
2. *I'll meet you at seven.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — soften or strengthen.**
1. Make it weaker: *I'll be there at six.* → __________________________
2. Make it stronger: *I'll send it.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — decline politely.** A friend asks you to help them move house on Saturday. You cannot. Refuse,
give a reason, and offer a conditional alternative.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex1:** b (1) · d (2) · c (3) · a (4) — accept d/b swapped with a reason. Ex4 is the assessment:
> listen for a refusal, a reason, and a condition.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- The ladder: *I might* → *I'll try* → *I'll* → *I promise I'll · I'll definitely*.
- A realistic plan has a **plan · condition · fallback**.
- Hedge honestly; a broken promise costs more than a cautious one.
- Decline the request, not the person — and offer a condition.
- Stress the commitment word, and keep the **'ll**.
