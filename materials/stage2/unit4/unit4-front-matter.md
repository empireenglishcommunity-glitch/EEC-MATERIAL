## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 4 you'll **reason about the future out loud** — set a condition, sequence
> the steps, say what will not happen unless something else does, and promise at the right strength.
> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تربط سبب بنتيجة في المستقبل، وتخطط بشروط وبديل.

**Why this unit is a different kind of skill:** Units 1–3 were about **reporting** — what you have done, what was
happening. This unit is about **reasoning**. It is the first grammar that lets you think aloud in English instead
of describing something already settled.

> ودي أول مرة تستخدم الإنجليزي للتفكير، مش للوصف.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | First conditional | *if* + present, *will* — and never *will* after *if* |
| **L02** | Future time clauses | *when · as soon as · before · after · until* · plus *if* vs *when* |
| **L03** | *unless* | one word for *if … not* — without the double negative |
| **L04** | Plans & promises | commit, hedge, decline — and always have a fallback |
| **L05** | Plans & consequences (milestone) 🎙️ | a 7–9 sentence plan + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** first conditional · future time clauses · *unless* — **all three governed by one rule**:
present tense in the condition or time clause, *will* in the result.
**Accent Lab:** weak but surviving ***'ll*** · stressed ***won't*** · *as soon as* → /əˈsuːnəz/ · *unless* →
/ənˈles/ · the audible clause break between condition and result · stress on commitment words.
**Mindset:** build one small **English-only zone** and stack English onto a fixed daily moment.
**Arabic support:** ~15% through L01–L03, easing to ~10% for L04–L05.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-4 plan** — reasoning aloud, with conditions and a fallback.
- A sentence worth owning: *"I won't improve unless I speak every day."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **One rule carries the whole unit, so teach it once and reuse it.** Present after *if* / *when* / *as soon as* /
> *before* / *after* / *until* / *unless*; *will* in the other half. Presenting L02 and L03 as separate rules
> wastes the transfer that makes this unit efficient.
> **L01's error is ❌ *"If I will study"*** — and it is persistent precisely because it is logical: the future
> *is* meant. Give learners the phrasing to carry, not just the correction. Also anchor the meaning as **real and
> likely**, because U5 introduces the unreal; without that contrast the two conditionals merge.
> **L02's genuinely new content is *if* against *when*.** *If* = it may happen; *when* = it will, timing unknown.
> A final-year student saying *"if I graduate"* has changed the meaning to doubt. And *until* means *up to that
> point*, pairing naturally with *won't*.
> **L03 is a rewrite, not new grammar.** *unless = if … not*. Drill the conversion **both ways**; learners who
> can convert own it. The trap is the double negative — ❌ *"unless you don't hurry"* — so keep every example
> positively conditioned.
> **L04 is integration: correct patterns only, by delayed feedback.** Its real content is social — the
> commitment ladder from *I might* to *I promise I'll*. Learners over-commit because the strong phrase is the one
> they know; give them a usable hedge. Reward a plan that has a **fallback**.
> **L05 closes the real-future block, so prime the pivot.** Say one second-conditional sentence at the end and
> let them hear the tenses move backwards. That thirty seconds materially improves U5.
