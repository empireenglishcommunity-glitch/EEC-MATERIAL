# First Conditional: *if* + present, *will*

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 4 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 3
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit4-first-conditional.md` · **S2-U4-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 7 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 7 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** build the first conditional · talk about real future possibilities and their results · put the present tense after *if* and *will* in the result.
> **The number-one error is *will* in the *if*-clause** — ❌ *"If I will study, I'll pass."* It is logical (the future is meant), which is why it is so persistent. Give them the phrasing to carry: **present after *if*, *will* in the result.**
> **That same rule powers L02 and L03.** Teach it as one rule with three surfaces rather than three separate facts; it is the highest-transfer thing in the unit.
> **Anchor the meaning as *real and likely*.** This matters because U5 introduces the second conditional for the unreal. If learners never hear "this one is for things that may actually happen", the two conditionals merge into one confused structure.
> **Support:** the two-clause frame card. **Stretch:** reverse the clause order and drop the comma correctly.
> **Arabic ~15%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Most adult conversation is about consequences: what happens if we leave late, if the price goes up, if you take
that job. This is the structure English uses for all of it — and it is the first grammar that lets you **reason
aloud** rather than only report.

> والقاعدة: مضارع بعد **if**، والمستقبل في النتيجة.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Two colleagues deciding about a trip (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** **If** we **leave** at six, we**'ll get** there before the traffic.
> **B:** And **if** we **leave** at seven?
> **A:** Then we**'ll be** late. **If** it **rains**, it**'ll be** even worse.
> **B:** Okay. **If** I **finish** this report tonight, I**'ll** be ready by five.
> **A:** Perfect. I**'ll drive if** you **navigate**.
> **B:** Deal. And **if** we **arrive** early, we**'ll get** coffee first.

**Notice two things.** Every *if*-clause uses the **present** — *leave · rains · finish · navigate* — even though
all of it is about the future. And the result half always has ***will*** or ***'ll***.

| English | بالعربي |
|---|---|
| If we leave at six… | لو مشينا الساعة ستة… |
| …we'll get there early. | …هنوصل بدري |
| If it rains… | لو الدنيا مطرت… |
| I'll drive. | أنا هسوق |

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. On the second pass learners underline the verb after every *if* and confirm none of
> them is *will*. Let the pattern be discovered.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Two halves. Each has its own tense.**

| Half | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| the **condition** — *if* … | **present simple** | *If it **rains**,* |
| the **result** | ***will* / *'ll***  | *we**'ll stay** home.* |

> ***If*** *you **study**, you**'ll pass**.*

> ⚠️ **دي أشهر غلطة:** إنك تقول *If I will study*. بعد **if** بييجي مضارع، والمستقبل بييجي في النتيجة بس.

**1) The order can flip — and the comma moves with it.**

> ***If** it rains,* *we'll stay home.* — comma after the *if*-clause
> *We'll stay home ***if** it rains*.* — no comma

**2) The meaning is *real and likely*.** This structure is for things that may genuinely happen:

> *If I miss the bus, I'll take a taxi.* ✅ a real possibility

**3) Other words can replace *will* in the result** once the pattern is solid:

| Result word | Use |
|---|---|
| *will / 'll* | a prediction or decision |
| *might* | a weaker possibility |
| *can* | an ability or option |
| imperative | an instruction — *If it rains, **take** an umbrella.* |

**4) The negative works in either half:**

> *If you **don't** hurry, we**'ll** miss it.* · *If you hurry, we **won't** be late.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** Four minutes on the two-tense split — that is the lesson — then the clause flip, then the
> alternatives to *will*. Drill by giving the *if*-clause aloud and taking results around the room.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — the contraction *'ll* is tiny and it must survive.**

> *I will* → *I**'ll*** /aɪl/ · *we will* → *we**'ll*** /wiːl/ · *it will* → *it**'ll*** /ˈɪtl̩/

Dropped entirely, the sentence loses its future: *"If it rains, we stay home"* means something else — a habit,
not a plan.

**Part 2 — the *if*-clause runs together, then a small break before the result.**

> *If it **RAINS*** ⟋ *we**'ll STAY** home.*

That break is the comma made audible, and it is what tells a listener where the condition ends.

**Part 3 — the two stressed words are the condition verb and the result verb.** Everything else is squeezed:

> *If you **STUDY**, you**'ll PASS**.*

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"If we leave at six, we'll get there before the traffic."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on *'ll* survival, two on the clause break, one on the two-beat stress. The dropped
> *'ll* is the priority because it changes meaning.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — put the verbs in the right form.**
1. If it ______ (rain), we ______ (stay) at home.
2. If you ______ (not / hurry), you ______ (miss) the train.
3. She ______ (call) you if she ______ (have) time.
4. If I ______ (see) him, I ______ (tell) him.

**Exercise 2 — join the halves.**
1. *you / finish early* → *we / go out* → __________________________
2. *the shop / be closed* → *I / come back tomorrow* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *If I will study, I will pass.* → __________________________
2. *If it will rain, we stay home.* → __________________________
3. *If you would help me, I'll finish faster.* → __________________________
4. *We'll be late, if we don't leave now.* (punctuation) → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — true for you.** Three real conditions about this week — *If I ___, I'll ___.*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~7 min.** **Ex1:** rains/'ll stay · don't hurry/'ll miss · 'll call/has · see/'ll tell. **Ex3:** *If I study,
> I'll pass* · *If it rains, we'll stay home* · *If you help me…* · remove the comma. Ex3.4 is worth naming —
> the comma only appears when the *if*-clause comes first.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- **Present after *if*. *will* in the result.**
- ❌ *If I will study* → ✅ *If I **study***.
- Flip the order freely; the comma only follows a leading *if*-clause.
- *might · can · imperative* can replace *will* in the result.
- Keep the **'ll** audible or the future disappears.
