> **Your mission:** by the end of Unit 11 you'll **use the whole stage at once** — tell a detailed story, describe

> مهمتك في الوحدة دي: تستخدم كل حاجة اتعلمتها مع بعض، وميوقفكش سؤال مش متوقع.

which is a different skill from the stage in pieces, and the one the summative actually measures.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Detailed storytelling | scene · events · **earlier** (*had*) · reflection |
| **L02** | Work & study | a reusable ninety-second answer you'll use for years |
| **L03** | Technology, media & city life | structured opinions, **anchored** in real numbers |
| **L04** | Unexpected topics | the anti-freeze kit: buy time, work around, land somewhere |
| **L05** | Story + topic (milestone) 🎙️ | a 4–5 minute two-part performance + **record your milestone** |

**Grammar spine:** integrated — narrative tenses, perfect aspect, passive, relatives, conditionals, connectors and
opinion structure, all in use rather than in isolation.
**Accent Lab:** full connected speech and **the American beat** · rhythm groups across a long turn · job- and
topic-word stress · *had* reducing to *'d* · unhurried time-buyers · protecting numbers at speed.
**Mindset:** use English in real life — and talk **through** the gaps instead of stopping at them.
**Arabic support:** ~5% throughout — near-immersion.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- Your **Unit-11 two-part performance** — the last rehearsal before the B1 summative.
- A polished work answer, and a strategy kit that stops you freezing.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L05 ~40 min, the rest ~35) plus this wrapper.
> **L01 is the payoff of the whole tense system.** Reward rich, flowing narration and correct only patterned errors
> by **delayed** feedback — a learner interrupted mid-story stops telling stories. The new element is the
> **reflection** layer (*"I've never forgotten it"*), which is what makes narration sound told rather than listed.
> Do not ask for a tense checklist: ask for a good story and the tenses arrive because the story needs them. Two or
> three *had* + participle forms are the B1 target; systematic past perfect is Stage 3.
> **L02 is the highest real-world-value lesson in the stage.** The deliverable is a **reusable work story** for
> interviews and introductions. The difficulty is the tense mix, not the vocabulary — learners default to
> present-only, which sounds like a job description rather than a person. Insist on *for/since* from U2-L02, and
> make them explain what their job title **actually means**. Adapt the labels for students and job-seekers; keep the
> structure.
> **L03 keeps topics familiar and opinion-based** — traffic, rent, phones. It previews B2's abstract range without
> entering it. No new grammar; the work is vocabulary, stamina, and above all the **personal anchor**: *"my rent has
> gone up forty percent"* rather than *"prices are high."*
> **L04 is the anti-freeze lesson and the plateau-breaker.** Praise **keeping going** above accuracy. Everything in
> it already exists — circumlocution from U6-L04, hedges from U10-L03, fillers from U10-L04 — assembled into an
> emergency kit and drilled under pressure with deliberately unfair topics. The rule is absolute: **no Arabic, no
> silence.**
> **L05's deliverable is a handover, not a grade.** Note each learner's **two** weakest points and give them as
> written U12 priorities. Two, not five. Have them keep the recording to compare against the summative.
