# Telling a Detailed Story (Tense Mix)

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 11 · **Lesson 1**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: Unit 10 · Units 1–3
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit11-storytelling-real-world-topics.md` · **S2-U11-L01***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** tell a detailed story mixing narrative and perfect tenses · layer background, events and reflection · sustain a long turn.
> **This is the payoff of the whole tense system, so reward rich, flowing narration and correct only patterned errors by delayed feedback.** A learner interrupted mid-story stops telling stories.
> **The new element is *reflection*.** A2 narration reported events. B1 narration adds a present-perspective layer — *"I'd never seen anything like it"*, *"I've told that story a hundred times"* — which is what makes a story feel told rather than listed.
> **Do not ask for a tense checklist.** Ask for a good story; the tenses arrive because the story needs them. If you request three tenses, you get three sentences.
> **Length is the skill here.** Two minutes of connected speech is the target, and it is genuinely hard. Fillers and hedges from U10 are legitimate tools for sustaining it.
> **Support:** the four-layer card from U3-L04 plus a reflection frame. **Stretch:** open in the middle of the action, then fill in the background.
> **Arabic ~5%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
Every tense you have learned since Unit 1 exists so that you can do this: hold the floor and tell somebody
something that happened to you, with enough detail that they can see it. This is not an exercise in grammar. It is
the thing the grammar was for.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
A detailed story, well told (📹 in the portal).

> **I've told** this story too many times, but it still makes me laugh.
>
> **One day** about four years ago I **was driving** to a wedding in Alexandria — my cousin's — and I **was already**
> an hour late **because** I **hadn't checked** the road. **While** I **was sitting** in traffic, my phone **rang**.
> It **was** my mother, asking where the flowers **were**.
>
> **Nobody had told me** about any flowers. **Apparently** I **had agreed** to collect them two weeks earlier, at a
> family dinner I barely remembered. **So** I **came off** the motorway, **found** a florist in a town I **had never
> been to**, and **bought** the most expensive arrangement they **had**.
>
> **When** I finally **arrived**, the ceremony **had finished**. My mother **took** the flowers, **looked** at them,
> and **said** they **were** the wrong colour.
>
> **I've never** been asked to collect anything since. **Honestly**, I think that **was** the point.

**Notice the layers.** It opens with a **present perfect** reflection, sets the scene in the **continuous**, moves
through **past simple** events, uses *had* forms for what happened **earlier**, and closes with a present-perfect
comment. Nobody planned that — the story needed each one.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners mark where the story moves **backwards** in time — *hadn't checked,
> had agreed, had never been*. That backward layer is what "detailed" means.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**A detailed story uses four layers, and you already have all of them.**

| Layer | Tense | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **scene** | past continuous | what was going on |
| **events** | past simple | what happened |
| **earlier** | *had* + participle | what had happened **before** that |
| **reflection** | present perfect | what it means **now** |

**1) The reflection layer is what makes it B1.** Open or close with it:

> ***I've told** this story too many times…* · ***I've never** forgotten it.* · ***I'd never seen** anything like
> it.*

**2) The *earlier* layer explains, and it is the one learners skip:**

> *I was late **because** I **hadn't checked** the road.*
> *I **had agreed** to collect them — I just didn't remember.*

You do not need to master the past perfect here. Two or three *had* + participle forms in a story are enough, and
they add a great deal.

**3) Movers keep a long turn going:**

> *One day… · Anyway… · So… · Apparently… · In the end… · The strange thing was…*

**4) Detail is concrete, not abstract.** *"The wrong colour"* is better than *"she didn't like them."* Numbers,
names, colours and quotes make a story visible.

**5) Sustaining length is a skill.** When you need a moment, use U10's tools — *"Anyway…"*, *"The thing is…"* — rather
than stopping.

> ⚠️ خد بالك: الطبقة اللي بتوضح "قبل كده" هي اللي الناس بتنساها.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on the four layers, two on the reflection frame, two on concrete detail. Do not teach the
> past perfect systematically — that is Stage 3. Two or three uses inside a story is the B1 target.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — a long turn needs rhythm groups, not slow speech.**

> *One day about four years ago* ⟋ *I was driving to a wedding in Alexandria* ⟋ *and I was already an hour late.*

Three groups, each quick, with light pauses. Learners who slow the whole story down sound laboured; the fix is
grouping, not pace.

**Part 2 — the American beat: stress the content, squeeze the rest.**

> *I wəz **DRIVING** tə ə **WEDDING** ən I wəz **AL**ready ən **HOUR** late.*

Read that aloud hitting only the capitals. Everything else compresses. That is what connected speech means in
practice.

**Part 3 — *had* reduces to *'d* and links straight in:**

> *I hadn't checked* → *I **hadn** checked* · *I'd never seen* → *I-**dnever** seen*

**Part 4 — the punchline slows down and drops.** Give the last line room:

> *…and she **said** they were* ⟋ *the **wrong colour**.* ↘️

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** the first two sentences of the model, grouped and beaten.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on grouping, two on the beat, one on the punchline. The beat is the Stage-2 Accent Lab
> thread and this is its best context.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — add the *earlier* layer.** Explain each with *had* + participle.
1. *I was late.* → *I was late because __________________________*
2. *She was annoyed.* → *She was annoyed because __________________________*
3. *I couldn't get in.* → *I couldn't get in because __________________________*

**Exercise 2 — add a reflection.** Open or close each story with a present perfect comment.
1. *I got lost in Cairo for three hours.* → __________________________
2. *My laptop died the night before a deadline.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — make it concrete.** Replace the vague part with a detail.
1. *The food was bad.* → __________________________
2. *He was upset.* → __________________________
3. *It took a long time.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — two minutes.** Tell a real story to your partner for **two full minutes**. They time you and count
your layers. Then swap.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** Give Ex4 five minutes. The timing matters — two minutes feels long, and learning that it is
> achievable is most of the lesson.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Four layers: **scene · events · earlier · reflection**.
- *had* + participle explains what happened **before** — the layer people skip.
- Open or close with a **present perfect** reflection.
- **Concrete detail** beats description: *the wrong colour*, not *she didn't like them*.
- **Group** a long turn; do not slow it down.
- Use *Anyway… · The thing is…* instead of stopping.
