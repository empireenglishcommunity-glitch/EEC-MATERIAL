# Talking About Work & Study

**Empire English** · Stage 2 — **Legionary** (B1) · Unit 11 · **Lesson 2**
⏱️ ~35 min · 📹 Recorded + live · Prerequisite: L01
*Built from: `curriculum/stage2/unit11-storytelling-real-world-topics.md` · **S2-U11-L02***

> [!TEACHER]
> **At a glance (~35 min):** Warm-up 4 · Input 6 · Decode 6 · Accent Lab 5 · Train 8 · Your Turn 4 · Assign 2.
> **Objectives:** describe a job or course across tenses · talk about responsibilities, skills and progression · build a reusable work story.
> **The deliverable is a reusable "work story" — say so explicitly.** This is the single highest real-world-value lesson in the stage: interviews, networking, introductions, visa conversations. A learner who leaves with ninety polished seconds about their work has something they will use for years.
> **The tense mix is the difficulty, not the vocabulary.** A good work answer moves through present (*what I do*), present perfect (*how long, what I've achieved*), past (*how I got here*) and future (*where I'm going*). Learners default to present-only, which sounds like a job description rather than a person.
> **Insist on the *for/since* from U2-L02.** *"I've been there for three years"* is the most common single sentence in this topic and the most commonly wrong.
> **Keep it honest.** Students, unemployed learners and career-changers need the same frame. Adapt the labels, not the structure.
> **Support:** the four-tense work-story card. **Stretch:** add one thing you would change about your job, using U5's second conditional.
> **Arabic ~5%.**

## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
*"So, what do you do?"* is the most predictable question in adult life, and in English it is usually the first one
you get. Answering it well is not vanity — it is the difference between being taken seriously and being asked
something easier.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Someone answering "what do you do?" properly (📹 in the portal).

> **I work** as a logistics coordinator for a shipping company in Alexandria — **I've been** there **for** about
> three years now.
>
> **Basically, I'm responsible for** tracking containers and sorting out problems when something is delayed, which
> **is** most days. **I deal with** drivers, customs and about forty clients, so **most of my day is spent** on the
> phone.
>
> **I didn't plan** any of this. **I studied** accounting, **graduated** in 2018, and **couldn't find** anything, so
> **I took** a temporary job here and **never left**. **Since then I've learned** far more about logistics than I
> ever **did** about accounting.
>
> **The part I enjoy** is problem-solving. **The part I don't** is the paperwork — **although** it's got better
> since we **changed** systems.
>
> **I'm hoping** to move into operations management. **If** a position **comes up** next year, **I'll apply**.

**Notice the movement through time.** Present for the role, present perfect for duration and learning, past for how
he got there, and future for where he is going. That is a person, not a job description.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners label each paragraph's tense. Then ask what would be lost if it were
> all present — the answer is the whole trajectory.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Four moves, four tenses.**

| Move | Tense | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| 1 **What you do** | present simple | *I work as… · I'm a… · I'm studying…* |
| 2 **How long** | present perfect | *I've been there for… · I've been studying since…* |
| 3 **How you got there** | past simple | *I studied… · I graduated… · I took a job…* |
| 4 **Where you're going** | future | *I'm hoping to… · If … , I'll…* |

**1) Responsibilities — three reliable frames:**

> *I'm **responsible for** + noun or -ing* · *I **deal with**…* · *My job **involves**…*

**2) Say what you **actually** do, concretely.** Job titles mean nothing across languages:

> *I'm a logistics coordinator* — meaningless to most listeners
> *…which basically means I track containers and fix delays* ✅

**3) The honest opinion layer** makes you sound human rather than rehearsed:

> *The part I enjoy is… · The part I don't is… · It can be repetitive, but…*

**4) If you are studying,** the same four moves apply:

> *I'm studying engineering · I've been at the university for two years · I chose it because… · I'm hoping to
> specialise in…*

**5) If you are between jobs,** so do they — and honestly:

> *I've been looking since March · I was working in retail before · I'm trying to move into…*

> ⚠️ خد بالك: **for** لمدة، و**since** لنقطة بداية.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on the four moves, two on responsibilities and concreteness, two on adapting for students
> and job-seekers. Every learner must leave with their own four sentences drafted.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — job words are long, and the stress is not where learners put it:**

> *ac-**COUNT**-ing · lo-**GIS**-tics · en-gi-**NEER**-ing · re-spon-si-**BIL**-i-ty · qua-li-fi-**CA**-tion*

Get the stress wrong on your own job title and listeners often mishear the whole word.

**Part 2 — *responsible for* is one chunk:**

> */rɪˈspɒnsəbl̩ fə/* — *for* reduces to /fə/, as in U2

**Part 3 — weak *for* and *since* in the duration sentence.** This is the sentence you will say most often, so it is
worth drilling as a unit:

> *I've been there **fə** about three years.*

**Part 4 — the beat across a work answer.** Content words only:

> *I **WORK** as ə **CO**ordinator fər ə **SHIPping** company.*

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"I work as a coordinator, and I've been there for about three years."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~5 min.** Two minutes on job-word stress — genuinely high value, because these are the words learners say about
> themselves — and three on the duration sentence.

## 🏋️ Train — تدرّب

**Exercise 1 — build your four moves.** One sentence each: what you do · how long · how you got there · where you're
going.

**Exercise 2 — make it concrete.** Rewrite each so a stranger understands it.
1. *I'm an administrator.* → __________________________
2. *I work in IT.* → __________________________
3. *I'm doing a master's.* → __________________________

**Exercise 3 — fix the mistake.**
1. *I work there since three years.* → __________________________
2. *I am responsible of the accounts.* → __________________________
3. *My job is involve a lot of travel.* → __________________________
4. *I am working there for 2019.* → __________________________

**Exercise 4 — interview each other.** Your partner asks *"So what do you do?"* and then **two** follow-ups. Answer
in at least six sentences, moving through the tenses. Swap.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~8 min.** **Ex3:** *I've worked there for three years* · *responsible **for*** · *My job **involves*** · *I've
> been working there **since** 2019.* Give Ex4 four minutes — the follow-ups are what force the tense range.

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Four moves: **what you do · how long · how you got here · where you're going.**
- Duration is **present perfect** with **for** or **since** — never the present simple.
- *I'm **responsible for**… · My job **involves**… · I **deal with**…*
- Explain what your title **actually means**.
- Add the honest layer: *the part I enjoy · the part I don't*.
- Stress: *lo-**GIS**-tics · en-gi-**NEER**-ing · re-spon-si-**BIL**-i-ty*.
