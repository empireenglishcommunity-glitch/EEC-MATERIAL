## 👑 Why this matters — ليه ده مهم
By now your English is good enough that the thing stopping you is rarely knowledge — it is the moment you hit a gap
and freeze. Fluent speakers hit gaps constantly. The difference is that they have something to say **while** they
find their way around.

## 👂 Watch & Listen — اسمع وشوف
Someone caught completely off guard (📹 in the portal).

> **A:** Quick one — what do you think about nuclear power?
> **B:** **Hmm.** **That's a big question.** **Let me think for a second.**
>
> **Honestly, I don't know much about it.** **What I do know is** that it produces a lot of energy without — **what's
> the word** — **the smoke, the pollution**, that side of it. **So in that sense** it seems better than coal.
>
> **On the other hand**, there's the — **I don't know the English word** — **the waste, the material that's left
> over**. **As far as I know** it stays dangerous for a very long time.
>
> **So I suppose** my view is: **it depends on** whether you trust the people running it. **Which**, in most
> countries, **I'm not sure I do**.
>
> **Does that answer the question?** I'm honestly making this up as I go.

**Notice she never stops and never switches language.** She admits ignorance, names what she does know, describes
two words she does not have, hedges twice, and finishes with an actual opinion — from a standing start.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Play twice. Second pass: learners count how many times she buys time or works around a word. Then ask
> whether she sounded fluent — she did, and she knew almost nothing about the topic.

## 🧩 Decode it — القاعدة

**Four moves. Learn one from each.**

| Move | Language |
|---|---|
| 1 **Buy time** | *Hmm. · That's a big question. · Let me think for a second. · Good question.* |
| 2 **Admit and narrow** | *I don't know much about it, but what I do know is… · I can only speak for myself…* |
| 3 **Work around the word** | *What's the word — · It's a kind of… · the thing that…* |
| 4 **Land somewhere** | *So I suppose… · So overall… · It depends on…* |

**1) Buying time is legitimate speech.** These are not filler; every fluent speaker uses them, and they are
completely acceptable in an assessment.

**2) Admitting ignorance is strong, not weak** — as long as you continue:

> *I don't know much about it, **but** what I do know is…*

The *but* is essential. Admitting without continuing is just stopping.

**3) Working around a missing word** is U6-L04's circumlocution under pressure:

> *What's the word — the stuff that's left over? The waste.*
> *It's a kind of… you know… a container?*

Naming the problem out loud (*what's the word*) is normal English, not failure.

**4) Always land somewhere.** End with a position, even a hedged one:

> *So I suppose it depends on… · So overall, I'd lean towards…*

**5) The two forbidden moves:**

> ❌ **silence** — the listener does not know if you have finished
> ❌ **switching to Arabic** — the conversation ends for everyone

> ⚠️ القاعدة: مفيش سكوت، ومفيش عربي. لفّ حول الكلمة وكمّل.

> [!TEACHER]
> **~6 min.** Two minutes on the four moves, two on admitting-and-continuing, two on the forbidden moves. Then get
> straight into Train — this lesson is practised, not explained.

## 🎙️ Accent Lab — معمل النطق

**Part 1 — time-buyers are slow and low.** That is what makes them sound thoughtful rather than panicked:

> *Hmm.* ⟋ *That's a big question.* ⟋ *Let me think.*

Three unhurried beats. Rushed, they sound like panic; unhurried, they sound like consideration.

**Part 2 — fillers are quick and quiet**, and they hold the floor:

> *well · I mean · you know · the thing is* — fast, low, almost under the breath

**Part 3 — *what's the word* is said at normal speed, not apologetically.** It is a normal English move:

> *…without — **what's the word** — the pollution.*

Said with confidence it sounds native. Said as an apology it sounds like failure.

**Part 4 — keep your rhythm through the gap.** The instinct is to slow down when you get stuck; the effect is that
the whole turn sounds like struggle. Keep the beat and route around the word.

> 🎧 **Record & compare:** *"Hmm — that's a big question. Let me think for a second."*

> [!TEACHER]
> **~4 min.** Two minutes on unhurried time-buyers, two on saying *what's the word* confidently. Both are attitude
> as much as pronunciation.

## 🏋️ Train — البروفة الكاملة

**Round 1 — thirty seconds each.** Your partner names a topic you did not prepare. Talk for thirty seconds. You may
not stop and you may not use Arabic.

> *the postal system · whether zoos should exist · electric cars · your city's traffic · beekeeping · school
> uniforms · working night shifts*

**Round 2 — sixty seconds**, harder topics. Use at least one time-buyer and one work-around.

**Round 3 — describe three things** whose English names you do not know. Your partner guesses.

**Exercise 4 — fix the freeze.** What would you say instead?
1. *(long silence)* → __________________________
2. *"…the… إزاي أقول…"* → __________________________
3. *"I don't know."* (and stopping) → __________________________

> [!TEACHER]
> **~10 min.** This is the lesson — give it the full ten minutes. Time each round out loud. **Ex4:** 1) *Hmm, let me
> think* · 2) *what's the word — the thing that…* · 3) *I don't know much about it, but what I do know is…*

## 🧠 Remember — افتكر
- Four moves: **buy time · admit and narrow · work around the word · land somewhere**.
- Time-buyers are **real speech**, not filler.
- Admit ignorance, then continue: *I don't know much, **but** what I do know is…*
- *What's the word — the thing that…* is normal English. Say it confidently.
- **Always land** on a position, even a hedged one.
- ❌ **Silence.** ❌ **Arabic.** Keep the beat and route around.
