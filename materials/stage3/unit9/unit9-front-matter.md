## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** stop sounding like a textbook. Match how you speak to the room you are in, soften without going
> vague, and start collecting the word partnerships that make English sound natural.

**The errors people notice about your English are no longer grammatical.** They are about **fit**. Register mistakes are
almost never experienced as language problems — they land as *he seems cold*, *she was quite blunt*, *he's trying too
hard*. The judgement attaches to you rather than to your English, which is exactly why it is worth fixing on purpose.

**And one myth gets dismantled here: formal is not better.** Too formal is a real error with a social cost, and it is
the one learners produce while believing they are being careful.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- The ability to say one message two ways, and the judgement to know which the room needs.
- ***I'm afraid…*** — two words that let bad news be heard.
- A **collocation notebook**, which is the one habit from this stage that keeps paying after the course ends.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L03 and L04 ~35 min, the rest ~40) plus this wrapper.
> **L01: the goal is APPROPRIATENESS, not formality.** Say it early and repeat it. Learners assume formal equals safer
> and produce stiff, distant English in casual settings — which is as much an error as being too casual, and harder to
> unlearn because they believe they are doing well. Two specific things to teach: **phrasal verbs are the INFORMAL
> option** (most learners have this backwards and have been sounding stiffer than they intended by avoiding them), and
> **consistency beats any single choice** — *I would be grateful if you could sort it out ASAP* is worse than either
> version done properly. The which-was-which prosody test is the assessment; many learners discover their two versions
> sound identical.
> **L02: intonation carries at least half of diplomacy, and here that is literal.** A grammatically perfect polite
> sentence delivered flat is rude. Protect the full Accent Lab. Frame softening as **strength, not deference** — learners
> resist it as weak or dishonest, and the answer is that it leaves the other person room to say no, which is why senior
> people use it most. Then hold the limit: **do not soften away the message.** *I'm afraid there may possibly be a
> slight issue with some of the figures* has communicated nothing. **Soft frame, hard content** is the formula — write it
> on the board. The hardest and most useful drill is holding a refusal through a second ask without hardening the tone.
> **L03 is lexical, so the mechanism is collection, not explanation.** Do not try to teach the tables — teach the
> *make/do/take/give* split, a couple of adjective pairs, and then spend the time setting up the **notebook habit**,
> which is what actually produces improvement. The critical insight to give them: these errors are **comprehensible and
> wrong**, so **nothing in conversation will ever correct them**. *Do a decision* is understood perfectly and the speaker
> never finds out. Also worth saying: fluency at this level is largely a **storage** skill, not a speed skill — every
> collocation owned as a chunk is a piece of sentence you no longer have to build. Protect the self-sourcing activity;
> it is the deliverable and the first thing cut for time.
> **L04: assess JUDGEMENT, not formality.** Two dials — **relationship** (slow) and **stakes** (fast) — and **topic does
> not set register**, which is the assumption to break. The box learners have no model for is **close + high stakes**:
> direct, slow, softened, specific, and still warm. Teach the **gear change** (a signal word plus a pause does the whole
> job) and the **closing signal**, without which the conversation stays heavy. The practical shortcut worth giving them
> is **follow the other person's register** — they are telling you what they need. Of the two failure modes, staying
> casual when stakes rise is more damaging (it reads as not understanding the situation); staying formal when they fall
> is more common (it reads as cold).
> **L05: the explanation is the assessment.** A learner who executes both versions perfectly and cannot say which they
> would send has missed the point. Insist on **two** reasons — relationship and stakes — because "it's more polite" is
> not a judgement. Round 4's blind prosody test catches the vocabulary-only performance, which is half a performance.
