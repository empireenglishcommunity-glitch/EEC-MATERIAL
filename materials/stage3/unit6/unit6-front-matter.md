## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** describe systems, rules and services precisely — and impersonally. By the end you can explain how
> something works without naming anyone, state a requirement without giving an order, and criticise a process without
> accusing a person.

**This unit completes the "structures for precision" set.** Units 1–5 gave you time and certainty. This one gives you
**register** — the ability to sound institutional, objective or professional when the situation asks for it.

**A word of warning that runs through all five lessons.** Once you own the passive you will want to use it
everywhere, and that makes writing vague and speech evasive. The passive is a **choice with a reason**: the doer is
unknown, obvious, or irrelevant. There is a fourth reason — hiding someone — and you should know when you are using
it.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- The ability to **state a requirement with no *you* in it** — the register of every policy and instruction in English.
- ***That should have been checked*** — a complete criticism with no subject anywhere in it.
- ***I'll get it looked at*** — a commitment to an action without a commitment to doing it yourself.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L02–L04 ~35 min, L01 and L05 ~40) plus this wrapper.
> **L01: anchor everything on one fact — only *be* changes tense.** Learners experience this as six new structures; it
> is one structure with a conjugated auxiliary, and the participle never moves. Say it, show it, return to it whenever
> someone stalls. Teach the **four they will actually use** (*is being*, *has been*, *was*, *will be*) and leave the
> monsters alone; if a form feels like an achievement to construct, it is the wrong sentence. Name the
> responsibility-removal use (*mistakes were made*) honestly — learners meet it constantly in corporate English and
> are confused by it if nobody explains it. Flag ***was happened***: no object, no passive.
> **L02 is the register of every rule the learner will ever be given in English**, and the transfer value to U10 is
> very high. Two things to hold: ***should have been*** criticises a process rather than a person (four words, no
> subject anywhere — the same softening move as U4-L02, built with passive grammar), and ***mustn't be*** means
> **forbidden** while ***doesn't have to be*** means **optional**. Learners collapse those two constantly. The
> Accent Lab point is small and decisive: **rules stress the modal, criticisms stress the participle** — a learner
> who stresses *should* produces exactly the confrontation they were avoiding.
> **L03 is often genuinely new even to strong B1s.** Model both patterns slowly and teach the **transformation**
> between them, because that is what makes the structure usable rather than memorised. The infinitive-time table
> (*to be* / *to have been* / *to be + -ing*) is the only real difficulty. Give ***it's supposed to be*** as the
> spoken door into the register. And be honest about the double edge: *it is believed that* can mean *the field
> agrees* or *I have no source*, and the grammar looks identical — so teach them to ask **by whom?** Correct on the
> spot any learner using it to dress up a personal opinion.
> **L04: contrast with the self-action every single time.** *I cut my hair* against *I had my hair cut* is the whole
> lesson, and learners produce the first while meaning the second. The error is **word order**, not concept: the
> object must sit between *have* and the participle, because *I had repaired my car* is a past perfect. Keep it
> everyday — cars, hair, teeth, documents, deliveries — and it will be used the same week. Give the
> *something-happened-to-me* meaning (*I had my phone stolen*) last, as a curiosity.
> **L05: the clarifying questions are the real assessment.** Explaining a process is preparation; **re-explaining it
> when someone hasn't followed** is the professional skill, and repeating the same sentence louder is the universal
> default that never works. Insist on rephrasing. Check participle endings by **recording**, not self-report —
> learners cannot hear their own dropped */t/*. And feed back on passive over-use as a **clarity** problem if it
> appears; that is this unit's failure mode.
