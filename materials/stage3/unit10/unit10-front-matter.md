## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** hold your own in a meeting, present so people can follow, negotiate without conceding for free, and
> run a call from opening to next steps. This is the **professional half of the flagship promise**.

**Everything from Unit 9 stops being an exercise here.** Register, softening, collocations — a meeting is where they get
used or they were never learned.

**The barrier in this unit is rarely language.** It is permission and speed: believing you need a complete, correct
sentence before you are entitled to speak. Meetings in English run on fragments, and the standard for entering one is far
lower than learners think.

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |

| **L04** | Calls & follow-ups | the **purpose sentence**, and a voicemail that gets returned |

**Grammar spine:** meeting, presentation, negotiation and call functions, integrating Stage-3 grammar — conditionals for
bargaining, modal passives for rules, reported speech for summarising, U9 diplomacy throughout.
**Accent Lab:** the **interruption lift-then-drop** · the **two-beat pause** at transitions · pause **after** the
important thing · stress the content not the signpost · numbers **twice and slowly** · final consonants surviving phone
compression · *fifteen* against *fifty* is **stress alone** · warm-and-immovable in negotiation · **dropping pitch for
Q&A**.
**Mindset:** English as a career multiplier.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- A **three-minute presentation with Q&A** — a genuine professional artefact and a strong proof clip.
- ***Building on that…*** — the cheapest entry into any English conversation.
- ***I don't know, I'll come back to you by Thursday*** — the most professionally valuable sentence in the stage.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L04 ~35 min, L01 and L03 ~40, L02 and L05 ~45) plus this wrapper.
> **L01: polite interruption is the skill learners avoid, and avoiding it is why they are silent in fast meetings.**
> Practise it out loud against real speech — nobody learns to interrupt by understanding the phrases. The talk-over
> exercise in the Accent Lab is essential: an interruption delivered **flat is simply not heard**, the learner concludes
> interrupting does not work for them, and the whole skill dies there. The signal **lifts**, the content **drops**, and
> *just* stays unstressed. Teach ***building on that*** first — it is the lowest-risk contribution in English and it gets
> the quietest learner in. And say the permission point explicitly: **meetings run on fragments**, so a finished sentence
> is not the entry requirement. Also reframe **clarifying as high-status** — the person who asks *do you mean the
> deliverables or the timeline?* has just prevented a three-week misunderstanding.
> **L02: delivery is assessed as heavily as content**, and the six-minute Accent Lab must not be shortened. **No slides**
> — slides let learners read and hide. **Three points** is a memory constraint, not a preference; enforce it, because
> deciding *which* three is where the preparation value lies. Two things to demonstrate rather than explain: the
> **two-beat pause** (time each other, because the speaker's sense of pause length is systematically wrong) and closing
> the old point before opening the new one. **Ban the apology opening** — *sorry, my English isn't very good* is
> reflexive and costs credibility before the content starts.
> **L03: the conditional trade is the grammatical heart** — *if you can do X, we could do Y* — and it converts a demand
> into an exchange. The rule with the highest real-world value is **never concede without asking for something**: a free
> concession teaches the other side that pushing is free. Model **warm and immovable**, because learners equate firmness
> with coldness and hardening the voice makes agreement harder for everyone. Do not let the **spoken confirmation** at
> the end be cut; half of post-negotiation disputes come from two people remembering differently.
> **L04: a call removes every visual cue**, so structure and enunciation carry the whole load — this is the most
> practical Accent Lab application in the stage. The **purpose sentence in the first fifteen seconds** is the single most
> important thing, and *is now a good time?* is four words that prevent a whole category of bad calls. **Voicemail
> deserves real practice**: thirty seconds, no feedback, no second chance, number twice. The write-down assessment is
> unforgiving in the right way. Drill *fifteen* against *fifty* explicitly — many learners have never been told the
> **stress** is the distinguishing feature.
> **L05: assess delivery and question-handling equally with content.** A well-structured presentation followed by a
> collapse in Q&A is the weaker performance. Peers **must** ask a question the presenter cannot answer — they will avoid
> writing it out of politeness, so insist, because the *I don't know · I'll find out · by Thursday · here's why it
> doesn't change the proposal* answer is the assessed skill and learners believe not knowing is a failure. **Never
> bluff** is the one absolute rule. Record the consent tier: this is a strong proof-clip candidate.
