## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** own the whole past. Narrate a complex event so clearly that a listener always knows what
> happened first — and explain **why** it happened, not just what.

**This unit completes the English tense system.** After it there is no time you cannot express. What remains in
Stage 3 is not new time but new work: conditions, deduction, register, argument. The past stops growing here.

**The skill being built is bigger than the grammar.** Sequencing and cause are what the Unit-12 benchmark spends
most of its twenty to thirty minutes on, and they are what makes someone easy to talk to. Half of professional
English is telling what happened and explaining why.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Past perfect — *had done* | mark the event that came **first**, and know when not to |
| **L02** | Past perfect continuous | the **activity behind a past state** — *I was tired because I'd been…* |
| **L03** | Sequencing a complex narrative | four past tenses, one **spine**, a marked **flashback** |
| **L04** | Causes & backstory | answer *why?* in **layers** — and survive being asked twice |
| **L05** | Unit task 🎙️ | a **2–3 minute turning point**, told with depth, plus probing questions |

**Grammar spine:** past perfect · past perfect continuous · the four-tense past system integrated · sequence
markers (*by the time, already, before, after, it turned out that*) · causal chaining.
**Accent Lab:** the ***'d*** that carries the tense · */aɪdbɪn/* as two beats with the /d/ intact · narrative
prosody — slow at the complication, **drop pitch** for the flashback, pause **before** the reveal · never stress
*because*.
**Mindset:** owning your progress story — you can now narrate cause and sequence, not just events.
**Arabic support:** **0%** — full immersion.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- A **turning-point narrative** you can tell for the rest of your life — and, with consent, one of the strongest
  proof clips in the programme.
- The ability to answer *why?* twice in a row without repeating yourself.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L02 and L04 ~35 min, the rest ~40) plus this wrapper.
> **L01: the predictable error is OVER-use.** The moment learners meet *had + participle* they start marking every
> past verb, and a story where everything steps backwards has no forwards left. Teach it as the marker for the
> **earlier** event, used only when the order needs clarifying, and give them the test: *are the events already in
> order?* Also drill the ***'d*** in perception — *I finished / I'd finished / I'd finish* — because a tense nobody
> can hear is a tense nobody uses.
> **L02: name the parallel to U1-L02 explicitly.** This is activity-versus-result moved one step back in time.
> Learners who saw that pattern once get this in half the time, but only if you point at it. Teach the form inside
> a ***because*** **clause** — that frame produces almost every natural use — and expect the same quantity error
> (*"I'd been writing three reports"*).
> **L03: ask for a good story, not a tense checklist.** The tenses arrive because the story needs them. Have
> learners identify the **spine** before anything else; once they see the past simple as the main line and the
> other three as departures from it, the over-use problem largely solves itself. Delayed feedback only — a learner
> interrupted mid-story stops telling stories.
> **L04 is the hinge into reasoning.** The same causal machinery becomes argument in U11 and U12. Push the
> follow-up *why* twice, deliberately: the second question is where learners run out of language, and it is exactly
> what the benchmark will do to them. Reward **mechanism over blame** — *"nobody had been checking the updates"* is
> stronger than *"they were careless"* and is the professional register.
> **L05: choose the topic with care and let anyone decline.** A turning point can be genuinely painful. Offer the
> ordinary and the professional alongside the profound, and never press toward the private — a story told
> reluctantly is worse language and worse teaching. Score **sequence clarity before tense accuracy**. If the clip is
> a proof-sample candidate, record the consent tier separately; participation never implies publication.

## End-of-Unit Check — تقييم آخر الوحدة (formative)

  complex story · error-spotting on **over-use** of the past perfect.
