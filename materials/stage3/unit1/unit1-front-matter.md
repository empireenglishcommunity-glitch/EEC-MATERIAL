## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** talk precisely about work in motion — what you've **been** doing, what you **have** done, and
> how long — then assemble it into a ninety-second status update you'll reuse for years.

**Stage 3 is written entirely in English.** Not as a test of endurance, and not to make things harder. The
support is gone because you no longer need it, and the last place to discover otherwise is a professional
conversation. Everything in this unit builds on B1 grammar you already own.

**Nothing here is a new tense so much as a new choice.** You arrive with a present perfect. This unit gives you a
second one and then makes you decide between them, sentence by sentence. That is the shape of the whole stage:
B2 is where the tense system stops growing and starts getting accurate.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Present perfect continuous | *have/has been + -ing* — activity that's still running |
| **L02** | Continuous **vs** simple perfect | choose: **activity and duration** or **result and quantity** |
| **L03** | *How long have you been…?* | duration with precision — and deliberate **vagueness** |
| **L04** | Ongoing projects & efforts | the **four-part status update**: effort · evidence · honesty · direction |
| **L05** | Unit task 🎙️ | a **2-minute talk** + an exchange — and your Stage-3 baseline recording |

**Grammar spine:** present perfect continuous · the continuous/simple contrast · *for* and *since* with duration
nuance · integration with the simple perfect and a future form.
**Accent Lab:** thought groups across a long turn · the squeezed *I've been* /aɪvbɪn/ · contrastive stress on
*been* and on quantities · weak *for* /fə/ · the falling tune on a *How long* question.
**Mindset:** identity lock-in — you are an English speaker now, not someone trying to learn.
**Arabic support:** **0%** — full immersion.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- A **reusable ninety-second status update** — the answer to *"what are you working on?"*, ready for interviews,
  stand-ups and introductions.
- Your **Stage-3 baseline recording**, which is what makes the Unit-12 comparison mean anything.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L03 ~35 min, the rest ~40) plus this wrapper.
> **Frame the immersion on day one.** Say plainly why the Arabic is gone. If a learner stalls, feed an English
> phrase rather than switching language — that is the strategy they need in the room and in every conversation
> after it.
> **L01: do not mark the B1 form wrong.** *"I've worked here for five years"* is correct. Push toward the
> continuous only where the activity or duration is the point. The one error to kill outright is
> *"I'm working here since March"* — a present tense cannot carry duration up to now in English, and this
> interference error survives into C1 if it is not named.
> **L02 is a meaning distinction, not a rule.** Teach the question test — *How long?* → continuous, *How many?* →
> simple — and nothing else. Then say explicitly that *I've lived / I've been living* barely differ, because
> learners at this level are hunting for a hard rule and will happily build one where none exists. The error that
> matters is a **quantity in the continuous** (*"I've been writing three emails"*), which contradicts itself.
> **L03 recycles B1 *for/since* into a better register.** The grammar is old; the naturalness is new. A learner
> who can only answer *for two years* is grammatically fine and conversationally flat. Spend the time on the
> vague column — *ages, a while, on and off, properly only since* — because that is what real answers sound like,
> and a precise answer is often the less useful one.
> **L04 is the highest real-world-value lesson in the unit.** The deliverable is an asset, not an exercise.
> Insist on a **real** project and on all four parts; learners drop **honesty** because it feels like admitting
> weakness, and difficulty is what makes an update credible. Model one yourself, out loud, including a genuine
> problem — that is what gives them permission.
> **L05 is formative and the exchange is the assessment.** A flawless monologue followed by silence is weaker
> than a rough talk with a confident follow-up. Reward **deliberate** aspect choice over reflexive production,
> and make sure everyone asks a question back. Log residual sounds: the U12 clinic is built from these notes.

  *for/since* · error-spotting on the two interference errors.
