## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** read the evidence and say what is probably true — about now and about the past — with an honest
> confidence level attached. Then learn to reason out loud so that other people can follow you.

**This unit is Unit 3's certainty scale pointed at the present and the past.** There you hedged predictions. Here you
hedge conclusions. The skill is the same and the modals are different, which is why the two units sit close together.

**It is also the direct run-up to the end of the stage.** The reasoning chain built in L04 becomes an argument in
Unit 11 and carries most of the twenty-to-thirty-minute conversation in Unit 12. Nothing here is a detour.

## The Lessons — دروس الوحدة

| # | Lesson | You'll be able to… |
|---|---|---|
| **L01** | Present deduction | *must be · might be · **can't** be* — and never *mustn't* |
| **L02** | Past deduction | *must have · might have · can't have* — and */mʌstəv/* |
| **L03** | Speculating | thinking aloud: hedges, revision, and **behaviour not character** |
| **L04** | Evidence & conclusions | the **reasoning chain** — and killing an alternative with a fact |
| **L05** | Unit task 🎙️ | **2 minutes** solving a scenario, then defending it against a partner |

**Grammar spine:** *must / might / could / may / can't* + base for present deduction · the same modals + *have* +
participle for the past · hedging (*it looks like, presumably, I get the impression, in which case*) · reasoning
connectors (*given that, this suggests, which would mean, therefore, on balance*).
**Accent Lab:** **stress the modal** — it is your confidence level · */əv/* on all six modal perfects · */kɑːntəv/*
keeps its **/t/** or the meaning inverts · **certainty falls, doubt rises** · never stress a connector · the upward
step that signals *I'm changing my mind*.
**Mindset:** confident uncertainty — reasoning aloud without needing to be sure.
**Arabic support:** **0%** — full immersion.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- The ability to **offer a hypothesis for correction** instead of asserting something you don't know.
- A **reasoning chain** you can use for any recommendation, diagnosis or disagreement in English.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L03 and L04 ~35 min, the rest ~40) plus this wrapper.
> **L01: the critical contrast is *must* against *can't*, NOT *must* against *mustn't*.** English is not symmetrical
> here — negative certainty is ***can't be***, and *mustn't* is a prohibition. This error is near-universal at B2
> because the symmetry looks obvious, and it **returns in L02** one tense later even in learners who fixed it the day
> before. Drill the pair aloud, both lessons. Also insist that **evidence is part of the sentence**: *"he must be
> busy"* is worth nothing without *"he's not answering"*, and a deduction with no clue is just deciding things about
> people.
> **L02: the reduction is for LISTENING as much as speaking.** */mʌstəv/* is what learners will hear, and unreduced
> production means unreduced expectation — they will wait for a *have* that never arrives and miss the whole
> deduction. This is the second half of the U4 */dəv/* work; do the perception pair */mʌstəv/* against */wʊdəv/*
> deliberately, because confusing a deduction with a conditional changes the meaning completely.
> **L03: reward tentativeness explicitly, and say you are marking the hedging rather than the conclusion.** Learners
> equate confidence with fluency and will over-assert. Two phrases nobody produces unprompted — ***presumably*** and
> ***in which case*** — do most of the work of sounding educated. And hold one line firmly: **speculate about
> behaviour and circumstance, never about character**. *"He must be lazy"* and *"he must be under pressure"* use
> identical grammar on identical facts and are entirely different sentences to receive. Interrupt the first kind.
> **L04 is the transferable one.** Evidence **first**, conclusion last — putting the conclusion first makes the
> evidence sound like a defence of a position already taken. The single most useful phrase in the unit is
> ***"but that wouldn't explain…"***, and insist the rejection contains a **fact**: *"but I don't think so"*
> eliminates nothing. Require the honesty line — *what would prove me right or wrong* — because that is the
> difference between analysis and advocacy, and at B2 they now have enough language to make a bad conclusion sound
> authoritative.
> **L05: the mystery format is doing pedagogical work, not entertaining.** It makes deduction communicative and
> removes the cost of being wrong, which is what lets learners produce long exploratory speech — the exact skill the
> U12 benchmark measures. The scenario has **no single designed answer**, deliberately; Round 3's standard is *fits
> all the evidence*, which is much harder than *sounds plausible*. **Mark the reasoning, not the answer.**
