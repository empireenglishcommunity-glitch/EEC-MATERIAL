## The Campaign — حملة الوحدة
> **Your mission:** talk for ninety seconds about something you have never thought about, build an argument somebody can
> follow, hold your position when it is challenged — and change your mind when you should.

**This is the intellectual peak of the stage.** Everything from Units 1–10 gets used here in service of thinking, and the
next unit is the benchmark itself.

**Two beliefs get dismantled.** That you need an opinion in advance to have something to say — you need a **shape**, not a
view. And that being challenged is an attack — it is **information**, and the strongest available response is sometimes
*you've convinced me*.

**Grammar spine:** thinking frameworks (pros and cons, causes and effects, short against long term) · argument structure
with signposting · concession and rebuttal language · conversational transition moves — all integrating Stage-3 grammar,
U5 hedging and U9 diplomacy.
**Accent Lab:** the **framework marker buys the pause, the pause buys the content** · **contrastive stress on every
comparison**, both halves · the concession **falls** and the hold **rises** · *but* and *although* **unstressed** · under
challenge: **slow down, drop pitch, breathe** · the transition drops, the **return lifts**.
**Mindset:** thinking and arguing in English.
**Arabic support:** **0%** — full immersion.

## The Prize — جايزة الوحدة
- **Three frameworks** that let you speak for ninety seconds on any topic without preparation.
- ***That's a fair point about X — and what I'd say is…*** — the move that lets you concede without collapsing.
- A **four-minute unscripted debate**, which is the clearest evidence of what you can now do.

> [!TEACHER]
> **Unit overview:** 5 lessons (L04 ~35 min, L01–L03 ~40, L05 ~45) plus this wrapper.
> **L01: frameworks are the antidote to freezing.** Teach two or three reusable ones and nothing more — a learner who owns
> *pros and cons*, *causes and effects* and *short against long term* can talk about anything for ninety seconds. Name the
> real diagnosis: the panic is about having **no opinion**, not no English, and a framework **generates content**, which
> is why it removes the fear. The mechanism to demonstrate is that the **framework marker buys the pause and the pause
> buys the content** — say a marker, pause visibly for two seconds, then produce. Learners are startled by how natural it
> sounds from outside. Also legitimise *I don't know enough to be confident, but…*, which they think is cheating.
> **L02: the audible structure is what makes a B2 argument persuasive.** Learners often have the reasoning and no
> signposting, so a good argument arrives as a stream. The part that gets dropped is **the example**, and it is the part
> that convinces — abstract claims propped on abstract evidence persuade nobody, while one instance with a number, a date
> or a name does more than three extra reasons. Enforce **two** pieces of evidence, not five. And make them **argue a
> position they disagree with** at least once: it separates argument construction from conviction and it is the direct
> preparation for L03.
> **L03: the emotional regulation is as important as the language, and it is the harder half.** Teach ***concede then
> hold*** as **one move** — a concession without a hold is collapse, a hold without a concession is stonewalling, and both
> come from treating a challenge as an attack. Insist on **specific** acknowledgement (*that's a fair point about the
> sample size*, not *that's a fair point*), because vague acknowledgement is the commonest weak version. The most
> valuable and most resisted item is the objection that **lands**: separating **principle from mechanism**, revising, and
> **naming what survived**. Learners believe changing their mind is a defeat; it is the highest-status move in the lesson.
> The Accent Lab is physical rather than lexical — the **pitch comparison recording** is objective and learners cannot
> self-assess it live.
> **L04 is exactly what the flagship tests.** Twenty to thirty minutes on everyday *and* professional topics means one
> conversation that **moves**, and the seams are where learners stall. Reframe small talk as an **entry protocol** with a
> job, not filler — learners who dislike it try to skip it and land badly. The transition is four phrases and a pitch
> change: ***speaking of which*** is best because it uses **their** material, and it must be a **question**, not a
> statement. Do not let the **return trip** be cut; without it the other person is left in a heavier register than they
> arrived in. And teach reading the signal — a short answer to your pivot is information, not rudeness.
> **L05: score Coherence and Interaction above Accuracy.** A learner who reasons well and concedes gracefully with three
> grammar slips outperforms a clean speaker who stonewalls, and saying so before they start changes what they attempt.
> **Swapping sides is compulsory** and will be resisted. Round 4 — finding the **distinction**, the two questions the
> topic was running together — is the intellectual payoff and transfers straight to the benchmark. Reset the goal
> explicitly: **nobody has to win**, and a good debate ends with a better position than either side started with.

## End-of-Unit Check — تقييم آخر الوحدة (formative)
- **Quiz:** matching topic frameworks · ordering an argument (claim → evidence → example → conclusion) · choosing a
  concede-then-defend response · small-to-deep transitions.
- **Speaking task:** the L05 four-to-five-minute debate, scored on the B2 rubric with **Coherence and Interaction
  weighted above Accuracy**.
- **Feeds:** the Cohort Tracker — **argument coherence**, **persuasive stress** and **composure under challenge** (all
  named U12 clinic inputs), plus a note of anyone who collapses or stonewalls.
